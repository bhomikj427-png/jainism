## Gloss / Divergence map

Nirvāṇa (Pali: *nibbāna*) is **liberation from conditioned existence** in Buddhism — the "blowing out" or extinguishing of the defilements (greed, hatred, delusion) and thereby the cessation of suffering (*dukkha*) and rebirth (*saṃsāra*).

**Etymology:** Sanskrit *nir-* (out/away) + *vā* (to blow) = "blowing out" — like a flame extinguished. The metaphor is not of annihilation but of the flame ceasing to consume fuel.

**The *Udāna* (8.1–4) definition** (Pali Canon, widely cited): "There is, monks, an unborn, unmade, unconditioned, unbecome. If there were not that unborn, unmade, unconditioned, unbecome, there would be no escape from the born, made, conditioned, become." Nibbāna is described negatively (what it is not) because positive language can only describe conditioned, relational things.

**Two forms:**

| form | Pali | condition | description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nirvāṇa with remainder | *sopadisesa-nibbāna* | alive after full liberation | defilements extinguished; physical body remains until natural death |
| Nirvāṇa without remainder | *anupādisesa-nibbāna* / parinibbāna | at death of an arhat or Buddha | five skandhas no longer arise; complete cessation |

**What ceases in nirvāṇa:** The *arising* of the five skandhas (the conditioned aggregates that constitute apparent personal existence). Since there was never a permanent self, nothing "persists" in liberation — there is no individual consciousness waiting at a location (unlike the Jain siddha at lokāgra).

**Internal Buddhist debate (Theravāda vs. Mahāyāna):**
- **Theravāda:** saṃsāra and nirvāṇa remain genuinely distinct; nirvāṇa is the real cessation of suffering and rebirth
- **Mahāyāna** (especially *Prajñāpāramitā* and Nāgārjuna's *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā*): the dichotomy of saṃsāra/nirvāṇa is itself a product of conceptual grasping; from the perspective of śūnyatā (emptiness), there is no ultimate difference — "form is emptiness, emptiness is form"

## Three-way liberation comparison: Buddhist nirvāṇa / Jain mokṣa / Advaita mokṣa

| dimension | Buddhist nirvāṇa | Jain mokṣa | Advaita mokṣa |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does a self persist? | no — anattā; "self" was never real; no individual at liberation | yes — the siddha is a distinct individual at lokāgra, omniscient | no (Advaita) — individual ātman merges into Brahman |
| What is extinguished? | the arising of conditioned skandhas | all karma matter (physical) | avidyā (ignorance of ātman=Brahman) |
| Is liberation spatial? | no fixed location | yes — lokāgra at universe summit | no location (Brahman is all-pervasive) |
| Mechanism | eightfold path + cessation of craving | karma removal (tapas/nirjarā) + saṃvara | jñāna (recognition of identity) |
| Positive content of liberation | deliberately left unconditioned/inexpressible | infinite knowledge/bliss/energy/perception (*ananta-catuṣṭaya*) | infinite being/consciousness/bliss (*sat-cit-ānanda*) |
