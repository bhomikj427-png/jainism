## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Brahmasūtra** (also **Vedānta-sūtra**, **Uttara Mīmāṃsā Sūtra**, **Śārīraka-sūtra**) of **Bādarāyaṇa** (traditionally identified with Vyāsa, "the arranger") is the founding text of the **Vedānta** darśana. Its core may go back to ~200 BCE–200 CE, with the surviving form ~400–450 CE. It is the primary-text anchor behind [[brahman]], [[atman-vedanta]], [[moksha-advaita]], and [[maya-advaita]]. It has **~555 sūtras** in **4 adhyāyas / 16 pādas / 223 adhikaraṇas**, and the sūtras are **extremely terse** — often two or three words — "intelligible only through a commentary (bhāṣya)." It is one of the three foundations of Vedānta, the **prasthānatrayī** (the principal Upaniṣads, the Bhagavad Gītā, and the Brahmasūtra).

**Project and openings.** The text **systematises and harmonises the Upaniṣads** on Brahman, refuting rivals (notably Sāṃkhya). Its opening is **BS 1.1.1: *athāto brahma-jijñāsā*** ("now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman"), followed by **BS 1.1.2: *janmādy asya yataḥ*** ("[Brahman is] that from which the origination, sustenance, and dissolution of this world proceed") — Brahman as the ultimate conscious source, support, and end of all that is.

**One text, three incompatible readings.** Because the sūtras are so compressed, the great **bhāṣyas founded the rival Vedānta sub-schools** on the *same* words: **Śaṅkara** (8th c.) reads strict non-dualism (**Advaita** — Brahman alone is real; the world and selves are appearance due to māyā; liberation = recognising identity with Brahman); **Rāmānuja** (11th–12th c.) reads **Viśiṣṭādvaita** (qualified non-dualism — Brahman is one but internally differentiated, with selves and matter as real modes; liberation = eternal participation, not identity); **Madhva** (13th c.) reads **Dvaita** (dualism); with further readings by Nimbārka and Vallabha. The Brahmasūtra is thus the corpus's clearest case of a single authoritative text generating divergent, mutually exclusive interpretations.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Brahma Sutras"): Bādarāyaṇa (=Vyāsa); ~200 BCE–200 CE composed, ~400–450 CE surviving form; ~555 sūtras / 4 adhyāyas / 4-pāda subdivision; terse memory-aid aphorisms needing commentary; BS 1.1.1 *athāto brahma-jijñāsā* (quoted); Brahman as sole source/sustainer/end; harmonises the Upaniṣads, refutes Sāṃkhya; prasthānatrayī; the Śaṅkara/Rāmānuja/Madhva bhāṣyas and their schools. **Signal 2** (search aggregation — sreenivasaraos, shlokam, mahavidya, ancientindianwisdom): independently gives Bādarāyaṇa=Vyāsa; ~400–450 CE / core older; **555 sūtras, 4 adhyāyas, 16 pādas, 223 adhikaraṇas**; terseness requiring bhāṣya; prasthānatrayī; Śaṅkara (8th c.) / Rāmānuja / Madhva / Nimbārka / Vallabha with their Advaita / Viśiṣṭādvaita / Dvaita readings spelled out.

The two are independent (an encyclopedia and a cluster of Vedānta-reference sites) and agree on author, dating, structure, the prasthānatrayī status, and the rival commentaries. Medium, not high: the Brahmasūtra Sanskrit was **not fetched from a critical edition** — BS 1.1.1 is quoted by the sources, while BS 1.1.2's wording (*janmādy asya yataḥ*) is the universally-cited form, not verified verbatim from an edition here; exact dating is a broad, debated range.
