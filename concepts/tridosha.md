## Gloss / Divergence map

**Tridoṣa** ("three doṣas") is the central physiological doctrine of Āyurveda: bodily and mental function is governed by three humour-like principles, each a combination of two of the five *mahābhūtas* (great elements; see `pancha-mahabhuta.md`):

| doṣa | elements | qualities | governs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vāta** | ākāśa (space/ether) + vāyu (air) | dry, light, cold, rough, subtle, mobile | movement, communication |
| **Pitta** | tejas/agni (fire) + ap (water) | hot, sharp, light, liquid, oily | digestion, metabolism, transformation |
| **Kapha** | ap (water) + pṛthvī (earth) | heavy, slow, cool, dense, soft, stable | structure, cohesion, lubrication |

**Etymology.** *Doṣa* derives from the root *duṣ*, "to become disturbed/spoiled" — the doṣas are named for their capacity to go *out* of balance, not for being intrinsically harmful; in balance they are the very principles that sustain the body. Monier-Williams glosses individual terms consistently with this (e.g., *kapha* as "phlegm, watery froth"), though the technical tridoṣa-as-system sense is a Āyurveda-specific narrowing beyond the lexicon's bare entries — the same lexicon-vs-commentary pattern seen throughout this corpus's Sanskrit terms.

**Textual home.** The theory "follows from the first chapter of the earliest text on Ayurveda, the Charaka Samhita" — i.e., the *Caraka Saṃhitā*'s Sūtrasthāna gives it its earliest and most systematic treatment (see `charaka.md`). The Suśruta Saṃhitā and the later Aṣṭāṅga Hṛdayam (Vāgbhaṭa) also expound it, with detailed variation across the three texts in emphasis and elaboration that a comparative-review source (International Journal of AYUSH) treats as its own subject of scholarly study — this batch did not fetch that comparative text directly (paywalled/PDF access failed; see confidence note).

**Prakṛti (constitution).** An individual's fixed dosha-ratio at conception is their *prakṛti* — not to be confused with the Sāṃkhya metaphysical *prakṛti* (primal matter); this conflation is flagged on `ayurveda.md` where the term is introduced. Some sources (a PMC personality-psychology paper) report that Charaka and Suśruta additionally classify individuals into **seven types by guṇa-dominance** (sattva/rajas/tamas), layering a second, Sāṃkhya-vocabulary classificatory scheme over the dosha-based one — flagged on `charaka.md`.

**Modern scientific standing.** As with `ayurveda.md`, the mainstream secular-scientific position (per Wikipedia's "Dosha" article) is skeptical: "critics note the concept lacks scientific support," with Harriet Hall's comparison of dosha-typing to horoscopes quoted directly. This is recorded as the external scientific assessment, held separate from — and not overwriting — the internal doctrinal content documented above (§0's "analogy is not identity" cuts both ways: neither smuggling in a physics validation nor smuggling in a debunking as if it settled what the doctrine *says*).

## Reasoning across signals

1. **WebSearch aggregation** (multiple ayurveda-practice sites, cross-checked against one another for internal consistency on the three doṣas' elemental pairings and qualities) — all agree on vāta=air+ether, pitta=fire+water, kapha=earth+water, and the descriptive-quality lists; internally consistent across many independently-run commercial/educational sites, though these are non-scholarly and largely derivative of one another (weak-independence, noted per CLAUDE.md §4).
2. **Wikipedia, "Dosha"** — independently confirms the same elemental composition and qualities, adds the etymology (*duṣ* root) and the Harriet Hall scientific-critique quote; this is the strongest single signal, being separately maintained from the commercial aggregation above.
3. **Wikipedia, "Ayurveda"** — independently corroborates that the theory "follows from the first chapter" of the Charaka Saṃhitā, and gives the same tridoṣa definitions in near-identical language, cross-checked for consistency.
4. **PMC personality-psychology paper** (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3215408) — independently corroborates the Charaka/Sushruta seven-type guṇa-based personality classification as a related but distinct scheme.

Three genuinely separate sources (Wikipedia "Dosha," Wikipedia "Ayurveda," and the PMC academic paper) converge on both the core tridoṣa definitions and their textual home in the Charaka Saṃhitā → **converged**.
