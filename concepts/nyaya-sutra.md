## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Nyāyasūtra** of **Gautama** (a.k.a. **Akṣapāda Gautama**; ~200 CE, with a compositional history possibly stretching earlier) is the founding text of the **Nyāya** ("logic / method") darśana — "the oldest extant Nyāya text." It comprises **five adhyāyas** (each in two āhnikas), ~528 sūtras, and is read through **Vātsyāyana's Nyāyabhāṣya** (~5th c.), the earliest surviving complete commentary. It is the primary-text anchor behind the project's Nyāya epistemology files: [[pramana-nyaya]], [[anumana-nyaya]], [[hetvabhasa]], and [[tarka]].

**The sixteen padārthas (NS 1.1.1).** The text opens by declaring that "perfection / the highest good (*niḥśreyasa*) is attained by correct knowledge of the true nature of sixteen categories": **pramāṇa** (means of valid knowledge), **prameya** (objects of knowledge), **saṃśaya** (doubt), **prayojana** (purpose), **dṛṣṭānta** (example), **siddhānta** (established tenet), **avayava** (members of the syllogism), **tarka** (hypothetical reasoning), **nirṇaya** (ascertainment), **vāda** (honest debate), **jalpa** (wrangling), **vitaṇḍā** (destructive cavil), **hetvābhāsa** (fallacies), **chala** (quibbling), **jāti** (futile rejoinders), and **nigrahasthāna** (points of defeat). These split into epistemology, metaphysics, the procedure of inquiry, and debate-theory.

**The four pramāṇas.** Nyāya admits exactly four: **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference), **upamāna** (comparison/analogy), and **śabda** (reliable testimony). This is the four-pramāṇa scheme that defines Nyāya against the Buddhist/Sāṃkhya count.

**The five-membered inference (pañcāvayava).** A formal proof has five steps: **pratijñā** (thesis: "there is fire on the hill"), **hetu** (reason: "because there is smoke"), **udāharaṇa** (universal rule + example: "wherever smoke, there fire, as in a kitchen"), **upanaya** (application: "the hill has such smoke"), **nigamana** (conclusion: "therefore there is fire").

**Debate-theory and soteriology.** The text systematises honest inquiry (**vāda**) against merely victory-seeking (**jalpa**) and purely destructive (**vitaṇḍā**) disputation, and catalogues the ways an argument fails (hetvābhāsa, chala, jāti, nigrahasthāna). Its ultimate aim (NS 1.1.2) is **apavarga / niḥśreyasa** — liberation of the self from false knowledge (mithyā-jñāna), fault, and suffering, attained through true knowledge of the categories. (Book 3 defends a substantial **ātman**, the engine of Nyāya's long debate with Buddhist anattā.)

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Nyāya Sūtras"): Akṣapāda Gautama; date span (6th c. BCE–2nd c. CE) with composite authorship; five adhyāyas / two āhnikas / 528 sūtras; Vātsyāyana's Nyāyabhāṣya (~5th–6th c.); the sixteen padārthas with NS 1.1.1 paraphrased; the four pramāṇas; the five-membered syllogism with the fire/smoke example verbatim; vāda/jalpa/vitaṇḍā and hetvābhāsa/chala/jāti/nigrahasthāna; NS 1.1.2 liberation purpose; the ātman-vs-anattā engagement. **Signal 2** (IEP, "Nyāya"): Gautama/Akṣapāda, Nyāya-sūtra "c. 200 C.E." as the oldest extant text; Vātsyāyana (~450); the sixteen categories grouped into epistemology/metaphysics/procedure/debate; the four pramāṇas (perception, inference, analogical reasoning, testimony); the five-membered inference; niḥśreyasa via correct apprehension of reality.

The two are independent (an encyclopedia and a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia) and agree on author, date, structure, the sixteen categories, the four pramāṇas, the syllogism, and the soteriology. Medium, not high: the Nyāyasūtra Sanskrit was **not fetched verbatim** (NS 1.1.1/1.1.2 are paraphrased from the encyclopedias, not quoted from a critical edition — only NS 1.2.4's five-fallacy Sanskrit is verified, in [[hetvabhasa]]); the date is a broad range; the enumeration of the sixteen follows the standard list both sources give rather than a fetched edition.
