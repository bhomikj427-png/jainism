## Gloss / Divergence map

**Devanandin**, called **Pūjyapāda**, is — by a very wide margin — **the most-cited author in this corpus**, and until this node he had no file and no name that the corpus recognised as one.

⭐⭐⭐ **The measurement, taken before writing anything:**

```
grep -l "Pūjyapāda"     concepts/*.md | wc -l    →  58
grep -l "Sarvārthasiddhi" concepts/*.md | wc -l  →  58
grep -l "Devanandin"    concepts/*.md | wc -l    →   2
files containing both                            →   0
```

Fifty-eight concept files cite **Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** — the Digambara commentary through which this corpus reads its own anchor text, the [[tattvartha-sutra|Tattvārthasūtra]]. **Two** files cite **Devanandin**, both as a *grammarian*, in Abhyankar's list of the eight post-Pāṇinian system-authors. **No file joins them.** They are one man.

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ How the corpus missed it, exactly — and the answer is not carelessness

The identity is **printed in the abbreviation list of the dictionary this corpus has been reading at first hand since Batch 47**:

> "**Jain., Jain. Vy. = Jainendra Vyākaraṇa by Pūjyapāda Devanandin.**"

And again in the *Paribhāṣāsaṃgraha* census, item (ix): a gloss "on 108 Paribhāṣās or maxims noticeable in the **Mahāvṛtti of Abhayanandin on the Jainendra Vyākaraṇa of Pūjyapāda Devanandin**."

⭐⭐⭐ **[[paribhasha]] read that sentence and tabulated it as:** `| ix | gloss **by Abhyankar himself**, on maxims in Abhayanandin's *Mahāvṛtti* | 108 | Jainendra |`. **The two names were in the line the corpus quoted, and the act of putting them in a table removed them.** ⚠ **The failure was not in reading. It was in compressing — a census keeps the countable columns and drops the nominal ones, and "who wrote the grammar the maxims belong to" was not a column.** ⭐ This corpus's own §2 makes tabulation the prescribed treatment for contested material; **this is the cost of that treatment, observed once and worth remembering.** *(The corpus's account of its own error; no source is involved.)*

### 2. ⭐⭐ It is the inverse of every collision the corpus has catalogued, and the corpus is the party that made it

Eight generators are now on the books, and **all eight describe one name covering several men**. [[durgasimha]] raised the inverse as candidate **#15** — "one man, two designations, one of them not a name" — and left it open, because it had no resolved case.

⭐ **This is the resolved case, and it is worse than #15**: not two designations but **four**, one of them a personal name, one an honorific, one a short form, and one a name that belongs to somebody else as well. ⚠ And the confusion was not in the sources. **Abhyankar joins the names; Vijay K. Jain joins the names; Wikipedia joins the names. This corpus separated them, silently, across fifty batches.** ⭐⭐ **A collision generated by the reader, not by the tradition — the second such this batch, after [[vyadi]]'s transliteration fold, and both were the corpus's own.**

### 3. ⚠⚠ Collision #17, and it is a real one: two grammarians named Jinendrabuddhi, each with a *Nyāsa*

Vijay K. Jain's Preface — **the corpus's own source for the *Sarvārthasiddhi*** — gives **Jinendrabuddhi** as one of Pūjyapāda's names. The corpus holds a node [[jinendrabuddhi]] for a **different** man: the **8th-century Buddhist** grammarian of the "eastern school," author of the *Nyāsa* (*Kāśikāvivaraṇapañjikā*) on the [[kasika|Kāśikā]] and — on the scholarly majority — of the *Viśālamalavatī* on Dignāga.

⭐⭐⭐ **And Abhyankar prints both halves of the collision in one entry, s.v. *Nyāsa*, one sentence apart:**
> "the commentary by **Devanandin on Jainendra grammar**… [is] named **Nyāsa**… In the same way, the learned commentary on the Kāśikāvṛtti by **Jinendrabuddhi**… is very widely known by the name **Nyāsa**."

**Two men, both grammarians, both named Jinendrabuddhi, each with a work called *Nyāsa*, two centuries and two religions apart — and the sentence that names them both calls one of them by his other name.**

⚠ **The corpus asserts no confusion in the literature**: no source consulted says anyone has conflated them, and they *cannot* be one man. ⭐ **Two generators are running at once here** — an **alternate name** and the **genre label *Nyāsa*** (Batch 49's fifth). Typed as `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` per §5, because the map's job is to teach the distinction, not to wait for someone to fall into it.

### 4. ⭐ What he actually is, taken on his own terms

**A Digambara ācārya of the Nandi Saṅgha, of Karnataka, in [[kundakunda|Kundakunda]]'s lineage** (Vijay K. Jain: born to Mādhavabhaṭṭa and Śrīdevī, a **Brahmin** family; his seat at Kanakagiri near Mysore), **guru of King Durvinīta of the Western Ganga dynasty**.

⭐⭐ **The shape of his output is the point.** The nine works attributed to him include a complete rival system of Sanskrit grammar; a gloss on grammar (*Śabdāvatāranyāsa*); the *Sarvārthasiddhi*, "a compendium of Jaina epistemology, metaphysics and cosmology" (Vijay K. Jain); a treatise on prosody (*Chandaśāstra*); 51 verses of ethics (*Iṣṭopadeśa*); 106 verses on meditation (*Samādhitantra*); hymns; and a ritual manual. ⭐ **This is the same figure as [[sakatayana-jain]] and [[hemacandra]] and [[candragomin]] — the monk who writes the grammar *and* the doctrine — and it is now the fourth instance in the corpus.** ⚠ **The corpus does not claim the pattern is causal**; what it can say is that in four separate traditions the person who built a non-Pāṇinian grammar was a religious of a competing school, which is [[katantra]]'s observation about the eight systems seen from the authors' side.

### 5. ⚠ What this does to fifty-eight files, and what it does not

**Nothing in those files is wrong.** They cite the *Sarvārthasiddhi* accurately, and Pūjyapāda is the name under which the Jain tradition knows the commentary. ⭐ What changes is what the corpus can now *see*: that its principal doctrinal witness on [[jiva]], [[ajiva]], [[paramanu]], [[naya]], [[upayoga]] and fifty-odd more **also wrote a grammar in which every one of Pāṇini's technical terms is replaced** ([[jainendra-vyakarana]]) — and therefore that the analytic vocabulary of the *Sarvārthasiddhi* is the vocabulary of a man who had rebuilt a metalanguage from scratch.

⚠⚠ **That is a hypothesis and it is explicitly not asserted.** No source consulted connects the grammar's method to the commentary's; **not one line of the *Jainendra* has been read**; and it is exactly the sort of pleasing structural claim §0 exists to stop. **Recorded as the sharpest open question this node produces**, and as a follow-up with a named test: whether the *Sarvārthasiddhi* uses Jainendra technical terms where a Pāṇinian would use Pāṇini's.

### 6. ⚠ The date: three ranges, and the corpus already carried two of them

**464–524** (Jyoti Prasad Jain 2005, cited in Wikipedia's lead) · **c. 510–600** / "mid-sixth century" (the same article, and search-level scholarship) · "**around 5th century**" (Vijay K. Jain). ⚠ **The corpus's own files say `~6th c.` twice and `~5th–6th c.` once.** ⭐ **This node does not adjudicate** — it records that the corpus's most-cited author is dated across a **century and a half** by sources it already uses, and that the discrepancy was invisible while he had no file. **A person with no node has no place where his date can disagree with itself.**

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand — and decisive here, because the identity statement is in his **abbreviation list**, i.e. an editorial datum rather than an argument. **Vijay K. Jain 2018** (Motilal Banarsidass), the corpus's existing *Sarvārthasiddhi* source, Preface read this batch — independent of Abhyankar and from the Jain side. **Wikipedia, s.v.**, citing Jyoti Prasad Jain 2005 — a third, and it supplies the works-list and the royal connection. ⚠ **All three agree on the identity; none of them argues for it**, which is what one expects of a fact nobody disputes, and is not the same as evidence.

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. The identity is converged; the **date is not**, spanning ~464 to ~600 across three sources the corpus uses; the works-list is unverified beyond the two major items; and the Āyurvedic attribution is search-level only. ⚠ **Not high**, and the reason is worth naming: **the corpus has read a great deal of this man's *Sarvārthasiddhi* in translation and nothing whatever of his grammar**, so half the subject of this node is known only by report.
