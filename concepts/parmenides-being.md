## Gloss / Divergence map

**Parmenides of Elea** (c. 515–450 BCE), founder of the **Eleatic** school, wrote a single hexameter poem, **On Nature**, framed as a **goddess's revelation** and divided into the **Way of Truth** (*alētheia*) and the **Way of Opinion/Seeming** (*doxa*). Roughly 160 of ~800 verses survive as quotations in later authors.

**The argument.** From the single premise — **"it is and cannot not be"** — and the claim that **"what is not" cannot be thought or spoken** ("neither could you apprehend what is not"), Parmenides deduces the "signs" (*sēmata*) of **what-is (*to eon*)**: it is **ungenerated and imperishable** (coming-to-be would require it to arise from not-being), **whole and uniform** (no internal variation or division), **unchanging and motionless** (all change involves not-being), and **complete/perfect** (likened to a well-rounded sphere) — and, on the strong reading, **one**. Consequently **change, motion, and plurality are illusory** — the deceptive content of the mortal Way of Opinion. The Eleatic position was defended by **Zeno of Elea** (the paradoxes of motion and plurality) and **Melissus of Samos**.

**The interpretive fault-line.** What Parmenides *meant* is contested: **strict numerical monism** (only one thing exists, the sensible world is unreal); a **logical/dialectical** reading (he exposes incoherences in earlier cosmology, not literal monism); a **meta-principle** reading (he states what *any* fundamental entity must be, not what uniquely exists); and a **modal** reading (necessary vs contingent being, leaving room for plural existents). Plato and Aristotle preferred an **aspectual** reading (two aspects of reality — the changeless intelligible and the mutable sensible). The later Presocratics (Empedocles, Anaxagoras, the atomists) built their pluralisms precisely to satisfy his strictures that what truly *is* must be ungenerated, imperishable, and unchanging.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (SEP, "Parmenides"): dates c. 515–450 BCE; the poem as goddess-revelation with Way of Truth / Way of Opinion; the premise "it is and cannot not be" and "neither could you apprehend what is not"; the sēmata (ungenerated-imperishable, whole-uniform, unchanging-motionless, perfect-spherical); change/plurality excluded as involving not-being; Zeno and Melissus; the four-plus interpretive options (strict monism / logical-dialectical / meta-principle / modal) and the ancient aspectual reading; the atomists' response. **Signal 2** (Wikipedia, "Parmenides"): dates c. 515–450 BCE; On Nature in dactylic hexameter (~160 of ~800 lines), proem + Way of Truth + Way of Opinion; "that which is, is, and that which is not, is not"; what-is as "unbegotten and indestructible," "whole and unique," "immovable," "perfect"; change/motion/plurality illusory; the Eleatic school (Zeno, Melissus).

The two are independent (a scholarly encyclopedia and a reference article) and agree on dates, the poem's structure, the core argument, the properties of Being, and the Eleatic succession. Medium, not high: the fragments themselves (Diels-Kranz B-fragments via Simplicius, Sextus, etc.) were not fetched verbatim — only the short phrases the two summaries quote; and the central interpretive question (just how strong Parmenides' monism is) is genuinely unsettled, so the gloss reports the readings rather than picking one.
