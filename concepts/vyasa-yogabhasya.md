## Gloss / Divergence map

"**Vyāsa**" is the name traditionally attached to the *Yoga-Bhāṣya* (c. 400 CE), the foundational commentary on Patañjali's *Yoga Sūtra* that effectively defines how the Yoga darśana is read — supplying its Sāṃkhya-Yoga metaphysics, its analysis of the *cittavṛtti*, and the meditative architecture. The attribution is the live problem, and it is genuinely **contested**:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| a distinct commentator "Vyāsa" (the legendary sage) wrote the bhāṣya on Patañjali's sūtra | traditional view — but clearly stated as *separate* authorship only from the 14th-c. *Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha* onward | two authors, sūtra and bhāṣya distinct |
| sūtra **and** bhāṣya are one work (*Pātañjalayogaśāstra*) by a single author, Patañjali; "two authors" is a post-~1100 CE misreading | **Philipp A. Maas** (philology), now widely cited | no separate "Vyāsa" at all |
| bhāṣya by Vindhyavāsin (late 4th c. CE) | minority scholarly view | a third, different attribution |

In every case the "Vyāsa" of the Yogabhāṣya is **not** the legendary Mahābhārata Vyāsa — that identification is a later honorific overlay.

**Independence caveat (§4 trap).** The dominant modern thesis (single-author *Pātañjalayogaśāstra*) traces to *one* scholar, Maas; Wikipedia and the wisdomlib essay both merely *report* Maas, so they are not independent confirmations. Confidence is therefore capped **low** — the convergence is on reporting one source, not on independent agreement.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Yoga Sutras of Patanjali"** — summarizes Maas's single-author thesis and the post-14th-c. emergence of separate "Vyāsa" authorship.
2. **Philipp A. Maas, *Pātañjalayogaśāstra* philology** (reported via wisdomlib's *Bhāṣya-Vivaraṇa* study) — the primary scholarly source for the single-author argument.
3. **Traditional attribution (post-*Sarvadarśanasaṅgraha*)** — independent of Maas, but the position Maas argues against.

The authorship is genuinely split and the modern consensus rests on a single philologist → **contested**, confidence **low**. The dispute *is* the finding.
