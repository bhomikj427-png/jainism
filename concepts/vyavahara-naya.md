## Gloss

The *vyavahāra-naya* is the **practical or systematic standpoint** — the third of the seven nayas listed in TS 1.33, and the third and most discriminating of the three dravyārthika nayas. The word *vyavahāra* means practical transaction, convention, or systematic use. This standpoint takes the unified class established by saṃgraha-naya and **differentiates it into practically distinct members** — it draws useful distinctions that saṃgraha's maximal abstraction deliberately ignored.

**Classic example:** After saṃgraha says "this is clay" (attending to the material class), vyavahāra says "this is a pot, that is a cup" — distinguishing between particular objects within the class of clay-things by their form, function, and practical identity. The difference is real from the standpoint of practical use; yet vyavahāra also recognizes that "the general aspects are not apart from the specific aspects" (WisdomLib, citing Nayavāda tradition). The class is not abolished; it is refined.

**Second example:** After saṃgraha establishes "all is substance (dravya)," vyavahāra distinguishes: "this is a jīva, that is pudgala, this is ākāśa." Each member fulfils the definition of dravya (saṃgraha's achievement), but they are not interchangeable from the practical standpoint (vyavahāra's contribution).

**Relation to saṃgraha:** WisdomLib describes vyavahāra as "the counterpoint to saṃgraha naya" — it emphasises the specific aspects that saṃgraha left undifferentiated. The two nayas are sequential: saṃgraha groups, vyavahāra distinguishes within the group. Vyavahāra is therefore the "purer" (more discriminating) of the two.

**Position in the naya hierarchy:** Vyavahāra is the most specific of the dravyārthika nayas. The paryāyārthika nayas (4–7) go further — ṛjusūtra, for example, restricts attention to the present-moment mode only. Vyavahāra still operates at the level of stable practical categories (substances, species, types); ṛjusūtra operates at the level of momentary states.

**The name-trap:** Vyavahāra-naya uses the same word (*vyavahāra*) as the Advaita Vedānta term for "conventional/transactional reality" (*vyavahārika satya* in the two-truths scheme). These are **not** the same concept. Advaita's *vyavahārika* is one level in a two-tier metaphysical hierarchy (conventional below, paramārthika above); Jain vyavahāra-naya is one standpoint in a seven-standpoint epistemological system where all nayas are equally partial and none is "higher."

## Why vyavahāra matters for anekāntavāda

Vyavahāra-naya is the standpoint that grounds practical knowledge — classifications, distinctions, and transactional language. Without it, saṃgraha's abstraction makes everything the same and action impossible. Together, saṃgraha (unity) and vyavahāra (distinction) illustrate anekāntavāda's core thesis: reality is neither purely one nor purely many — both perspectives are valid, partial truths that must be held together.
