## Gloss / Divergence map

Ṣaḍāvaśyaka (*ṣaṭ* "six" + *āvaśyaka* "obligatory/essential") are the **six obligatory daily observances** that structure the religious life of the Jain practitioner — monk and committed layperson alike. *Āvaśyaka* literally means "what must be done": the six are the non-negotiable daily routine by which the soul keeps the vows alive, registers its lapses, and renews its restraint. They are the *practice-layer* of Jain ethics — where the standing vows (mahāvrata/aṇuvrata) meet the clock.

**The six (the classical Āvaśyaka-Sūtra list):**

| # | Sanskrit | gloss | what is done |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *sāmāyika* | equanimity | a fixed period (~48 min, one *muhūrta*) of stable-posture meditation, viewing all souls as equal, suspending all sinful activity "in mind, body and speech" |
| 2 | *caturviṃśati-stava* | praise of the twenty-four | devotional recitation honouring the twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras (*Chauvīsattho*) |
| 3 | *vandana(ka)* | homage | reverent salutation to one's ascetic teachers (monks and nuns) |
| 4 | *pratikramaṇa* | repentance / "turning back" | confession and renunciation of the day's transgressions — "I ask pardon of all living creatures; may all creatures pardon me" |
| 5 | *kāyotsarga* | "abandoning the body" | motionless standing/sitting in bodily non-attachment, dissociating awareness from the body |
| 6 | *pratyākhyāna* | renunciation | a formal resolve of abstention from specified faults/foods *going forward* |

The sequence has an inner logic: settle the mind (1), orient it to the ideal (2–3), audit the past against that ideal (4), detach from the body that records the lapses (5), and bind the future (6). **Sāmāyika** and **pratikramaṇa** are the two that also stand alone as named practices — sāmāyika is one of the layperson's twelve vows (a *śikṣāvrata*), and pratikramaṇa is the central penitential rite Jains perform daily and at festival peaks (notably *Saṃvatsarī* / *Paryuṣaṇa*).

**Sectarian variation — two senses of "the six essentials":**

| tradition | the six | context |
|---|---|---|
| **Śvetāmbara** (Āvaśyaka Sūtra) | sāmāyika · caturviṃśati-stava · vandana · pratikramaṇa · kāyotsarga · pratyākhyāna | the *obligatory rites* of ascetics and observant laity |
| **Digambara** (lay *ṣaṭ-karma*) | dev-pūjā (worship) · guru-upāsti (reverence of teachers) · svādhyāya (scripture study) · saṃyama (restraint) · tapa (austerity) · dāna (charity) | the *daily duties of the householder* (*śrāvaka*) |

The Digambara monastic tradition (*Mūlācāra*) retains essentially the Āvaśyaka-Sūtra six; the divergence above is between the Śvetāmbara *rite-list* and the Digambara *layperson's-duty-list*, which are sometimes both called "the six essentials" and so get conflated. Both schools agree on the *function*: daily, repeated self-correction that produces saṃvara (stopping new karma) and nirjarā (shedding old karma).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Āvaśyaka":** the canonical six from the Āvaśyaka Sūtra (a Śvetāmbara Mūlasūtra in Jain Prakrit), with the primary-text phrasings of sāmāyika ("I abandon all bad activity … threefold by threefold, in mind, body and speech") and pratikramaṇa ("I ask pardon from all living creatures …").

**Signal 2 — Jain teaching aggregations (jainavenue.org, wisdomlib "Six Avasyakas", Jyoti Kothari):** independent of Wikipedia; confirm the same six in the same order, supply the 48-minute (muhūrta) duration of sāmāyika and the gloss of each, and document the *Digambara lay ṣaṭ-karma* (dev-pūjā / guru-upāsti / svādhyāya / saṃyama / tapa / dāna) as the parallel set.

**Independence & confidence:** the two signals converge on the membership and order of the classical six and on their saṃvara/nirjarā function.
