## Gloss / Divergence map

**Candrakīrti** (early 7th c.) is the commentator through whom the [[madhyamaka|Madhyamaka]] reached Tibet, and — on the Tibetan doxography that later organised the school — the champion of *prasaṅga*-only argument against **Bhāvaviveka's** independent inferences.

⚠⚠ **He was a gap the corpus itself named.** [[candragomin]] recorded, as its fifth unread item: "**Candrakīrti** — ⚠ the corpus has **no node for him**, though it has [[madhyamaka]], [[nagarjuna]] and [[yogacara]]; a real gap this node exposes." He is cited in **nine** committed files. This node fills the gap and, in doing so, finds **two errors and one large omission** in files that were citing him.

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The correction that matters: the corpus has been teaching a Tibetan map as an Indian one

[[madhyamaka]] says: "**Svātantrika sub-school** (Bhāvaviveka, ~500–570 CE) reformulates arguments as formal independent inferences… — rejected by **Prāsaṅgika** (Candrakīrti)." [[prasanga-nagarjuna]] says: "Later Madhyamaka **split** over whether prasaṅga is sufficient."

⚠⚠ **Both read as though two Indian schools existed under those names.** They did not. **SEP**: the terms are "not used by Indian Mādhyamikas themselves"; **Wikipedia**: the categorisation "only arose in Tibet during the **12th century**." ⭐ **What is Indian and real is a specific argument**: Candrakīrti defended **Buddhapālita** against Bhāvaviveka's criticism and held that a Mādhyamika, having no thesis, cannot supply the shared subject an autonomous inference requires.

⭐⭐ **And the corpus already had half of this and did not carry it across.** [[santaraksita]] says, correctly, that "**Later Tibetan doxography** names the resulting school Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka" — ⭐ **the corpus knew the *Svātantrika* label was Tibetan in the node about the Svātantrika, and not in the node about the split.** *(An internal-consistency finding, §4 signal 4; no source points it out.)*

⚠ **This does not make the split unreal or the label useless.** It makes it a **later organising scheme applied to a real earlier disagreement** — which is exactly what this corpus's [[rasesvara]] records for the *Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha*'s "schools," and what [[vishishtadvaita]] records about Veṅkaṭanātha's retrospective systematisation. ⭐ **Third instance of one pattern: a doxographer's category read back as a thing that existed.**

### 2. ⭐⭐ Three hundred years of obscurity, and what it costs the corpus's picture

If Candrakīrti was not influential in India from the 7th to the 10th century, and was **never translated into Chinese**, then the corpus's implicit picture — Nāgārjuna → Buddhapālita → Bhāvaviveka → Candrakīrti as a settled lineage of authority — is a **Tibetan** retrospect. ⚠ **[[santaraksita]] (725–788) is the Indian Madhyamaka who actually dominated the century after Candrakīrti**, and he goes the other way: he uses precisely the autonomous inference Candrakīrti rejected, and he is the one who went to Tibet first.

⭐ **So the corpus's own two Madhyamaka nodes describe the winner of a Tibetan argument and the winner of an Indian one, and had no way of telling which was which.** *(The corpus's inference from separately sourced facts.)*

### 3. ⭐ What he argued, stated so it is not just a label

Against **Bhāvaviveka**: the Mādhyamika "shows the problems involved in the positions of others" (SEP) and therefore refutes "only… on the opponent's own terms" (Wikipedia); an autonomous (*svatantra*) inference needs a subject both parties grant, and between a Mādhyamika and an essentialist there is none. ([[prasanga-nagarjuna]] carries the fuller form: "two irreconcilable points of view.")

Against **Dignāga**: he "critiqued non-Madhyamika epistemologists like Dignāga for having failed to provide a sufficiently indisputable foundation," and (SEP) held that "any proposition requiring a warrant raises infinite regress or self-validation problems." ⭐⭐ **That is Nāgārjuna's *Vigrahavyāvartanī* argument aimed at the pramāṇa tradition a few centuries on** — and the corpus holds both ends: [[prasanga-nagarjuna]] has the VV reply, [[pramana-samuccaya]] has Dignāga's system. ⚠ **No source consulted was asked whether Dignāga's school replied**; recorded as an open question.

Against **Yogācāra**: [[madhyamaka]] already records, from IEP, that the *Madhyamakāvatāra* "rejects all ontological privileging including consciousness-only — *vijñapti*s are also empty."

### 4. ⚠⚠ A striking claim the corpus went looking for and refused to make

A search summary offered: *the MMK's Sanskrit is available today **only** as embedded in the Prasannapadā, which is the **only** commentary on it surviving in Sanskrit.* ⭐ **If true it would be the single most important structural fact about [[mulamadhyamakakarika]], and that node does not have it.**

⚠ **It is not confirmed.** **SEP** says only that the *Prasannapadā* "survives in Sanskrit as well as in Tibetan translation." **Wikipedia's Candrakīrti article does not make the claim.** **Wikipedia's MMK article, asked directly, does not either** — it says the *Prasannapadā* "survives in Sanskrit and Tibetan translation" and "does not compare this to the survival status of the root text."

⭐⭐ **So the corpus records: the *Prasannapadā*'s Sanskrit survival is confirmed twice; the exclusivity claim is search-level and stands unverified.** ⚠ **This is Batch 50's finding 6 in the other direction** — that batch was faulted for discounting a source *wholesale*; here the risk was adopting a summary wholesale because it was interesting. **The claim is carried as the node's top follow-up, with the exact test named: La Vallée Poussin's 1903–13 edition, and whether an independent Sanskrit MMK manuscript tradition exists.**

### 5. ⭐⭐ Two defects found in files that were already citing him

**(a) A wrong attribution.** [[bodhisattva]] states, in its corpus-internal signal: "**madhyamaka.md notes Candrakīrti's *Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra*** as a Madhyamaka bodhisattva text." ⛔ **The *Bodhi(sattva)caryāvatāra* is Śāntideva's**, 8th c. — and this corpus **already knows that**: [[bodhicitta]] gives it correctly to Śāntideva five times and carries an `expressed-by: santideva` edge.

**(b) A cross-check that cites something its target does not say.** ⚠⚠ **`madhyamaka.md` does not mention the *Bodhisattvacaryāvatāra* at all** — a grep over `concepts/` returns the work only in `bodhicitta.md` and in the erroneous sentence itself. ⭐⭐ **So the §4-signal-4 "corpus-internal" check was performed from memory rather than by reading the file it names**, and it produced a false attribution and a false citation in one sentence. **Both corrected this batch.** ⭐ **The lesson is the charter's own (§8): "Never recall a linked concept from memory — read its file."** This is what that rule is for.

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**SEP, "Madhyamaka" (Richard Hayes, 2010, rev. 2023)** — a named specialist, fetched. **Wikipedia, "Chandrakirti"** — fetched, and it names its own authorities (Vose, Lang, Thubten Jinpa, Thakchoe, Hayes, Dunne, Ruegg). ⚠ **Hayes appears on both**, so the two are **not fully independent**; they nonetheless agree on the load-bearing point (the labels are Tibetan) from different framings, and Wikipedia adds the reception history and the second Candrakīrti, which SEP does not. **In-corpus**: nine committed files, read rather than recalled — which is how the two defects surfaced.

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. Contested on dates (one article gives two), on the exclusivity of the Sanskrit survival (unverified), and on what "Prāsaṅgika" names. ⚠⚠ **Not high, and the reason is stark: not one line of the *Prasannapadā* or the *Madhyamakāvatāra* has been read, in Sanskrit, Tibetan or translation** — this is a node about the corpus's most important Buddhist commentator built entirely from two encyclopedia entries. `DRIFT.md` **D1** exactly.
