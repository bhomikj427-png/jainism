## Gloss

**Hypothetical / reductive reasoning** — the 8th of the sixteen Nyāya padārthas (*NS* 1.1.1). Tarka is not an independent pramāṇa; it is an auxiliary device used to *confirm* a doubtful vyāpti by showing that its denial leads to absurd consequences. If one doubts whether smoke is invariably concomitant with fire, tarka applies: "Suppose smoke arose without fire — then our normal causal perception would be systematically deceptive," which is absurd; therefore the vyāpti holds.

**Mechanism**: tarka is *reductio ad absurdum* deployed *in service* of a positive conclusion. The sequence is: (1) vyāpti is doubted; (2) the negation of vyāpti is assumed hypothetically; (3) absurd consequences follow; (4) the negation is rejected; (5) vyāpti is confirmed. Tarka thus functions as a doubt-remover (*saṃśaya-nirodha*), not as an inferential engine. Gaṅgeśa (*Tattvacintāmaṇi*) treats tarka as the last resort when repeated co-observation has not dispelled doubt.

**What tarka is NOT**:
- Not a pramāṇa: it generates no new knowledge on its own; it only removes the obstacle of doubt.
- Not Madhyamaka *prasaṅga*: Nāgārjuna's prasaṅga refutes ALL four positions without endorsing any alternative. Tarka endorses the positive claim that survived the reductio. The two are structurally similar (both show absurd consequences of a hypothesis) but methodologically opposite in aim.
