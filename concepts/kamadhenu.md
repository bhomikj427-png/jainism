## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kāmadhenu** ("the wish-cow"; also **Surabhi**, "the fragrant one") is "a divine bovine-goddess described in Hinduism as the **mother of all cows**" — a miraculous cow who "provides her owner whatever they desire" (*kāma* "wish" + *dhenu* "cow"). She is less an individually-worshipped deity than the **theological archetype of the sacred cow**: "rather than having independent temples, it is the living animal which is the perpetual object of adoration," every cow being an earthly manifestation of Kāmadhenu, in whose body "all the gods are believed to reside."

**Origin — the Samudra-manthana.** Kāmadhenu "rose from the churning of the cosmic ocean (*kṣīra-sāgara*) by the gods and demons" — the same churning that yielded the amṛta and [[lakshmi|Lakṣmī]]. As one of the **ratnas** (treasures) of the churning she was given to the **Saptarṣi** (seven seers) and so came to the sage **Vasiṣṭha**, for whom she supplied the needs of his sacrifices. (Other accounts give alternative births — from a creator-deity's breath, etc.; the variants are recorded, not reconciled.)

**The Vasiṣṭha–Viśvāmitra conflict.** In the Rāmāyaṇa, King **Viśvāmitra** tries to seize Kāmadhenu (here called **Sabalā/Nandinī**) from Vasiṣṭha; the cow defends her master by **producing whole armies from her body** ("from her mouth emerged the Kambojas, from her udder the Barbaras"). His defeat drives Viśvāmitra to renounce kingship for ascetic/brahminical attainment — the myth dramatising the supremacy of brahminical-spiritual power over kṣatriya force.

**Symbolism.** Her body is read as a cosmic map: the **four legs = the four Vedas**, her teats = the four *puruṣārthas*, her horns = the gods, her face = sun and moon. She is the standing emblem of **abundance, purity, and the sanctity of the cow** in Hindu culture.
