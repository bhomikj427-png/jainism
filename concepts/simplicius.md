## Gloss / Divergence map

**Simplicius** of Cilicia (dates contested within a narrow range, see below) was a pupil of **Ammonius Hermiae** in Alexandria and then of **[[damascius|Damascius]]** in Athens, making him one of the **last generation of the Athenian Neoplatonic school** before its closure. When Emperor **Justinian's 529 CE edict** banned pagan philosophy teaching — the event conventionally called "the closure of the Platonic Academy" — Simplicius was among **seven philosophers** (including Damascius) who travelled to the Persian court of **Khosrow I** seeking refuge, departing again after a few years once Khosrow negotiated their safe return in a peace treaty (c. 532/533 CE). This episode is often called the philosophers' migration.

**Commentator, not innovator — but a hugely consequential one.** Simplicius's own surviving output is entirely **commentary**: on Aristotle's *Categories*, *Physics*, and *De Caelo* (authorship of a *De Anima* commentary is disputed), plus a commentary on Epictetus's *Handbook*. His governing project was to **harmonize Plato and Aristotle** — arguing their apparent disagreements are differences of formulation, not substance — while still being willing to criticize Aristotle on particular points. He is also known for vigorously **opposing John Philoponus's** Christian argument that the world was created in time, defending instead the Aristotelian doctrine of an eternal cosmos.

**Why he matters beyond his own doctrines.** Simplicius's commentaries **quote earlier philosophers at enormous length**, and for the Presocratics in particular he alone preserves an estimated two-thirds of all surviving verbatim fragments — he is one of the single most important transmission channels for material that would otherwise be entirely lost.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Simplicius"** (fetched): dates ca. 480–560 CE; studied under Ammonius (Alexandria) and Damascius (Athens); the 529 edict and the journey to Chosroes's Persian court (c. 531), returning disappointed after a few months; four surviving commentaries (Epictetus's *Handbook*, Aristotle's *Categories*/*Physics*/*De Caelo*; *De Anima* authorship debated); the Plato–Aristotle harmonization project; opposition to Philoponus on the eternity of the world; preservation of ~two-thirds of all verbatim Presocratic quotations.
2. **Wikipedia, "Simplicius of Cilicia"** (fetched, independent): dates c. 480–540 CE (a narrower end-date than SEP's 560); same teachers; the 529 persecution and flight of seven philosophers to Khosrow I, returning c. 533 after a negotiated peace treaty; same four Aristotle-commentary list; the harmonization project and the Philoponus dispute; the same Presocratic-preservation significance, adding that modern appreciation of Simplicius's own philosophical voice (versus treating him as a mere quarry for fragments) only developed in the late 20th century.

Two independent, authoritative reference works (a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia and an independently-sourced general encyclopedia entry) agree on the teachers, the 529 closure and Persian journey, the commentary list, the harmonizing project, the Philoponus dispute, and the Presocratic-transmission significance — **converged**. Confidence **medium**, not high: the two sources give slightly different death-dates (560 vs. 540) — a minor but real discrepancy left unresolved rather than silently picked one way; and no commentary text itself was fetched verbatim, only the secondary characterizations of it.
