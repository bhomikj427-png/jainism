## Gloss / Divergence map

**Padmapāda** (*Padmapādācārya*; modern scholarship places him **around the middle of the 8th century**) was **a direct disciple of [[shankara|Śaṅkara]] and his younger contemporary** — the earliest of the students, and the counterpart to [[sureshvara|Sureśvara]] in the pair of exegetical lineages through which Advaita was transmitted. Textual sources place his origin in the **Chola region** of South India; tradition makes him the **first head of the Purī Govardhana maṭha** (and credits him with founding the Thekke Maṭham at Thrissur).

**His one authenticated surviving work: the *Pañcapādikā***, composed — per tradition — at Śaṅkara's request, as a commentary on the *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*. It is **an extended gloss on the first four aphorisms only**, and is therefore either unfinished or truncated; legend supplies the explanation that an envious uncle destroyed the original manuscript. The corpus records the fragmentary state as the fact and the uncle as the legend.

**The school it founded.** Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā* was itself commented on by **Prakāśātman (10th c.)** in the ***Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa***, and that commentary gives the **Vivaraṇa school (Vivaraṇa-prasthāna)** its name. So the lineage is three deep and each layer is a commentary on the last: Śaṅkara's *bhāṣya* on Bādarāyaṇa's sūtras → Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā* on the *bhāṣya* → Prakāśātman's *Vivaraṇa* on the *Pañcapādikā*.

***Pratibimbavāda*** — the **reflection theory** — is his signature doctrine and the Vivaraṇa school's foundation: the *jīva* is the "**face in the mirror**," and is **none other than Ātman**, the original face. Individuality is reflection, not division.

### The locus of avidyā — the split that defines post-Śaṅkara Advaita

The two sub-schools divide on one question: **where does ignorance reside?** (All parties agree its *object*, *viṣaya*, is Brahman; the dispute is the *āśraya*, the locus — see [[sureshvara]].)

| | **Vivaraṇa** (Padmapāda → Prakāśātman) | **Bhāmatī** (Vācaspati Miśra, 9th c.) |
|---|---|---|
| **locus of avidyā** | **Brahman** is the locus / source of avidyā | the **jīva** is the locus *and* object of avidyā |
| Brahman's relation to avidyā | Brahman is the source | Brahman "can **never** be the locus of avidyā but is the **controller** of it, as *Īśvara*" |
| named for | **Prakāśātman's** *Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa* (10th c.) | **Vācaspati Miśra's** *Bhāmatī* on Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya (9th c.) |
| individuality | *pratibimbavāda* — the jīva as reflection of Ātman | *tula-avidyā*, individual ignorance, performing two functions: **veiling and projecting** |

This places **Padmapāda with [[sureshvara|Sureśvara]]** (avidyā located in Brahman) and against **[[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]]** and the later Bhāmatī line (avidyā in the jīva) — an independent corroboration of the doctrinal opposition already recorded in `sureshvara.md` from a different source-set. The founding generation of Advaita divides into **two camps on one question**, not four scattered positions.

### The dissent that questions the whole debate

Recorded because it is a genuine third position and not a detail:

> **Satchidānandendra Sarasvatī argued that Padmapāda and Prakāśātman had *misconstrued* Śaṅkara's stance** — that Śaṅkara gave no "location" for avidyā at all, giving precedence instead to its *removal*.

IEP states Śaṅkara's own position in a form that supports the objection without asserting it: **Śaṅkara described avidyā as *beginningless*, and held that to search for the origin of avidyā is itself a process founded on avidyā, and hence fruitless.** His disciples then gave the concept much greater attention, and the two sub-schools are the result.

So the locus dispute may be a **post-Śaṅkara construction** — a question the master declined, pressed by successors who did not. The corpus does not adjudicate this; it records that the debate's own faithfulness to its root text is contested, which is the kind of finding a commentary-layered tradition tends to generate (cf. [[vyasa-yogabhasya]], where the commentary may not be a commentary at all).
