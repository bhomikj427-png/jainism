## Gloss

A ***paribhāṣā*** is a **metarule**: not a rule of the language but a rule about how the rules are to be read — "meta-rule, hermeneutic rule, or interpretative rule," a convention that fixes scope, resolves conflicts between rules, and supplies what the sūtras leave unsaid. Abhyankar: it "can also be briefly defined as **the convention of a standard author**," and Puruṣottamadeva applies the word "to the maxims of standard writers" (*Puru. Pari.* 119). **The [[astadhyayi|Aṣṭādhyāyī]] contains some as sūtras of its own** — Abhyankar: certain rules "are **in fact Paribhāṣā rules laid down by Pāṇini**" — but the bulk of the literature is later, and it is **not the property of any one school**.

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐⭐ T4 confirmed, and with a census rather than an impression.** The batch asked whether *paribhāṣā* is Pāṇinian property or shared technology, because that is the direct test of whether the corpus has been reading the winner's account. Abhyankar answers it twice — once in prose, and once by **having edited the collection himself.**

In prose, and he marks it as remarkable in his own voice:

> "In the different systems of grammar there are different collections of Paribhāṣās… **It is a noticeable fact that many Paribhāṣās are common, with their wordings quite similar or sometimes identical in the different systems.** … **The total number of Paribhāṣās in the different systems of grammar may well-nigh exceed 500.**"

And in the ***Paribhāṣāsaṃgraha*** — ⭐ "a work containing a collection of independent works on Paribhāṣās **in the several systems of Sanskrit Grammar, compiled by M. M. K. V. Abhyankar**," i.e. **by the author of the dictionary this batch has been reading at first hand.** Its fifteen items are a census of the discipline's metalanguage:

⭐⭐ **Nine systems, fifteen collections, counts from 62 to 140, and "many Paribhāṣās are common, with their wordings quite similar or sometimes identical."** The Kātantra alone has three. **The metalinguistic layer is the discipline's, not one school's** — and the batch's premise, that the corpus had been reading a winner's account, is answered here in its strongest form: **there was no winner at this level.**

**⭐ And the corpus can watch a single maxim travel, with the numbers.** Abhyankar cites two maxims across systems, and the pattern is the same both times: **the content is shared, the numbering is not.**

| maxim | Vyāḍi | Cāndra | Kātantra | Kālāpa |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *uktārtha* (against repeating what is already expressed) | **51** | **28** | **46** | **46** |
| the mute-indicatory *adarśana* maxim | **11** | **14** | **54** | **71** |

⭐ **Note what falls out of the second row.** [[katantra]] records Abhyankar's judgement that Kātantra and Kālāpa are "different versions of the other." Here they **agree exactly (46 = 46)** on one maxim and **diverge widely (54 vs 71)** on another — which is precisely the profile of two recensions of one list that have been separately augmented. ⚠ **This is the corpus's own inference from two of Abhyankar's citations, not his claim**, and it is offered as *consistent with* his verdict rather than as evidence for it — two data points cannot establish a recension history.

**⭐⭐ Stronger than shared maxims: a shared *method for generating* them.** Abhyankar, s.v. *jñāpaka*: "The earliest use of the word *jñāpaka* in the sense given above is found in the *Paribhāṣāsūcana* of **Vyāḍi**. **The Paribhāṣā works on other systems of grammar such as the Kātantra, the Jainendra and others have drawn similar Jñāpakas from the wording of the Sūtras *in their systems*.**" ⭐ A *jñāpaka* is an **indicator** — a feature of the wording that is taken to imply a convention the author never states. **So every system runs the same inferential machinery over its own text**: not merely borrowing each other's maxims, but independently *manufacturing* maxims by a shared technique. ⚠ **And note what that technique is.** It is reading a doctrine out of a text's incidental features — the same move [[sphota|Haradatta makes with the name Sphoṭāyana]] and [[panini|Jinendrabuddhi with Śalātura]]. ⭐ **Here it is not an error but a licensed, named, technical procedure with its own vocabulary.** [[astadhyayi]]'s formulation holds and gets its clearest case: *the mechanism is not corrupting, it is invisible* — and in the paribhāṣā literature it is not even invisible, it is **institutionalised**.

**⭐⭐ The literature grows, and Abhyankar says how.** "Many times **the writers of Sūtras lay down certain conventions for the proper interpretation of their rules, to which additions are made in course of time according to necessities that arise, by commentators.**" ⭐ **The metarules accrete exactly as the commentaries do** — a few authorial, then more as difficulties surface. And the stack goes as deep: **Nāgeśa's *Paribhāṣenduśekhara* has been "commented upon by more than twenty-five scholars during the last two or three centuries,"** with named treatises by Viṣṇuśeṣa of the Śeṣa family, Udayaṃkara Pāṭhaka ("called also Nānā Pāṭhaka, a Nāgara Brāhmaṇa, who lived at **Benares** in the middle of the 18th century"), Haribhāskara Agnihotri, Śeṣādriguddhi, and Vaidyanātha Śāstrin. ⚠ ⭐ **This bears directly on an open question [[bhattoji-dikshita]] recorded and declined to answer** — whether the *prakriyā* re-ordering "made the *paribhāṣā* literature more necessary." **It is still not answered**: the paribhāṣā literature demonstrably predates Bhaṭṭoji by centuries (Vyāḍi is pre-Patañjali), so it was not *created* by the re-ordering; whether the re-ordering **accelerated** it is a question about rates that no source consulted addresses. ⭐ What this node adds is that the question is now well-posed and the counting is possible.

**⭐ *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* is a sixth genre-name.** Abhyankar: it is "a **general name** given to an explanatory independent work on Paribhāṣās… **irrespective of the system of grammar**, whether it be that of Pāṇini, or of Kātantra, or of Jainendra or of Hemacandra." ⭐ **The same discovery [[jinendrabuddhi]] made about *Nyāsa*, in the adjacent genre** — and it means the corpus should expect *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* and *Nyāsa* citations to be ambiguous by default across the whole literature. ⚠ Note also the Haima list calls its paribhāṣās ***nyāya***: **the technology is shared and the terminology is not**, which is a useful corrective to reading vocabulary agreement as doctrinal agreement.

**⭐⭐ It leaves grammar altogether — and the corpus already held the evidence.** [[indu]] records that the Āyurvedic commentator Indu appended **119 *paribhāṣā* verses** to his work, edited and translated by N. S. Mooss, and that Meulenbeld argues about what they show. ⭐ **A medical commentator, in a different śāstra, writing a numbered paribhāṣā collection of the same order of size as the grammarians'.** ⚠ **The corpus does not claim borrowing** — no source consulted connects Indu's *paribhāṣā*s to the grammatical ones, and the word is ordinary Sanskrit for a governing convention. **But the form is the same form**: a numbered appendix of interpretive conventions attached to a technical text. ⭐ **T4's answer is therefore larger than the question**: *paribhāṣā* is not shared across the grammatical systems, it is shared across **śāstra**, and the corpus met it in Āyurveda two batches before it met it in grammar.

**⚠ Where it sits in Pāṇini's own text, and what is genuinely his.** Abhyankar names specific Aṣṭādhyāyī rules as "in fact Paribhāṣā rules laid down by Pāṇini," and points to *Mahābhāṣya* on **II.1.1** for the technical distinction between a paribhāṣā and its neighbouring category. **Haribhāskara**, at the end of his own treatise, "states that **Vyāḍi was the first writer on Paribhāṣās**." ⚠ **The corpus does not adjudicate the origin question** — whether the free-standing metarule is Pāṇini's device, [[katyayana|Kātyāyana's]] and Patañjali's, or a later abstraction from commentary — and flags that a peer-reviewed treatment exists and was **not obtainable**: "From Commentary to *paribhāṣās*: Kātyāyana and Patañjali…," *Asiatische Studien* (2018), which is exactly this question and returned HTTP 405.

⭐⭐ **PARTLY DISCHARGED IN BATCH 51, and the abstract alone changes the question.** The article is **Maria Piera Candotti (Pisa) & Tiziana Pontillo (Cagliari), *Asiatische Studien – Études Asiatiques* 72.2 (2018), 515–566**, and its **full abstract was recovered through the Crossref API** when every reading route failed (De Gruyter 405, academia.edu 403, Pisa IRIS 403). ⚠ **What it says cuts against the row this node reports from Haribhāskara.** It calls the *Paribhāṣāsūcana* the collection "**handed down as** the first independent collection… and **attributed to** Vyāḍi" — two hedges — and it places the collecting **after** Patañjali, as a response to him: "a shift… between Kātyāyaṇa's integrative approach (*vacana*) and Patañjali's recourse to implicit *paribhāṣā*s… **A shift that helps in interpreting the need for a validation and collection of implicit pāṇinian *paribhāṣā*s as carried out by authors such as Vyāḍi.**" ⭐ **So the origin question this node declined to adjudicate now has a specialist answer on the record that is incompatible with Abhyankar's dating of Vyāḍi**, and [[vyadi]] carries the full divergence map. ⚠ **The argument itself is still unread** — an abstract is a claim, not a demonstration — and the node does **not** adopt it.

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** **Abhyankar** at first hand and overwhelmingly — the definition, the Pāṇinian paribhāṣā-sūtras, the *Mahābhāṣya* II.1.1 pointer, Puruṣottamadeva's usage, Haribhāskara on Vyāḍi's priority, the accretion mechanism, the "noticeable fact" of cross-system sharing, the 500+ total, the *Paribhāṣenduśekhara*'s 25+ commentators, the *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* genre-name, the **fifteen-item census**, and the two maxim-tracking citations. ⚠ **He is also an interested party — the compiler of item (ix) and of the collection itself** — which cuts **in favour** of the contents (he handled these texts) and is a reason for care about the framing. **Independent search-level corroboration**: *paribhāṣā* as "meta-rule, hermeneutic rule, or interpretative rule"; "**over five-hundred paribhāṣās written, with 19 independent treatises available**" — ⭐ **matching Abhyankar's 500+ from a different direction**; Nīlakaṇṭhadīkṣita in the 17th c.; and **Kielhorn's** critical edition and translation of the *Paribhāṣenduśekhara*. **In-corpus**: [[karaka]] already cites *Paribhāṣenduśekhara* 94 at first hand for the *kārakavibhakti*/*upapadavibhakti* priority — ⭐ an independently-arrived instance of a paribhāṣā **doing work** in this corpus before the concept had a node. **CONVERGED**, confidence **medium** — the convergence is real but ⚠ **rests very largely on one man, no Paribhāṣāpāṭha has been read**, and the two peer-reviewed articles directly on the origin question were both blocked.
