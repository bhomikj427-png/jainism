## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vārṣagaṇya** (Vṛṣagaṇa) is an early-classical **Sāṃkhya teacher whose entire œuvre is lost** and whose importance is a **modern reconstruction**. He is named in the standard teacher-lists alongside Kapila, Āsuri, Pañcaśikha, Vindhyavāsa, Jaigīṣavya and Īśvarakṛṣṇa, and he is the figure Erich Frauwallner placed at the hinge of classical Sāṃkhya's formation.

### The claim, and who is making it

> **Frauwallner suggested, based primarily on citations of Vārṣagaṇya's views in the works of [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]], that Vārṣagaṇya was the author of a *revised version* of the *Ṣaṣṭitantra*, older versions of which had been attributed to [[kapila|Kapila]] or Pañcaśikha.**

Everything load-bearing about Vārṣagaṇya is inside that sentence, and it is worth unpacking what it costs.

1. The evidence is **quotation in a source seven-or-more centuries later**. Vācaspati Miśra (9th–10th c.) is the corpus's own newly-written node; he is a compulsive citer of positions he does not hold, which is exactly what makes him usable — and exactly what makes the reconstruction a reconstruction. **Vārṣagaṇya reaches us as material inside a doxographer.**
2. The claim is about **a revision**, not an original — so the reconstruction has to separate his layer from the older attributed layers it displaced, in a text nobody has.
3. The *Ṣaṣṭitantra* itself is contested in kind: it may be "either itself a text or groups of texts, or simply an **enumerated list of a system for oral teaching**." If the third, "authorship" is not quite the right question.

### The authorship split

| reading | source | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **Pañcaśikha** wrote it | Wikipedia's "Shashti Tantra" article, flatly — Pañcaśikha "the revered Vedic sage," also called *Kāpileya*, whence the text's alternative name *Kāpilīya-śāstra*; the original "has been lost before Ādi Śaṅkarācārya" | a single traditional author, and Īśvarakṛṣṇa's seventy verses as "an excellent, concise version of Pañcaśikha's *Ṣaṣṭitantra*" |
| **Vārṣagaṇya** revised an older Kapila/Pañcaśikha version | Frauwallner, transmitted via the *Encyclopedia of Indian Philosophies* vol. 4 and the secondary literature | a **layered** text with a decisive later redactor — and it makes Vārṣagaṇya, not Pañcaśikha, the author of the system the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* summarises |

Note that the Wikipedia article, as fetched, **does not mention Vārṣagaṇya at all** — so this is not two scholars arguing but **two literatures that do not meet**: a traditional-attribution account and a philological-reconstruction account, each internally coherent.

### The date split

| dating | source | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **beginning of the 4th century CE** | Frauwallner — "the older Sāṃkhya epistemology derives from a revised version of *Ṣaṣṭitantra* composed by Vārṣagaṇya at the beginning of the fourth century" | he is nearly a **contemporary** of Īśvarakṛṣṇa, and [[vindhyavasin|Vindhyavāsin]] (fl. c. 4th c., his pupil) sits in the same generation — a tight, crowded window |
| **1st–2nd century CE** | secondary literature reporting an older version behind his | two or three centuries of development separate him from the *Kārikā*, and his "revision" is itself old enough to have been revised again |

The corpus does not pick. What it does record is that **the two datings answer different questions**: Frauwallner's is derived from where the *epistemology* must sit relative to Buddhist interlocutors; the earlier one from where the *text* must sit relative to its own predecessors.

### What he actually held (as reconstructed)

Three positions are attributed to him with some consistency, and they are the three that matter for classical Sāṃkhya:

- **On the *guṇas*.** "A discussion of the nature of the guṇas formed an important feature of Vārṣagaṇya's treatment," specifically the **ultimate nature of the guṇas, which cannot be directly perceived but can be inferred from their visible manifestation.** That is an *epistemological* move, not just a metaphysical one — it makes the [[guna-samkhya|guṇa]] triad an inferred posit, and puts the whole system's foundation on [[anumana-nyaya|inference]] rather than on perception.
- **On existence and change.** The formula associated with him: **"there is neither production of something new nor extinction of something existent. What exists is always existent; what does not exist will never be existent."** This is [[satkaryavada|satkāryavāda]] stated as a conservation principle — and, if the attribution holds, it means the doctrine the whole school is known for was given its sharpest form by the man whose text is gone.
- **On *pariṇāma*.** He "seems to have devoted considerable space to a discussion of the **evolution of tattvas from *pradhāna***" — i.e. the derivation sequence that [[samkhya-karika|Īśvarakṛṣṇa]] later compresses into verses.

Together these make him, on the reconstruction, **the author of the system rather than a name in its genealogy**: Īśvarakṛṣṇa's seventy verses become a *schoolbook summary* of a fuller work, and the *Sāṃkhyakārikā*'s famous compression stops looking like originality and starts looking like abridgement.

### The transmission problem, stated plainly

Vārṣagaṇya and [[vindhyavasin|Vindhyavāsin]] "exerted a great influence on [[vyasa-yogabhasya|Vyāsa]]" — so the Yoga commentary is a further channel. But every channel here is the same *kind* of channel: **a later author quoting him for that author's own purposes.** The corpus already records this pattern for Vindhyavāsin (known through his Buddhist opponents), [[carvaka]] (known through hostile doxography), and the Chaldean Oracles. Vārṣagaṇya is the **friendliest** version of it — his citers are broadly sympathetic — and it makes no difference to the epistemics: *a reconstruction from citations tells you what the citers found quotable.*

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Search aggregation of the Frauwallner line** (*Encyclopedia of Indian Philosophies* vol. 4, ed. Larson & Bhattacharya, via JSTOR/vdoc listings; Frauwallner's *Die Erkenntnislehre des klassischen Sāṃkhya-Systems*, 1958, located on the Internet Archive; Larson's *Classical Yoga Philosophy and the Legacy of Sāṃkhya* preface): the **Vācaspati-citation basis** of the whole reconstruction; the revised-*Ṣaṣṭitantra* attribution against older Kapila/Pañcaśikha versions; the **early-4th-century** date; the *Ṣaṣṭitantra* as possibly "an enumerated list of a system for oral teaching" rather than a text; the *Kārikā* as its "schoolbook" summary; and the possibility that **Īśvarakṛṣṇa was himself in Vārṣagaṇya's lineage**.
2. **Wikipedia, "Shashti Tantra"** (fetched, independent of the Frauwallner literature): the **Pañcaśikha** attribution, *Kāpileya*/*Kāpilīya-śāstra*, the 60-topics gloss, the loss "before Ādi Śaṅkarācārya," Īśvarakṛṣṇa's seventy verses as its concise version, and — usefully — the note that **Vācaspati Miśra "must have known some parts of Ṣaṣṭi Tantra, since he had mentioned its quotes."** That last item is the same evidentiary fact Frauwallner builds on, reported by a source that draws the opposite conclusion about authorship.
3. **Search aggregation on doctrine** (sreenivasaraos.com's Sāṃkhya-teachers series; the *Satkāryavāda* article; general Sāṃkhya secondary literature): the teacher-list placement; the guṇas-inferred-not-perceived position; the "neither production of something new nor extinction of something existent" formula; the *pradhāna*-to-*tattva* evolution emphasis; and the Vārṣagaṇya-and-Vindhyavāsin influence on the *Yogabhāṣya*. **Weakest provenance in the node** — a personal scholarly blog and encyclopedia articles — and the source of the most quotable material, which is exactly the wrong way round.
4. **Corpus cross-check** ([[vindhyavasin]], [[samkhya-karika]], [[vacaspati-mishra]], [[satkaryavada]], [[guna-samkhya]]): [[vindhyavasin]] was written in an earlier batch and already records him as "a pupil of Vārṣagaṇya (Vṛṣagaṇa)" and a reviser of the *Ṣaṣṭitantra* line "older versions of which were ascribed to Kapila / Pañcaśikha" — from the same Frauwallner reconstruction, so this is **corroboration of transmission, not of fact**. [[vacaspati-mishra]], written earlier in this same batch from an entirely unrelated source-set, independently confirms that he was a prolific quoter of positions across all six darśanas — which is what makes the citation-basis plausible.

**CONTESTED** on authorship and on date; the doctrine is reported consistently but from a single weak layer. Confidence **low**, and deliberately so — this is the [[vindhyavasin]] problem in a stronger form:

- **no surviving work**, and the work attributed is itself of uncertain kind;
- **weak independence** — signals 1, 3 and 4 all ultimately descend from **Frauwallner's reconstruction**, and signal 2 is the only source not in that stream, which is precisely why it disagrees;
- **no primary text was read**, in any language, and Frauwallner's 1958 monograph — located on the Internet Archive, in German — was not consulted;
- the most striking item, the conservation formula, is sourced from the **thinnest** signal.

This node is a map of an argument about a person, not a record of the person. Recorded as such under §4 rather than left out.
