## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite** is the conventional scholarly name for the anonymous author of the **Corpus Areopagiticum** (four treatises + ten letters, in circulation before 532 CE), who **falsely wrote under the name of Dionysius the Areopagite** — the Athenian convert of St. Paul mentioned in Acts 17:34 — thereby claiming first-century apostolic authority for a text now dated to the late 5th/early 6th century. The deception went essentially unchallenged for a millennium; it was first seriously questioned in the Renaissance (Lorenzo Valla, 1457; John Grocyn, 1501) and only became the settled scholarly consensus in modern times.

**The corpus and its doctrine.** *On the Divine Names* develops **affirmative (cataphatic) theology** — the names Scripture and tradition attribute to God. *The Mystical Theology* develops the complementary **negative (apophatic) theology**: the claim that every affirmation about God must in turn be negated — including the negations themselves — so that language self-subverts and what remains beyond all speech is "divine darkness," an unknowing union. *The Celestial Hierarchy* organizes the angelic orders into nine ranks mediating between God and creation; *The Ecclesiastical Hierarchy* maps the Church's own sacramental orders onto that same hierarchical structure.

**A real, documented borrowing from Proclus — not a smuggled equivalence.** Pseudo-Dionysius writes in **distinctly Proclan vocabulary** — the abiding/procession/return (*monē–proodos–epistrophē*) rhythm and graded hierarchical mediation are structurally lifted from [[proclus|Proclus]]'s system. But the borrowing is selective and theologically re-grounded: he replaces Proclus's mediated causation (the [[proclus-henad|henad]]-structured chain by which the transcendent One reaches multiplicity only through intermediaries) with a doctrine of **direct divine-creature dependence**, and folds the whole apparatus into **Trinitarian theology and a Christian doctrine of love (*erōs*) as the motive force of both procession and return** — commitments wholly absent from, and in real tension with, Proclus's pagan metaphysics.

**Contested: who was he?** No candidate identity commands scholarly consensus:

| candidate | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| A pupil of Proclus, perhaps Syrian, otherwise unknown | SEP's own working position | the author is simply lost to history; only the intellectual profile (deep Proclus fluency + Christian formation) is recoverable |
| Peter the Iberian | Shalva Nutsubidze, Ernest Honigmann, Alexander Golitzin | a specific 5th-c. Georgian-born bishop and monastic founder authored the corpus under this pseudonym |
| Damascius (the last Athenian scholarch — see [[damascius]]) | a more recent proposed identification (noted in secondary aggregation, not SEP) | the very last pagan head of the Neoplatonic Academy would have secretly authored the most influential Christian mystical corpus of the Middle Ages — a striking claim, and this file explicitly does **not** endorse it; it is recorded as one contested hypothesis among several, not a finding |

This file takes no side; the identity question is presented as genuinely open, per CLAUDE.md's mandate to map disagreement rather than resolve it by preference.

**Reach.** The corpus shaped **John Scottus Eriugena** (who translated it into Latin), **Aquinas**, **Bonaventure**, and later Spanish mystics (John of the Cross); its apophatic method is also cited by modern theorists of negative theology and deconstruction (Derrida).
