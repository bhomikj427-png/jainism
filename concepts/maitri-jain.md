## Gloss

**Maitrī** (Sanskrit, "friendship, benevolence") is the **first of the four right sentiments (bhāvanās)** enjoined in **Tattvārtha Sūtra 7.11**, the attitudinal supports a Jain cultivates toward all living beings to strengthen the vows and stop karmic influx. TS 7.11: *maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni ca sattva-guṇādhika-kliśyamānāvineyeṣu* — "benevolence toward all living beings (maitrī), joy at the sight of the virtuous (pramoda), compassion for the afflicted (kāruṇya), and tolerance toward the insolent and ill-behaved (mādhyasthya) are the right sentiments."

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* glosses maitrī as **the desire that all beings be free from suffering and pain** — directed without limit at every *jīva* (sattva). To be a friend to all is to be unable to wish anyone harm; maitrī is thus the positive affective ground beneath the **ahiṃsā** vow: where ahiṃsā is the restraint (do no injury), maitrī is the cultivated goodwill from which the restraint flows naturally.

**Cross-tradition note (parallel, not identity).** Jain *maitrī* is literally the same word as Buddhist *mettā/maitrī* (loving-kindness, the first brahmavihāra), and the same four-fold set — *maitrī, karuṇā, muditā, upekṣā* — appears in Patañjali's Yoga-Sūtra 1.33. The vocabulary is genuinely shared and the structure parallel, but each set is embedded in a different soteriology (karma-as-matter purification / no-self liberation / kaivalya) and is not the same doctrine. The Jain set is oriented to **stilling the passions (kaṣāya)** that bind karmic matter, not to radiating boundless states for their own sake.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **TS 7.11 — directly fetched** (Jainworld translation): the verse text (*maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni…*) and the gloss of maitrī as "benevolence toward all living beings."
2. **Wikipedia, "Maitrī"** (search): attributes the four right sentiments to TS 7.11, accepted as authoritative by all Jain sub-traditions; quotes the classical-commentary gloss "the desire that others should be free from suffering and pain is benevolence (maitrī)."
3. **Jainism Simplified, Ch.13 (Bhavanas)** + Jain Square (Maitri Bhāvanā): independent devotional exposition — friendship with all beings, leading the practitioner to avoid harsh thought/word/deed and thereby avoid bad karma.

Three signals (a direct verse translation + an encyclopedia + Jain pedagogical sources) converge on definition, source-verse, and ahiṃsā-grounding. Confidence medium: verse directly fetched, but the Sarvārthasiddhi commentary read via secondary quotation, not from the critical edition.
