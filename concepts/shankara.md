## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śaṅkara** (Ādi Śaṅkara, Śaṅkarācārya; **c. 700–750 CE**, dating debated — see table) is the systematiser and "most authoritative philosopher of Advaita Vedānta" (SEP). Born, per the oldest hagiographies, at Kālaḍi in Kerala; intellectually descended through his teacher **Govinda** from **Gauḍapāda** (his *parama-guru*, whose Māṇḍūkya-kārikā he studied), and self-consciously "part of a long lineage of teachers" running back through [[badarayana|Bādarāyaṇa]] to the Upaniṣads. He is the long-referenced but previously unwritten author-anchor behind [[advaita-vedanta]], [[atman-vedanta]], [[brahman]], [[maya-advaita]], [[moksha-advaita]], and [[vivartavada]].

**Authentic works (both sources converge):** the **Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya** (the masterpiece), commentaries on the **ten principal Upaniṣads** and the **Bhagavad Gītā**, and one independent treatise, the **Upadeśasāhasrī**. Over 300 further texts travel under his name; "most of these are not authentic works of Shankara" (Wikipedia) — SEP: likely "composed in later centuries by monastic heads" holding the Śaṅkarācārya title. The beloved **Vivekacūḍāmaṇi** is specifically "questioned and mostly rejected by scholarship" as his — a caution already relevant to this corpus, since two existing files (atman-vedanta.md, brahman.md) list it as a future confidence-upgrade fetch *as Śaṅkara's*; it should be fetched as *attributed*, not authentic.

**Core doctrine (worked out in the linked files; one-paragraph summary):** the ātman is "pure non-intentional consciousness… one without a second… numerically identical with brahman" (SEP); the world-appearance rests on avidyā/māyā superimposition; empirical (*vyāvahārika*) and ultimate (*pāramārthika*) truth form an asymmetric hierarchy in which "Brahman never undergoes genuine transformation into the world" (= [[vivartavada]]); liberation is epistemic — "one becomes brahman simply by knowing brahman." His commentarial method: anvaya-vyatireka, lakṣaṇā, and *neti neti*, all built "to indicate brahman's presence without objectifying it."

**Dating (genuinely spread):**

| claim | who holds it |
|---|---|
| 788–820 CE | early Indological scholarship |
| c. 700–750 CE | Nakamura; modern mainstream (both sources) |
| late 7th–early 8th c. | Potter (via Maṇḍana Miśra's dating) |
| 5th c. BCE | the traditional Advaita mathas' own chronology |

**The history-vs-hagiography gap (the honest finding; do not smooth over).** "Reliable information on Shankara's actual life is scanty." The famous biography — the *digvijaya* conquest-of-the-quarters, the founding of the four cardinal mathas (Dvārakā, Purī, Śṛṅgeri, Badrī), the debate-victory over [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]] who converts and becomes Sureśvara — comes from **at least fourteen hagiographies written centuries to a millennium after his death**, "politically motivated… abound[ing] in legends and improbable events" (the most-cited, Mādhava's Śaṅkaradigvijaya, is 17th-century). Historically, Śaṅkara "was relatively unknown during his life-time, and probably for several centuries after" — unmentioned by Buddhist, Jain, or 9th–10th-century Hindu philosophers, and in his own period "probably overshadowed by his older contemporary Maṇḍana Miśra," whose Brahmasiddhi was then the normative Advaita treatise. His towering fame is a **construction of the 14th-century Vijayanagara period** (Vidyāraṇya's promotion of him as "divine folk-hero"), further amplified by 19th–20th-century Neo-Vedānta and Western Orientalism. The philosophical authority of the bhāṣyas and the biographical legend are two different layers with two different evidential standings.

**The crypto-Buddhist charge:** Vaiṣṇava opponents called him a *pracchanna-bauddha*; his Advaita "showed influences from Mahāyāna Buddhism, despite Shankara's critiques" — but the tradition's rebuttal stands on the real doctrinal difference (positive permanent Brahman vs śūnyatā's denial of any self-existent substrate), already typed as a NOT-equivalent edge on [[advaita-vedanta]].

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Adi Shankara"** (fetched): dating debate incl. the mathas' 5th-c.-BCE claim; Kālaḍi; authentic-works list and the 300+ attribution problem; Vivekacūḍāmaṇi "mostly rejected"; Gauḍapāda/Govinda lineage; four-matha attribution questioned; fourteen late hagiographies; Maṇḍana Miśra overshadowing + the debate-conversion legend as hagiographic construction; pracchanna-bauddha charge; "relatively unknown during his life-time"; Vijayanagara-period fame-construction; Neo-Vedānta amplification.
2. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Śaṅkara"** (fetched): 788–820 vs Nakamura 700–750 vs Potter late-7th; the same authentic-works list (bhāṣyas + Upadeśasāhasrī, the rest questionable/monastic); ātman-as-nondual-consciousness doctrine; two-truths asymmetry; mokṣa as knowledge; the three commentarial methods; Sureśvara and Padmapāda as direct disciples; the Bādarāyaṇa-ward lineage self-conception.

Two genuinely independent reference works (a general encyclopedia and a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia) agree on the works-list, the doctrine, the lineage, the dating band, and — importantly — on the thinness of the biographical record. **Converged** on authorship, doctrine, and the hagiography-gap; confidence **medium**: dating spread is wide, no bhāṣya text was fetched verbatim, and the biographical layer is irreducibly legendary.
