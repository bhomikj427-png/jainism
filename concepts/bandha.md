## Gloss / Divergence map

Bandha is **karmic bondage** — the intermingling and adhesion of karma particles to the soul (jīva). It is the fourth of the seven tattvas and the direct product of āsrava (influx) combined with kaṣāya (passions). Bandha is what āsrava becomes when passions are present.

**TS 8.1 Sanskrit/English:** "Wrong-belief (*mithyādarśana*), non-abstinence (*avirati*), negligence (*pramāda*), passions (*kaṣāya*) and activities (*yoga*) are the causes of bondage." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**Five causes of bandha** (Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* commentary, via Vijay K. Jain 2018):

| # | Sanskrit | English | nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | mithyādarśana | wrong belief | 5 varieties: absolutistic, contrary, skeptical, superficial, ignorant |
| 2 | avirati | non-abstinence | relating to 6 classes of beings and senses |
| 3 | pramāda | negligence | indifference to scriptural injunctions |
| 4 | kaṣāya | passions | anger, pride, deceit, greed (with 16 sub-types by intensity) |
| 5 | yoga | activities | 13 kinds of bodily, mental, and speech activity |

**Correction note:** karma.md (this project) cited "TS 8.1, four causes." The actual TS 8.1 text lists **five** causes including yoga. Wikipedia's Bandha article omitted yoga from its TS 8.1 summary — that summary was inaccurate. The five-cause list is confirmed by direct fetch.

**Four kinds of bondage and who causes what (TS 8.3):**
- *Yoga* (activities) → **prakṛti-bandha** (nature/species of karma) + **pradeśa-bandha** (quantity/space-points of karma)
- *Kaṣāya* (passions) → **sthiti-bandha** (duration: how long karma persists) + **anubhāga-bandha** (intensity/fruition: how strongly it will be felt)

**The mechanism in full:** Yoga opens the soul to incoming karma particles (āsrava); kaṣāya acts as the adhesive that makes them stick and determines *how* they bind. Without kaṣāya, yoga causes only *īryāpatha āsrava* — a transient, non-binding touch of karma. With kaṣāya, karma binds with duration and intensity (*sāmparāyika āsrava* → bandha).

**Spiritual stage (*guṇasthāna*):** Pūjyapāda notes these five causes operate differently at different stages of spiritual development. The fourteen *guṇasthānas* map how the causes progressively fall away: mithyādarśana is overcome at stage 4; avirati gradually at stages 5–6; pramāda at stage 7; kaṣāya at stages 10–12; yoga at stage 13–14. Complete bandha-cessation (saṃvara) is complete at stage 13; final mokṣa at 14. (Guṇasthānas are a separate concept not fully developed here.)
