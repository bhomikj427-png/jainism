## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Tattvārthasūtra** ("Aphorisms on the Meaning of the Reals"; also *Tattvārthādhigama-sūtra*, and *Mokṣa-śāstra*, "scripture on liberation") of **Umāsvāti** (a.k.a. Umāsvāmī, c. 2nd–5th c. CE) is the **keystone text of the entire project's Jain material** — and the Jain text *par excellence*: it is **the earliest extant Jain work in Sanskrit** and **the only text authoritative for and accepted by both the Digambara and the Śvetāmbara sects** (Wikipedia: "its importance within Jainism parallels that of the Brahma Sūtras and Yoga Sūtras in Hinduism"). It comprises **ten chapters (adhyāyas)** and ~350 aphoristic sūtras, and is a compressed, systematic summa of all of Jain doctrine.

**The opening verse (TS 1.1): the three jewels.** *samyagdarśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi mokṣa-mārgaḥ* — "right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct [together] are the path to liberation" (the **ratnatraya**). This single sūtra organises the whole system and names the text's purpose (hence *Mokṣa-śāstra*).

**Coverage.** Ch. 1 — epistemology (the five jñānas; pramāṇa/naya); the **seven tattvas** are enumerated at **TS 1.4** (jīva, ajīva, āsrava, bandha, saṃvara, nirjarā, mokṣa). Chs. 2–4 — the soul (jīva), its states, and the cosmography of rebirth-realms. Ch. 5 — the non-soul substances (ajīva: pudgala, dharma, adharma, ākāśa, kāla) and the analysis of being (TS 5.29–5.30, *sat* as utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya). Chs. 6–8 — karmic influx, bondage, and the kinds of karma. Ch. 9 — saṃvara, nirjarā, the ethical vows, austerity, meditation. Ch. 10 — **mokṣa**. (The verse-fetch trail TS 1.1→1.2→1.3→1.4→5.1→5.6→5.17→5.18→5.22→5.30→5.38→6.2→6.5→7.5–7.8→8.1→9.1→9.19–9.20→10.1–10.2, spread across the corpus's concept files, is the most heavily verified single text in the project.)

⭐⭐⭐ **Who Pūjyapāda is — established only in Batch 51, and the corpus should be embarrassed by how late.** He is **[[devanandin|Devanandin]]**, and "Pūjyapāda" ("worshipped feet") is an **honorific**; the tradition also calls him *Deva* and *Jinendrabuddhi*. ⚠ **Fifty-eight concept files in this repository cite his *Sarvārthasiddhi*; two cited "Devanandin"; none joined them** — and the identity is printed in the abbreviation list of the grammar dictionary the corpus has read at first hand since Batch 47, and in the Preface of the very translation these verses were fetched from. ⭐ He is also the author of the ***Jainendra Vyākaraṇa***, a complete rival system of Sanskrit grammar ([[jainendra-vyakarana]]). ⚠ **His date is disputed across a century and a half** (464–524 / c. 510–600 / "mid-6th c."), and **this file's "~6th c." is one reading among three, not a settled figure.** See [[devanandin]].

**Commentaries.** The two great early commentaries are **Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (Digambara, ⚠ dated ~5th–6th c.; see [[devanandin]] for the spread) and the **Tattvārthabhāṣya** (Śvetāmbara, traditionally ascribed to Umāsvāti himself), with Siddhasena's ṭīkā and 25+ later commentaries — the sūtra is read differently by the two sects on points such as the exact wording of TS 1.1 and the status of kevalin-bodily-functions, even as both treat the text as canonical.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Tattvartha Sutra"): Umāsvāti; c. 2nd–5th c. CE; Sanskrit; the only text authoritative in both Digambara and Śvetāmbara; ten chapters / ~350 sūtras; TS 1.1 ratnatraya ("right faith, right knowledge and right conduct constitute the path to liberation"); coverage (epistemology, seven tattvas, cosmology, karma, liberation); 25+ commentaries incl. Sarvārthasiddhi (Pūjyapāda) and Siddhasena. **Signal 2** (Jainpedia + search aggregation): Umāsvāti; ~2nd c. CE; Tattvārthādhigama / Mokṣa-śāstra; **first Sanskrit Jain text**; accepted by both/all Jain traditions; ten chapters; Sarvārthasiddhi (Pūjyapāda, ~6th c.) as the oldest/most famous commentary. **Signal 3** (the corpus's own directly-fetched TS verses across prior batches — Vijay K. Jain 2018 ed.): TS 1.2, 1.3, 1.4, 5.1, 5.6, 5.17, 5.18, 5.22, 5.30, 5.38, 6.2, 6.5, 7.5–7.8, 8.1, 9.1, 9.19–9.20, 10.1–10.2 verified verbatim.

Signals 1 and 2 are independent (an encyclopedia and a Jain-studies portal + reference aggregation) and agree on author, dating, two-sect authority, structure, the ratnatraya, and the commentaries. Medium, not high: the *text-level* facts here rest on encyclopedia/portal sources, not a critical-edition front-matter fetch (Tatia 1994's introduction was never fully fetchable — a ceiling flagged since Batch 1); TS 1.1's Sanskrit is given in its universally-cited form (Wikipedia confirms the English; the corpus has the adjacent TS 1.2 verbatim), and the Digambara/Śvetāmbara textual variants are noted but not collated here.
