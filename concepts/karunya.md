## Gloss

**Kāruṇya** (Sanskrit, "compassion"; from *karuṇā*) is the **third of the four right sentiments (bhāvanās)** of **Tattvārtha Sūtra 7.11**: *maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni ca…* It is **compassion and sympathy for the afflicted** — those who are suffering (*kliśyamāna*, named in the verse).

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* glosses kāruṇya as **"the disposition to render assistance to the afflicted."** It is thus not mere feeling but the active will to relieve suffering, directed at all distressed *jīvas*. Together with maitrī (its affective ground), kāruṇya is a primary motivation behind the Jain commitment to **ahiṃsā** — non-injury extended to every being capable of suffering.

**Cross-tradition note (parallel, not identity).** Kāruṇya is the Jain counterpart of Buddhist **karuṇā** (compassion, the second brahmavihāra) — literally the same root word, occupying the same slot in the four-fold set (cf. Yoga-Sūtra 1.33). But the surrounding doctrine differs: Jain compassion motivates non-injury grounded in the *ontology of harm to a jīva* (harming any living being binds karmic matter), whereas Buddhist karuṇā is intention/empathy-based within a no-self framework, and in Mahāyāna *mahākaruṇā* fuels the bodhisattva's vow. Shared vocabulary, parallel structure, different metaphysics.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **TS 7.11 — directly fetched** (Jainworld translation): "compassion and sympathy for the afflicted" as the third right sentiment; verse text *…kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni…* with object *kliśyamāna* (the suffering).
2. **Wikipedia, "Maitrī"** (search) + classical-commentary gloss: kāruṇya = "the disposition to render assistance to the afflicted."
3. **Jainism Simplified, Ch.13 (Bhavanas)**: independent exposition of karuṇā/kāruṇya as compassion for those in distress.

Three signals converge on definition, the *kliśyamāna* (afflicted) object, and the source-verse. Confidence medium: verse fetched; Sarvārthasiddhi gloss via secondary quotation.
