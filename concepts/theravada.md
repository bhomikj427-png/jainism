## Gloss / Divergence map

**Theravāda** ("Way / Doctrine of the Elders"): the oldest surviving Buddhist school. Its scriptural canon is the **Pali Tipitaka** ("Three Baskets"); it is the predominant tradition in Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, and Cambodia (~100 million practitioners today).

**Historical origin.** Theravāda developed from the **Vibhajjavāda** movement (analytical interpreters of early Buddhism) following the second or third Buddhist council (3rd c. BCE). The tradition was established in Sri Lanka under King **Devānampiya Tissa** with the missionary monk **Mahinda** (son/disciple of Emperor Aśoka). The Pali Canon was committed to writing in Sri Lanka (~29 BCE, Āluvidena Council).

**The Pali Tipitaka (Three Baskets):**

| basket | contents |
|---|---|
| Vinaya Piṭaka | monastic discipline and rules |
| Sutta Piṭaka | discourses of the Buddha (including Digha, Majjhima, Saṃyutta, Aṅguttara Nikāyas + Khuddaka) |
| Abhidhamma Piṭaka | systematic philosophical analysis of mind and matter (82 dharmas) |

**Key doctrines:**
- **Arhat (*arahant*) ideal**: the goal is personal liberation — becoming an arahant (one who has destroyed all āsavā/defilements and achieved nibbāna). This is NOT the bodhisattva path.
- **Four Noble Truths and Noble Eightfold Path** as the soteriological structure.
- **Anattā** (Pali; no-self), **anicca** (impermanence), **dukkha** (suffering) — the three characteristics.
- **Abhidhamma analysis**: consciousness, mental factors, and matter analysed into irreducible *dharmas* (82 in Theravāda; see abhidharma.md for the Sarvāstivāda vs Theravāda count).

**Contrast with Mahāyāna:**

| dimension | Theravāda | Mahāyāna (Yogācāra / Madhyamaka) |
|---|---|---|
| primary ideal | arhat (personal nibbāna) | bodhisattva (liberation for all beings) |
| scriptural canon | Pali Tipitaka | Sanskrit sūtras (Prajñāpāramitā, Laṅkāvatāra, etc.) + Agamas |
| ākāśa status | not a primary element | varies by school |
| attitude to new texts | conservative (Pali Canon authoritative) | new sūtras as "Buddha's word" |
| view of Buddha | historical teacher now in parinibbāna | cosmic Buddha / multiple Buddhas / Buddhafield |

**The arhat/bodhisattva contrast is the deepest doctrinal divide**: Walpola Rahula (a Theravāda monk and scholar) argued both traditions accept the bodhisattva ideal in principle, but Theravāda restricts it to the historical Gotama's past lives (Jātaka tales) and sees it as a virtually inaccessible path.
