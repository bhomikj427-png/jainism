## Gloss / Divergence map

Astikāya (*asti* "exists" + *kāya* "body / collection") is the Jain term for a **substance that possesses spatial body — i.e. extension, a manifoldness of space-points (*pradeśa*)**. It is the classification that sorts the six substances (*dravya*) by whether they *occupy space as an extended magnitude* or not. Five of the six do; one does not.

**The five astikāyas (*pañcāstikāya*):**

| astikāya | what it is | extension |
|---|---|---|
| *jīva* | soul | occupies space-points (a soul fills the body it animates; a liberated soul, the size of its last body) |
| *pudgala* | matter | from a single *paramāṇu* up to vast aggregates (*skandha*) |
| *dharma* (dravya) | medium of motion | pervades the whole occupied cosmos (*lokākāśa*) |
| *adharma* (dravya) | medium of rest | pervades the whole occupied cosmos |
| *ākāśa* | space | infinite; the only astikāya extending beyond the cosmos (*alokākāśa*) |

**The one exclusion — *kāla* (time) is *anastikāya*.** Time is the sixth dravya, but it is *not* an astikāya: it has no body. This is the doctrine's sharp edge, and the *Tattvārtha Sūtra* names it in the very word it uses.

**TS 5.1, Sanskrit + Pūjyapāda's commentary directly fetched (Vijay K. Jain 2018):**
> **ajīvakāyā dharmādharmākāśapudgalāḥ**
> "The non-soul *bodies* (*ajīva-kāya*) are the medium of motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma), space (ākāśa), and matter (pudgala)."

Pūjyapāda explains the word *kāya* by analogy: "Just as the body is a conglomeration of matter, so also there is **manifoldness of space-points** (*pradeśa*) in the medium of motion, etc." A substance earns the name *kāya* precisely because it has *more than one* space-point. And time fails the test: time "is of the extent of one atom … it pervades one space-point only. Therefore it has no second space-point" — so kāla is *anastikāya*, the bodiless substance. (The Digambara tradition treats kāla as innumerable distinct one-point time-atoms, *kālāṇu*; even so, no single one has a *second* point, so none is a kāya.)

**Why this matters — the count "six dravyas / five astikāyas."** The astikāya classification is *not* a second list of things; it is a *property cut* across the one list of six substances:

```
SIX DRAVYAS
 ├── FIVE ASTIKĀYA  (have body / extension / multiple pradeśa):
 │     jīva · pudgala · dharma · adharma · ākāśa
 └── ONE ANASTIKĀYA (no body / a single point):
       kāla
```

So "the universe is made of five extended substances plus time" and "the universe is made of six substances" are the *same* statement; the astikāya scheme just records which ones have spatial magnitude. *Pradeśa* — the indivisible space-point, the unit of extension defined by ākāśa — is the measuring rod: a substance is an astikāya iff it occupies more than one pradeśa.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain 2018 (Motilal Banarsidass):** TS 5.1 Sanskrit fetched directly, *with* Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* gloss on *kāya* = manifoldness of pradeśa and the explicit exclusion of kāla (single space-point). Critical-edition provenance for the core doctrine.

**Signal 2 — Dravyasaṃgraha (Nemicandra, c. 10th c.) + Pañcāstikāyasāra (Kundakunda) via reference relay:** independent of the TS edition. Both confirm the *pañcāstikāya* (five-substances-with-bodily-existence = jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, ākāśa) and kāla as *anastikāya* (no spatial body). Kundakunda's text is *named for* the five astikāyas; Nemicandra's 58-verse Dravyasaṃgraha expounds all six dravyas and the astikāya/anastikāya split.

**Independence & confidence:** a direct critical-edition fetch (TS 5.1 + Pūjyapāda) converges with two classical Digambara texts (Kundakunda, Nemicandra) relayed via reference works. Genuine convergence on membership and on the kāla exclusion. Confidence medium: TS 5.1 + commentary fetched directly; the Dravyasaṃgraha/Pañcāstikāyasāra verses are relayed, not fetched at verse level; TS 5.2–5.3 (jīva as substance; eternal/fixed/formless) obtained in summary only this run.
