## Gloss / Divergence map

A guṇasthāna ("stage of virtue-quality") is one of **fourteen progressive stations of spiritual development** through which a soul advances from complete karmic delusion toward liberation. The scheme maps the entire soteriological arc — bandha → saṃvara → nirjarā → kevala-jñāna → mokṣa — onto a single graduated sequence, showing exactly which karmas operate, are suppressed, or are destroyed at each step.

**The fourteen guṇasthānas** (Wikipedia, citing Padmanabh Jaini, Nathmal Tatia, and others):

| # | Sanskrit name | English gloss | key event |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | mithyātva | false world-view; complete delusion | all five bondage-causes operative |
| 2 | sāsvādana | lingering taste of right faith (brief, transitional) | momentary glimpse then fall |
| 3 | samyak-mithyātva | mixed right/wrong view | unstable intermediate |
| 4 | samyag-dṛṣṭi | right faith without restraint | *darśana-mohinīya* karma destroyed |
| 5 | deśa-virata | partial self-restraint | layperson's *aṇuvratas* commence |
| 6 | sarva-virata | complete self-restraint without laxity | monk's full *mahāvratas* |
| 7 | apramatta-virata | perfect vows; no negligence (*pramāda*) | deep meditation becomes possible |
| 8 | apūrva-karaṇa | novel karmic purification; gross passions still present | |
| 9 | anivṛtti-karaṇa | progressively purer experiences; passions persist | |
| 10 | sūkṣma-sāmparāya | subtlest flickering greed only | near-complete passion cessation |
| 11 | upaśānta-kaṣāya | passions suppressed (not destroyed) | can fall back to stage 1 from here |
| 12 | kṣīṇa-moha | passions destroyed | *cāritra-mohinīya* karma eliminated |
| 13 | sayoga-kevalī | omniscience with bodily activity | three remaining *ghātīya* karmas destroyed; kevala-jñāna arises |
| 14 | ayoga-kevalī | omniscience without any activity | four *aghātīya* karmas destroyed; mokṣa |

**The karma arc across stages:**
- **Stages 1–6:** all five causes of bandha (TS 8.1 — mithyādarśana, avirati, pramāda, kaṣāya, yoga) operative; karma accumulates
- **Stages 7–12:** pramāda removed at 7; kaṣāya progressively suppressed (11) then destroyed (12); saṃvara deepens
- **Stage 12→13:** the final three destructive karmas (jñānāvaraṇīya, darśanāvaraṇīya, antarāya) are annihilated → kevala-jñāna arises
- **Stage 14:** all yoga (even subtle) ceases; the four aghātīya karmas (vedanīya, āyuh, nāma, gotra) exhaust → physical death = mokṣa

**Stage 11 vs 12:** A crucial distinction — at stage 11, passions are *suppressed* (*upaśama*) not destroyed; the soul can slip back to stage 1. At stage 12, passions are *destroyed* (*kṣaya*) — no regression possible. This is the Jain distinction between *upaśama-samyaktva* (suppressional right faith) and *kṣayika-samyaktva* (destructional right faith).

**Connection to saṃvara.md:** Pūjyapāda's commentary on TS 9.1 (fetched directly) explicitly states that saṃvara "operates at each of the fourteen stages" — different karmas cease being bound at each stage as saṃvara deepens.

**Primary source note:** The TS itself provides the framework for the guṇasthānas (Chapter 9 context; Pūjyapāda's commentary on bandha/saṃvara maps stages). The most detailed enumeration with all 14 names and their karma-states is in Gommaṭasāra (*Jīva Kāṇḍa* 9–10) by Nemicandra (~10th c. CE, Digambara), not the TS itself.
