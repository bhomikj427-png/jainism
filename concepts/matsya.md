## Gloss / Divergence map

**Matsya** is the **fish** — in classical Vaiṣṇavism the **first of the [[avatara-vedanta|daśāvatāra]]** (before [[kurma|Kūrma]] the tortoise). The core narrative is the **flood (pralaya)**: a small fish begs **Manu** for protection, grows until no vessel holds it, then warns him of a world-deluge; Manu builds a boat, loads the seven sages (*saptarṣi*) and the seeds of all species, ties it to the fish's horn, and is towed to the **northern mountain** to survive and re-found creation. In the Bhāgavata Purāṇa a second motive is added: Matsya slays the demon **Hayagrīva** and recovers the stolen **Vedas**, restoring them to Brahmā.

As with Kūrma, the **narrative is stable but the fish's divine identity shifts diachronically** — a recorded finding, not a source-dispute:

| layer | who the fish *is* | source |
|---|---|---|
| Vedic | a fish, **not associated with any deity** | Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa I.8.1 |
| epic | reveals himself as **Brahmā / Prajāpati** and grants Manu the power of creation | Mahābhārata, Vana Parva |
| Purāṇic | explicitly **Viṣṇu** (Manu/Satyavrata recognizes the fish as Viṣṇu) | Matsya / Bhāgavata Purāṇas |

Reading the Vedic flood-fish and the Vaiṣṇava Matsya-avatāra as one being flattens the same Brāhmaṇa-cosmogony → Purāṇic-Vaiṣṇavism shift mapped on [[kurma|Kūrma]]: continuous in image, not in theology.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Matsya"** — first of the daśāvatāra; the flood and the towing to the northern mountain; the explicit text-layer shift (ŚB associates the fish with no deity → MBh = Brahmā → Matsya/Bhāgavata Purāṇa = Viṣṇu); the Hayagrīva/Veda-recovery motive; and the Mesopotamian flood parallel (Ea/Utnapishtim) flagged as a debated diffusion, not an identity.
2. **ExoticIndiaArt, "Matsya Avatar"** (independent scholarly-popular source) — corroborates the flood arc, the Veda-recovery from Hayagrīva, and the same identity evolution: "earliest sources (Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa, Mahābhārata): the fish is Prajāpati or Brahmā; later texts (Matsya, Bhāgavata Purāṇa): the fish becomes explicitly Viṣṇu."

Two independent sources agree on the narrative **and** on the diachronic reattribution → **converged**, confidence **medium** (the identity-shift is the substantive content; the Mesopotamian comparison is structural, not asserted as descent).
