## Gloss

A fully liberated jīva — a soul that has destroyed all eight types of karma (four ghāti and four aghāti), shed its physical body, and ascended to the Siddhāśilā at the apex of the universe. The siddha is the highest of the pañca-parameṣṭhi (five supreme beings) and represents the completed endpoint of the Jain soteriological arc: jīva → karma-bondage → karma-purification (tapas/samvara/nirjara) → arihant → siddha. Unlike the arihant, who still inhabits a body, the siddha is formless, genderless, and permanently free from the cycle of rebirth.

## Distinction from arihant

| state | body | karma remaining | activities |
|---|---|---|---|
| **arihant** (gunasthāna 13–14) | physical body present; aghāti karmas active | 4 ghāti karmas destroyed; 4 aghāti karmas (āyu, nāma, gotra, vedanīya) still active | teaches, is venerated, can still be perceived |
| **siddha** (post-liberation) | no body at all | all 8 karma types destroyed | formless; no interaction with world; eternal bliss |

## Eight attributes of the siddha (aṣṭa-mahāguṇa)

### Four infinities (ananta-catuṣṭaya)
| attribute | Sanskrit | content |
|---|---|---|
| infinite knowledge | ananta-jñāna | complete awareness of all substances and modes across past, present, future — identical to kevala-jñāna |
| infinite perception | ananta-darśana | unlimited undifferentiated perception |
| infinite power | ananta-vīrya | inexhaustible spiritual energy |
| infinite bliss | ananta-sukha | permanently free from all pain and obstruction |

### Four further attributes
| attribute | Sanskrit | content |
|---|---|---|
| right-faith absolute | kṣāyika-samyaktva | permanent, unqualified right faith — not the partial samyaktva of the spiritual path |
| fineness | sūkṣmatva | occupies a single spatial unit (pradeśa) — not extended in space |
| interpenetrability | avagāhanā | multiple siddha-souls can co-occupy the same pradeśa |
| weightlessness | agurulaghutva | neither heavy nor light — free from all material properties |

## Location: Siddhāśilā

TS 10.8 (directly fetched): *dharmāstikāyābhāvāt* — "There is no movement beyond the universe's boundary because no medium of motion (dharmāstikāya) exists there." The siddha soul, propelled upward by its intrinsic momentum (the last trace of action cleared), travels to the top of the loka and stops permanently at the Siddhāśilā — a crescent-shaped region at the apex of the universe, beyond which aloka (non-universe) contains neither matter nor medium of motion nor medium of rest.

## TS 10.3 — liberation mechanism (directly fetched)

**Sanskrit:** *aupaśamikādibhavyatvānāṃ ca* (10.3)
**Translation:** "Liberation is attained on destruction of the dispositions like the subsidential (aupaśamika) and the capacity for liberation (bhavyatva)."
**Pūjyapāda:** Both material karmas and psychic dispositions (bhāva) must be fully eliminated. The aupaśamika (subsidential, temporary) disposition is replaced by the kṣāyika (destructive, permanent) state. Bhavyatva — the soul's inherent capacity to become liberated — is exhausted when liberation is achieved.

## TS 10.9 — thirteen differentiation criteria (directly fetched)

Despite being fundamentally undifferentiated (all siddhas are equally liberated), Pūjyapāda identifies thirteen criteria that distinguish different siddhas from each other historically:

*kṣetrakālagatiliṃgatīrthacāritrapratyekabuddhabodhitajñānāvagāhanāntarasaṃkhyālpabahutvataḥ sādhyāḥ*

Criteria: region, time, state, sign (gender of birth), Tīrthaṅkara affiliation, conduct type, self-enlightened, enlightened-by-others, knowledge type at liberation, stature, interval since prior liberation, number, and comparative enumeration.
