## Gloss

"Science of causes/reasoning" — the Buddhist epistemological and logical tradition founded by Dignāga (~480–540 CE) and systematised by Dharmakīrti (~7th c.), also called the *pramāṇavāda* ("epistemological school") or Dignāga–Dharmakīrti line. Hetuviḍyā is one of the Five Major Sciences in Tibetan Buddhist learning (alongside grammar, medicine, crafts, and inner science). Its core project: establish that Buddhist doctrines (impermanence, no-self, dependent origination) can be justified through rigorous inference — and that only two pramāṇas (valid cognitions) are needed: direct perception (pratyakṣa) and inference (anumāna). This 2-pramāṇa stance directly challenges Nyāya's 4-pramāṇa system and Mīmāṃsā's 5–6-pramāṇa system.

## Key doctrinal features

**2-pramāṇas only:** Pratyakṣa (perception — non-conceptual, self-validating) and anumāna (inference — conceptual, via trairūpya). This directly excludes Nyāya's additional upamāna (comparison) and śabda (testimony).

**Trairūpya (three-condition theory of inference):** A valid reason (hetu) must be (1) present in the subject-locus (pakṣa), (2) present in similar cases (sapakṣa), (3) absent in all dissimilar cases (vipakṣa). This is Dignāga's reconstruction of vyāpti without relying on Nyāya universals.

**Apoha theory:** Concepts refer not to positive universals but to exclusion-classes (*anyāpoha* — "exclusion of the other"). "Fire" means "not-non-fire." This eliminates the Nyāya need for universals as real entities while preserving functional meaning.

**Causal grounding of inference:** Dharmakīrti — inference is valid only when the hetu is connected to the sādhya by causal dependence (kāryahetu: fire → smoke) or essential identity (svabhāvahetu: oak is-a-tree). Purely formal pattern-matching is insufficient.

## Contrast with Nyāya logic

| feature | Nyāya | Hetu-vidyā (Buddhist) |
|---|---|---|
| pramāṇas | 4 (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) | 2 (perception, inference) |
| syllogism members | 5 (pratijñā, hetu, udāharaṇa, upanaya, nigamana) | 3 (pakṣa, hetu, dṛṣṭānta) |
| universals | real, perceivable via sāmānya-lakṣaṇa | rejected; replaced by apoha (exclusion) |
| grounding of vyāpti | repeated observation + tarka + universals | causal necessity or identity relation |
| testimony (śabda) | valid pramāṇa | reduced to inference (testimony valid only if the speaker's reliability is inferred) |
