## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pārijāta** is the divine **night-flowering tree** (popularly identified with *Nyctanthes arbor-tristis*, coral jasmine) that rises among the **ratnas of the [[samudra-manthana|Samudra-Manthana]]** and is planted in **Indra's heaven** (Amarāvatī). Its famous narrative is the **Kṛṣṇa–Satyabhāmā episode**: to please his consort Satyabhāmā, **[[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]]** wars with Indra and uproots the Pārijāta to Dvārakā — yet (in the tale's moral twist) its blossoms fall into the rival queen Rukmiṇī's yard, rewarding her greater devotion.

**The honest conflation (this is the node's main point — §5):**

| relation | what it asserts | source |
|---|---|---|
| Pārijāta **is** the Kalpavṛkṣa | "Pārijāta the Kalpavṛkṣa" — Pārijāta is *a* wish-fulfilling tree, so the generic class-name **[[kalpavriksha|Kalpavṛkṣa]]** is applied to it | popular/devotional usage |
| Pārijāta **≠** Kalpavṛkṣa | in the **five celestial trees** (*pañca-vṛkṣa*) of Indra's garden — **Mandāra, Pārijāta, Santāna, Kalpavṛkṣa, Haricandana** — Pārijāta and Kalpavṛkṣa are **distinct named members** | five-trees enumeration |

Both are true at once *because* "Kalpavṛkṣa" is simultaneously the **class** (any wish-tree) and **one specific sibling** in the five-list. So Pārijāta is "a kalpavṛkṣa" generically while remaining **not** the tree *named* Kalpavṛkṣa. Flattening the two (a common move) erases the five-trees taxonomy.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Parijata"** — churning-origin; one of five celestial trees; the Kṛṣṇa–Satyabhāmā uprooting from Indra's Amarāvatī to Dvārakā; identification with *Nyctanthes arbor-tristis*.
2. **Wikipedia, "Kalpavriksha"** (independent article) — the five-trees list (Mandāra/Pārijāta/Santāna/Kalpavṛkṣa/Haricandana) and the statement that Kalpavṛkṣa "is identified with many trees such as parijata" — supplying exactly the class-vs-member distinction.
