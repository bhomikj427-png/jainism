## Gloss

*Sarasvatī* is the Hindu goddess of knowledge, speech (*vāk*), arts, music, and wisdom — the intellectual dimension of the feminine divine. Her trajectory is a documented theological evolution: in the Ṛgveda she is primarily a **river-goddess** (pure, purifying, flowing with knowledge); by the Brāhmaṇa literature she is **identified with Vāc** (the Vedic speech-deity), becoming "the mother of the Vedas" and of the Vedas themselves; by the Purāṇic period she is a fully personal goddess of learning and creative intelligence. This evolution does *not* represent contradictory traditions — each layer adds a semantic register without cancelling the earlier one.

**Tridevi:** Sarasvatī, Lakṣmī, and Pārvatī form the *Tridevi* — the feminine counterpart to the male Trimurti (Brahmā/Viṣṇu/Śiva). Sarasvatī is Brahmā's domain (creation and knowledge); Lakṣmī is Viṣṇu's (preservation and prosperity); Pārvatī/Śakti is Śiva's (transformation and power). In Śākta theology, all three are manifestations of *Mahādevī* (the Supreme Goddess).

**Brahma relationship:** The Purāṇas present three different accounts (daughter only / consort only / both daughter and spouse) across different texts — a real theological discrepancy, not a single unified doctrine. Sarasvatī's identity as *Vāgdevī* (goddess of speech) is the stable core across all versions.
