## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bodhisattva** (Sanskrit) / **bodhisatta** (Pali): "one whose being (*sattva*) is oriented toward awakening (*bodhi*)." Standard definition (Encyclopedia of Buddhism, citing Candrakīrti / Yogācāra consensus): "someone who, motivated by great compassion (*mahākaruṇā*), has generated **bodhicitta** — the aspiration to attain full buddhahood for the benefit of all sentient beings."

The bodhisattva **does not seek personal liberation alone** — this is the defining contrast with the *arhat* path. The arhat achieves nirvāṇa for oneself; the bodhisattva vows to remain in saṃsāra to liberate all beings before entering complete nirvāṇa.

| dimension | bodhisattva | arhat |
|---|---|---|
| aspiration | buddhahood for all beings | personal liberation |
| path length | vast (multiple world-cycles) | shorter; one life under ideal conditions |
| primary virtue | bodhicitta + compassion | wisdom + renunciation |
| Theravāda status | historically rare ideal (only for future Buddhas) | primary model |
| Mahāyāna status | the central ideal; all practitioners aspire | a lower goal |

**The six pāramitās** (perfections practiced by a bodhisattva on the path): generosity (*dāna*), ethical conduct (*śīla*), patience (*kṣānti*), energy (*vīrya*), meditation (*dhyāna*), wisdom (*prajñā*). The Pali ten *pāramīs* add truthfulness, determination, loving-kindness, equanimity.

**Bodhicitta** (mind of awakening): the motivating aspiration; both *aspiration bodhicitta* (the wish) and *engaged bodhicitta* (the practice). Without bodhicitta there is no bodhisattva path.

**The vow**: a formal ritual pledge, taken under a teacher, not to enter parinirvāṇa until all beings are liberated. This vow is the initiating act of the Mahāyāna path and distinguishes the bodhisattva from ordinary practitioners.

**Theravāda vs Mahāyāna caveat**: scholar Walpola Rahula argues both traditions "unanimously accept the bodhisattva ideal as the highest," but Theravāda historically restricted it to extraordinary beings destined to become Buddhas, while Mahāyāna opened it to all practitioners. This is a difference in scope, not doctrine.
