## Gloss / Divergence map

**Stoicism** is the Hellenistic school founded by **Zeno of Citium** (c. 334–262 BCE) around 300 BCE — named for the **Stoa Poikile** ("painted porch") in Athens where its members met — systematised by **Chrysippus** ("but for Chrysippus, there had been no Porch"), and later carried by the Roman Stoics **Seneca, Epictetus, and Marcus Aurelius**. It divides philosophy into **three interconnected parts: logic, physics, ethics**. It is the school-level anchor behind [[stoic-logos]].

**Physics — materialist pantheism and fate.** Reality has two corporeal principles: an **active principle** — *logos* / God / Zeus / **pneuma** ("divine breath/reason," a "designing fire") — pervading a **passive principle**, matter. The cosmos is therefore a single rational living being, and everything happens by **fate (*heimarmenē*)**: "a certain natural everlasting ordering of the whole," an unbroken chain of antecedent causes in perfect rational order (providential **determinism**). The world periodically dissolves into fire and is reborn (**ekpyrosis**, eternal recurrence).

**Ethics — virtue is the only good.** The *telos* is **living in agreement with nature** (*homologoumenōs tēi physei*) — harmonising one's reason with the universal reason. **Virtue (*aretē*) alone is good and is sufficient for happiness (*eudaimonia*)**; everything else — health, wealth, reputation, even survival under torture — is an **indifferent (*adiaphora*)**, though some indifferents are "**preferred**." The **passions (*pathē*) are false value-judgments** to be extirpated, yielding **apatheia** (freedom from destructive emotion) — while "healthy" rational affects (caution, wishing, joy) remain. Because all humans share in reason, justice extends to all: **cosmopolitanism**.

**Apatheia vs ataraxia.** Both Stoics and Epicureans aim at tranquillity, by **opposite routes**: Stoic *apatheia* is reached by *embracing virtue and social duty* (engagement with the world); Epicurean *ataraxia* by *withdrawing from society* and avoiding disturbance — a contrast Epictetus drew explicitly.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (SEP, "Stoicism"): Zeno; the Stoa Poikile; Chrysippus; Roman Stoics; the three parts; physics as two principles (active God/Zeus + passive matter), pneuma, fate as "a certain natural everlasting ordering of the whole," ekpyrosis; ethics — "living in agreement with nature," virtue as the only good, externals as indifferents, the sage's happiness untouched by torture, passions as false judgments → apatheia; cosmopolitanism. **Signal 2** (IEP, "Stoicism"): Zeno founds the school ~300 BCE at the Stoa Poikile, Chrysippus systematises; the three parts; active rational principle (Logos/God/pneuma) + passive matter, pantheism, providential determinism, fate as "antecedent causes"; virtue the only good sufficient for eudaimonia; preferred/dispreferred indifferents; passions eliminated → apatheia while cultivating healthy passions; and the explicit **apatheia-vs-ataraxia** contrast (virtue-and-duty vs withdrawal-and-pleasure).

The two are independent (a scholarly encyclopedia and a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia) and agree on the founders, the tripartite system, the physics, and the ethics. Medium, not high: the early Stoa survives only in fragments and doxography (Diogenes Laërtius VII, Stobaeus, Cicero, Plutarch) — none fetched verbatim; the four cardinal virtues (wisdom/courage/justice/temperance) are standard Stoic doctrine but were not explicitly enumerated in the fetched summaries, so they are not stated as a verified list here.
