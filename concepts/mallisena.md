## Gloss / Divergence map

**Malliṣeṇa** (**Malliṣeṇa Sūri**), a **Śvetāmbara** monk of the 13th century and a disciple of **Udayaprabhasūri**, is the author of the ***Syādvādamañjarī*** — "a cluster of flowers of the syādvāda doctrine" — a Sanskrit commentary on [[hemacandra|Hemacandra]]'s thirty-two-verse ***Anyayogavyavacchedadvātriṃśikā***. It is, on every source consulted, his **only work**, and it is the text this corpus has been naming as an upgrade path from three separate nodes ([[syadvada]], [[saptabhangi]], [[hemacandra]]) without anyone having opened it.

This pass opened it. What follows is drawn from **A. B. Dhruva's 1933 critical edition itself** (Bombay Sanskrit and Prakrit Series No. LXXXIII, Bhandarkar Oriental Research Institute, Poona — Sanskrit text with an English introduction, notes and appendices), read through the Internet Archive scan, and it changes two things the corpus had recorded on a bookseller's authority.

### The colophon: a 13th-century Indian text that dates itself to the day

Malliṣeṇa completed the work **"on Saturday, Āśvina Kṛṣṇa Amāvāsyā in the Śaka year 1214, with the help of Jinaprabhasūri."** Śaka 1214 + 78 = **1292 CE**, which is exactly the date **Dasgupta** independently assigns in his *History of Indian Philosophy* — "*Syādvādamañjari* of Malliṣeṇa (1292 A.D.)" — from the scholarship of 1922, before any of the web sources existed.

Register how unusual that is against this corpus's own experience of Indian dating. [[dalhana|Ḍalhaṇa]] is placed by *terminus ante quem* argument from a later citation; [[badarayana|Bādarāyaṇa]] is `contested`; [[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]] carries a three-century spread; [[shankara|Śaṅkara]]'s hagiography contradicts itself. Malliṣeṇa names **the weekday, the lunar fortnight, the tithi and the year**. Where a colophon like this survives, the dating problem simply does not arise — which is a useful reminder that the discipline's chronic vagueness is a **fact about what survived**, not a fact about how these authors wrote.

The same clause carries something the corpus should not skip past: **"with the help of Jinaprabhasūri."** The commentary is, by its own statement, not the work of one man alone. The corpus's working picture of the genre — one author, one base text, one gloss — is complicated in the very first colophon it reads directly.

### The six schools: what the corpus asserted, and what the edition shows

[[hemacandra]] records that the thirty-two verses argue "against **six named schools** — Vaiśeṣika, Nyāya, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Buddhism and Cārvāka." That sentence came from an edition's descriptive blurb, reproduced across booksellers. Two things now qualify it.

**First, the descriptions do not agree on which six.** Set them side by side:

| source | the six |
|---|---|
| edition/bookseller description (as used in [[hemacandra]]) | Vaiśeṣika · Nyāya · **Vedānta** · Sāṃkhya · Buddhism · Cārvāka |
| Hindu Blog, summarising the work | Vaiśeṣika · Naiyāyika · Sāṃkhya · **Mīmāṃsā** · Lokāyata (= Cārvāka) · Buddhism |

Four members are common. The fifth slot is filled by **Vedānta** in one list and **Mīmāṃsā** in the other — and those are not interchangeable schools. "Six rival schools" is stable; *which* six is not, across the descriptive literature.

**Second, the edition's own analysis of contents localises only four of them.** Dhruva's contents table attaches school-labels to stanzas from XIII onward:

| stanzas | treated |
|---|---|
| XIII–XIV | Vedānta |
| XV | Sāṃkhya |
| XVI–XIX | Buddhism |
| XX | Cārvāka |
| XXI–XXX | Jaina doctrine established (ten stanzas) |
| XXXI–XXXII | conclusion |

So **Vedānta is in the poem** — that settles the fifth slot in favour of the first list — and the ten stanzas XXI–XXX are constructive rather than polemical, which means the work is not, as "thirty-two verses refuting six schools" implies, refutation end to end. Roughly a third of it builds.

Where Vaiśeṣika, Nyāya and any Mīmāṃsā material sit is **not established by this pass**: they would have to fall in stanzas I–XII, and that part of the contents table did not come back legibly. Two separate reads of the same OCR'd scan returned *different* characterisations of I–XII — one calling them introductory matter and maṅgalācaraṇa, one reporting page-ranges with no school headings at all. That disagreement is evidence about the **channel**, not about the book: the OCR of that page is not reliably readable this way. It is recorded as open rather than guessed.

### What this does to the upgrade path

[[syadvada]] and [[saptabhangi]] each list the *Syādvādamañjarī* as "not yet fetched, needed for confidence upgrade." That is now **partly discharged and partly sharpened**:

- **Discharged:** the work is located, identified, dated from its own colophon, its edition is a genuine critical one, and its architecture is known.
- **Not discharged:** **no doctrinal content was read.** The excerpts reached did not discuss the **saptabhaṅgī** at all. The claim in [[saptabhangi]] that this is "the key text for the scheme" comes from elsewhere and remains **unconfirmed against the text** — the sevenfold scheme would live in stanzas XXI–XXX, which is exactly where the next read should go.

Bibliographic contact is not doctrinal contact, and this node marks the difference rather than letting the fetch count as a reading.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence.** (a) **The Dhruva 1933 edition itself** — the primary and by far the strongest signal; (b) **Dasgupta 1922**, whose "1292 A.D." is a genuinely independent scholarly datum, arrived at before the web and matching the colophon; (c) **Hindu Blog**, an independent popular summary supplying the Śvetāmbara affiliation, the Udayaprabha Sūri discipleship and the *other* six-school list; (d) **bookseller/publisher descriptions** (Exotic India, Vedic Books, AbeBooks, Garuda, Biblia Impex) — these reproduce one editorial blurb across many sites and are counted as **one** signal, not five. Copies are not confirmation.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — this node *is* a commentator, and the anchor works in the usual way: the base text ([[hemacandra]]'s thirty-two verses) is known, the gloss is dated, and the teacher is named. The unusual feature is the second name in the colophon.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — *syādvāda-mañjarī*, "flower-cluster of syādvāda," and *anyayoga-vyavaccheda-dvātriṃśikā*, "the thirty-two [verses] excluding the other doctrine." The titles are transparent and describe the two halves of the same operation: rule out the rivals, then bloom. **Note what is *not* done here:** the introduction fetched does not gloss *anyayoga-vyavaccheda*, so the rendering above is morphological reading, offered as such and not as an edition's translation. `term_devanagari` is **omitted** — no source consulted printed it, and §3 forbids inventing script.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/` for *Malliṣeṇa*: four files name him ([[hemacandra]], [[syadvada]], [[saptabhangi]], [[anekantavada]]) and none had a node to point at. The sweep is also what exposed the six-school discrepancy, because [[hemacandra]] had committed to one of the two lists in a link note.
5. **Provenance** — a **critical edition with apparatus** (BORI, Bombay Sanskrit and Prakrit Series), consulted directly, is the best provenance available to this corpus for a Jain philosophical text. That is what lifts this above the bookseller layer. What holds confidence at **medium** rather than high is the *channel*: an OCR'd scan read through a summarising fetch, from which structure and colophon came back cleanly and doctrine did not.

**CONVERGED** on author, sect, teacher, single-work output, base text, and date — the last confirmed twice over, by an independent 1922 scholarly dating and by the work's own colophon. Confidence **medium**: the reading was bibliographic, not doctrinal; the stanza-assignment for I–XII failed to resolve; and one sub-claim the corpus had asserted (the exact six schools) turns out to be **unstable across the descriptive literature** and is corrected below rather than repeated.
