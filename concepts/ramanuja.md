## Gloss / Divergence map

**Rāmānuja** is the systematiser of [[vishishtadvaita|Viśiṣṭādvaita]] and the central ācārya of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism — "the most influential exponent of a theistic interpretation of Vedāntic philosophy" (Carman). This node is **about the man, not the doctrine**: the doctrine lives in `vishishtadvaita.md`, which stood for forty-odd batches as *a school with no node for its founder*. What the man-node adds is the part the doctrine-node cannot carry — **that almost every biographical fact about him is disputed, and that the disputes are of three different kinds.**

He is **contested**, and the contest is not about what he thought. On the philosophy the sources converge completely. They diverge on **when he lived**, on **what he wrote**, and on **whether the tradition's own account of him is history at all**.

### Divergence 1 — the dates

| reading | who holds it (source) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **1017–1137 CE** (120 years) | Śrī Vaiṣṇava hagiographical tradition; carried in *Encyclopedia of Religion* (Carman 1987) and the popular literature | Accepting a 120-year lifespan — a **schematic number**, not an observation. |
| **c. 1077–1157 CE** (80 years) | John B. Carman, from "11th- and 12th-century temple records and regional literature **outside** the Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition" | A normal lifespan, and the principle that external epigraphy outranks in-tradition hagiography. Moves him a **full sixty years** later. |
| **neither is establishable** | Jon Paul Sydnor (2012), 20–22: reconstructing his "historical life" is "nearly impossible"; Carman's dating is "well-reasoned" but speculative and "ultimately unverifiable" | That the question is **not open pending evidence but closed for want of it** — the strongest reading, and the one this file adopts. |

⚠ **A caution that is itself a finding.** The revised dating is Carman's; yet the reference article on Rāmānuja **signed by Carman** in the *Encyclopedia of Religion* prints **1017–1137**. Whether that is editorial house-style, an artefact of the entry's date, or Carman distinguishing "the tradition's dates" from his own reconstruction is **not established here**. The point to carry away is narrower and safe: *a scholar's name attached to a date in a reference work is not evidence that he argued for that date.* Compare `shrikantha.md`, where a title was read as a thesis.

### Divergence 2 — the works

Nine works are traditionally attributed. The sources **do not agree on which survive scrutiny**:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **Three** are secure — *Śrī Bhāṣya*, *Vedārthasaṅgraha*, *Bhagavadgītā-bhāṣya*; everything else doubted, **including the Gadya Traya** | IEP (Ranganathan): "the authenticity of all but the three large works … have come into question" | The devotional prose-hymns are **not** the philosopher's, so the prapatti soteriology in `vishishtadvaita.md` rests on the commentaries alone |
| **Four** — the same three **plus the Gadya Traya** | Wikipedia, citing Sydnor 2012 | The surrender-theology has a first-person authorial voice behind it |

The remainder — *Vedāntadīpa*, *Vedāntasāra* (two shorter Brahmasūtra commentaries) and *Nityagrantha* (a manual of daily worship) — are doubted on both readings, though Carman's *Encyclopedia of Religion* entry lists all three Brahmasūtra commentaries without flagging any.

⚠ **Title collision, logged for the librarian.** Rāmānuja's short *Vedāntasāra* is **not** Sadānanda's 15th-c. Advaita primer of the same name — the text `prasthanatrayi.md` chased for the earliest attestation of *prasthānatraya* and recorded as "checked and not confirmed." Two different works, two different schools, one title. Neither should be searched for by title alone.

### Divergence 3 — the lineage, and whether they ever met

Rāmānuja is the **third of the three great ācāryas** — **Nāthamuni → Yāmuna → Rāmānuja** (Carman) — and, per Wikipedia, Yāmuna's **great-grandson through a granddaughter**. But the tradition itself holds that **they never met**: Rāmānuja arrived at Śrīraṅgam to find [[yamunacarya|Yāmuna]] already dead. The succession is therefore transmitted by **sign, not by teaching** — the hagiographies report either three curled fingers uncurling, or the corpse rising to name its heir.

This is structural, not curious: **the Śrī Vaiṣṇava chain's central link is a posthumous designation.** Rāmānuja's actual instruction came from others — Yādavaprakāśa, whom he broke with; Mahāpūrṇa, for six months; and the śūdra devotee Kāñcīpūrṇa as spiritual mentor.

### What he argued — the seven untenables

Against Advaita's [[maya-advaita|avidyā/māyā]] Rāmānuja mounts the **saptavidhā anupapatti**, the "seven untenables" — a polemic debated by Advaitins and their opponents "for several centuries." IEP frames his critique in three broad registers — **logical** (the opponent's dialectic ends in absurdity), **epistemological** ("if all means of cognition involve error … we could never know that all cognition involves error"), and **hermeneutic** (the opponent privileges monistic scripture and ignores pluralistic scripture) — and **does not use the sevenfold scheme at all**.

⚠ The scheme's **existence and importance** are well attested: John A. Grimes devoted a monograph to it (*The Seven Great Untenables: Sapta-vidhā Anupapatti*, Motilal Banarsidass, 1990, 130 pp.), whose ch. 2 is "The Locus of Avidyā" — corroborating **āśraya-anupapatti** as the first member. But **the enumerated list of all seven was not obtained from a source of adequate provenance this session**: only a listserv archive and a blog, which between them name six and disagree on wording. The node therefore asserts only the scheme's existence, its first member, and its target.

*Unverified, pending Grimes 1990 or the Śrī Bhāṣya itself:* **āśraya** (avidyā has no possible locus — neither Brahman nor jīva can bear it), **tirodhāna** (it cannot conceal a self-luminous Brahman), **svarūpa** (it can be neither positive nor negative nor both), **anirvacanīya** (defining it as indefinable is self-contradictory), **pramāṇa** (no valid means of cognition establishes it), **nivartaka** (nothing can remove it). A seventh is named by neither source.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — IEP, "Rāmānuja," Shyam Ranganathan (York University).** Peer-reviewed, named author. Both datings; the works-list with the three-secure verdict; Yādavaprakāśa / Mahāpūrṇa / Kāñcīpūrṇa; Yāmuna's designation; the three registers of anti-Advaita critique; *dharmabhūtajñāna*. **Signal 2 — *Encyclopedia of Religion* (1987), John B. Carman**, via encyclopedia.com — independent of signal 1 in platform, author and date: the Nāthamuni→Yāmuna→Rāmānuja sequence, the Kashmir journey to consult ancient commentaries, the nine works with all three Brahmasūtra commentaries listed unflagged, and the hermeneutic principle that all scriptural texts stand at one level, so apparent contradictions must be *resolved* rather than ranked. **Signal 3 — *Concise Oxford Dictionary of World Religions*, John Bowker** — same aggregator, different work and author: the rejection of the world as *māyā*/projection of *avidyā*, selves and matter real-but-dependent. **Signal 4 — Wikipedia, "Ramanuja"**, whose value here is its **footnotes**: the only source naming Sydnor 2012 and quoting "ultimately unverifiable," and the only one placing the Gadya Traya on the undisputed side. **Signal 5 — Grimes 1990** (bibliographic record + partial contents), for the saptavidhā anupapatti.

**Independence.** Signals 1, 2 and 5 are genuinely independent — three platforms, three named authors, 1987 / 1990 / 2000s. Signals 2 and 3 share an aggregator but are separate reference works by different authors. Signal 4 is weakly independent as prose and is used as a **pointer to a source**, not as a source; `vishishtadvaita.md` already cites it, so re-use adds no new independence to the corpus — which is why signals 1–3 were sought.

**Why contested, not converged.** Agreement on *doctrine* is total, and if this node were about the philosophy it would be `converged`. It is not: on the three biographical questions the signals split, and one holder (Sydnor) argues the split is **unresolvable in principle**. Recording this as "converged, dates c. 1017–1137" would be exactly the flattening §0 forbids.

**Why medium, not low.** The split is *mapped*, not merely noticed; every reading has a named holder. **Not high**: no primary Sanskrit was read; Sydnor 2012 and Carman's monograph (*The Theology of Rāmānuja*, 1974) were reached only through citation; the seven untenables could not be enumerated from adequate provenance.
