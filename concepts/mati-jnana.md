## Gloss

*Matijñāna* (sensory/empirical knowledge) is the first of the five Jain knowledge-types. It arises from contact between the sense organs (*indriya*) and/or the mind (*manas*) with an external object. Despite relying on direct sensory contact, Jain epistemology classifies it as *parokṣa* (indirect/mediated knowledge) because the sense organ intermediates between the soul and the object — the soul does not apprehend the object directly. Matijñāna covers all six substances but not all their infinite modes (TS 1.26), and proceeds through four internally structured stages from raw impression to retained memory.

**Signal 1 — TS 1.15 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda):** *avagrahehāvāyadhāraṇāḥ* — "Impression, inquisitiveness, comprehension, and retention are the four stages." The crane example from commentary: *avagraha* = seeing something white; *īhā* = is it crane or flag?; *avāya* = it is a crane (wings confirmed); *dhāraṇā* = recognising it as the same crane later.

**Signal 2 — TS 1.26 (directly fetched):** *matiśrutayornibandho dravyeṣvasarvaparyāyeṣu* — "The range of sensory and scriptural knowledge extends to all six substances but not to all their modes." ## The four stages

| Stage | Term | Description | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Avagraha** (impression) | Initial sensory contact; raw impression before identification | Seeing something white in the distance |
| 2 | **Īhā** (inquisitiveness) | Desire to identify; deliberation over the impression | "Is it a crane or a white flag?" |
| 3 | **Avāya** (comprehension) | Definite identification after investigation | "It is a crane" — wings are moving |
| 4 | **Dhāraṇā** (retention) | Memory fixing the cognition for recall | Recognising it as the same crane seen this morning |

The progression is a cognitive arc from raw sensation to structured memory. Mati-jñāna is not a single act but a structured four-stage process.

## The parokṣa paradox

Jain epistemology calls mati-jñāna *parokṣa* (indirect), even though it involves direct sensory contact. The rationale: the soul does not apprehend the object unmediated — it operates *through* the sense organs, which are themselves material (*pudgala*). The sense organ is an instrument (*karaṇa*), and any knowledge that requires an instrument is indirect.

This inverts the usual assumption: Dignāga's Buddhist epistemology calls sensory perception *pratyakṣa* (direct) because it is non-conceptual and pre-verbal. Jainism calls it *parokṣa* because it is sense-mediated. Both frameworks use "direct/indirect" but with incompatible criteria.

| Tradition | Sensory perception classified as |
|---|---|
| **Jain** | *Parokṣa* (indirect — sense-mediated) |
| **Buddhist (Dignāga)** | *Pratyakṣa* (direct — non-conceptual, pre-verbal) |
| **Nyāya** | *Pratyakṣa* (direct — sense-object contact) |
