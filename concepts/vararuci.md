## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vararuci** is not one man. Abhyankar's entry is a **four-way disambiguation**, numbered by him:

| # | who | what he wrote | date |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(1)** | the figure "identified with [[katyayana|Kātyāyana]], the prominent author of the Vārttikas" | the Vārttikas — **on an identification Abhyankar goes on to reject** | 3rd–4th c. BCE, if he exists |
| **(2)** | ⭐ a real, separate grammarian, "**Kātyāyana by Gotra name**" | a **Prākṛt grammar** (the *Prākṛtaprakāśa*), and — with **Sarvavarman**, author of the first three — the **fourth adhyāya of the Kātantra Sūtras** | "**later than Patañjali**" |
| **(3)** | "a comparatively modern grammarian" | a treatise on the **genders** of words, ~125 stanzas, with a commentary *Liṅgavṛtti* "possibly written by the author himself" | modern |
| **(4)** | "another modern grammarian" | a work **on syntax**, discussing four topics | modern |

⭐ **And Vararuci (2) is the mechanism of the confusion, stated in one clause.** Abhyankar: "This Vararuci, who **also was [Kātyāyana] by Gotra name**, was a grammarian later than Patañjali." **Two different men bore *both* names.** The conflation is therefore not a copyist's slip or a pious invention — it is what happens when a personal name and a gotra name coincide twice over. That is why the tradition merged them, and it is a better explanation than "later writers identified them."

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐ T2 — falsified, in the queue's own favourite way: the test was aimed at the wrong target.** The test asked whether a *node* could hold the Vararuci split whole, given Batch 47's finding that Abhyankar's dictionary "does not check itself across entries." Reading the **whole** entry rather than its opening changes the picture:

- **What Batch 47 said, and what remains true:** the *Vararuci* entry's opening calls the Kātyāyana identification "**very likely**," and the same entry then supplies the evidence refuting it — "There is **no reference at all in the Mahābhāṣya to Vararuci as a writer of the Vārttikas**; there is only one reference which shows that there was a scholar by name Vararuci known to Patañjali, **but he was a poet**" (M.Bh. on A 4.2.4). ✅ **Accurate.**
- **What Batch 47's framing got wrong:** that this makes the entry a specimen of a *pathology*. It does not. **Sense (1) is an argument** — it states the received view, tests it against the earliest witness, and rejects it, concluding "it is only the **later writers** who identified" them. Senses (2)–(4) then separate three further men. ⭐ **The entry is not self-contradictory; it is a disambiguation that argues.** Batch 47 read the opening of sense (1) plus the *Kātyāyana* entry, and diagnosed the book from two-thirds of one entry.

⭐ **The defect is real but differently located: it is the missing cross-reference, not the entry.** Abhyankar's *Kātyāyana* entry reports the identification with a rider — "Some scholars say that Vararuci was also another name given to him, **in which case** the Vārttikakāra Vararuci Kātyāyana has to be looked upon as **different from the subsequent writer named Vararuci**" — and never points at the *Vararuci* entry where the question is settled. **The same book gives you two different answers depending on which headword you enter by.** ⚠ **That is a weaker and more precise claim than Batch 47's, and it is the one the evidence supports.** The corpus's Batch 46 generalisation — that reference works are unreliable *as a set of witnesses* because they do not check themselves across articles — survives intact and is in fact **exactly** what this shows; what does not survive is the stronger reading, that the entry itself was incoherent. Corrected in [[katyayana]] this batch.

**⭐ Four attributions to one name — and the corpus's previous record was three.** [[patanjali]] holds a **three-way** conflation (grammarian, yogin, physician) with an identified theological motor. Vararuci is **four-way**, and its motor is entirely mundane: **a name collision compounded by a gotra collision.** ⚠ The two cases are worth holding side by side precisely because their causes differ — one is driven by a doctrine (an avatar of Ādi Śeṣa for whom mastering several sciences follows from the premise), the other by nothing more than nomenclature. **"Same name, therefore same man" needs no motive to do damage.**

**What Vararuci (2) opens that the corpus does not have.** He is the author of a **Prākṛt grammar** and of one quarter of the **Kātantra** — a *non-Pāṇinian* Sanskrit grammar, associated with **Sarvavarman**. ⚠ **The corpus has no node for the Kātantra, for Sarvavarman, or for Prākṛt grammar at all**, and has treated "Sanskrit grammar" as effectively coextensive with the Pāṇinian tradition throughout Batches 47–48. **That is a real gap, now named**: there were rival grammatical systems with their own sūtras and their own commentators, and this batch has met two of them in passing (the Kātantra here; the Buddhist **Cāndra-Vyākaraṇa** at [[kasika]]). Recorded as a follow-up, not filled in — §8 scope-lock.

**The legendary layer, and the corpus turning a source's verdict on another source.** Wikipedia supports the identification from the ***Kathāsaritsāgara***, where Kātyāyana is another name of Vararuci, a reincarnation of Śiva's gaṇa Puṣpadanta. ⚠ [[katyayana]] already records the decisive point and it is repeated here because it belongs to this node: the *Kathāsaritsāgara* is the very text **Abhyankar elsewhere calls "only legendary and hence, it has very little historical value"** ([[panini]]). **A story in an 11th-century tale-collection is the evidential basis for a claim about a 4th-century-BCE grammarian.**

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** ⚠ **Weak, and stated as such.** The four-way split rests on **Abhyankar alone** — read at first hand and argued from the *Mahābhāṣya*, which is the strongest kind of single source, but still one source. Wikipedia (via [[katyayana]]) supplies only the legendary identification. **No independent confirmation of senses (3) and (4) was sought or found**, and the *Prākṛtaprakāśa* attribution was not checked outside this entry. **CONTESTED**, confidence **medium** — the *reasoning* is first-rate and first-hand; the *corroboration* is thin, and the corpus should not mistake the one for the other.
