## Gloss / Divergence map

**Prasthānatrayī** — "the three *prasthāna*s" — is Vedānta's name for its **canonical base**: the [[upanishad|principal Upaniṣads]], the [[brahma-sutra|Brahmasūtra]], and the [[gita|Bhagavadgītā]], taken together as the set of texts a Vedānta school must interpret in order to be a Vedānta school. It is **not itself a text**. It is a *category-term* — the name of a required reading list — and this node exists to keep that distinction visible in a corpus where every neighbouring node *is* a book.

The three members carry technical designations, and the designations are **where the sources vary**, though the membership never does:

| member | primary name | also called (by source) | what the name claims |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[upanishad\|principal Upaniṣads]] | **śruti-prasthāna** | *upadeśa*-prasthāna, "injunctive texts" (Wikipedia) | the axiom of **revelation** |
| [[gita\|Bhagavadgītā]] | **smṛti-prasthāna** | *sādhana*-prasthāna, "practical text" (Wikipedia) | the axiom of **remembered tradition** |
| [[brahma-sutra\|Brahmasūtra]] | **nyāya-prasthāna** | *sūtra*-prasthāna, *yukti*-prasthāna (Wikipedia); *tarka*-prasthāna (Svāminārāyaṇa study) | the axiom of **reasoning** |

Read the third column and the set stops looking like an anthology. The trayī is **one text per source of authority** — revelation, tradition, argument — which is a claim about *epistemology* wearing the costume of a bibliography. What the tradition canonised was not three books it happened to like, but a **three-way division of where warrant comes from**, with a text nominated to occupy each slot.

### Three is a count of slots, not of books

The corpus should not let "three" pass unexamined. The first member is not a text but a **list**, and the list is not fixed:

- Śaṅkara's Upaniṣad-bhāṣyas cover the **ten** *mukhya* Upaniṣads (Īśa, Kena, Kaṭha, Praśna, Muṇḍaka, Māṇḍūkya, Taittirīya, Aitareya, Chāndogya, Bṛhadāraṇyaka), so the "three" sources are, counted as volumes, **twelve**.
- "According to most Hindu traditions, ten Upanishads are considered as Principal Upanishads, but some scholars include Śvetāśvatara, Kauṣītaki and Maitrāyaṇīya into the list."
- And the boundary case is *inside* the attributed corpus: a Śvetāśvatara commentary **is** attributed to Śaṅkara, but "on comparison with his other commentaries, there is some doubt that it is indeed composed by him."

So the canon's first slot has a **soft edge**, and one of the texts that would widen it is held there by an attribution its own tradition doubts. Nothing here destabilises the trayī — the *slot* is secure and no source disputes it — but "the three canonical texts of Vedānta" is a phrase that survives only because the first of the three is allowed to be plural.

### What the term does: it is an entry requirement

The sources agree on the *function* far more strongly than they explain the *history*. A Vedānta ācārya establishes a school by writing bhāṣyas on **all three** — Śaṅkarācārya, Rāmānujācārya, Madhvācārya, Nimbārkācārya, Vallabhācārya and Rāmānandācārya are named as having done so, and the Svāminārāyaṇa Sampradāya's modern *Bhāṣyam* is presented as the same move made again in the present.

That makes the trayī an unusual object: **a canon defined by what commenting on it entitles you to claim.** The commentary is not optional homage paid to a scripture that already had its authority; on the tradition's own account the two constitute each other, and a metaphysics that has not been shown to fall out of all three is not yet a Vedānta. The Sringeri Śāradā Pīṭham calls Śaṅkara's bhāṣya "on Prasthānatriya … the most influential work in Indian thought" — praise aimed, note, at a *commentary set*, not at a doctrine.

### The `prasthāna` pun this corpus was already using

Monier-Williams gives *prasthāna* a literal sense — "setting out, departure, procession, march (especially of an army or assailant)" — and, alongside it, "**a way to attain (any object), course, method, system**" and "**a sect**"; Apte agrees ("a method, system"; "a religious school, sect"). Both technical senses are live, and **this corpus was already running both without having named the fact**: `prasthānatrayī` uses *prasthāna* = "canonical starting-point," while [[prakasatman]], [[amalananda]] and [[vacaspati-mishra]] carry the Advaita sub-schools as the **Vivaraṇa-prasthāna** and **Bhāmatī-prasthāna**, where *prasthāna* = "branch, line of interpretation." Same word, two jobs one level apart: the texts you set out from, and the road you took from them. The lexicon licenses both, and the double usage is a fair index of how thoroughly the tradition thought of doctrine as *itinerary*.

### What is not established here

**No source consulted dates the term.** Neither Wikipedia, nor the Sringeri Pīṭham, nor either academic study says when *prasthānatrayī* was first used as a collective designation, or whether Śaṅkara — who supplies its paradigm case — ever used it of his own three bhāṣyas. A promising lead (that Sadānanda's 15th-c. *Vedāntasāra* opens by identifying the Vedānta writings as the *prasthānatraya*) was **checked and not confirmed**: the article on that text names the three works but does not use the collective term. It is recorded below as an unverified lead and is **not** asserted here. The safe statement is that the *category* is at least as old as the practice it describes, and that its earliest attestation was not established in this pass.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — Wikipedia (encyclopedia), the Sringeri Śāradā Pīṭham (the maṭha traditionally traced to Śaṅkara — a *tradition-internal institutional* source, not derivable from the encyclopedia), and **two separate academic studies** hosted on Wisdomlib: one on Pratyabhijñā and Śaṅkara's Advaita, one on the Svāminārāyaṇa *Bhāṣyam*. The last pair are the useful ones, because they reach the trayī from **opposite directions** — a Kashmir-Śaiva comparison, and a modern Vaiṣṇava sampradāya's own bhāṣya project — and still give the same three members and the same slot-logic.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor, and here it *is* the concept: the trayī is defined by the bhāṣya-sets written on it, and the six ācāryas named are the evidence.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — Monier-Williams and Apte on *prasthāna* supply both technical senses *and* the literal "setting out," which is what makes "starting point / point of departure" a translation rather than a gloss reverse-engineered to fit. *Trayī* is the ordinary feminine "triad" (compare *trayī vidyā*, the three Vedas) — the word choice already frames the set as a canon-shaped object.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/` and `chapters/` for *prasthāna*: it was already in the corpus in its *second* sense (Vivaraṇa-, Bhāmatī-, Vārttika-prasthāna) before this node existed. The two usages are consistent with the lexicon, and only the sweep surfaced them.
5. **Provenance** — no critical edition is involved and none could be: the term names a set, not a text. Weighting falls on reference works and studies, which is one reason confidence stops at medium.
6. **Corpus cross-check** — the trayī's deliberate **heterogeneity** (one śruti member, one smṛti member) is already recorded in Ch 19, and it sits in real tension with [[mimamsa-pramana|Mīmāṃsā's]] grounding of scriptural authority in śruti alone. The Svāminārāyaṇa study preserves the tradition's own resolution: the Gītā qualifies **because** it clarifies and reaffirms principles already in the Vedas and Upaniṣads — derivative authority, not independent authority.

**CONVERGED** on membership, function, and the three slot-names. Every source gives the same three texts, the same śruti/smṛti/nyāya division, and the same account of what commenting on all three achieves. Confidence **medium**, not high, for three stated reasons: **no source dates the term**; the alternative designations (*upadeśa* / *sādhana* / *sūtra* / *yukti* / *tarka*) are attested unevenly across sources rather than agreed; and the membership of the first slot has a soft edge — ten, or eleven, or twelve, with one disputed attribution sitting on the boundary.
