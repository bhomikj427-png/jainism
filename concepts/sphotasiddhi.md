## Gloss

The ***Sphoṭasiddhi*** — "the establishment of *sphoṭa*" — is [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra's]] (fl. 9th c.) treatise defending the [[sphota|sphoṭa]] doctrine of linguistic meaning. Its method is what makes it worth a node of its own: **it argues by assembling both sides out of quotations.** It "present[s] the main arguments of **Kumārila** against the sphoṭa doctrine **supported by quotations from the *Ślokavārttika***, while also presenting the arguments of **Bhartṛhari** to establish the existence of sphoṭa, **with quotations from the [[vakyapadiya|*Vākyapadīya*]]**" — and then establishes sphoṭa.

⭐⭐ **The author is refuting his own teacher.** [[mandana-mishra]] records that Maṇḍana was **[[kumarila-bhatta|Kumārila's]] student in Mīmāṃsā**. In the *Sphoṭasiddhi* he takes his teacher's position as the *pūrvapakṣa* and defeats it with the grammarians' arguments. ⭐ **A Mīmāṃsaka defends a Vyākaraṇa doctrine against the greatest Mīmāṃsaka** — and this is the same man the corpus otherwise knows as a Mīmāṃsaka-turned-Advaitin and Śaṅkara's rival. **His "second career" is a third one.**

## Reasoning across signals

**⚠⚠ T4 — FALSIFIED, and the false premise was the corpus's own.** Batch 47's follow-up #2 read: *"Maṇḍana Miśra wrote a Sphoṭasiddhi — **absent from `mandana-mishra.md`**, which holds him purely as Mīmāṃsaka-turned-Advaitin and Śaṅkara's rival. A node the corpus thought it knew has a second career."*

**`mandana-mishra.md` was not missing it.** The *Sphoṭasiddhi* stands in that file's `source_text` front-matter, and its gloss says in as many words: "He defended the *sphoṭa* theory of language (Sphoṭasiddhi)." ⭐ **The gap was in the follow-up, not in the corpus.** A batch working on [[sphota]] met Maṇḍana's name in a grammatical source, recorded a discovery, and did not check the node that already held it.

⚠ **This is a §4 signal-4 failure, and it is worth naming precisely because that signal exists to catch exactly this.** The charter's fourth signal is *internal consistency — "is the term rendered the same across every passage? **Sweep with grep and check**."* A one-line grep would have closed the follow-up before it opened, and instead it was carried into a batch queue as a named test. ⭐ **The corpus's recurring finding about reference works — that they do not check themselves across their own entries — has now been observed in the corpus itself.** [[vararuci]] this batch narrowed that charge against Abhyankar to "a missing cross-reference"; the identical defect, in the identical form, is here. **Recorded rather than quietly dropped, because a batch that only reports the tests it passes is not running tests.**

**Why the node is worth writing anyway — on better grounds than the queue's.** The *Sphoṭasiddhi*'s interest is **structural, and it is the corpus's own recurring pattern in a new place**: it is a text that **preserves the position it refutes**. Kumārila's anti-sphoṭa arguments reach a reader through the treatise written to defeat them, quoted from the *Ślokavārttika*. ⭐ That is the shape [[katyayana]] records at its sharpest — the Vārttikas surviving only inside [[mahabhashya|the commentary that discusses them]] — and it recurs here without any commentarial relation at all: **not a commentary preserving its root text, but a polemic preserving its opponent.** ⚠ Unlike the Kātyāyana case, the *Ślokavārttika* survives independently, so nothing here is *only* known through Maṇḍana; the parallel is one of method, not of dependence, and is drawn no further.

**What the corpus can and cannot say about the argument.** ⚠ **Nothing.** No part of the *Sphoṭasiddhi*'s reasoning has been read — neither Kumārila's objections nor Maṇḍana's answers — and this node reports only **that** the text does this and **from which sources** it quotes. The one substantive doctrinal claim ("establishes the existence of sphoṭa") is the sources' summary, not a reading. ⭐ **The corpus knows the architecture of an argument it has not read**, which is precisely the D1 condition `DRIFT.md` measures — and here the remedy is unusually cheap, because a Sanskrit text with facing English translation exists.

**⭐ A pleasing convergence of editors, noted for what it is worth.** The *Sphoṭasiddhi*'s standard edition and English translation (1966) is by **K. A. Subramania Iyer** — the same scholar whose Deccan College critical edition of *Vākyapadīya* kāṇḍa III with [[helaraja|Helārāja]] (1963) this batch identified at [[helaraja]]. ⚠ **This is a fact about 20th-century Indology, not about the 9th century**, and the corpus draws no edge from it. It does mean **one editor's output would supply two of this batch's unread primary texts.**

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** ⚠ **Thin, and the status reflects only what is claimed.** The description of the text's method and sources is reported consistently across the publishing and secondary literature but was **not traced to a named scholar**; `mandana-mishra.md`'s independently-established biography (Kumārila's student; the Advaita turn) supplies the second signal and is what makes the "refuting his teacher" observation possible. **CONVERGED** — because nothing about *this text's* authorship, contents or purpose is in dispute in any source consulted — with confidence **medium**, and with the warning above that no line of it has been read. ⚠ The genuinely contested question in this neighbourhood is **Maṇḍana's identity** (the Maṇḍana = Sureśvara legend), and that is held at [[mandana-mishra]], not duplicated here.
