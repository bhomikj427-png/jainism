## Gloss / Divergence map

The **henads** (*henádes*, "unities") are divine participated unities in the metaphysics of [[proclus|Proclus]] (412–485 CE), introduced to solve the central Neoplatonic problem: how can the absolutely simple, transcendent One be the cause of all multiplicity without itself becoming multiple? The solution: the One remains **unparticipated** (untouched, beyond participation), while a rank of participated henads — individual unities, identified with the **gods** of traditional polytheism — proceeds from it and is participated by the manifold of Being below. Each henad is supremely one yet distinct, so the henads form the first articulated multiplicity, mediating between the unparticipated One and the realm of Being. The doctrine integrates Greek polytheism into Neoplatonic metaphysics and is systematized in Proclus's *Elements of Theology*.

**Converged on function, contested on origin.** The *mediating function* is firmly attested; the doctrine's *origin* is genuinely disputed:

| claim | who holds it |
|---|---|
| originated with Iamblichus (from his "law of mean terms") | some scholars (per SEP) |
| originated with Syrianus, Proclus's teacher | others (per SEP) |
| Proclus systematized but did not necessarily invent it | consensus framing |
