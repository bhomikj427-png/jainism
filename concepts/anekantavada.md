## Gloss / Divergence map

**Philological caution:** The term *anekāntavāda* does not appear in the earliest texts considered canonical by the Śvetāmbara tradition (Wikipedia, citing Jain sources). The *Tattvārthasūtra* provides the foundational naya (standpoint/partial perspective) doctrine — especially TS 1.6 "pramāṇanayaiḥ" (by valid cognitions and standpoints) — on which anekāntavāda is built; but the named, formulated doctrine emerges in post-TS authors: Samantabhadra (*Āptamīmāṃsā*, ~2nd c.), Siddhasena Divākar (~5th c., credited with coining the term), Mallavādin (~5th–6th c., first formulator of *saptabhaṅgī*), and Akalanka (~8th c., called "Master of Jain logic"). The divergence map below covers the contested readings scholars hold; the disagreements themselves are the finding.

| reading | who holds it (source) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-one-sidedness** (negative, polemical): the doctrine's work is *rejecting* any single ekānta (absolutist) view; what it affirms beyond that is implicit | Paul Dundas, *The Jains* (Routledge, 2002): "a fearsome weapon of philosophical polemic"; Mookerjee: "non-absolutism"; Sanghvi: "non-extremism" | Anekāntavāda is primarily a dialectical tool, not a positive metaphysical claim; the plurality is not in reality but in the inadequacy of single-perspective claims |
| **Many-sidedness** (positive, ontological): reality is genuinely multifaceted; the doctrine is a positive claim about the structure of existence | Most popular translations ("many-sidedness"); Charitrapragya; general Jain tradition presentation | The doctrine asserts that substances (dravya) have genuinely simultaneous, contradictory-seeming attributes (permanent/impermanent, one/many) and philosophy must acknowledge all of them |
| **Epistemological locus**: anekāntavāda concerns the limits of finite knowing — no finite perspective grasps the whole; only kevala-jñāna (omniscience) can | Anil Mundra (U. Alabama, 2022, barefield.ua.edu): "epistemic respect for the view of others"; John Koller: "not relativism — it means epistemological respect"; Matilal: "syādvāda is not a theory of uncertainty, doubt or relative probabilities" | The doctrine is about human cognitive limitation, not about metaphysical indeterminacy; omniscience is in principle attainable |
| **Methodological/hermeneutical locus**: a systematic framework for incorporating partial perspectives in philosophical argument | Samantabhadra's *Āptamīmāṃsā* v. 105 (via Mundra 2022): syādvāda "illuminates substances" indirectly where kevala-jñāna illuminates directly | Anekāntavāda is a procedure for philosophy, not a doctrine about reality's ultimate structure |
| **syāt = "maybe/perhaps"** (skeptical reading, based on general Sanskrit optative usage) | Early Western translators; general Sanskrit grammar: 3rd-person singular optative of *as* = "perchance, may be, perhaps" | Anekāntavāda entails skepticism and relativism — no proposition unequivocally holds; this commits to the view that Jainism cannot make any definite truth-claims |
| **syāt = "in a certain respect / conditionally"** (standpoint-relative reading) | Bimal K. Matilal (*The Central Philosophy of Jainism*, 1981): "conditional yes or conditional approval"; John Koller; Mundra 2022; most contemporary scholars | *Syāt* parameterizes an assertion to a defined standpoint (naya); the doctrine is perspectival, not skeptical; Jain omniscience claims remain coherent |
| **Tolerance / pluralism / intellectual ahiṃsā** (modern ethical-political reading) | Matilal (following Dhruva, Kapadia): "intellectual ahiṃsā [nonviolence]" and "toleration"; Ganeri: "syncretism" | The doctrine is an ethics of epistemic tolerance; widely criticized as importing modern liberal values into an ancient polemical tradition |
| **Quantum complementarity parallel** | D.S. Kothari, "The Complementarity Principle and Eastern Philosophy," *Niels Bohr Centenary Volume*, Harvard UP; various popularizers; ResearchGate (2023): "anekāntavāda = complementarity" | Bohr's complementarity (measurement-context-dependent quantum properties) is structurally mapped onto syāt-predication; **scholars consider this an anachronistic overlay** — see conflation link below |

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia (Anekāntavāda article):** Cites Matilal, Koller, Dundas, Charitrapragya, Mookerjee, Ganeri, Sanghvi, Balcerowicz. Summary: scholarly consensus firmly rejects the skeptical reading of *syāt* (standpoint-relative reading prevails); negative/positive formulation divide persists; no consensus on epistemological vs. ontological vs. methodological locus. Provenance: aggregated; individual citations need primary-source verification in a future run.

**Signal 2 — Anil Mundra, globalcritical.barefield.ua.edu (2022):** Independently presents the three-register analysis (ontological + semantic + epistemological); confirms standpoint-relative *syāt*; cites Samantabhadra's *Āptamīmāṃsā* v. 105 directly. Provenance: academic web essay, U. Alabama; not a peer-reviewed article — medium weight.

**Signal 3 — New World Encyclopedia entry:** Confirms *syāt* = "in some ways" / "from a perspective"; discusses nayavāda's role; does not resolve negative/positive or epistemological/ontological debate. Derivative — not independently sourced.

**Signal 4 — Philological signal (Śvetāmbara canonicity):** Term absent from early Śvetāmbara canon; attributed by search results to Siddhasena Divākar. This weakens any reading that treats anekāntavāda as a single, unified TS doctrine rather than a post-TS systematisation of TS nayas. Needs independent scholarly verification (Dundas *The Jains* would be the right primary check — not fetched).

**Signal 5 — Quantum/physics comparison:** ResearchGate paper exists explicitly making the comparison. D.S. Kothari cited as its academic originator. No scholarly rebuttal article was found in fetching (search returned only pro-comparison sources); the critique is based on the conceptual analysis that quantum complementarity is operationally defined via measurement outcomes (no parallel in the TS's naya doctrine). Flagged as `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`; the absence of a fetched rebuttal is noted.

**Independence assessment:** Signals 1–3 converge on the standpoint-relative *syāt* and on the tolerance reading being contested. Signals diverge on the epistemological/ontological/methodological question — that divergence is the finding. Only Signal 2 is independently authored (not aggregated); Signal 1 cites multiple named scholars. Confidence medium: the contested readings are well-mapped but individual primary sources (Matilal 1981, Dundas 2002) were not directly fetched.
