## Gloss / Divergence map

**Satkāryavāda** (sat = real/existent; kārya = effect): the causal axiom that **the effect pre-exists in its material cause before it is produced**. Nothing new comes into existence — production is manifestation of what was already latent. The pot was already there in the clay; cloth in the threads.

**Sāṃkhyakārikā 9 (Īśvarakṛṣṇa)** — the five-argument verse (directly fetched):
> *asadakaraṇādupādānagrahāṇāt sarvasa mbhavābhāvāt | śaktasya śakyakaraṇāt kāraṇabhāvācca satkāryam*

The five arguments (pañca-kāraṇa):
1. **Asadakaraṇāt** — non-being cannot be produced (nothing from nothing)
2. **Upādānagrahāṇāt** — every effect requires a material cause (the cause provides the substance)
3. **Sarvasa mbhavābhāvāt** — not everything arises from everything (clay → pot, not gold → pot)
4. **Śaktasya śakyakaraṇāt** — a cause produces only what corresponds to its inherent capacity
5. **Kāraṇabhāvāt** — the effect shares the nature of its cause (the pot partakes of the clay's nature)

**Two variant forms** (both accept the satkāryavāda axiom):

| variant | school | ontological status of the manifested effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Pariṇāmavāda** (real transformation) | Sāṃkhya, Viśiṣṭādvaita | the effect is *real*; cause genuinely transforms into it |
| **Vivartavāda** (apparent transformation) | Advaita post-Śaṅkara | the effect is *apparent* only; cause (Brahman) does not really change |

The Chāndogya Upaniṣad (6.2.1: *sat eva somidam agra āsīt* — "In the beginning, this was Being [sat] alone") provides the Upaniṣadic precedent, interpreted by Sāṃkhya and Vedānta as supporting the pre-existence of the effect.

**Contrast with asatkāryavāda (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika)**: the effect is entirely new — it does NOT pre-exist in the cause; production is a fresh beginning (*ārambha*). This is the direct logical contrary; both theories cannot be simultaneously true.
