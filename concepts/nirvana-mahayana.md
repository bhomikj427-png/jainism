## Gloss

**Apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa** ("non-abiding cessation"; also translated "non-localized nirvāṇa") is the Mahāyāna conception of Buddhahood — a liberation that is neither static withdrawal (*pratiṣṭhita*-nirvāṇa, the Hīnayāna model) nor continued saṃsāric bondage. A Buddha in this state abides in neither extreme: the distinction between saṃsāra and nirvāṇa dissolves, enabling enlightened activity for the benefit of all beings while remaining free from afflictive entanglement.

**The structural contrast with Theravāda/Hīnayāna nirvāṇa:**

| tradition | goal | state at parinirvāṇa | relation to beings |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Theravāda / Hīnayāna** | arhat (personal liberation) | nirvāṇa without residue (*anupādāparinibbāna*); aggregates cease entirely | ends; no further return |
| **Mahāyāna** | Buddhahood (for benefit of all) | apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa; untainted aggregates persist (Form + Wisdom Dharmakāya) | Buddhas can manifest (*nirmāṇakāya*, *saṃbhogakāya*) and return to saṃsāra to help beings |

**Why the distinction matters**: The Mahāyāna critique of the Hīnayāna ideal centers on the bodhisattva vow: a being who has completed the path but delays final withdrawal out of compassion is *more* advanced, not less complete. Arhatship is recast not as the final goal but as an intermediate stage, after which arhats "will go on to the bodhisattva path" (Study Buddhism, citing Abhisamayālaṃkāra).

**Diamond Sūtra connection**: The Platform Sūtra quotes the Diamond Sūtra's formulation of non-abidance: *"Responding to the non-abiding, yet generating the mind"* (應無所住，而生其心; *anena ca na kiṃcid* analogue) — generating bodhicitta without fixing on any object, including liberation itself.

**NOT the same as saṃsāric existence**: Buddhas manifesting in saṃsāra are not *re-entangled*; the non-abidance is precisely the freedom from fixing on either saṃsāra or static nirvāṇa. Buswell and Lopez: "enables them to freely return to samsara to help sentient beings, while still remaining in nirvāṇa and being a buddha."

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Study Buddhism, "Nirvana in the Different Buddhist Systems":** Defines apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa as the Mahāyāna Buddhahood state; distinguishes it from Hīnayāna "nirvāṇa without residue"; explains that in the Prasaṅgika-Gelug system it encompasses Form Bodies and Wisdom Dharmakāya simultaneously; notes arhat continuity toward bodhisattva path (citing *Abhisamayālaṃkāra*). **Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Non-abidance":** Defines apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa; attributes it to Diamond Sūtra tradition; cites Buswell & Lopez (*Princeton Dictionary of Buddhism*) on the "freely return to saṃsāra" formula. Two independent sources converge on the structural definition. Medium confidence: the *Abhisamayālaṃkāra* verse and Diamond Sūtra passage were not fetched in Sanskrit from a critical edition.
