## Gloss / Divergence map

**Taṇhā** (Pali, "thirst"; Sanskrit cognate: *tṛṣṇā*): the **Second Noble Truth** (*samudaya sacca*) — the proximate cause of *dukkha* (suffering). Taṇhā is not mere desire or preference but **compulsive craving rooted in ignorance** (*avijjā*): the grasping impulse that generates kamma and keeps consciousness bound to the cycle of rebirth (*saṃsāra*). Its cessation (*taṇhā-nirodha*) is the **Third Noble Truth** and the definition of nibbāna.

**The Second Noble Truth** (SN 56.11, *Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta* — First Turning of the Wheel):
> *"Idaṃ kho pana, bhikkhave, dukkhasamudayaṃ ariyasaccaṃ: yāyaṃ taṇhā ponobhavikā nandirāgasahagatā tatratatrābhinandinī, seyyathidaṃ— kāmataṇhā, bhavataṇhā, vibhavataṇhā."*
> "And this, monks, is the noble truth of the origin of suffering: it is this craving which leads to renewed existence, accompanied by delight and lust, seeking delight here and there — that is, craving for sensual pleasures, craving for existence, craving for extermination."

**Three types of taṇhā:**

| type | Pali | meaning | what it grasps at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kāma-taṇhā | craving for sensual pleasure | desire for pleasant sense objects (sights, sounds, smells, tastes, tactile sensations) | the six sense objects (*ārammaṇa*) |
| Bhava-taṇhā | craving for existence / becoming | desire to persist, to be someone, to continue as a self; drives rebirth | existence itself; future states of being |
| Vibhava-taṇhā | craving for non-existence | desire for annihilation, for erasure of an unpleasant experience or of the self entirely | cessation of what exists (but through aversion, not wisdom) |

Note: vibhava-taṇhā is itself a form of craving — the desire to *not-be* is as much a grasping impulse as the desire to *be*. This closes the loophole of nihilism (seeking liberation through annihilation) as equivalent to wisdom.

**Taṇhā in the 12-link chain of paṭicca-samuppāda:**

avijjā (ignorance) → saṅkhārā (formations) → viññāṇa (consciousness) → nāmarūpa (name-and-form) → saḷāyatana (six sense-bases) → phassa (contact) → **vedanā** (feeling: pleasant/unpleasant/neutral) → **taṇhā** (craving) → **upādāna** (clinging) → **bhava** (becoming) → jāti (birth) → jarāmaraṇa (aging-and-death)

Taṇhā is the **8th nidāna** and arises directly from *vedanā* (feeling): whenever feeling is experienced, if avijjā is present, taṇhā arises. This is why eliminating taṇhā requires not eliminating feeling but eliminating the ignorance that converts feeling into craving. The sequence taṇhā → upādāna → bhava is the psychological engine of rebirth: craving solidifies into clinging, which generates the "becoming" that produces a next existence.

**Third Noble Truth — cessation (nirodha):** Taṇhā-nirodha is the "complete fading away and cessation of this craving; its abandonment and relinquishment; getting free from and being independent of it" (SN 56.11). Nibbāna *is* the cessation of taṇhā — not a state to crave for, but the natural consequence of craving's extinction. The Eightfold Path is the *means* (Fourth Noble Truth) by which taṇhā is extinguished through cultivated insight into the three characteristics (anicca, dukkha, anattā).

**Cross-tradition comparison — the "first-cause-of-bondage" problem:**

| tradition | "first cause" of suffering/bondage | mechanism | psychological or physical? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theravāda | taṇhā (craving / thirst) | vedanā → taṇhā → upādāna; kamma generated | psychological; avijjā is the deeper root |
| Jain | āsrava (karmic influx) via kaṣāya (passions) | yoga (activity) + kaṣāya → karma-matter bonds to jīva | both: physical karma-matter + psychological passions |
| Advaita Vedānta | avidyā (ignorance/māyā) superimposing apparent multiplicity | superimposition of name-and-form on brahman | primarily epistemic / ontological |
| Hindu (Sāṃkhya-Yoga) | puruṣa's mis-identification with prakṛti | aviveka-khyāti (failure of discrimination) → attachment | epistemic / psychological |

The mechanism differs in each case. Taṇhā is specifically a **craving impulse arising from feeling**, not a metaphysical ignorance or a physical influx — though avijjā underlies it and kamma-matter is the Jain alternative.
