## Gloss

**Prajñākaragupta** (mid-8th to 9th century) is the Buddhist epistemologist whose commentary on [[dharmakirti|Dharmakīrti]] stopped being a commentary. His ***Pramāṇavārttikālaṅkāra*** ("Ornament of the *Pramāṇavārttika*"), also transmitted as the *Pramāṇavārttikabhāṣya*, is an extended commentary on the [[pramanavarttika|*Pramāṇavārttika*]] running to **over 16,200 Sanskrit ślokas** — and its distinguishing feature is that, "unlike other commentaries," it "involves the author's **independent arguments** on several controversial topics of Buddhist philosophy of religion, epistemology, and logic."

The corpus has been referring to him since Batch-era work on Dharmottara without having a node for him: [[dharmottara]] and [[dharmottara-nyayabindu]] both record that Dharmottara's *niścaya-pratyaya* "was subsequently challenged by Prajñākaragupta," and Ch 20 uses him as evidence that "the tradition kept arguing after its founders." This node is that name given its own file.

### What he rejected: the ascertaining cognition

[[dharmottara|Dharmottara]]'s repair to Dharmakīrti's epistemology was a **three-stage model** — raw non-conceptual perception → ***niścaya-pratyaya*** (ascertaining cognition, operating through [[apoha|exclusion]]) → action/propositional understanding. The middle stage exists to solve a real problem: a bare sensory contact with no conceptual content cannot by itself tell you what to do, so something must convert it into something usable.

**Prajñākaragupta rejected the intermediary stage.** The corpus records the rejection as a fact and does **not** claim to know his positive replacement — no source consulted here states his own account of how *niścaya* is achieved, and the specialist literature that would (Miyo's work on *vyavasthā* in Dharmottara's and Prajñākaragupta's explanations of *pramāṇa* and *pramāṇaphala*) was identified and not read. That is a hole, and it is named rather than filled.

What the exchange establishes is structural and does not depend on the missing detail: **the Buddhist epistemological tradition was still repairing its own foundations two generations after Dharmakīrti, and its sharpest arguments were internal.** Ch 20 §"the running exchange is Buddhist-internal" already collects the pattern — Nāgārjuna against Abhidharma, Vasubandhu against his own root text, Prāsaṅgika against Svātantrika, Śāntarakṣita adjudicating Yogācāra against Madhyamaka — and Prajñākaragupta against Dharmottara is the epistemological instance.

### The two doctrines he is known for

| doctrine | what it is | where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| **omniscience** (*sarvajñatva*) | a sustained defence of the Buddha's omniscience and, with it, of **religious authority** — Moriyama's monograph on him is titled *Omniscience and Religious Authority*, and the *Pramāṇavārttikālaṅkāra* on PV III.281–286 treats **yogic perception** as the faculty involved | the join between epistemology and soteriology: if perception can be trained to a limit, the founder's authority is an *epistemological* result rather than a claim of faith |
| **backward causation** | his "unique theor[y] of… backward causation," treated in the literature as **"Prajñākaragupta on *pratītyasamutpāda* and Reverse Causation"** | a claim inside the theory of [[pratityasamutpada|dependent origination]] — the standard Buddhist account of conditioned arising — not a claim about anything else |

⚠️ **§0 warning, stated because this term invites exactly the error this project exists to prevent.** "Backward causation" here is a **technical position in an 8th–9th-century Indian debate about *pratītyasamutpāda***, argued in the vocabulary of that debate and answering to its problems (among them: how a future goal can be said to condition a present act, and how the Buddha's omniscience relates to what has not yet happened). It is **not** retrocausality in modern physics, it does not become so because the English phrase is shared, and the corpus draws **no** physics edge here. What can be said honestly is narrower and more interesting: the Buddhist tradition, having made *causation* the whole content of its ontology, was forced to argue about whether causal order is strictly temporal — and one of its major thinkers concluded it is not.

### His idealism

He is placed on the **idealist** side of the school's internal division. The epistemological school's standard argument for idealism is the ***sahopalambha-niyama*** — "certainty of [subject and object] being perceived together" — which Śāntarakṣita, Kamalaśīla and Dharmottara read as *eka evopalambhaḥ*, "**a single apprehension of both**": if the object is never apprehended apart from the apprehension of it, positing it as external is idle. Prajñākaragupta's contribution is developed under the heading of "**multifaceted nonduality**" in the recent literature. The corpus records the placement and does not reconstruct the argument, which was not read.

### How the text survives — the provenance is unusually good

Worth recording precisely, because it is much better than the norm for this literature:

- **Full Sanskrit edition, 1953** — Rāhula Sāṅkṛtyāyana, *Pramāṇavārtikabhāshyam or Vārtikālaṅkāraḥ of Prajñākaragupta*, Tibetan Sanskrit Works Series 1, K. P. Jayaswal Research Institute, Patna.
- **Facsimile of the Sanskrit manuscripts, 1998** — Shigeaki Watanabe, Patna/Narita.
- **Tibetan translation** extant.
- **Two Sanskrit commentaries on the commentary**: Jayanta's *Pramāṇavārttikālaṅkāraṭīkā* and **Yamāri's** *Pramāṇavārttikālaṅkāraṭīkā Supariśuddhā*.

So: an original Sanskrit text with manuscripts, a Tibetan witness, and its own commentarial layer. Set that beside [[varsaganya|Vārṣagaṇya]] (no text at all, reconstructed from citations) or [[vindhyavasin|Vindhyavāsin]] (known through opponents) and the contrast is the point — **the corpus's confidence levels track evidence, and here the evidence is present.** That this node is still only `medium` is a statement about *what was read in this session*, not about what exists.

4. **Corpus cross-check** ([[dharmottara]], [[dharmottara-nyayabindu]], [[pramanavarttika]], [[dharmakirti]]): all three of those files, written in earlier batches from different source-sets, independently record his rejection of *niścaya-pratyaya* and his place among the *Pramāṇavārttika*'s major commentators alongside Devendrabuddhi and Dharmottara. The rejection is thus **quadruply attested inside this corpus alone**, which is why it is stated as fact while his positive alternative is left open.

**CONVERGED** on dates, work, scale, doctrines and transmission — three independent source-lines agree and none contradicts. Confidence **medium**, not high, and the reason is specific rather than evidentiary: **the primary text was not read, in any of the three forms in which it survives**, and neither Moriyama's nor Franco's monograph was consulted. The consequence is a real content hole — the node can say what he **rejected** in Dharmottara but not what he **proposed** — and confidence is capped for that, not for want of sources.
