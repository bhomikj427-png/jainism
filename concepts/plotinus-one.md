## Gloss

Plotinus (~205–270 CE) structures reality as three descending hypostases: **The One** (*to hen*) → **Intellect** (*Nous*, with the Platonic Forms as its intelligible content) → **Soul** (*Psychē*) → matter. The One is the first and highest principle: absolutely simple, utterly self-sufficient, and beyond all predication. "No predicate can be accurately attributed to it, insofar as it transcends everything" (Enneads V 3.31.2, V 4.2.39-40).

**The One is above thought**: Intellect (*Nous*) is the second hypostasis — it is where knowing and being first coincide, where the Forms live. The One is even simpler than thought; to think is already to introduce a subject-object duality. The One cannot think itself; it simply *is* the fullness beyond all distinction.

**Emanation** (*prohodos*): each perfect entity generates a secondary "external" activity directed outward (V 1.6.38-39); this overflow is not a willed act — it flows like light from the sun or heat from fire, without diminishing the source. The One → Nous → Soul → sensible world is this cascade.

**Return** (*epistrophē*): the soul, recognising its descent, turns back toward Nous and ultimately toward The One through philosophical ascent and mystical union beyond discursive thought.
