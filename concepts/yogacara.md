## Gloss / Divergence map

**Yogācāra** ("practice of yoga / the yogins' school"), also **Vijñānavāda** ("consciousness doctrine") and **Cittamātra** ("mind-only"), is — with Madhyamaka — one of the **two great philosophical schools of Indian Mahāyāna Buddhism**. It is the school-overview anchor behind the doctrine files already in the corpus: its core thesis is worked out in [[vijnaptimatrata]], and its signature mechanism in [[alaya-vijnana]].

**Lineage.** Traditionally founded by **Maitreya(-nātha)**, who transmitted teachings to the half-brothers **Asaṅga** and **Vasubandhu** (both c. 4th–5th century CE), the classical systematisers. The movement arose as **yogis from the Sarvāstivāda and Sautrāntika traditions adopted Mahāyāna** — so Yogācāra builds directly on Abhidharma consciousness-analysis (especially the Sautrāntika seed-theory).

**Three structural commitments** (distinctive of the *school*, beyond the bare consciousness-only thesis):

- **Eight consciousnesses (aṣṭa-vijñāna).** The traditional six (five senses + mano-vijñāna) are extended by two: the **kliṣṭa-manas** (7th — defiled self-consciousness that clings to a self) and the **ālaya-vijñāna** (8th — the "storehouse" holding karmic seeds, *bīja*). See [[alaya-vijnana]].
- **Three natures (trisvabhāva)** — one reality from three angles: **parikalpita** (the imagined / falsely constructed subject–object duality), **paratantra** (the dependent, causally-flowing stream of consciousness), and **pariniṣpanna** (the perfected — non-dual reality, the paratantra purified of the parikalpita).
- **Revolution of the basis (āśraya-parāvṛtti).** Liberation is the "overturning" of the defiled eight consciousnesses into non-conceptual direct knowledge (jñāna), dissolving the subject–object split.

**Texts.** *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra* (the doctrinal seed-text), the encyclopaedic *Yogācārabhūmi*, Asaṅga's *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* and *Abhidharmasamuccaya*, Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikā* (Thirty Verses) and *Viṃśatikā* (Twenty Verses). Transmitted to East Asia by **Xuanzang** (7th c., *Cheng Weishi Lun*) and to Tibet via **Śāntarakṣita** (8th c.), whose Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka synthesis harmonises the two schools.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Yogachara"): Maitreya-nātha + Asaṅga & Vasubandhu (4th–5th c.); names Vijñānavāda / Cittamātra / Vijñaptimātratā; origin in Sarvāstivāda/Sautrāntika yogis turning Mahāyāna; eight consciousnesses (kliṣṭa-manas 7th, ālaya 8th, bīja); trisvabhāva (parikalpita/paratantra/pariniṣpanna, "one reality, three angles"); āśraya-parāvṛtti; texts (Saṃdhinirmocana, Mahāyānasaṃgraha, Yogācārabhūmi, Vasubandhu's verses, Cheng Weishi Lun); built on Abhidharma/Sautrāntika seed-theory; Madhyamaka rivalry (Yijing: "for Yogācāra the real exists, the conventional does not; for Madhyamaka the reverse") + Śāntarakṣita synthesis + Xuanzang transmission. **Signal 2** (Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Yogachara"): 4th c. emergence; Asaṅga & Vasubandhu systematise, Maitreya the founding transmitter; vijñapti-mātra/citta-mātra (with the cataract-hairs simile); the eight consciousnesses incl. ālaya as seed-container; the same trisvabhāva triad; āśraya-parāvṛtti as enlightenment; the same text list; complementary-but-disputed relation to Madhyamaka, harmonised later by Śāntarakṣita; own Mahāyāna Abhidharma.

The two are independent (a reference article and a Buddhist-studies encyclopedia) and agree on lineage, dates, the eight-consciousness and three-nature frameworks, āśraya-parāvṛtti, the canonical texts, and the Madhyamaka relation. **Medium, not high**: the Sanskrit texts were not fetched verbatim; the idealism-vs-phenomenology reading of the core thesis is itself **contested** (see [[vijnaptimatrata]]); Maitreya's historicity is disputed.
