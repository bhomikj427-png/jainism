## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jayatīrtha** (c. **1345–1388**; pontiff **1365–1388**) is [[madhva|Madhva]]'s leading commentator and the man who made Dvaita a *system*. He is the second of the Mādhva ***munitraya***, the "three sages" — Madhva, Jayatīrtha, [[vyasatirtha|Vyāsatīrtha]] — and he "**structured the philosophical aspects of Dvaita**," which the founder's own elliptical works had not.

`vyasatirtha.md` and `madhva.md` both named him repeatedly with no node. He is the missing middle term: **Madhva writes, Jayatīrtha makes it arguable, Vyāsatīrtha attacks with it.**

His masterwork is the ***Nyāyasudhā*** ("Nectar of Logic"), a commentary on Madhva's *Anuvyākhyāna* running to some **24,000 verses** and taking on Buddhism, Jainism and the rival Vedāntas. In all, **21 works**: 18 commentaries on Madhva and 4 independent treatises.

### The divergence I — ⭐ **a chronological impossibility in the standard reference layer**

Nearly everything known about his life comes from **two hagiographies**, the *Aṇu Jayatīrtha Vijaya* and the *Bṛhad Jayatīrtha Vijaya*, which the sources attribute to **"his disciple Vyāsatīrtha"** — one bookseller record for a printed edition calls the author "**Śrī Jayatīrtha's direct disciple**," and an 1889 Belgaum printing survives under the title *Jaya Tirtha Vijaya by Vyāsa Tīrtha*.

**This cannot be right as it stands.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Jayatīrtha dies | **1388** |
| Vyāsatīrtha — the *munitraya* Vyāsatīrtha, `vyasatirtha.md` — lives | **1447/1460 – 1539** |

**A direct disciple cannot be born some sixty to seventy years after his teacher's death.** Wikipedia carries both figures, in two of its own articles, without noticing — the same failure mode `gangesha.md` documents for Gaṅgeśa's date.

**Two ways out, and this file adopts neither:**
1. **There are two Vyāsatīrthas** — the hagiographer being an earlier, otherwise obscure namesake, and the famous *rājaguru* of Vijayanagara a different man. Mādhva pontifical titles are *repeated* down a lineage, so this is entirely ordinary rather than exotic.
2. **The reference layer has conflated them**, attaching a hagiography to the famous bearer of the name because he is the one anyone has heard of.

⚠ **What the corpus must do either way**: `vyasatirtha.md` is the **1447/1460–1539** man, and **no hagiography of Jayatīrtha may be attributed to that node** on the strength of these sources. This is the **third** name-collision the corpus has logged — after **Vācaspati Miśra I / II** (`vacaspati-mishra.md`, confirmed twice) and the two **Harirāma Tarkavāgīśa**s implied by `gadadhara.md`. **A pattern is now visible: in a tradition of inherited monastic titles and repeated Brahmin family names, "same name" is weak evidence of "same person," and the encyclopedia layer does not check.**

⚠ Note also that the third witness Wikipedia names — a compilation by **Chalāri Saṁkarṣaṇācārya, c. 1700** — is **three centuries after the fact**, and a bookseller record independently titles a *Jayatīrtha Vijaya* to "Śrī Chalāri Śaṅkarṣaṇācārya, with his own commentary." **The life is known from hagiography, at a distance, in disputed attribution.**

### The divergence II — did he know Gaṅgeśa?

This matters because it dates the arrival of [[navya-nyaya|Navya-Nyāya]] in Vedānta, and because of **how** the question was settled.

| claim | who | status |
|---|---|---|
| Jayatīrtha **was aware of Gaṅgeśa** | **B. N. K. Sharma** (1961, p. 252), citing a verse | ⚠ **not verified** |
| Jayatīrtha had "apparently **no awareness of Gaṅgeśa's work specifically**," despite detailed knowledge of contemporary Nyāya | **Michael Williams** (2020) | the specialist's reading |
| **Vyāsatīrtha** was "the **first** member of the Mādhva tradition to engage in detail with the ideas of Gaṅgeśa and his followers in Mithilā" | Williams | — |

⚠⚠ **The reason this is recorded at length is Williams's sentence, not his conclusion.** On Sharma's claim he writes: *"I have not been able to locate this verse in any modern edition of the text."*

**A non-partisan specialist went to check a partisan scholar's citation and could not find it.** Batch 45 closed `vyasatirtha.md` with the complaint that "everything in `vyasatirtha.md` currently leans on a committed Dvaita partisan" and named **Michael Williams** as one of the two modern specialists who could correct it. **He has now been read, and the first thing he does is fail to verify a Sharma citation.** That is not a refutation of Sharma — an unlocated verse is not a fabricated one, and Williams says only what he checked — but it is exactly the independent check §4 asks for, and it landed.

### What he actually did

**He made nonexistence respectable, in Navya-Nyāya's own vocabulary.** Williams shows the Mādhvas held, against Nyāya, that **we can think, speak and reason about nonexistent things**. Jayatīrtha is the hinge:

- **On illusion.** He adapts Nyāya's *anyathākhyātivāda* (error as misattribution) with a decisive twist: "Unlike the Naiyāyikas, however, **Jayatīrtha accepts that the object of our false perception (the 'snake,' the 'silver' etc.) is *simply nonexistent*.**" Real individuals serve as **prototypes** for what is falsely seen.
- **On what nonexistence is.** He defines it as "**being the counterpositive of a constant absence**," and glosses the Advaitin's *mithyā* accordingly — from the *Tattvoddyotaṭīkā*: "'It does not exist.' And so the [sublating judgment] 'The silver is in fact *mithyā*' means 'There is the **constant absence** of silver'."

⚠⚠ **Read that definition against [[abhava|`abhava.md`]], written earlier in this same batch.** *Pratiyogin* (counterpositive) and *atyantābhāva* (constant absence) are **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika's technical apparatus for negative entities** — the seventh *padārtha* and its specifying term. **A 14th-century Dvaita Vedāntin defines nonexistence in the Naiyāyika's own words, to defeat an Advaitin.** This is the export Ingalls described from the other end: "one finds Mīmāṃsakas and **Vedāntins** coming to use Navya-nyāya logic even in their diatribes against the Nyāya school." The corpus reached both halves of that observation independently, from a Harvard monograph's preface and from a 2020 journal article, and they meet here.

His pupils' successor Vyāsatīrtha then extends the doctrine into **"substrate-free" (*anāśrita*) qualities** — properties not requiring an existent locus — and turns it against Gaṅgeśa's exclusion of empty terms from inference (*āśrayāsiddhi*), by a **pragmatic contradiction**: the Naiyāyika's own argument against reasoning about nonexistent things must itself reason about nonexistent things.

⚠ **Williams's methodological verdict, which the corpus should adopt and which cuts against a common shortcut**: he "distances himself from scholars like **Karl Potter** who emphasize Navya-Nyāya's contribution primarily as 'rigorous terminology'," and argues that "**The available Sanskrit literature does not suggest that Mādhva philosophers regarded their encounter with Navya-Nyāya primarily as a matter of embracing an updated technical language.**" **What crossed between the schools were arguments, not vocabulary.**

### Life

Born **Dhoṇḍopant Raghunāth Deshpande**, 1345, at **Maṅgalveḍhā** near Paṇḍharpur (Solapur district, Maharashtra), into an aristocratic Deshastha Brahmin family of military rank; father Raghunāth Pant Deshpande, mother Sakubāī. At twenty he met the ascetic **Akṣobhya Tīrtha** on the bank of the Bhīmā, renounced, and took the name Jayatīrtha. He succeeded Akṣobhya as pontiff **1365–1388**, and his *bṛndāvana* is at **Malkheḍa** on the Kagiṇā (Kalaburagi district).

⚠ **All of this is hagiography.** Wikipedia itself says "historical sources on Jayatirtha's life are scant." The corpus records it as what the tradition says about him, at the same arm's length as the Kālī legend in `gangesha.md` and the *digvijaya* material in `shankara.md`. **The one thing here that is not narrative — the 1365–1388 pontificate — is institutional rather than miraculous, and is the most likely to be sound.**

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Michael Thomas Williams, "The Impact of Navya-Nyāya on Mādhva Vedānta: Vyāsatīrtha and the Problem of Empty Terms," *Journal of Indian Philosophy* 49.2 (2020), 205–232, doi 10.1007/s10781-020-09453-y** — **peer-reviewed, open access, read directly**; author at the Institute for the Cultural and Intellectual History of Asia, Austrian Academy of Sciences. The load-bearing source for doctrine: Jayatīrtha's late-14th-c. floruit, the *Nyāyasudhā* and *Tattvoddyotaṭīkā*, the *anyathākhyāti* modification, the definition of nonexistence as counterpositive-of-a-constant-absence with the *Tattvoddyotaṭīkā* quotation, the "no awareness of Gaṅgeśa specifically" verdict, the failure to locate Sharma's verse, Vyāsatīrtha as the first Mādhva to engage Gaṅgeśa in detail, *anāśrita* qualities, the *āśrayāsiddhi* and pragmatic-contradiction arguments, and the anti-Potter methodological stance.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia, "Jayatirtha."** The biography: 1345–1388, pontificate 1365–1388, birth name and family, Akṣobhya Tīrtha, Maṅgalveḍhā, Malkheḍa, the 21 works (18 + 4), the *Nyāyasudhā*'s 24,000 verses, the *munitraya*, "structured the philosophical aspects of Dvaita" — **and its own admission that "historical sources on Jayatirtha's life are scant."**

**Signal 3 — bookseller and archive records**, read as bibliographic evidence: an 1889 Belgaum printing, *Jaya Tirtha Vijaya by Vyāsa Tīrtha* (archive.org), and a *Jayatīrtha Vijaya of Śrī Chalāri Śaṅkarṣaṇācārya, with his own commentary*, "five cantos (200 verses)," described as by "Śrī Jayatīrtha's **direct disciple**." **These are what make the chronological impossibility concrete rather than a suspicion about a Wikipedia phrase.**

**Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `vyasatirtha.md` (1447 vs 1460–1539, the *munitraya* line, "sixth in succession from Jayatīrtha," and Batch 45's explicit complaint that the file leans on a Dvaita partisan — **this node closes part of that follow-up**), `madhva.md` (the Sharma datings, and Sharma holding two published positions on Madhva's dates), `abhava.md` (the *pratiyogin*/*atyantābhāva* apparatus Jayatīrtha borrows), `dvaita-vedanta.md`, `gangesha.md`, `navya-nyaya.md`, `avacchedaka.md` (Ingalls on the export to Vedāntins).

**Independence.** Signals 1 and 2 are strongly independent — a 2020 Vienna specialist article and an encyclopedia biography — and **do not overlap**: Williams says almost nothing about the life, Wikipedia almost nothing about the doctrine. Neither confirms the other; each is load-bearing in its own area, which is a weakness to state rather than a strength. Signal 3 is bibliographic and is the only thing that turns the Vyāsatīrtha attribution from a phrase into a problem. Signal 4 supplies the check that matters: the *abhāva* vocabulary was written into this corpus **hours earlier**, from Matilal, with no knowledge of Jayatīrtha — and Jayatīrtha's definition of nonexistence lands exactly on it.

**Why contested.** The authorship of the principal biographical sources is **chronologically impossible as reported**; the life is hagiographic and thin; and whether he knew Gaṅgeśa is disputed between a partisan scholar and a specialist who could not verify the partisan's citation. **Why medium.** One peer-reviewed specialist article read directly, plus an encyclopedia, plus a corpus-internal convergence that neither could have engineered. **Not high**: **no work of Jayatīrtha's was read** — not the *Nyāyasudhā*, not the *Tattvoddyotaṭīkā*, whose one quotation here comes through Williams's English; Sharma 1961 was not consulted, so the disputed verse is judged only on Williams's report; the two *Jayatīrtha Vijaya*s were not examined; and the Devanāgarī is **omitted** rather than guessed, per §3.
