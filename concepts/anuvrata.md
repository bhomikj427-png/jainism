## Gloss / Divergence map

Aṇuvrata (*aṇu* "small/atomic" + *vrata* "vow") are the **five lesser vows of the Jain layperson (*śrāvaka*)** — the householder's partial, moderated form of the same five vows the ascetic takes in full as the **mahāvratas** (great vows). The vows are one set; the *degree of observance* is what differs. The monk renounces totally (*sarva-virati*); the layperson renounces partially (*deśa-virati*), abstaining from the *gross* forms of each transgression while continuing ordinary household life.

**TS 7.1 (the fivefold vow), Sanskrit fetched (Vijay K. Jain 2018):**
> *hiṃsā'nṛtasteyābrahmaparigrahebhyo virativratam*
> "Desisting from injury, falsehood, stealing, unchastity, and attachment-to-possessions is the (fivefold) vow."

**TS 7.2 (the partial/total division), English fetched (Vijay K. Jain 2018; Jain Square relay):**
> "The vow is of two kinds, small and great (*aṇu / mahā*), from its being partial (*deśa*) and total (*sarva*)."

So the *same* five abstinences (ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha) become **aṇuvrata** when taken partially and **mahāvrata** when taken completely. Per TS 7.3, each vow carries five observances (*bhāvanā*) for stabilising it.

**The aṇuvrata vs mahāvrata contrast (per vow):**

| vow | mahāvrata (ascetic — total) | aṇuvrata (layperson — partial) |
|---|---|---|
| ahiṃsā | no harm to any being, including one-sensed | abstain from *gross* intentional violence (esp. to mobile, *trasa*, beings) |
| satya | absolute truthfulness | abstain from *gross* falsehood / ruinous lies |
| asteya | take nothing not given, at all | abstain from *gross* theft |
| brahmacarya | complete celibacy | *svadāra-santoṣa* — contentment with one's own spouse |
| aparigraha | total non-possession (incl. clothing, Digambara) | *parimāṇa* — a self-set limit on possessions |

**The twelve vows of the layperson (the full śrāvaka programme):** the five aṇuvratas are the core, supplemented by seven further vows that regulate the householder's scope of action:

| group | Sanskrit | members |
|---|---|---|
| 5 lesser vows | *aṇuvrata* | the five above |
| 3 multiplicative/merit vows | *guṇavrata* | *digvrata* (limit movement by direction) · *bhogopabhoga-parimāṇa* (limit consumable/non-consumable goods) · *anartha-daṇḍa-viramaṇa* (avoid purposeless harm and harmful livelihoods) |
| 4 disciplinary vows | *śikṣāvrata* | *sāmāyika* (periodic equanimity-meditation) · *deśāvakāśika* (limit movement to a set area) · *proṣadhopavāsa* (periodic fasting) · *atithi-saṃvibhāga* (sharing food with ascetics/the needy) |

The guṇavratas and śikṣāvratas are not *new* abstinences; they progressively *tighten the radius* within which the aṇuvratas operate — a graduated ladder by which a householder approaches the ascetic's total renunciation without yet taking it.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain 2018 (Motilal Banarsidass):** TS 7.1 Sanskrit fetched directly; TS 7.2 English (partial/total → small/great) fetched via WisdomLib search relay and confirmed independently by the Jain Square chapter-7 translation.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Anuvrata" + Britannica "anuvrata":** independent of the TS edition. Both give the five aṇuvratas as moderated mahāvratas (*aṇu* "tiny" vs *mahā* "big"), the gross/subtle distinction, and the 12-vow scheme (5 aṇuvrata + 3 guṇavrata + 4 śikṣāvrata). Britannica and Wikipedia agree on membership; minor naming variation in the śikṣāvrata list (deśāvakāśika/deśavrata) noted.

**Independence:** Signal 1 is critical-edition (TS verse); Signal 2 is two reference works converging on the 12-vow structure. Genuine convergence on the core claim (layperson = partial, monk = total, same five vows). Confidence medium: TS 7.2 obtained in English only (Santhal *deśasarvato'ṇumahatī* not independently verified this run); the guṇavrata/śikṣāvrata membership is from reference works, not the TS critical edition.
