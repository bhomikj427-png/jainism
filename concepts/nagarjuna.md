## Gloss

**Nāgārjuna** (~150–250 CE) is the founder of the Madhyamaka school of Mahāyāna Buddhism and the author of the *Mūlamadhyamakakārikā* (MMK), the foundational text of Indian Madhyamaka philosophy. Likely born into a Brahmin family in South India (Andhra region), his correspondence with King Gautamīputra Sātakarṇi (r. ~166–196 CE) provides the most reliable biographical anchor; later Tibetan and Chinese biographies incorporate extensive legendary material.

**What Nāgārjuna argued** (converged across scholarship): All phenomena are **empty of intrinsic nature** (*svabhāva*) because they arise dependently. This is not nihilism — things exist conventionally — but it means no phenomenon has self-sustaining, independent existence. The MMK systematically applies this argument to: causation, motion, time, the self, suffering, and nirvāṇa (all 27 chapters pick a fundamental concept and deconstruct it). His most radical claim: "There is not the slightest distinction between *saṃsāra* and *nirvāṇa*" (MMK 25.19) — the theoretical separation reified by Buddhist scholastics dissolves once both are understood as empty of fixed essence.

**Prasaṅga method** (*reductio ad absurdum*): Nāgārjuna explicitly disavows holding a positive thesis (*pratijñā*). Instead, he borrows an opponent's own logical framework and shows it generates internal contradictions — infinite regress, circularity, self-refutation. Against both Brahminical metaphysics and Buddhist Abhidharma substantialism, the method is destructive without establishing a counter-doctrine. This is the *prasaṅga* (consequentialist reductio) technique, distinguished from standard Indian *anumāna* (inference).

**Works well-attested vs. disputed** (per IEP "Nagarjuna"):
- *Well-attested Buddhist-focused*: MMK, *Śūnyatāsaptatī*, *Yuktiṣaṣṭikā*, *Vigrahavyāvartanī*, *Vaidalyaprakaraṇa*
- *Disputed* (no Sanskrit original; attributed via Tibetan/Chinese; heavy redaction): *Ratnāvalī*, *Suhṛllekha*
