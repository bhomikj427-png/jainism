## Gloss

The **founding text of the Vaiśeṣika darśana**, attributed to **Kaṇāda** (also Kaṇabhakṣa / Ulūka / Kāśyapa), one of the six orthodox (āstika) schools. Dated broadly to the 6th–2nd c. BCE, "before the advent of Buddhism" in the tradition's own reckoning (modern critical dating is later and disputed). Structure: **10 chapters (adhyāyas)**, each in two sections (āhnikas), ~370 sūtras. This file is the **primary-text anchor** for the school whose developed doctrines are treated in [[dravya-vaisheshika]] and [[paramanu-vaisheshika]].

**The category-scheme (padārtha).** Vaiśeṣika analyses all of reality into **six categories** — *dravya* (substance), *guṇa* (quality), *karma* (motion/action), *sāmānya* (generality/universal), *viśeṣa* (ultimate particular), *samavāya* (inherence) — with *abhāva* (absence/non-existence) added as a seventh by later authors. The school is *named* after the fifth: *viśeṣa*, the ultimate differentiator that makes two otherwise-identical atoms distinct.

⚠ **SIX, and the corpus can now say why that matters (added Batch 46).** Later Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika works list **seven** categories, adding *abhāva* (absence). That seventh is **not** in this text. Matilal, *The Navya-Nyāya Doctrine of Negation* (Harvard 1968), p. 100: "the early Vaiśeṣikas ... did not speak of absence as a separate category. One might very well explain the ***Vaiśeṣika-sūtras* 9.1.1–10** without assuming that Kaṇāda was speaking here of absence as a separate category." **Śrīdhara** (c. 991) and **Udayana** (c. 1050) later argued that although absence "had not been mentioned by Praśastapāda (or by Kaṇāda) as a separate *padārtha*, it was nevertheless so approved by them **implicitly**." So the seventh category is a **retrofit onto this text**, argued for by name and datable — see [[abhava|`abhava.md`]]. This file's six, read from *VS* 1.1.4 verbatim, is correct for the root text and should not be "corrected" upward.

⚠ **A named gap this opens**: *VS* **9.1.1–10** is the passage the whole dispute turns on, and it was **not read here** — this file read 1.1.1–1.1.4 only. The Nandalal Sinha 1923 translation used for 1.1.4 covers it and is the obvious next fetch.

**The opening — knowledge of the categories yields the highest good.** *VS* 1.1.1: *athāto dharmaṃ vyākhyāsyāmaḥ* ("now, therefore, an exposition of dharma"). *VS* 1.1.2 defines dharma functionally: that from which arise *abhyudaya* (prosperity/worldly well-being) and *niḥśreyasa* (the highest good / liberation). **The category-list sūtra, *VS* 1.1.4, fetched verbatim:**

> *dharmaviśeṣaprasūtāt dravyaguṇakarmasāmānyaviśeṣasamavāyānāṃ padārthānāṃ sādharmyavaidharmyābhyāṃ tattvajñānānniḥśreyasam*
> "The Supreme Good results from the knowledge, produced by a particular dharma, of the essence of the predicables — Substance, Attribute, Action, Genus, Species, and Inherence — by means of their resemblances and differences." (Nandalal Sinha tr.)

This makes Vaiśeṣika unusual: it is a soteriology whose *means* is correct **ontological taxonomy** — liberation through getting the categories of being right, by sorting things by their likenesses (*sādharmya*) and differences (*vaidharmya*).

**Atomism.** Substances include the four atomic elements (earth, water, fire/light, air), whose ultimate units are partless *paramāṇu*; observable bodies are aggregates built up through dyads (*dvyaṇuka*) and triads (*tryaṇuka*). (Aggregation mechanism and the role of *adṛṣṭa* / God are in [[paramanu-vaisheshika]].)
