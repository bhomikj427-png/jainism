## Gloss / Divergence map

**Advaita Vedānta** ("non-dual Vedānta") is the strict **monist** reading of the Vedānta school, **systematised by Ādi Śaṅkara** (c. 8th century CE — flourished ~700–750, debated 700–820) on the basis of his commentaries (bhāṣyas) on the [[brahma-sutra]], the principal Upaniṣads, and the Bhagavad Gītā. Śaṅkara is the *systematiser*, not the founder: he stands in a lineage running back through **Gauḍapāda** (7th c.) and Bādarāyaṇa. It is the school-anchor behind the Advaita doctrine files already in the corpus — [[maya-advaita]], [[moksha-advaita]], [[atman-vedanta]].

**The one thesis.** Brahman alone is ultimately real — "one only, without a second" (*ekam eva advitīyam*). The empirical world of plurality is **not fully real**: it is appearance, neither identical with Brahman nor wholly separate, indeterminable (*anirvacanīya*). The individual self (**ātman**) is **numerically identical with Brahman**, understood as pure, non-intentional consciousness; the apparent difference is the work of **avidyā** (ignorance) and **māyā** — beginningless superimposition (*adhyāsa*).

**Two (or three) levels of truth.** *Pāramārthika* (ultimate — only Brahman), *vyāvahārika* (conventional/empirical — the workaday world), and *prātibhāsika* (merely apparent — the rope-snake, the dream). From the ultimate standpoint the lower levels collapse into non-duality. Brahman has two descriptions: **nirguṇa** (without attributes — the higher) and **saguṇa** (with attributes — Īśvara, the lower, world-related aspect).

**Liberation.** *Mokṣa* is a **purely epistemic shift** — the removal of ignorance through direct knowledge (*jñāna*, *vidyā*), not action or new experience. It is conveyed by the **mahāvākyas** ("great sentences"), above all *tat tvam asi* ("that thou art"). Liberation can be attained while living (**jīvanmukti**).

**The Buddhist charge.** Because the world's unreality (*māyāvāda*) and Śaṅkara's apophatic Brahman resemble Madhyamaka emptiness, opponents (notably later Vaiṣṇava Vedāntins) accused Advaita of being **pracchanna-bauddha** ("crypto-Buddhism"). The decisive difference: Advaita's Brahman is a *positive, permanent reality* (sat-cit-ānanda), whereas Buddhist [[sunyata]] denies any such self-existent substrate — see the NOT-equivalent edge.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (SEP, "Śaṅkara"): 8th c. (700–820 debated); systematiser in a Gauḍapāda–Bādarāyaṇa lineage; "one only, without a second"; avidyā as root of suffering; māyā as indeterminable status of the world; ātman = Brahman = pure non-intentional consciousness; vyāvahārika vs pāramārthika; nirguṇa (higher) vs saguṇa/Īśvara (lower); *tat tvam asi*; mokṣa as a purely epistemic shift; prasthānatrayī as the triple canon. **Signal 2** (Wikipedia, "Advaita Vedanta"): Śaṅkara 8th c. systematiser, preceded by Gauḍapāda/Maṇḍana Miśra; jīvātman = Brahman; māyā/avidyā; three truths (pāramārthika/vyāvahārika/prātibhāsika); nirguṇa vs saguṇa; mokṣa via vidyā and the mahāvākyas; jīvanmukti; prasthānatrayī; the *māyāvāda* / crypto-Buddhism charge.

The two are independent (a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia and a reference article) and agree on dating, the systematiser-not-founder point, the core monism, the māyā/avidyā mechanism, the truth-levels, nirguṇa/saguṇa, the epistemic account of mokṣa, and the canon. **Medium, not high**: Śaṅkara's bhāṣya Sanskrit was not fetched verbatim; his dates and the relation to Maṇḍana Miśra remain debated; the content is cross-checked against the already-written maya-advaita / moksha-advaita / atman-vedanta rather than read from the commentaries directly.
