## Gloss / Divergence map

**Gaṅgeśa Upādhyāya** wrote the *Tattvacintāmaṇi*, the book that made [[navya-nyaya|Navya-Nyāya]] a school. On the majority reckoning he founds it; on the two minority reckonings he is its greatest exponent rather than its origin. Either way, SEP's verdict on what the book did is unusually blunt: it "not only cast the works of the old school of logic into oblivion but the neo-logical works of his predecessors also faded into insignificance."

He is **contested on his date**, and the contest has an instructive shape — one this file thinks is worth naming, because it is not really a disagreement between scholars.

### The divergence: a date that has drifted, in one direction, for a century

| date | who says it | what kind of claim it is |
|---|---|---|
| **"13th cent."** | University of Toronto catalogue record for the **1888–1901 Calcutta edition** | 19th-c. library cataloguing |
| **"late 12th century"** | a **static mirror of the Wikipedia article** (hlt.bme.hu), still live and still indexed | a superseded encyclopedia revision, fossilised |
| **c. 1200** | Hindupedia; Wisdomlib's *Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika categories* study | bare assertion, no argument |
| **first half of the 14th c.; *fl.* c. 1325** | **SEP**; live Wikipedia; Deb; the arXiv paper; Vidyābhūṣaṇa (via Wikipedia) | **argued**: see below |
| **"1300 and 1350 for his period of flourishing"** | **D. C. Bhattacharya**, quoted by SEP as resting on "evidence that has not been corrected to date" | the sharpest form of the argued position |

⚠ **These five are not five opinions. They are one date being revised later, and the earlier states of the revision not going away.** The oldest witness (a 19th-c. cataloguer) is earliest; the current specialist consensus is latest; the intermediate values survive because encyclopedia mirrors and aggregators do not update when their source does. Live Wikipedia says so in as many words — "recent opinion now places him in the fourteenth century" — while a mirror of its own older text still says the twelfth. **A reader who samples "several independent sources" here will sample the same source at four different ages and mistake it for corroboration.** That is the finding, and it is a methodological one: §4's independence test fails silently on encyclopedia strata.

⚠ **The 14th-c. position is the only one with an argument attached**, and it is triple:
1. **Named authority.** D. C. Bhattacharya's 1300–1350, which SEP explicitly describes as uncorrected since.
2. **Synchronism forward.** Maṇikaṇṭha Miśra (c. 1300) "slightly preceded Gaṅgeśa (ca. AD 1325)" (Deb).
3. **Synchronism backward.** Matilal has Śaśadhara — whose *Nyāyasiddhāntadīpa* is "not later than AD 1200" — precede Gaṅgeśa "by almost 150 years."
**This file adopts c. 1300–1350 as the better-justified reading and records the rest as strata, not as rivals.** It is not called *settled*, because no primary dating evidence was read here.

⚠ **A corpus-internal convergence worth flagging.** SEP's authority for Gaṅgeśa's date is **D. C. Bhattacharya** — the same scholar `udayana.md` names as its own priority upgrade path (*History of Navya-Nyāya in Mithilā*), on the strength of his four-argument case for **redating Udayana a century later**. The man who unsettled Udayana's date is the man whose Gaṅgeśa date SEP says has never needed correcting. The corpus reached him twice, from two nodes, by two routes, in two batches.

### Life: a very short list of facts and one long legend

**What is known** (SEP): he taught in **Mithilā**, in north-eastern India; he taught **both Nyāya and Mīmāṃsā**; he was styled ***jagad-guru***, which SEP glosses as "roughly equivalent to 'Distinguished University Professor'"; he had a wife, three sons and a daughter, one of them the Nyāya author **Vardhamāna**. "About the person little more is known."

**Where**: Wikipedia locates him at **Karion**, a village on the river Kamalā — given as "19 km south-east of Darbhanga" in one article and "twelve miles southeast of Darbhanga" in another. ⚠ These look like two witnesses and are one: 12 miles ≈ 19.3 km. **The same measurement in two unit systems is not corroboration.** What *is* substantive is the further claim that Karion is [[udayana|Udayana]]'s own village — the two greatest Nyāya authors, three centuries apart, from one place.

**The legend**: he was illiterate as a youth; he propitiated the goddess **Kālī** on the cremation ground beside his uncle's house and received mastery of logic as a boon. ⚠ **Treated here as hagiography, not biography** — the same genre as the *digvijaya* stories the corpus discounts in `shankara.md` and `appayya-dikshita.md`. Its interest is what it presupposes: that the *Tattvacintāmaṇi*'s difficulty seemed to require an explanation, and that the explanation reached for was divine gift rather than long training.

⚠ **One epithet is dropped.** Wikipedia calls him "an Indian philosopher, logician and **mathematician**" and lists him among Indian mathematicians. No source consulted — SEP included, at length — attributes a single mathematical work or result to him. **"Mathematician" is not asserted by this file.**

### The book

The ***Tattvacintāmaṇi*** ("Jewel of Reflection on the Truth"), also ***Pramāṇacintāmaṇi***, in four *khaṇḍa*s, one per Nyāya knowledge-source: **pratyakṣa** (perception), **anumāna** (inference), **upamāna** (analogy), **śabda** (testimony). ⚠ Ganeri notes the plan itself is borrowed — "a pattern employed by the Buddhist logician Dignāga, and copied by Gaṅgeśa."

Hindupedia adds a negative fact that matters: it treats **only** the four *pramāṇas* and **deliberately excludes *prameya***, the objects of knowledge. A book that declines the whole second half of the *Nyāyasūtra*'s subject-matter is making a claim about what philosophy now is.

**On his own account, the newness is method, not doctrine.** The passage that says so is quoted in the secondary literature and is worth having exactly:

> "Gaṅgeśa refers to his own teachings as the New Nyāya. This term New Nyāya is not to be understood as implying any great originality in theory on Gaṅgeśa's part, but rather originality in method... He is far more precise, more careful to define his terms, than were his predecessors, and these virtues of his work are responsible for the fact that perhaps half of Navya-Nyāya literature is based either directly on the T.C. or on a commentary on the T.C."

⚠ **Attribution flagged.** This reaches the file through search-level aggregation of the Wikipedia article; its style and the "T.C." abbreviation point to **Ingalls, *Materials for the Study of Navya-Nyāya Logic***, which was **not read**. Recorded as a reported quotation with an unverified source. If the attribution holds, its first clause is significant for `navya-nyaya.md`'s three-way periodisation dispute: **it would make Gaṅgeśa an internal witness that the school's name is his own.**

### What is in it

- **Knowledge as reliably produced.** SEP: Gaṅgeśa "advances what may be called a reliabilist theory of knowledge and justification, indeed a super-reliabilism, an infallibilism for externalists." The knowledge-sources are **factive** — "genuine sources are inerrant" — and error is assigned not to a source malfunctioning but to a ***pramāṇābhāsa***, an *apparent* source, a knowledge-imitator.
- **Presumptive but defeasible.** Awarenesses are presumed veridical and can be defeated; certification is a **separate** cognitive act, achieved through success in action and by identifying the source's *guṇa* (merits) and *doṣa* (flaws). This is an **extrinsicality** position, held explicitly *against* [[mimamsa-pramana|Mīmāṃsā]]'s self-certification. The one exception SEP records: certain matters known by ***anuvyavasāya*** (apperception, "after-cognition") are "known without the possibility of being wrong."
- **Pervasion.** The centre of gravity of the whole later literature. ⚠ **The two specialist sources disagree on the count**: **SEP** says he rejects **twenty-four** attempted definitions before endorsing his own; **Ganeri** says **twenty-one** rejected — of which the first five are the famous *vyāpti-pañcaka* and two more the "Lion and Tiger" definitions of his Navya-Nyāya predecessors — followed by **seven** he accepts. **Not reconciled here.** What both agree on is the shape: a long serial demolition of candidate definitions, ending in one that survives.
- **God.** An inference from both positive and negative correlation: *"Earth and the like have a conscious agent as an instrumental cause, since they are effects, like a pot and unlike an atom."* Its distinctive move is a **restriction on the domain**: nothing inside the subject-class may serve as supporting example or counterexample, "since that would beg the question." The standard objection is an ***upādhi*** — that the real condition is *having-a-perceptible-body*, which a creator lacks; Gaṅgeśa answers by denying that the *upādhi* pervades the probandum, and defends "a bodiless Creator whose knowledge is appropriate to the material forming earth," on the model of the potter's knowledge of clay.
- Also: definitions of knowledge, conditions on testimonial meaning (*ākāṅkṣā* expectation, *yogyatā* semantic fit, *lakṣaṇā* indirect indication), a treatment of the fallacies, an argument for the self as substance, and a defence of *mukti*.

### Reception

"The work of Gaṅgeśa became highly popular very soon and was studied and commented upon in various centers of culture of India" — and then displaced everything behind it, old school and new alike. SEP records the long tail precisely: "the latest phase of Navyanyāya studies in India for about two centuries flowed through a large number of channels cut by **single sentences or phrases** of this part of Gaṅgeśa's work." Ganeri says the same from the other end: post-Gaṅgeśa scholars "focused more and more exclusively" on the inference chapter, and study of those sub-commentaries "forms an essential part of the curriculum" in traditional Indian education **today**.

### ⚠ A divergence about why the book was written

| reading | who holds it | note |
|---|---|---|
| The *Tattvacintāmaṇi* was written **in response to [[sriharsa|Śrīharṣa]]'s *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya*** — Gaṅgeśa judged the attack unsuccessful but accepted the need to "improve and refine the logical and linguistic tools of Nyāya" | Wikipedia, "Navya-Nyāya" | the story the corpus already half-carried: `sriharsa.md` records SEP's Śrīharṣa entry placing the Naiyāyika answers **after** Gaṅgeśa |
| **Śrīharṣa is not mentioned at all** | **SEP, "Gaṅgeśa"** — a full specialist entry, revised 2024 | the strongest single source on Gaṅgeśa **does not tell this story** |

**Neither is adopted.** The absence of Śrīharṣa from SEP's Gaṅgeśa entry is not a denial, and an encyclopedia's founding-narrative is not evidence. But the asymmetry is worth recording: **a widely repeated account of why the most important book in late Indian philosophy exists is missing from the best available treatment of it.**

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Gaṅgeśa"** (first published 2020, substantive revision June 2024). A full specialist entry with a bibliography; the load-bearing source. Supplies the 14th-c. date with D. C. Bhattacharya's 1300–1350; the *jagad-guru* epithet, the Mīmāṃsā teaching, the family; the reliabilist/infallibilist reading; the extrinsicality position and *anuvyavasāya* exception; the twenty-four rejected definitions; the *īśvarānumāna* with its domain restriction and the *upādhi* objection; the technical lexicon; the reception; and the editions/translations.

**Signal 2 — Jonardon Ganeri, *Navya-Nyāya: Analytical Philosophy in Early Modern India*.** Independent of SEP in authorship and method (he expounds via Annaṃbhaṭṭa's *Tarkasaṅgraha*). Supplies the Dignāga book-plan, the 21-rejected/7-accepted count, the *vyāpti-pañcaka* and "Lion and Tiger" labels, and the curriculum claim.

**Signal 3 — Debabrata Deb, *The Navya-Nyāya Theory of Pakṣatā*.** Independent again; supplies the Maṇikaṇṭha and Matilal/Śaśadhara synchronisms.

**Signal 4 — the encyclopedia strata**: live Wikipedia "Gaṅgeśa"/"Tattvachintamani"/"Navya-Nyāya"; a static hlt.bme.hu mirror of an older Wikipedia revision; Hindupedia; Wisdomlib's categories study; the Toronto catalogue record. Used **as evidence about themselves** — the date-drift table above — as much as for content.

**Signal 5 — bibliographic verification.** The 1888–1901 Calcutta edition was located on archive.org (`tattvachipt403ganguoft`) and its metadata read directly; Vattanky's 1984 monograph title and imprint were verified from a bookseller record.

**Independence.** Signals 1, 2 and 3 are genuinely independent — three authors, three methods, three purposes — and they **agree on the 14th c. and disagree on the definition-count**, which is exactly the pattern a real independence check should produce. Signal 4 is emphatically **not** a set of independent witnesses, and this file's principal argument is that treating it as one is the error. Signal 5 is provenance only.

⚠ **Weak independence flagged explicitly.** Hindupedia, the hlt.bme.hu mirror, and the Toronto catalogue do **not** corroborate each other on the early date; they are three ages of one transmission. Counting them as three signals would have produced a spurious 3-vs-3 tie.

**Why contested.** The date is disputed across ~150 years; the two best sources give different counts for the rejected *vyāpti* definitions; and the standard account of the book's motivation is absent from the best source. **Why medium.** A specialist encyclopedia entry, a specialist article and a specialist monograph-study were all read, and each divergence is attributed. **Not high**: **not one word of the *Tattvacintāmaṇi* was read.** Its editions were located and its OCR inspected and found to be unusable (see Sources); Phillips's complete English translation exists and was not consulted; the Ingalls quotation is unverified at source; and the front-matter Devanāgarī is a standard rendering, not taken from an edition's title page.
