## Gloss

*Sarvajñatva* (omniscience, "all-knowing-ness") in Advaita Vedānta is attributed to *Saguṇa Brahman* (Brahman considered with attributes = Īśvara, the personal God), **not** to *Nirguṇa Brahman* (pure attributeless Brahman). At the ultimate (*pāramārthika*) level, Brahman transcends all attributes, including omniscience — it is pure consciousness without an object to know. At the conventional (*vyāvahārika*) level, Īśvara is described as *sarvajna* (omniscient), *sarvaśaktimān* (omnipotent), etc. The liberated soul (*mukta-jīva*) does not attain personal omniscience; rather, it recognises its identity with Brahman and thereby transcends the knower/known distinction. This is structurally unlike Jain *kevala-jñāna*, where the individual soul itself becomes omniscient and remains individual.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Advaita-l Vedānta discussion archive (primary Advaita practitioner source):** "While attributing omniscience to Brahman, these are *taṭastha-lakṣaṇa* and not *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*" — contextual/incidental attribute versus intrinsic nature. Omniscience applies to Brahman in relation to the world, not as Brahman's essential being.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Advaita Vedanta" and Philosophy Institute article (independent secondary sources):** Confirm the saguṇa/nirguṇa distinction and that omniscience belongs to Saguṇa Brahman = Īśvara. Nirguṇa Brahman is "entirely beyond human comprehension, describable only by negation."

**Signal 3 — Kevala jnana Wikipedia article (cross-tradition comparison):** "In Advaita Vedanta, omniscience is a property of the ultimate reality (Brahman) only in relation to the world; in Jainism, omniscience is an intrinsic potential of individual souls." This formulation is the key comparative finding.

**Independence:** Signal 1 is primary tradition (mailing list, not scholarly publication — lower weight); Signals 2–3 are independent secondary sources. Confidence medium because Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtrabhāṣya was not directly fetched.

## Vedānta: two-level omniscience

| Level | Reality-status | Brahman's epistemic status |
|---|---|---|
| *Pāramārthika* (ultimate) | Brahman alone is real | Pure consciousness; no subject/object split; omniscience is inapplicable or transcended |
| *Vyāvahārika* (conventional) | World of appearance (māyā) | Brahman-as-Īśvara = *sarvajña* (omniscient, knowing all objects in the world) |

The liberating insight removes the *vyāvahārika* distinction. The mukta-jīva doesn't "know everything" the way a Jain kevali does — it recognises that the apparent individual knower was never separate from pure consciousness.

## Comparison: Vedānta sarvajñatva vs. Jain kevala-jñāna

| Feature | Advaita *sarvajñatva* | Jain *kevala-jñāna* |
|---|---|---|
| **Locus** | Saguṇa Brahman / Īśvara | Individual jīva (soul) |
| **Nature** | Attributed to Brahman in relation to world; not ultimate | Intrinsic quality of every soul, obscured by karma |
| **Liberated individual** | Merges with Brahman; knower/known distinction dissolves | Remains individual; knows all three times, all modes |
| **Achievement** | Recognition of always-existing identity | Active realisation by shedding karma-particles |
| **Individual survives?** | No — individuality was māyā | Yes — siddha retains individual upayoga |
| **Scope of knowledge** | Transcends enumerable objects | Exhaustive: all substances, all time, all modes |

## Nyāya and Vishishtadvaita contrasts

- **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika:** God (Īśvara) is omniscient — inferred as a necessary first cause of cosmic order. This is an empirical inference, not a claim that the individual soul ever becomes omniscient. Individual souls at liberation have *apavarga* (release from pain) but not omniscience.
- **Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja):** Brahman-as-God is genuinely, essentially omniscient. Individual souls at liberation gain *dharma-bhūta-jñāna* (attributive knowledge) expanded to cover all objects — a kind of individual omniscience, but always distinct from and subordinate to God's.
