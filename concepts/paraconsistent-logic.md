## Gloss

A logic is **paraconsistent** if it rejects the **principle of explosion** (*ex contradictione quodlibet*, ECQ): in classical logic, if (A ∧ ¬A) holds, then every proposition whatsoever becomes derivable — the theory becomes trivially true of everything. Paraconsistent logics block this while still being logically useful: they allow theories that contain contradictions without those theories collapsing into triviality.

**Motivation**: any inconsistent theory (containing both a claim and its negation) is classically trivial — yet Bohr's atomic model had real internal contradictions between classical orbital mechanics and Maxwell's equations and was nonetheless non-trivially scientific. Paraconsistent logic provides a formal framework for such situations.

**Graham Priest's LP (Logic of Paradox)**: introduces three truth-values — *true-only* (T), *false-only* (F), and *both-true-and-false* (B). When a proposition gets value B, both it and its negation are "designated" (inference-preserving). ECQ fails: premises can be B while conclusions remain F. Cost: modus ponens becomes invalid in LP — a significant departure from classical logic.

**Paraconsistency ≠ dialetheism**: paraconsistency is a property of a consequence relation (a logical system). Dialetheism is the metaphysical thesis that SOME contradictions are genuinely TRUE. One can use LP without being a dialetheist; most paraconsistent logicians reject dialetheism.

**Why it is NOT saptabhaṅgī**: saptabhaṅgī's seven modes use *syāt* ("from a standpoint") as a perspectival qualifier, not a truth-value. "Syāt A" and "syāt ¬A" from different standpoints are not a single contradiction — they are relativised to distinct nayas. There is no explosion problem because the two assertions are made from DIFFERENT contexts. Paraconsistent logic addresses sentences that are BOTH true AND false in THE SAME context (or from a context-free point of view). The mechanisms are orthogonal.
