## Gloss / Divergence map

Pramāṇa is **valid cognition** — complete, comprehensive knowledge of a substance in all its aspects simultaneously. The *Tattvārthasūtra* pairs it with naya as the two instruments by which finite beings access the seven tattvas:

**TS 1.6 Sanskrit:** *pramāṇanayairadhigamaḥ* — "The knowledge (of the seven categories) is attained by means of pramāṇa and naya." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass.)

**Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (ibid.): "Pramāṇa is *sakalādeśa* — comprehensive, absolute cognition. Naya is *vikalādeśa* — partial, relative cognition." Pūjyapāda adds that "pramāṇa is mentioned first as it is of superior excellence because it generates naya and encompasses all substance attributes comprehensively."

**The sakalādeśa / vikalādeśa distinction** is the sharpest Jain formulation of the pramāṇa/naya difference: pramāṇa takes the whole substance with all its attributes at once (*sakala* = complete/all); naya takes one attribute or mode (*vikala* = partial/one). Both are valid; neither alone is sufficient for finite knowers.

**TS 1.9** (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018, WisdomLib doc1084592): मतिश्रुतावधिमनःपर्ययकेवलानि ज्ञानम् — *matiśrutāvadhimanaḥparyayakevalāni jñānam* — "Knowledge is of five kinds: sensory-knowledge (matijñāna), scriptural-knowledge (śrutajñāna), clairvoyance (avadhijñāna), telepathy (manaḥparyāya-jñāna), and omniscience (kevalajñāna)."

**Two divisions of pramāṇa** (from Jain epistemological tradition; mapping onto TS 1.9's five types):

| division | Sanskrit | what it covers |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | pratyakṣa | unmediated cognition: kevala-jñāna (omniscience), avadhi (clairvoyance), manaḥparyāya (telepathy) |
| Indirect | parokṣa | mediated cognition: mati (sensory) + śruta (scriptural) — dependent on senses and language |

**Note on Jain usage vs. other schools:** In Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, *pratyakṣa* = sense perception (the most ordinary kind of knowledge). In Jainism, sense perception is classified as *parokṣa* (indirect) because it is mediated by the sense organs. Jain *pratyakṣa* is unmediated — the highest, not the most ordinary. This is the opposite assignment. Scholars of comparative Indian epistemology note this terminological divergence (not sourced independently in this run; flag for future verification).

**Pramāṇa and the kevala-jñāna connection:** The perfect, complete pramāṇa is the omniscient kevalin's kevala-jñāna — the only being for whom sakalādeśa is fully actual. Finite beings approximate it via nayas (vikalādeśa) arranged by syādvāda.
