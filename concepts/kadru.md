## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kadrū** is a daughter of Prajāpati Dakṣa and co-wife of the sage Kaśyapa who, in the Mahābhārata's Ādi Parva, becomes the **mother of the nāgas** — a thousand serpent sons, the eldest being [[shesha|Śeṣa]], Vāsuki, and Takṣaka. Her defining narrative is the rivalry with her co-wife [[vinata|Vinatā]]: the two wager over the colour of the divine horse Ucchaiḥśravas's tail (risen from the churning of the ocean). Kadrū wins by deceit — instructing her serpent sons to coil around the tail so it shows black — and Vinatā loses and is enslaved. The wager seeds the perpetual enmity between the nāgas (Kadrū's line) and the birds (Vinatā's son Garuḍa). Kadrū functions less as a worshipped deity than as a mythic **etiological figure**: the source-mother of the serpent race and the origin-point of the snake-vs-bird antagonism.
