## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śākaṭāyana** is one of the ten grammarians **[[panini|Pāṇini]] names in his own text**, and one of the two whose *arguments* [[yaska|Yāska]] preserves. He held the strongest available position on derivation — **that every word is derivable from a verbal root** — and the weakest available position on prefixes, that separated from a root they carry no sense of their own. **No work of his survives** that can be shown to be his.

⚠ **This node is the *ancient* Śākaṭāyana only.** The 9th-century Jain grammarian who took the name has his own node, [[sakatayana-jain]], and the two are linked as a typed split (§8). **Abhyankar's dictionary gives them separate headwords; his abbreviation list does not** — see below.

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The eighth collision generator, and it is not a mistake: the patronymic

Monier-Williams does not gloss *Śākaṭāyana* as a name. He glosses it as a **patronymic** — a *vṛddhi* derivative meaning "descendant of Śakaṭa" — and the *Purāṇa Index* has it as a ***pravara* of the Bhārgavas**, i.e. a lineage-name used in ritual invocation. ⭐ **A patronymic is a label whose normal semantics is that many people bear it.** Two men called Śākaṭāyana are not a confusion; they are the word working correctly.

⚠ **This is a generator the corpus's taxonomy does not have.** The six from Batch 49 — personal name, place-name, doctrine-name, short form, genre label, title in another śāstra — are all **accidents**: two things happen to share a label. Batch 50 added a seventh, the **name as warrant**, which is *deliberate*. ⭐ **This eighth is neither accidental nor deliberate: it is structural.** The naming system itself is many-to-one, and no one is making an error at any point.

⭐⭐ **And it cuts back into this batch's own first node.** [[vyadi]] records Abhyankar's argument that Vyāḍi was Pāṇini's relative: *dākṣāyaṇa* "indicates that Vyāḍi was a descendant of Dakṣa," and Pāṇini is *dākṣīputra*, "so critics say that Pāṇini and Vyāḍi were relatives." ⚠⚠ **That argument treats a patronymic as if it individuated a person, and a patronymic is precisely the kind of name that does not.** *(The corpus's inference from two sourced facts; no source consulted draws it.)* **The node written first is weakened by the node written second, and both stay.**

### 2. ⚠ How Abhyankar keeps the two apart — and the one place he does not

For **Vyāḍi** Abhyankar printed a single headword and let two incompatible datings collide inside it ([[vyadi]] §2). For Śākaṭāyana he does the **opposite**: two separate headwords — an "ancient reputed scholar of Grammar and Prātiśākhyas quoted by Pāṇini," and "Pālyakīrti Śākaṭāyana… ninth century… Rāṣṭrakūṭa king Amoghavarṣa." ⭐ **Same lexicographer, same kind of name, two different editorial decisions, and this one is right.**

⚠⚠ **But his abbreviation list undoes it.** The front matter reads "**Śāk. = Śākaṭāyana's *Śabdānuśāsana*; Śāk. Pari. = Śākaṭāyana Paribhāṣāpāṭha**" — both of which are the **Jain's**. So throughout the dictionary an unqualified "Śākaṭāyana" **followed by a sūtra number** (e.g. "Śāk. IV.3.55," "Śāk. I.1.46") silently means Pālyakīrti, while "Śākaṭāyana" in prose about pre-Pāṇinian grammarians means this node's man. ⭐ **The reader is expected to tell two men apart by whether a numeral follows the name.** The corpus records this as a *retrieval hazard for itself*, since it greps this scan.

### 3. ⭐⭐ What he actually held, and why the tradition kept the loser

Two doctrines, and both are extreme positions that the mainstream declined:

**(a) Everything is derivable.** Abhyankar, s.v. *vyutpattipakṣa*: "The grammarians hold that **Pāṇini** held the *avyutpattipakṣa*, i.e. the view that **not all words in a language can be derived** but only some of them can be so done, and contrast him with an **equally great grammarian Śākaṭāyana who stated that every word has to be derived**; cf. Kāś. on P. VII.3.6." ⭐ **The Kāśikā stages its own founder as the moderate and Śākaṭāyana as the maximalist** — and gives Śākaṭāyana the epithet "equally great" while doing it.

**(b) Prefixes are empty by themselves.** Abhyankar, s.v. *upasarga*, citing ***Nirukta* I.8***: "Some scholars like **Śākaṭāyana** hold the view that separated from the roots, prefixes **do not express any specific sense** as ordinary words express, while scholars like **Gārgya** hold the view that prefixes **do** express a sense." ⚠ Abhyankar then declines to settle it for Pāṇini: "**It is doubtful, however, which view Pāṇini himself held.**"

⭐⭐⭐ **And (b) has a downstream life the corpus can now trace, and a gap in the tracing.** Abhyankar elsewhere writes that "**Bhartṛhari, possibly following Yāska and Vyāḍi**, has developed the theory of *dyotaka* as contrasted with *vācaka* and laid down the dictum that **indeclinables, affixes and prepositions do not directly convey any specific sense**." ⚠ **That is Śākaṭāyana's position, stated in Śākaṭāyana's terms — and Abhyankar names Yāska and Vyāḍi as its ancestors and not Śākaṭāyana**, in the same book in which he attributes the position to Śākaṭāyana. ⭐ **A reference work failing to join two of its own entries.** *(The corpus's observation.)* [[sphota]] and [[bhartrhari]] hold the *dyotaka* end; this node holds the other.

### 4. ⭐ The corpus's firmest fact about him is a *convergence of two disciplines*, and it was already recorded

[[nirukta]] §4-signal-4 established, independently of this node, that **Śākaṭāyana and Gārgya are both in Pāṇini's ten-name list and both are disputants in Yāska**. ⭐ **The same two men, in the same disagreement, in two different vedāṅgas.** That is the strongest thing here: not one source's report but two disciplines' texts, and it is what makes him a person rather than a name.

⚠ **It does not date him relative to Yāska or Pāṇini**, and this node does not try — [[panini]] already records that neither man can be dated from the other.

### 5. ⚠ Everything attributed to him is a genre or a dispute

- The **Uṇādisūtras**: Abhyankar records that "similar Sūtras in **Kātantra, Āpiśali, Śākaṭāyana and other systems** of grammar are **also called Uṇādi Sūtras**" — ⭐ so "Śākaṭāyana's Uṇādisūtra" is a slot every system fills, exactly like [[jinendrabuddhi]]'s *Nyāsa* and [[paribhasha]]'s Paribhāṣāpāṭha. **The genre-label generator (Batch 49's fifth) is running here too.**
- The **Prātiśākhya**: attributed to him "according to **Nāgeśa**" — an 18th-century grammarian — with two rival attributions, and Abhyankar cannot even say whether the text is pre- or post-Pāṇinian.
- The **arrangement**: Abhyankar has the Kātantra "based on Pāṇini but composed on the models of **ancient grammarians viz. Indra, Śākaṭāyana** and others whose works, although not available now, **were available to the author**." ⚠ [[katantra]] already flags this as **inferring the availability of lost works from the shape of a surviving one** — real, and unfalsifiable in the direction it runs.

⭐ **So the sum is: two arguments, preserved by the men who rejected them; a name in a list; and a set of attributions each of which turns out to be a genre.**

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Abhyankar 1961** at first hand (the dominant source, five separate entries located by body text); **the *Nirukta*** via [[nirukta]] and [[yaska]], where Sarup's critical edition was read; **the *Kāśikā*** via Abhyankar's citation (⚠ not read directly); **Monier-Williams, Aufrecht and the *Purāṇa Index*** through the Cologne aggregation — genuinely independent of Abhyankar for the **patronymic** point, which is the node's headline and which he does not make. ⚠ **The two Wikipedia/Abhyankar ten-name lists are NOT independent** — both report Pāṇini's own text.

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. The doctrines are firmly attested and cross-checked in two disciplines; the *identity* is not, and the node's own finding is that the name is a lineage-label. Not lower, because unlike [[sarvavarman]] this man's *arguments* survive in texts that argue back at him.
