## Gloss / Divergence map

**Hemacandra** (**c. 1088–1173**), born *Caṅgadeva* at Dhandhuka in Gujarat, initiated c. 1110 as *Somacandra* and consecrated *ācārya* under the name by which he is known, is the Śvetāmbara polymath the tradition calls ***kalikālasarvajña*** — "the knower of all knowledge in his times." He worked at the Caulukya court under **Jayasiṃha Siddharāja** (r. 1092–1141) and then as adviser to **Kumārapāla**, whom he moved toward Jainism, with animal-slaughter bans across Gujarat as the visible result. He died at Pāṭan c. 1173, having "announced about his death six months in advance and fasted in his last days" — [[sallekhana|sallekhanā]], performed by the man who had codified the lay and ascetic discipline in his own *Yogaśāstra*.

His output spans fields that are not usually one person's: the *Siddhahemaśabdānuśāsana* (a grammar covering six languages, made for Siddharāja), the *Abhidhānacintāmaṇi* (lexicon), the *Yogaśāstra* ("the most comprehensive treatise on Śvetāmbara Jainism known to us"), the *Pariśiṣṭaparvan* (the ācārya lineage), the *Pramāṇamīmāṃsā* (epistemology), the ***Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra*** (the 63 illustrious persons), and the ***Anyayogavyavacchedadvātriṃśikā*** — thirty-two verses.

### The finding: two of this corpus's open upgrade paths run through him

This node was queued because Ch 33 §7 named Hemacandra's epic as an upgrade path. Writing it turned up a second, which the corpus had recorded from the other end without noticing where it led.

**1. The *Syādvādamañjarī* is a commentary on Hemacandra.** [[syadvada]] and [[saptabhangi]] both list **Malliṣeṇa's *Syādvādamañjarī* (1292)** as the primary classical text "not yet fetched, needed for confidence upgrade." That work is a commentary on the ***Anyayogavyavacchedadvātriṃśikā***: thirty-two verses in which Hemacandra argues against Vaiśeṣika, Nyāya, Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, Buddhism and Cārvāka **in favour of [[anekantavada|anekāntavāda]]**. Malliṣeṇa, a disciple of Udayaprabhasūri, dates his own completion precisely — "Saturday, Āśvina Kṛṣṇa Amāvāsyā in the Śaka year 1214." So the two Jain nodes' shared upgrade path is a **32-verse base text with a dated commentary**, which is a materially better prospect than it looked when recorded as one 13th-century title. Standard edition: A. B. Dhruva, *Syādvādamañjarī of Malliṣeṇa, with the Anyayoga-vyavaccheda-dvātriṃśikā of Hemacandra*, Poona 1933.

**2. The *Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra* is fully translated and reachable.** Helen M. Johnson's complete English translation (six volumes, from 1931; she held a Guggenheim Fellowship for Jain research in 1927) is on the Internet Archive and reproduced on Wisdomlib. [[balarama]] flags its central claim — that Baladevas attain liberation while **Vāsudevas are first reborn in hell for their violence** — as **single-sourced**, with Johnson named as what would settle it. **This pass did not settle it:** the translation's opening material was fetched and does not contain the statement, and the specific passage was not located. The flag stays open, and the corpus records the attempt rather than the appearance of closure.

What the same fetch **did** yield is a terminological correction worth having. Johnson's apparatus gives the technical names under which the 63 are counted:

| conventional name | Johnson's term | how many |
|---|---|---|
| Tīrthaṅkara | Tīrthaṅkara | 24 |
| Cakravartin | Cakravartin | 12 |
| Vāsudeva | ***Ardhacakrin*** — "half-cakravartin" | 9 |
| Baladeva / Balabhadra | ***Rāma*** | 9 |
| Prativāsudeva | ***Pratyardhacakrin*** | 9 |

*Ardhacakrin* is the load-bearing one. The scheme does not merely *include* Kṛṣṇa; it **measures him against the cakravartin and finds him half** — the rank is built into the category name, before any verdict about hell is reached. And *Rāma* as the generic term for the Baladeva class is why Jain Rāma-narratives sit where they do in the scheme. Both sharpen [[balarama]]'s finding that Jainism enrols the Hindu heroes and then reverses their ranking: the reversal is not only in the outcome, it is in the vocabulary.

### The doxographic turn, seen across three of this batch's nodes

Hemacandra's thirty-two verses are a survey of six rival schools written to defeat them. [[appayya-dikshita|Appayya Dīkṣita]]'s *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha* (16th c.) is a survey of one school's internal divergences written to reconcile them. Mādhava's *Sarvadarśanasaṃgraha* (14th c., see [[rasesvara]]) arranges every system in order, including the mercurial one. Ḍalhaṇa's *Nibandhasaṅgraha* (12th c., see [[dalhana]]) compiles the commentators before him.

These are not the same genre and the corpus does not merge them — **refutation-doxography** (Hemacandra), **reconciling doxography** (Appayya), **ordering doxography** (Mādhava) and **compilation** (Ḍalhaṇa) do different work and commit their authors to different things. What they share is a **stance**: from roughly the 12th century onward, a scholar's characteristic move is increasingly to write *about the map of positions* rather than only *from* one. Four nodes written in one batch, from four traditions, all doing it. That is an observation about the period, offered as a pattern the corpus noticed and not as a thesis it has argued.

### The Fibonacci claim, handled in both directions

Wikipedia records that around 1150 Hemacandra "described the Fibonacci sequence… about fifty years before Fibonacci (1202)," in the course of analysing Sanskrit poetic rhythms. **This is a §0 case and the corpus states it exactly.**

What is true: the problem is a real **prosody** problem — how many *mātrā-vṛtta* patterns of a given total duration exist, where a long syllable counts two morae and a short one — and its answer obeys the recurrence *f*(n) = *f*(n−1) + *f*(n−2). The priority is datable and is not in dispute in the source consulted.

What is **not** licensed: that Hemacandra was studying "the Fibonacci sequence." He was counting metres. The recurrence is the same recurrence, and the *object* is not the same object — a Sanskrit prosodist enumerating admissible verse-patterns and a Latin arithmetician modelling rabbit populations are not two people working on one topic, and the sequence's later career as a number-theoretic and aesthetic totem belongs to neither. The corpus asserts the **structural identity of the recurrence** and refuses the identification of the enterprises. Naming the sequence after the later of two independent solvers is a fact about European historiography, not evidence about either man.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Hemachandra"** — the biography: dates, birth name and place, Devacandra Sūri, both Caulukya patrons, the *kalikālasarvajña* title, the works-list, the Śiva-liṅga episode read through anekāntavāda, the Fibonacci priority, and the death by sallekhanā.
2. **The *Syādvādamañjarī* bibliographic and descriptive material** (Dhruva's 1933 edition as catalogued by several booksellers and by HereNow4U/JainQQ) — that it is a commentary on Hemacandra's thirty-two verses; the six schools targeted; Malliṣeṇa's teacher and his dated colophon. Multiple platforms carrying one editorial description; treated as **one** signal with good bibliographic corroboration, not as several.
3. **Helen M. Johnson, tr., *Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra*** (1931–), via Wisdomlib and the Internet Archive — **opening material read only**: the 63-fold breakdown and the *Ardhacakrin* / *Rāma* / *Pratyardhacakrin* terminology from the translator's footnote. The Vāsudeva-hell passage was **sought and not found** in what was read.
4. **Corpus cross-check** — [[balarama]] (the flagged single-sourced claim and its named upgrade path), [[syadvada]] and [[saptabhangi]] (which both name the *Syādvādamañjarī* and neither of which connects it to Hemacandra), and Ch 33 §7. Signal 4 is what makes this node useful: three existing files pointed at texts without knowing they pointed at the same author.

**CONVERGED.** The biography is uncontested across the sources consulted and internally coherent — court, patrons, works and death all sit in one 12th-century Gujarat. Confidence **medium** and capped for a specific reason: **the biography rests substantially on a single encyclopedia article**, with the other sources supplying bibliography rather than independent life-data; **no work of Hemacandra's was read**, in Sanskrit or translation, beyond the opening pages of one; and the one claim this node was written to verify (the Vāsudeva/Baladeva fates) was **not** verified. The node's value is in the routes it opens, not in what it settles.
