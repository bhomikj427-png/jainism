## Gloss / Divergence map

**Sampāti** is a demi-god vulture, the elder son of [[aruna|Aruṇa]] and elder brother of [[jatayu|Jaṭāyu]]. In youth the brothers raced toward the sun; when Jaṭāyu soared too high and began to scorch, Sampāti spread his own wings above him as a shield, burning them off and falling to the Vindhya mountains, where the sage Niśākara tended him and foretold their regrowth. His decisive role comes in the Kiṣkindhā-Kāṇḍa: the despairing vānara search-party (Hanumān, Aṅgada, Jāmbavān) reaches the southern shore, and Sampāti — on learning of his brother Jaṭāyu's death — uses his supernatural long-range sight to declare that Sītā is held across the sea in Rāvaṇa's Aśoka-grove on Laṅkā. This intelligence redirects the search and enables Hanumān's leap; as foretold, his wings then regenerate. Sampāti is the narrative hinge that relocates the lost Sītā.

**Variants (recorded, not reconciled).** Sources describe his bird-form inconsistently (vulture vs. eagle); the same genealogical variant as Jaṭāyu applies — encyclopedias give Śyenī as the brothers' mother, while the Vālmīki text (Araṇya-Kāṇḍa 14, in Jaṭāyu's own mouth) makes Aruṇa their father and Śyenī a distant ancestress in the Kaśyapa → Tāmrā → Vinatā avian line.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-Kāṇḍa 14** (translation of primary text): Jaṭāyu names "Sampāti my elder brother" and their shared lineage — the firm anchor.
2. **"Sampati," Wikipedia** (independent): elder son of Aruṇa, the sun-flight origin, the Vindhya exile under Niśākara, and the Sītā-locating role in the Kiṣkindhā-Kāṇḍa.
3. **TemplePurohit retelling** (devotional, weak provenance): corroborates the wing-burning and the search-party episode.

Lineage and basic role agree across a primary translation and an encyclopedia → **converged**. Held at **medium**: the Kiṣkindhā-Kāṇḍa specifics rest partly on derivative retellings, a key source (Dharmawiki) returned HTTP 403, and bird-form/genealogy variants exist.
