## Gloss

**Muditā** is **sympathetic (or altruistic) joy** — gladness at the happiness, success, and good fortune of others, "undiminished by envy or comparison." It is the **third of the four *brahmavihāra*** and third of the four ***appamaññā*** (immeasurables). Muditā is the deliberate counter to the most corrosive social emotion: instead of resenting another's gain, one rejoices in it. Nyanaponika: *"Your life will gain in joy by sharing the happiness of others as if it were yours."*

**Near and far enemy:**
- **Far enemy:** **envy / resentment** (and jealousy) — the direct opposite, begrudging others their good.
- **Near enemy:** frivolous, exuberant, or **conditional pleasure** that depends on personal benefit or approval — a worldly elation that mimics muditā but is self-referential rather than altruistic.

Muditā is the least-discussed brahmavihāra in popular practice yet structurally essential: mettā and karuṇā orient the mind toward beings who lack well-being or who suffer, but without muditā the practitioner has no trained response to beings who are *flourishing* — and untrained, that response defaults to envy. The four immeasurables together cover the full field of others' fortune: wish-well (mettā), relieve-suffering (karuṇā), rejoice-in-success (muditā), and remain-balanced (upekkhā).

**Cross-tradition note (parallel, not identity).** Jainism's parallel four-reflection set names this *pramoda* — joy/appreciation directed especially at the virtuous and spiritually advanced. The structural slot is the same; the framing (delight in *guṇa*/virtue vs. delight in others' *happiness*) differs.
