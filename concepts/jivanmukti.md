## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jīvanmukti** (Sanskrit *jīvan* "while living" + *mukti* "liberation") is **liberation attained while still embodied** — the state of one who has realised liberating knowledge yet continues to live in the body. It is defined against **videhamukti**, liberation at the moment of death (literally "bodiless liberation"). The liberated person is a **jīvanmukta**: "he who sees no difference between Self (ātman) and Brahman" (Advaita), free *now*, regardless of the body's continuance.

**The prārabdha-karma mechanism (the central puzzle).** If liberating knowledge has dawned, why does the body persist at all? Advaita answers by classifying karma: liberating knowledge burns up **sañcita** (stored) and **āgāmin** (future) karma, but **prārabdha** karma — the portion already "begun," that produced *this* body and is now bearing fruit — must simply **play itself out**. The jīvanmukta lives out the prārabdha like a potter's wheel still spinning after the potter's hand is withdrawn, **generating no new karma**, until the body falls and videhamukti supervenes.

**The lived portrait.** The jīvanmukta is described (Nāradaparivrājaka Upaniṣad; Gītā's *sthitaprajña* passage) by equanimity in pleasure and pain, *ahiṃsā*, inner peace independent of circumstance, and freedom from ritual attachment — knowledge valued above all. The Gītā's **sthitaprajña** ("one of steady wisdom," BG 2.54–72) is essentially the jīvanmukta drawn as a character.

**Disputed across the Vedāntas.** Advaita makes jīvanmukti the supreme goal (liberation is *knowledge*, so it can be complete while alive). **Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita** tend to resist or reframe it: if liberation is *union with / nearness to a personal God* attained only on shedding the body, then full mukti is **videhamukti**, and a living person can be at most a devotee-in-progress, not already liberated. The fork is the same individuality question that divides the three Vedāntas over mokṣa itself.
