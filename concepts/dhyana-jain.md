## Gloss / Divergence map

In Jain soteriology, dhyāna ("meditation/concentration") is classified into **four types** that fall into two opposed pairs: the first two generate karma and perpetuate saṃsāra; the latter two destroy karma and lead toward liberation.

**TS 9.28 Sanskrit:** *ārtaraudradharmyaśuklāni* — "The four subdivisions of meditation (*dhyāna*) are: painful/sorrowful (*ārta*), cruel (*raudra*), virtuous (*dharmya*), and pure (*śukla*)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**Pūjyapāda's** critical distinction (ibid.): **ārta and raudra** are "the cause of āsrava of inauspicious karmas (*pāpa*)"; **dharmya and śukla** are "capable of destroying karmas."

**The four dhyānas:**

| type | Sanskrit | character | karmic effect | guṇasthāna range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sorrowful | ārta | repeated thinking to remove disagreeable contacts or obtain agreeable ones; frustrated attachment | generates *pāpa* karma; binds | 1–6 (any unrestrained being) |
| Cruel | raudra | thinking about harming others, deceit, theft — malicious fixation | generates *pāpa* karma; deeply binds | 1–5 (violent beings) |
| Virtuous | dharmya | four sub-objects: Jina's teaching (*ājñāvicaya*), misfortunes of beings (*apāyavicaya*), karma-fruition (*vipākavicaya*), universe-structure (*saṃsthānavicaya*) | destroys karma; enables saṃvara/nirjarā | 7–12 (advanced practitioners) |
| Pure | śukla | the ultimate: first two subtypes for the kevalin (sayoga), last two for the final moments (ayoga); meditation on the soul's pure nature | complete karma annihilation → mokṣa | 13–14 (omniscient) |

**The four subtypes of dharmya dhyāna** (from search-result aggregation):
1. *Ājñāvicaya*: contemplating the Jina's teachings and scripture
2. *Apāyavicaya*: contemplating the misfortunes of beings in saṃsāra
3. *Vipākavicaya*: contemplating the fruition of karmas
4. *Saṃsthānavicaya*: contemplating the structure of the universe (loka)

**Connection to guṇasthānas:** Dharmya dhyāna becomes possible at guṇasthāna 7 (*apramatta-virata* — perfect vows). Śukla dhyāna is the defining activity of guṇasthāna 13 (*sayoga-kevalī*) and 14 (*ayoga-kevalī*). The four dhyānas thus map onto the entire guṇasthāna arc: ārta/raudra dominate in stages 1–6; dharmya in 7–12; śukla in 13–14.

**Note on terminology:** Jain dhyāna is not the same as Pātañjala yoga's *dhyāna* (7th limb of aṣṭāṅga yoga) or Buddhist *jhāna* (the four jhānas of Pali Canon). All three traditions use the word but mean structurally different things — the Jain classification is by karmic effect, not by depth of absorption.
