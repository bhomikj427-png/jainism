## Gloss / Divergence map

**Praśastapāda** (tentatively 6th c. CE) is the author of the **Padārthadharmasaṃgraha** ("compendium of the properties of the categories"), also called the **Praśastapāda-bhāṣya**. Though named a *bhāṣya* (commentary) on **[[kanada|Kaṇāda]]'s** **[[vaiseshika-sutra|Vaiśeṣika Sūtra]]**, it is in fact an **independent systematic treatise** that reorganizes Vaiśeṣika doctrine — and it **overshadowed the mūla-sūtra**, becoming the text on which later Vaiśeṣika commentary (Vyomaśiva, Śrīdhara, Udayana) was actually written. He expanded the scheme of *guṇas* (qualities) — adding gurutva, dravatva, sneha, dharma, adharma, śabda, saṃskāra — and sharpened the realist account of the categories (*padārthas*). As a realist he held universals/determinates to be **objective constituents of reality**, against the Buddhist Dignāga's view that they are mental constructs.

**Note (commentary-tradition nuance, §4).** That the most influential "commentary" is really an independent compendium is itself the finding: in Vaiśeṣika the bhāṣya, not the sūtra, became the doctrinal anchor — the reverse of the usual mūla-primacy.
