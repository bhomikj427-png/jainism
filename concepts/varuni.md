## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vāruṇī** ("belonging to Varuṇa," an adjectival/patronymic formation) names **both** the wine/liquor produced by the [[samudra-manthana|churning of the cosmic ocean]] **and** the goddess who personifies it — a genuine dual usage attested across the sources themselves, not a project artifact. She is consistently treated as a daughter of (or arising alongside) **Varuṇa** — no `varuna` concept file exists yet in this repository; this is recorded here as a reference to an unwritten node, not linked as if it existed. This file covers Vāruṇī the churning-product, not the churning mechanics told in `samudra-manthana.md`.

**CONTESTED — who takes her, devas or asuras?** Sources genuinely diverge on this point, not merely in narrative color but in the substance of who accepts and who rejects her:

| reading | what it commits you to | source |
|---|---|---|
| **devas accept, asuras reject** | the *sura*/*asura* wordplay locus classicus: "the sons of Diti [asuras] have not espoused... but the sons of Aditi [suras] have espoused that impeccable Vāruṇī" | Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.45.36–37 |
| **asuras take her** | Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.30 has the demons, headed by Bali, "take possession" of Vāruṇī with Hari's permission — the reverse assignment | Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.30 |

Wikipedia's "Varuni" entry offers a harmonizing reading of the second version — that it reflects "a growing ambivalence toward intoxicants" and that she becomes "a consolation for the defeated Asuras" — but this is an interpretive gloss on the divergence, not an independent primary source resolving it, and is recorded here as interpretation rather than fact.

**The sura/asura etymology is folk, not scholarly — flagged explicitly.** The Rāmāyaṇa passage above plays directly on a narrative etymology ("those who accept *sura* [wine] are *sura*; those who reject it are *a-sura*"). Modern historical linguistics instead derives **asura** from Proto-Indo-Iranian *Hásuras* ("lord, powerful one") — a term applied even to gods (including Varuṇa himself) in the early Ṛgveda, only shifting toward "demon" later, and **unconnected** to *sura* "wine." The Vāruṇī-based wordplay is a genuine in-text Purāṇic/epic narrative etymology — real, attested, worth recording — but it is not the accepted historical-linguistic derivation, and the file does not let the two blur together.

**Later currency:** a claim that Vāruṇī later appears as a minor Mātṛkā-goddess and attends Balarāma (Kṛṣṇa's brother — no dedicated concept file exists yet for Balarāma in this repository) (per a secondary report of H.H. Wilson's Viṣṇu Purāṇa translation) could not be independently verified against the primary translation in this research pass (source access blocked) — recorded as **low-confidence, unverified**, not asserted as established.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva §XVIII** (Ganguli translation) — treats "Vāruṇī" primarily as the *name of the wine* produced by the churning, without personifying her as a distinct goddess-actor in that passage — a genuine internal-consistency data point: the earliest telling is substance-focused, the personification is fuller elsewhere.
2. **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa 1.45.36–37** (direct primary-text consultation, valmikiramayan.net) — the locus classicus for the devas-accept/asuras-reject reading and the in-text sura/asura wordplay.
3. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.30** (Sanskrit + translation) — *"athāsīd vāruṇī devī... asurā jagṛhus tāṁ vai harer anumatena te"*, directly contradicting the Rāmāyaṇa's assignment by having the asuras, led by Bali, take her.
4. **Wikipedia "Asura"** (etymology cross-check, search-summarized rather than directly fetched in this pass — flagged as a gap for a future verification pass) — the Proto-Indo-Iranian *Hásuras* derivation, used as the hard lexical constraint against which the in-text folk etymology is measured.

Two independent primary texts (Rāmāyaṇa, Bhāgavata Purāṇa) genuinely disagree on the devas/asuras assignment, while a third (Mahābhārata) treats her mainly as a substance rather than weighing in on the assignment at all → **contested**, not converged. Core identity (daughter/creation of Varuṇa, wine-goddess, churning-product) is itself convergent across all three, supporting **medium** rather than low confidence — the divergence is specific and well-documented, not a sign of thin sourcing.
