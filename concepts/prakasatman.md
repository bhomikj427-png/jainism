## Gloss / Divergence map

**Prakāśātman** (also **Prakāśānubhava**) is the Advaitin whose commentary **gave the Vivaraṇa school its name**. His *Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa* is a gloss on [[padmapada|Padmapāda]]'s *Pañcapādikā*, which is itself a gloss on [[shankara|Śaṅkara]]'s *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* on the first four sūtras of [[brahma-sutra|Bādarāyaṇa]] — a **four-storey commentarial stack**, and the *Vivaraṇa* is the storey the school is named for, not the one Śaṅkara wrote.

His one doctrinal first is decisive for everything after him: **he was the first to propound the theory of *mūlāvidyā* / *māyā* as being of "positive beginningless nature"** — a single, universal **root ignorance** (*mūlāvidyā*) which is the **material cause of the world**, *bhāvarūpa* (positive in form) rather than a mere absence of knowledge. Sources agree this framework "became the leitmotif of all subsequent developments in the evolution of the Advaita tradition." He also holds ***pratibimbavāda*** with Padmapāda, and the Vivaraṇa position that **Brahman is the locus of avidyā** (against the Bhāmatī line — see the table in [[padmapada]]).

### The three theories of the jīva — where he sits

The post-Śaṅkara tradition offers three competing accounts of how the one Brahman appears as many selves. They are not synonyms, and the corpus keeps them distinct:

| theory | claim | who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| ***pratibimbavāda*** — reflection | *bimba* (prototype) is Brahman, *pratibimba* (reflection) is the jīva; "the Jiva is a mere reflection…of Brahman, and therefore, identical with its essence, Brahman." Consciousness reflected **in nescience** is Īśvara; the same consciousness reflected **in the inner sense** is the jīva | [[padmapada|Padmapāda]], **Prakāśātman**, [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]] |
| ***avacchedavāda*** — limitation | the jīva is Brahman **delimited** by an adjunct, not reflected in one; its followers object that reflection theory "fails to explain" how a consciousness with no sensible qualities could be reflected at all | [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]] (Bhāmatī) |
| ***ābhāsavāda*** — appearance | jīva as *ābhāsa*, a partial manifestation resembling the real without the real's properties | [[vidyaranya|Vidyāraṇya]] |

The trade-off is clean: **pratibimbavāda buys non-difference (*abheda*) at the cost of a reflection-medium; avacchedavāda buys a coherent account of the medium at the cost of leaning on difference (*bheda*)** — and neither, on the reporting source's own assessment, fully escapes the dualism that Śaṅkara's *anirvacanīya māyā* was meant to sidestep. This is the shape of the whole post-Śaṅkara literature: **the master's un-analysed terms get analysed, and the analyses split.**

### Why *mūlāvidyā* matters beyond Advaita

Making avidyā *bhāvarūpa* and the **material cause** of the world puts Advaita's ignorance into the same job-slot that [[prakriti-samkhya|prakṛti]] occupies in Sāṃkhya — a single positive beginningless substrate out of which the world is made. That is a **structural parallel and not an identity**: prakṛti is *real* and independent of puruṣa, whereas mūlāvidyā is precisely what is cancelled by Brahman-knowledge. The very availability of the comparison is why **Satchidānandendra Sarasvatī's objection** (recorded in [[padmapada]]) has force — he held that this whole apparatus misconstrues Śaṅkara, for whom avidyā was beginningless and its origin fruitless to investigate. Whether *mūlāvidyā* is Advaita's completion or its Sāṃkhyisation is the live question, and the corpus does not settle it.

The Vivaraṇa position also attracts a standing internal objection, which the reporting sources state rather than suppress: if **Brahman is the locus of avidyā**, then ignorance is generated by *pure consciousness*, and knowledge and ignorance are predicated of one substrate. That objection is precisely what drives the Bhāmatī line to move the locus into the jīva.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Prakasatman"** and **Wikipedia, "Vivarana"** (fetched separately): the school-naming; **c. 1200–1300**; the *mūlāvidyā*/māyā "positive beginningless nature" first, and that it "became the leitmotif of all subsequent developments"; Brahman as source/locus of avidyā, **with the objection stated**; the karma/śravaṇa tenets ("Karma is responsible for the rise of knowledge of the Self"; textual study primary, reflection and meditation auxiliary).
2. **Hindupedia, "Prakāśātman"** (fetched, different platform and editorial line): **A.D. 1200**; the alias **Prakāśānubhava**; **disciple of Ananyānubhava**; the *Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa* as "a voluminous commentary" reaching down through Padmapāda's gloss to Śaṅkara's bhāṣya on the **first four sūtras**; the *Vivaraṇaprasthāna* as "a separate and special school."
3. **Wisdomlib, "Prakāśātman"** (fetched; a lexicon/catalogue aggregator, hence used for the *bibliographic* and *lexical* signals specifically): **c. 10th century**; three works — *Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa*, ***Śabdanirṇaya***, ***Nyāyamuktāvalī*** (on the Brahmasūtras); Monier-Williams-style lexical senses of the word (*bright, shining*; the sun; epithet of Śiva or Viṣṇu — signal 3, §4); and the **multiple-persons** warning.
4. **Wikipedia, "Pratibimbavāda"** (fetched): the three-way *pratibimba* / *avaccheda* / *ābhāsa* division with its proponents, the bimba/pratibimba definitions, the Īśvara-vs-jīva reflection-medium formula, the avacchedavādin critique, and the abheda/bheda trade-off.
5. **Corpus cross-check** ([[padmapada]], [[sureshvara]], [[mandana-mishra]], [[vidyaranya]], [[maya-advaita]]): the locus-of-avidyā split and Prakāśātman's 10th-c. date were recorded in Batch 41 from **IEP**, i.e. a fifth source-set — which is what puts the 10th-c. reading in genuine competition rather than leaving Wikipedia unopposed.

**CONTESTED**, on the date and the teacher; **converged** on the doctrine, which every source states identically. Confidence **medium**: four independently-published reference sources agree on the *Vivaraṇa*, on *mūlāvidyā*'s positive beginningless nature, and on the reflection theory — but no critical edition or translation of the *Pañcapādikā-vivaraṇa* itself was read (an Internet Archive scan was located, not consulted), and the two extra attributed works are catalogue entries only. Signals 1 and 4 share a platform, which is why the date-dispute rests on 2/3 versus 1/4 rather than on Wikipedia alone.
