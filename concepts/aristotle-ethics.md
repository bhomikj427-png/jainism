## Gloss

Aristotle's ethical system, developed in the *Nicomachean Ethics* (EN). The highest good is **eudaimonia** (flourishing / happiness): it is desirable for itself alone and never as a means to something else (EN I.1, 1094a14-16). The **function argument** (EN I.7, 1097b24–1098a4): every kind of thing has a characteristic function; the human function is rational activity; therefore the good human life is *activity of the soul in accordance with virtue* over a complete life.

**Virtue** (*aretē*): two kinds, distinguished at EN 1103a1-10. *Intellectual virtues* (*dianoetikai*) — wisdom, understanding, prudence — perfected by teaching. *Character virtues* (*ēthikai*) — courage, justice, temperance, etc. — acquired by habituation. The **doctrine of the mean** (*mesotēs*): each character virtue is a stable disposition to choose the intermediate between excess and deficiency relative to the person and situation (EN II.6, 1106a26-b28). The mean is not arithmetic average but the perception-guided appropriate response.

Eudaimonia is **this-worldly and political**: it is achieved through virtuous activity within a human community (*polis*), not through transcendence, withdrawal, or liberation from embodied existence. EN X completes the argument: the highest activity is philosophical contemplation (*theōria*), but contemplation requires a virtuous community as its foundation.

**Cross-tradition diagnostic:** Indian liberation-ethics (Jain tri-ratna, Buddhist Noble Path) aims *out of* the world (saṃsāra → mokṣa/nirvāṇa). Aristotelian ethics aims *into* the world (bios → eudaimonia). The direction is reversed.
