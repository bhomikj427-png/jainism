## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śivādvaita** (also **Śiva-viśiṣṭādvaita**, "Śaivite qualified non-dualism") is the Vedānta school that identifies **Brahman with Śiva** and takes [[shrikantha|Śrīkaṇṭha]]'s *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya* as its root commentary. It is a **16th-century South Indian formation**, and its constitution as an *independent school* is the work of [[appayya-dikshita|Appayya Dīkṣita]] — who won from a 1580 inscription the title ***śaivādvaitaikasāmrājya***, "sole emperor of Śaiva Advaita," and whose patron Cinnabomma of Vellore is recorded as having "bathed him in gold" on the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā*'s publication.

### The finding: the school is made by moving one text across an axis this corpus already holds

Duquette's *Journal of Hindu Studies* paper states the mechanism exactly, and it is not a vague "reinterpretation":

> Appaya's interpretation of Śrīkaṇṭha's ***pariṇāmavāda* as *vivartavāda*** establishes Śivādvaita Vedānta as an independent school, distinct from the mainstream Śaiva Siddhānta of this period.

Both terms are already nodes here, and they are the **axis** on which this corpus sorts Vedānta:

| | [[parinamavada]] | [[vivartavada]] |
|---|---|---|
| the claim | causes **really transform** into effects; manifestation is a genuine unfolding | the cause **appears** to transform without really doing so; the change is appearance on an unchanging substrate |
| what it makes the world | real, a true modification of Brahman | appearance |
| whose | Bhedābheda, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Sāṃkhya's *prakṛti* | Advaita (traditionally, Śaṅkara's own) |

Śrīkaṇṭha's *bhāṣya* is a **pariṇāmavāda** text: Śiva really becomes the world, which is why the school is a *qualified* non-dualism and why it sits naturally beside Śaiva Siddhānta. Appayya reads it as **vivartavāda** — and the school is thereby detached from Śaiva Siddhānta and stood up as Vedānta in its own right.

That is a remarkably precise account of how a school gets founded. Not by a new revelation, a new text, or a new deity — **by relocating an existing commentary on the causation question.** The corpus should register what this implies: the pariṇāma/vivarta distinction it inherited from Ch 11 as a *taxonomy for sorting schools* turns out to be, in at least one case, the **instrument that made a school**. Move a text across the line and its community's affiliation moves with it.

### What Appayya did, stated at the right strength

Wikipedia's summary of **McCrea 2016** is the most careful formulation available to this pass, and it is worth quoting rather than paraphrasing: Appayya "**retrieved the obscure commentary of Śrīkaṇṭha**" and "**introduced it to a broad scholarly audience while simultaneously reinterpreting it through his own theology of Śivādvaita**."

Read against the paper's title — "Appayyadīkṣita's Invention of Śrīkaṇṭha's Vedānta" — that sentence **settles the question [[shrikantha]] had to leave open.** McCrea's claim is *not* that the man was fabricated or the text forged. The commentary was **real and obscure**; what Appayya invented was the *Vedānta* — the system read out of it, and the audience that received it as a system. The three-way distinction that node drew (inventing a man / a text / a Vedānta) resolves to the third, on the strongest source available.

Wikipedia itself notes the same restraint from the other side: the sources "don't explicitly claim he 'invented' the school — rather, he became its **most prominent representative**." Both statements can stand. A school can be constituted by a reinterpretation while the tradition remembers its constituter as its greatest exponent; those are the same event described from inside and from outside.

### Not Śaiva Siddhānta, and not Advaita with Śiva substituted

Two distinctions the map has to teach, because the name invites both errors:

**Against Śaiva Siddhānta.** The relationship is the explicit subject of Duquette 2015, whose title is a question — *Is Śivādvaita Vedānta a Saiddhāntika School?* — asked across the 15th–18th centuries and answered **no**, on the strength of the pariṇāma-read-as-vivarta move. That the question needed a paper is the point: the two were close enough that the distinction had to be argued.

**Against [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedānta]].** "Śiva-advaita" is not Advaita with a deity inserted. Advaita's Brahman is *nirguṇa*, without qualities; Śivādvaita's Śiva is a **supreme personal deity possessing qualities**, and the world is grounded in him rather than superimposed on him. The *rapprochement* is real — Dasgupta describes the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* as "attempting to harmonize [Śrīkaṇṭha's positions] with Śaṅkara's monism," and Appayya was an Advaitin of the first rank — but harmonisation performed by one man is not identity of two schools, and the corpus keeps them distinct nodes.

### What is not established

**Whether the school outlived its founder.** No source consulted says anything about Śivādvaita's adherents, institutions or literature after Appayya; the encyclopedia entry checked for exactly this returned nothing. A school established "single-handedly" invites the question of what happened next, and this pass cannot answer it. Elaine Fisher's *Remaking South Indian Śaivism: Greater Śaiva Advaita and the Legacy of the Śaktiviśiṣṭādvaita Vīraśaiva Tradition* (*IJHS*, 2017) is the located-not-read item most likely to.

`term_devanagari` is **omitted** — not printed by any source consulted (§3).

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — **Duquette 2015** (*JHS*, peer-reviewed; abstract obtained); **McCrea 2016** (*JIP*, peer-reviewed; characterised through Wikipedia's summary, the paper itself unobtainable); **Duquette 2021** (Oxford monograph, publisher's description); **Wikipedia's Appayya article**, cited here as a reporter of Duquette/McCrea/Bronner/Ramesan rather than as an authority; and **Dasgupta vol. 5** from the previous generation of scholarship. The two Duquette items and the McCrea item are independent of the encyclopedia and of each other.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor, and here it does something unusual: the school is not defined by *what the root text says* but by *how the sub-commentary reads it*. The mūla-bhāṣya-ṭīkā chain is present in full, and the school's identity is fixed at the outermost layer.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — *śiva-advaita* is a transparent compound and, precisely because it is transparent, misleading: it looks like "Advaita, of Śiva," and the doctrine is a **qualified** non-dualism. The name is one of the two conflations the links below are drawn to block.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/`: [[parinamavada]] and [[vivartavada]] were already written as the sorting axis of Vedānta, and [[appayya-dikshita]] already carried the Vellore patronage and the *śaivādvaitaikasāmrājya* inscription. **Nothing in this node needed new vocabulary** — Duquette's mechanism lands exactly on terms the corpus had already defined for other reasons, which is the strongest internal-consistency result of this batch.
5. **Provenance** — the best available: two peer-reviewed journal articles and an Oxford monograph, all located, with one abstract obtained verbatim. What holds confidence at **medium** is that **none of the three was read in full**, and no primary text — neither the *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya* nor the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* nor the *Śivādvaitanirṇaya* — was opened.

**CONVERGED** on the school's doctrine, its 16th-century constitution, Appayya's decisive role, the pariṇāma→vivarta mechanism, and its distinctness from Śaiva Siddhānta. The **dating and authorship of its root text remain `contested` — that dispute lives in [[shrikantha]]**, and this node deliberately does not restate it. Confidence **medium**: sources of the best available kind, none of them read in full, and the school's post-Appayya history entirely unknown to this corpus.
