## Gloss / Divergence map

**Mohinī** ("the enchantress," from *moha*, "delusion") is **Viṣṇu's female form** — his only one — who appears at the climax of the **[[samudra-manthana|Samudra-Manthana]]**: when the asuras seize the **[[amrita|amṛta]]**, Viṣṇu becomes the irresistibly beautiful Mohinī, beguiles them into letting her **distribute** the nectar, and serves it **to the devas alone**, securing their immortality. During this distribution the asura **[[rahu|Rāhu]]** sneaks into the gods' line; Sūrya and Candra expose him and Viṣṇu beheads him with the discus. In later South Indian myth, Mohinī's union with **[[shiva|Śiva]]** produces **Ayyappa / Śāsta** (Hariharaputra).

**Classificatory divergence (recorded, not forced):**

| reading | what it commits you to |
|---|---|
| Mohinī = an **avatāra** of Viṣṇu (his only female one) | she is a genuine descent-form, counted among Viṣṇu's manifestations |
| Mohinī = Viṣṇu's **māyā** (illusion), a temporary guise | she is a deluding appearance, not a full avatāra in the daśāvatāra sense |

The sources "present her ambiguously — sometimes as a temporary avatar, sometimes as Viṣṇu's *māyā*." Either way her **function** (deception in service of cosmic order) is stable; the label is what varies.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Mohini"** — Viṣṇu's only female form; the amṛta-distribution and asura-deception; the Rāhu beheading; the Śiva/Ayyappa sequel; the explicit avatāra-vs-*māyā* ambiguity.
2. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva (Ganguli tr.)** — independently: after the asuras seize the nectar, Viṣṇu "assuming the form of an enchanting female" deludes them and recovers it for the gods, and Rāhu is beheaded by the discus — the churning-anchor text.

Encyclopedia and critical-translation agree on the role and the Rāhu episode → **converged**, confidence **medium** (the avatāra/*māyā* classification is the one open point, recorded as a divergence).
