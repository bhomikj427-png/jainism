## Gloss / Divergence map

**Nibbāna** (Pali; Sanskrit: *nirvāṇa*): the **Theravāda liberation goal** — the complete cessation of taṇhā (craving), lobha/dosa/moha (greed/hatred/delusion), and consequently of dukkha (suffering) and saṃsāra (rebirth). It is the **Third Noble Truth** (*nirodha-sacca*) in the four noble truths framework. The Pali tradition distinguishes nibbāna sharply from Mahāyāna readings — in Theravāda, saṃsāra and nibbāna remain genuinely distinct; nibbāna is not a function of ignorance about saṃsāra's nature (against Nāgārjuna's MMK 25 reading).

**The unconditioned formula (Udāna 8.1):**
> *"Atthi, bhikkhave, ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ. No ce taṃ, bhikkhave, abhavissa ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ, nayidha jātassa bhūtassa katassa saṅkhatassa nissaraṇaṃ paññāyetha."*
> "There is, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned (*ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ*). Were there not that unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned, there would be no escape from the born, become, made, conditioned."

This is the central Pali warrant for nibbāna's positive referent: it is not mere negation but an **asaṅkhata dhamma** (unconditioned reality) — apprehendable by noble ones by the path appropriate to it (Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga*).

**Two kinds of nibbāna (Itivuttaka 43–44):**

| type | Pali term | condition | description |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nibbāna with remainder | *sa-upādisesā-nibbānadhātu* | arahant who is still alive | Defilements (taṇhā, lobha, dosa, moha) extinguished; five khandhas remain until natural death; the arahant perceives, acts, and teaches — but without craving |
| Nibbāna without remainder | *anupādisesā-nibbānadhātu* (parinibbāna) | at the death of an arahant or Buddha | No further arising of the five khandhas; no rebirth; complete cessation of conditioned existence |

The "remainder" in *sa-upādisesā* refers to the residue of the physical-mental aggregates (*upādā-khandhas*) — not to any defilement or craving, which is fully extinguished on awakening.

**Nature of nibbāna — the contested question:**

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| "Mere extinction" reading | Some modern scholars (Stcherbatsky; early Rhys Davids) | Nibbāna = cessation of process; no positive referent; the fire metaphor means literally nothing remains |
| "Unconditioned dhamma" reading | Theravāda Abhidhamma; Buddhaghosa (*Visuddhimagga*); Bhikkhu Bodhi | Nibbāna is a real asaṅkhata (*unconditioned*) dhamma — the only unconditioned reality; it is transcendent, not "nothing"; it is apprehendable in *cessation of perception and feeling* (*nirodha-samāpatti*) |
| "Highest happiness" reading | K.N. Jayatilleke, Nyanatiloka, Bhikkhu Ñāṇamoli | Udāna 8.1-4 implies positive content; SN 38.1 (*nibbānaṃ paramaṃ sukhaṃ* — "nibbāna is the highest happiness") should be taken literally |

Note: all three readings agree nibbāna is **not annihilation** (*ucchedavāda*) — the nihilistic reading is explicitly rejected in the canon (Pali Canon passim). The Buddha refuses to say "the Tathāgata exists / does not exist after death" precisely because nibbāna cannot be mapped onto conditioned-existence categories.

**Theravāda vs. Mahāyāna:**
- **Theravāda**: saṃsāra and nibbāna are genuinely distinct; nibbāna is the real and complete cessation of suffering; the arhat's parinibbāna is final. An arahant who achieves parinibbāna does not return.
- **Mahāyāna**: *apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa* ("non-abiding nirvāṇa") — the bodhisattva vow to remain active in saṃsāra out of compassion; Nāgārjuna's MMK 25 equates saṃsāra with nirvāṇa from the ultimate-truth standpoint (śūnyatā-lens). The Theravāda tradition rejects this as misreading.

**What nibbāna is NOT (common conflations):**
- NOT the Jain *mokṣa*: no individual siddha persists at lokāgra; the anattā doctrine means no permanent self is "liberated."
- NOT Advaita *mokṣa*: no ātman merges into Brahman; no permanent individual consciousness persists as a component of a cosmic absolute.
- NOT annihilation (*ucchedavāda*): the fire-metaphor means "no more fuel" not "fire = nothing"; something is described in Udāna 8.1-4.
- NOT a place or realm: nibbāna is not spatial — there is no "where" one goes.
