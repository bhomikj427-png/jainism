## Gloss / Divergence map

The paramāṇu is the ultimate, partless unit of matter (pudgala) in Jain ontology: the smallest possible division, possessing no spatial extension (no pradeśa / space-points), and imperceptible to ordinary sense faculties in isolation. It is not arrived at empirically but is an ontological posit: the aggregation/dissolution structure of matter requires that division terminate somewhere. Every paramāṇu possesses all four sensory qualities — one touch (either snigdha "oily/adhesive" or rūkṣa "dry/repellent"), one taste, one smell, one colour — not none of them. Aggregates (skandha) arise when paramāṇus with complementary snigdha/rūkṣa qualities bind.

TS 5.11 states tersely that paramāṇu has no space-points (pradeśa), which is both the definition of partlessness and the argument for its uniqueness: nothing can be smaller. The explicit infinite-regress argument ("division cannot proceed infinitely, therefore there must be a terminal unit") appears in the commentarial literature (Pūjyapāda, Sarvārthasiddhi) rather than in the aphoristic sūtra itself.

**Verse-numbering note:** TS 5.11 (Vijay K. Jain 2018 edition, Motilal Banarsidass) gives "absence of space-points in the indivisible atom." A WisdomLib aggregation cites a similar formulation under "5.1" — the discrepancy likely reflects Digambara/Śvetāmbara recension differences in verse counting and is unresolved in this file; treat 5.11 (Vijay K. Jain) as the verified reference.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain translation (2018):** TS 5.11 directly: "no space-points (pradeśa) in the indivisible atom (paramāṇu)." Commentary (Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi): "the indivisible unit of matter has one space-point, and there can be no further division of its spatial unit." Strong provenance (Motilal Banarsidass critical edition).

**Signal 2 — Nathmal Tatia translation (1994):** Confirmed by Cambridge Core review as including TS with Pūjyapāda and Siddhasenagaṇi commentaries. Chapter 5 text not independently fetched in this run; Tatia's rendering of TS 5.11 is not directly quoted here. Confidence would rise to high if Chapter 5 wording is verified in a future run.

**Signal 3 — WisdomLib definitions aggregation:** Cites "Tattvārthasūtra 5.5" for qualities: "round shape, two touches, one taste, one smell and one colour." Multiple Jain source texts (Jñānārṇava, Atma Dharma) converge on the indivisibility definition. Provenance is aggregated/derivative — not treated as independently sourced.

**Signal 4 — Commentarial tradition:** Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi (cited via Vijay K. Jain 2018) is the Digambara anchor commentary. Siddhasenagaṇi's ṭīkā (Śvetāmbara) included in Tatia 1994 but not independently fetched.

**Signal 5 — Vaisheshika comparison:** One search-result summary states the contrast cleanly ("Vaisheshika: different kinds with different qualities; Jain: one kind, all four qualities in every atom") but this is aggregated web content, not a critical scholarly source. Provisionally accepted; needs a second independent source in a future run.

**Independence assessment:** Signals 1 and 2 are genuinely independent editions. Signals 3–5 are derivative or partially derivative. Convergence on the core definition (partless, four qualities, no space-points) is strong; verse-number attribution and the Vaisheshika comparison are lower-confidence.
