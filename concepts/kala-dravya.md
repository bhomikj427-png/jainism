## Gloss

Kāla (time) is the sixth Jain dravya. Its defining function is *vartanā* — the external instrumental cause enabling all substances to undergo continuous, imperceptible change (to "persist through gradual modification"). It assists the five other substances (jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, ākāśa) to actualise their paryāyas (modes). Kāla is unique among the dravyas: each time-atom (*kāla-paramāṇu*) occupies exactly one space-point (*pradeśa*) and no more, which means kāla is **not** an *astikāya* (spatially extended substance) — it has the quality of a substance but lacks spatial extension. This makes it structurally different from the five astikāyas.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 5.22 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda):** *vartanāpariṇāmakriyāḥ paratvāparatve ca kālasya* — "Time assists substances through continuity of being (*vartanā*), modification (*pariṇāma*), movement (*kriyā*), endurance (*paratva*), and non-endurance (*aparatva*)." Five functions, with *vartanā* (persistence/continuity) as the primary mark.

**Signal 2 — TS 5.39 (directly fetched, Pūjyapāda):** *kālaśca* — "Time also is a substance." The *ca* ("also") is structurally significant: TS 5.38 defines dravya as "that which has qualities and modes" (*guṇaparyāyavat dravyam*); TS 5.39 applies this to kāla as a standalone assertion, confirming its substance-status.

**Signal 3 — Pūjyapāda's commentary (via WisdomLib fetch):** Kāla satisfies both definitions of substance — (1) origination/destruction/permanence (TS 5.30), and (2) possession of guṇas and paryāyas (TS 5.38). Kāla's specific guṇa: lifeless, formless, minute; its specific paryāya: the moments (past/present/future).

**Independence:** Two distinct verses fetched; Pūjyapāda's commentary is Digambara tradition. Confidence medium because the Shvetambara perspective is not independently verified from primary text.

## The astikāya distinction

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dravya?** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| **Astikāya?** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ |
| **Many pradeśas?** | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✗ (1 per atom) |
| **Pervades universe?** | (many) | (many) | ✓ (whole loka) | ✓ (whole loka) | ✓ (whole + aloka) | ✗ (point-local) |

The five astikāyas (*pañcāstikāya*) are the standard Jain group for cosmological analysis (the Ācārya Kundakunda's *Pañcāstikāyasāra* groups them). Kāla is acknowledged as the sixth dravya but excluded from this group.

## Real-time vs. conventional time

**Nishcaya-kāla (real/ultimate time):** The time-atoms themselves — the actual substrate of temporal change; imperceptible; localised at each space-point.

**Vyavahāra-kāla (conventional time):** The sequence of past/present/future; measured change; the familiar experiential time. This is a function of how substances change, not a separate substance.

## Sectarian note

The Digambara texts (Pūjyapāda, Kundakunda, Nemicandra) consistently count kāla as the sixth dravya. Some older Shvetambara sources list only five *dravyas* (treating kāla as an attribute rather than a substance) or acknowledge the six but emphasise the five-astikāya grouping. TS 5.39 is accepted by both traditions as part of the TS text, but the *kālaśca* ("time also") form of the verse has historically prompted commentary on why kāla requires a separate assertion — precisely because its non-astikāya status makes it anomalous. The core position (kāla = 6th dravya) is converged.
