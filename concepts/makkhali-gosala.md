## Gloss / Divergence map

**Makkhali Gosāla** (6th–5th c. BCE; A. L. Basham dates his death to 484 BCE) was the founder/leader of the **Ājīvika** school — one of the heterodox (*nāstika*) **śramaṇa** movements that flourished alongside early Buddhism and Jainism in the Gangetic plain. He is the historical anchor of the otherwise sparsely-documented Ājīvika tradition, and the **principal exponent of niyati-vāda** — thoroughgoing fatalism.

**The doctrine: niyati (cosmic determinism).** Gosāla held that *everything* that has happened, is happening, or will happen is **entirely preordained** by *niyati* (the "inner order/fixed destiny" of things). Crucially this includes liberation itself: every soul transmigrates through a fixed, immense number of rebirths and is freed **on a predetermined schedule regardless of conduct or effort**. Ascetic practice, ethics, and striving change nothing.

**Why this is the inverse of Jain/Buddhist karma.** Gosāla keeps the *word* karma but **strips it of all efficacy** — the exact opposite of the Jain claim that ascetic effort both stops karmic influx (saṃvara) and burns off accumulated karma (nirjarā). Where Jainism and Buddhism make liberation effort-driven, the Ājīvika makes it effort-*independent*. This is why the corpus marks `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent: karma` for the Ājīvika school.

**The Mahāvīra connection.** Per the Jain *Bhagavatī Sūtra*, Gosāla was for about six years the companion (and by Jain account, disciple) of **Mahāvīra**, before a bitter doctrinal split. The Jain and Buddhist accounts are openly hostile sectarian sources — a fact §4 requires flagging: we have **no surviving Ājīvika scripture**, only the polemics of two rival schools.
