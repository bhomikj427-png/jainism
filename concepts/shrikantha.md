## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śrīkaṇṭha** — also **Śrīkaṇṭha Śivācārya**, and in the editions also **Nīlakaṇṭha Śivācārya** — is named as the author of the ***Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya***, the Śaiva commentary on the [[brahma-sutra|Brahmasūtra]] that reads Śiva as Brahman and is the base text of [[appayya-dikshita|Appayya Dīkṣita]]'s *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā*.

That sentence is about as much as can be said without entering a dispute. **When he lived, and whether the philosophical system carried under his name is his, are both contested** — and the second question is the interesting one, because the challenger is his own commentator.

### Reading the fourth row honestly

McCrea's title says the invention of "**Śrīkaṇṭha's *Vedānta***." It does **not** say the invention of Śrīkaṇṭha, and this corpus will not upgrade it to that. The distinction is the whole thing:

- inventing a **man** — fabricating an author who never existed;
- inventing a **text** — forging a *bhāṣya* and backdating it;
- inventing a ***Vedānta*** — taking a real text by a real author and constructing, out of it, a philosophical system it did not previously constitute.

Only the third is what the title states, and it is by far the most ordinary of the three: it is what a powerful commentator does. **And the reading is now confirmed rather than inferred.** Wikipedia's summary of McCrea has Appayya *retrieving* "the obscure commentary of Śrīkaṇṭha" — a real text by a real author, merely neglected — and reinterpreting it. The commentary was **found**, not forged; what was invented was the *Vedānta* read out of it, and the audience that received it as one. [[sivadvaita]] carries the mechanism, which turns out to be exact: Appayya reads Śrīkaṇṭha's *pariṇāmavāda* as *vivartavāda*. That a popular-web source encountered in this pass had already slid from the title to "**Śrīkaṇṭha Śivācārya was a creation of Appayya Dīkṣita**" is a live specimen of the failure this project exists to prevent — the same shape as the physics-analogy slide, in a different domain. **The paper itself was still not obtained** (Springer authorization-redirect, PhilPapers 403, ResearchGate 403), so what is recorded here is a citation plus a **third party's summary of the argument** — one level better than a bare title, and one level short of having read it. Named as this node's upgrade path 1.

### The internal evidence that makes the dispute a real one

There is a concrete, checkable datum underneath: **Appayya's doctrine and Śrīkaṇṭha's do not match.** Śrīkaṇṭha's position is a *qualified* non-dualism — Śiva as supreme personal deity possessing qualities, the universe emerging from God's energy while remaining grounded in divine reality, "avoiding both pure monism and strict dualism" (Dasgupta). Appayya, on Wikipedia's account of the scholarship, "**affirms a form of pure non-dualism**," and in the *Śivādvaitanirṇaya* "recasts Śrīkaṇṭha's work."

So the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* is a commentary whose author holds a different position from the text he is commenting on — and Dasgupta says as much from the other end, describing it as "attempting to **harmonize** [Śrīkaṇṭha's positions] with Śaṅkara's monism." Harmonising is not expounding. That is the seed of the invention thesis, and it is visible without reading McCrea at all.

**For Ch 34, this is the limiting case.** That chapter asks what a commentator does; the corpus's answers so far have been *consolidate* ([[amalananda]]), *define* ([[citsukha]]), *transmit faultlessly and add nothing* ([[nrisimhasrama]]). Śrīkaṇṭha's case supplies a fourth: **a commentator can constitute the position he claims to be explaining** — and can be so effective at it that scholarship four centuries later argues about whether the position pre-existed him.

### What is not in dispute

The bibliographic core is stable across every source: a *Brahmasūtra* commentary attributed to Śrīkaṇṭha/Nīlakaṇṭha Śivācārya exists, is printed (the *Brahma Mīmāṃsā Bhāṣya*, catalogued under both names), identifies Brahman with Śiva, and is glossed by Appayya's *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* — whose completion is corroborated externally by contemporary poets and by **Appayya's own Adaiyapālam inscription of 1582**. The *commentary*'s date is thus far better fixed than the *base text*'s, which is an inversion of the usual situation and is itself part of the problem.

`term_devanagari` is **omitted**: no source consulted printed it, and §3 forbids supplying script from memory.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — **Dasgupta vol. 5** (a full "Introduction to the philosophy of Śrīkaṇṭha," 1920s); **Wikipedia's "Shiva Advaita"**, citing Duquette 2016, Chaudhuri 1962 and Ramesan 1972; **peer-reviewed citations located directly** (McCrea 2016 and Duquette 2016, back-to-back in one *JIP* issue — evidently a special issue on Appayya); and **traditional Śaiva accounts** via *Hinduism Today*. These do not derive from one another, and they **disagree**, which is the finding.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor is present but points the wrong way. Normally the commentary stabilises the base text; here the base text's date and doctrine are argued *from* the commentary, and the commentary is the better-dated object.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — of limited use for a proper name; noted only that *Śrī-kaṇṭha* and *Nīla-kaṇṭha* ("blue-throated") are both Śiva-epithets, so the two names under which the *bhāṣya* circulates are **both titles of the god**, which is worth keeping in view when weighing how firmly the author is individuated.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/`: [[appayya-dikshita]] already records the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* as "the specialised corner of a career whose centre is the *Parimala*," and offers as a live reading that it is "an exercise — a scholar demonstrating he can build any system." **That reading now has scholarly company**, and this node supplies the citation the earlier file could only gesture at.
5. **Provenance** — mixed, and weighted accordingly: two peer-reviewed *JIP* papers and an Oxford monograph at the top (**located, not read**); Dasgupta next; Wikipedia used as a *reporter* of Duquette rather than as an authority; the traditional accounts recorded as tradition, not as dating.

**CONTESTED**, and the disagreement is the finding. The date range across defensible readings runs from the 7th to the 15th century, and the deeper dispute is not about *when* but about *whose*: whether Śivādvaita is a system Appayya expounded or one he built. Confidence **medium** — the *map* of the disagreement is well sourced from four independent directions, which is what this rating covers. It is **not** a claim to have settled anything: **neither the *bhāṣya* nor McCrea's paper was read**, and every characterisation of Śrīkaṇṭha's doctrine here reaches this file through Dasgupta or through Wikipedia's report of the scholarship.
