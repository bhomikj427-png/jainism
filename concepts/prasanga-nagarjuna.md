## Gloss

**Prasaṅga** ("[unwanted] consequence"; *prasaṅgāpādana*, "fastening the consequence") is Nāgārjuna's signature argumentative method: a *reductio* that draws absurd consequences out of an opponent's **own** premises, without the Mādhyamika ever asserting an independent counter-thesis. The Mādhyamika grants the essentialist's assumptions provisionally, shows they collapse into contradiction or infinite regress, and stops there — endorsing no positive alternative.

**The "no thesis" claim** is the method's philosophical core. Confronted by Naiyāyika logicians who objected that "everything is empty" must itself be either a true (hence non-empty) thesis or a self-refuting one, Nāgārjuna replies in *Vigrahavyāvartanī* 29 (paraphrased in the sources): *"If my statement [about emptiness] were a philosophical thesis, then it would indeed be flawed; but I assert no thesis, and so the flaw is not mine."* He casts himself as a *vaitaṇḍika* — a debater who refutes rival positions while advancing none of his own. This dovetails with emptiness being itself empty: emptiness is not a meta-substance or a final position, only the absence of intrinsic nature, so claiming it is not claiming a thing exists.

**Prāsaṅgika vs. Svātantrika.** ⚠⚠ **Read the label with Batch 51's correction in hand: "Prāsaṅgika" and "Svātantrika" are *Tibetan doxographic* names of the 12th century, not Indian school-names — SEP (Hayes) says they are "not used by Indian Mādhyamikas themselves." The *argument* below is Indian and real; the *schools* are a later organising scheme. See [[candrakirti]].** Later Madhyamaka split over whether prasaṅga is *sufficient*. Buddhapālita used bare prasaṅga; Bhāvaviveka (c. 500–578) criticised this and demanded *autonomous* inferences (*svatantra-anumāna*) with premises both parties accept. Candrakīrti defended prasaṅga-only: because the Mādhyamika and the essentialist hold "two irreconcilable points of view," there is no shared object to ground an autonomous syllogism, and accepting conventional characteristics "according to their own marks" smuggles in a subtle grasping at inherent existence. Tsongkhapa's gloss: debate must be "founded on what the parties accept as valid," so one refutes opponents "in terms of what they accept," never one's own commitments.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Svatantrika–Prāsaṅgika distinction"): prasaṅga as reductio operating entirely within the opponent's framework; the Buddhapālita / Bhāvaviveka / Candrakīrti dispute with dates; Candrakīrti's "two irreconcilable points of view" and "no common ground" argument; Tsongkhapa's "what the parties accept as valid." **Signal 2** (IEP, "Nāgārjuna"): the *Vigrahavyāvartanī* "no thesis" reply quoted in paraphrase; the *vaitaṇḍika* self-characterisation; prasaṅga as borrowing "the full battery of accepted rejoinders … infinite regress, circularity and vacuous principle." The two are independent (one doxographical/Tibetan-oriented, one on Nāgārjuna's own corpus) and agree on the method and the no-thesis stance. Medium confidence: VV 29 is given in paraphrase, not fetched from a critical Sanskrit edition; the verse number rests on standard scholarly citation.
