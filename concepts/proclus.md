## Gloss / Divergence map

**Proclus** (Proklos Lycius, **412–485 CE**) was "the most authoritative philosopher of late antiquity" and the **great systematizer of Neoplatonism** — head (*scholarch*) of the **Platonic Academy in Athens** for nearly fifty years (from 437, succeeding Syrianus, to his death). He turned the [[neoplatonism|Neoplatonism]] founded by [[plotinus|Plotinus]] into a rigorous, axiomatic deductive system while defending pagan Hellenic theology against a Christianizing empire.

**The Elements of Theology — geometry of the divine.** His most famous work derives the entire emanative hierarchy as a chain of demonstrated propositions "building from the One to individual souls" (the two sources give the count as **211** and **217** — a small discrepancy left unresolved here, see §4). Its governing principle is the **triad of remaining / procession / reversion** (*monē – proodos – epistrophē*): **"every effect remains in its cause, proceeds from it, and reverts upon it."** This is Plotinus' proodos/epistrophē made into the universal three-beat rhythm of all being.

**The henads.** Proclus' signature doctrinal innovation is the **henads** — a rank of "individual ones" interposed **between the utterly transcendent One and multiplicity** ([[plotinus-one]]). Identified with the traditional Greek gods, the henads are participated divine unities that **preserve the One's absolute simplicity** (the One itself stays unparticipated and unknowable) while still enabling the cosmos to proceed and the many to participate in unity — "each henad participates in every other henad."

**Reach.** Though a pagan, Proclus "conquered Europe in the guise of an early Christian": **Pseudo-Dionysius the Areopagite** absorbed his theology wholesale into Christian mysticism, and the **Liber de Causis** (an Arabic epitome of the *Elements*) circulated through the medieval universities *as an Aristotelian work* until Aquinas exposed its Proclan source — making Proclus a hidden engine of both Islamic and Christian metaphysics.
