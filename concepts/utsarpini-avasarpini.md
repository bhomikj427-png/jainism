## Gloss / Divergence map

Utsarpiṇī and avasarpiṇī are the two halves of the Jain **wheel of worldly time (*kālacakra*)** — a beginningless, endless cycle in which the quality of life, the lifespans, the stature, and the very possibility of religion rise and fall. *Avasarpiṇī* (*ava-sṛp*, "to creep down") is the **descending** half: things get steadily worse. *Utsarpiṇī* (*ut-sṛp*, "to creep up") is the **ascending** half: things steadily improve. The two together make one complete turn of the wheel, after which it repeats — forever. There is no creation and no first moment (consistent with the Jain non-creator cosmos): time is a wheel, not a line.

Each half-cycle is divided into **six *aras*** ("spokes"). In the descending half they run from best to worst; in the ascending half the same six run in reverse, worst to best:

| ara | Sanskrit (descending order) | character | conditions |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *suṣama-suṣamā* | "happy-happy" | utopia; wish-fulfilling trees (*kalpavṛkṣa*); huge stature, immense lifespans; no religion *needed* |
| 2 | *suṣamā* | "happy" | diminished but still idyllic |
| 3 | *suṣama-duḥṣamā* | "happy-misery" | mixed; the age turns; **the first Tīrthaṅkara, Ṛṣabhanātha, appears near its end** |
| 4 | *duḥṣama-suṣamā* | "misery-happy" | the religious age — "renunciation, austerity and liberation are possible"; **the remaining 23 Tīrthaṅkaras, ending with Mahāvīra, appear here** |
| 5 | *duḥṣamā* | "misery" | **the present age**; decline; no liberation now possible, though religion survives |
| 6 | *duḥṣama-duḥṣamā* | "misery-misery" | nadir; religion practically impossible; lifespans fall to ~16–20 years |

At the bottom (end of avasarpiṇī ara 6) the wheel reverses: utsarpiṇī begins with its own *duḥṣama-duḥṣamā* and climbs back up to *suṣama-suṣamā*. **Twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras arise in each half-cycle** — in the descending half during aras 3–4, in the ascending half during the mirror-image improving aras. We are presently in the **fifth ara of an avasarpiṇī**, some millennia after Mahāvīra (the 24th Tīrthaṅkara of this descending half), in an age where the path is known but final liberation is not attainable until the wheel turns.

**Crucial scope limit — this applies only to the *karma-bhūmi*.** The rising-and-falling time cycle operates *only* in the regions of "action," **Bharata and Airāvata** (and their continental counterparts), where human moral-religious life and liberation happen. In the *bhoga-bhūmi* ("lands of enjoyment") and the rest of the vast Jain cosmos, time does not drive this fluctuation. The kālacakra is a feature of the *inhabited, karma-bearing* zones, not a universal clock — a point routinely lost when the cycle is described as "Jain cosmic time" flatly.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Avasarpiṇī" / "Kalachakra (Jainism)":** the two half-cycles; the six *aras* in order with their names and declining conditions; the kālacakra wheel; Ṛṣabhanātha in ara 3 and the other 23 Tīrthaṅkaras (through Mahāvīra) in ara 4; present age = 5th ara (*duḥṣamā*); the *kalpa-kāla* durations in *sāgaropama*/*palyopama*.

**Signal 2 — Jain teaching aggregations (jainworld, HereNow4U, encyclopedic relays):** independent confirmation of the same six-ara structure, the 24-Tīrthaṅkara-per-half-cycle rule, and the *karma-bhūmi* (Bharata/Airāvata) scope restriction; the *bhoga-bhūmi*/*karma-bhūmi* distinction comes from the broader Jain-geography material.

**Independence & confidence:** two reference streams converge on structure (2 halves × 6 aras), on the Tīrthaṅkara placements, and on the regional scope. Confidence medium: this is *cosmological-narrative* material (lifespans in palyopama, stature in miles) for which there is no single fetchable critical-edition verse the way TS Chapter 5 supplied for the dravyas; the canonical source (Jambūdvīpa-prajñapti and later compendia) was not fetched at text level. The *structure* is well-attested; the *numbers* are traditional and not independently verified here.
