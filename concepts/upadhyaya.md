## Gloss

*Upādhyāya* ("preceptor," "teacher") is the **fourth** of the five supreme beings (*pañca-parameṣṭhī*) saluted in the Ṇamokāra mantra: *Ṇamo Uvajjhāyāṇaṃ*, "I bow to the upādhyāyas." The upādhyāya is a monk whose distinguishing role is **mastery and teaching of the scriptures (*āgamas*)** — they impart the canonical texts and philosophical systems to other ascetics and to laypeople. Like the ācārya, the sādhu, and unlike the arihant and siddha, the upādhyāya is a *living, non-liberated* practitioner still on the path, fully observing the five mahāvratas. The division of labour is the point: where the **ācārya leads and disciplines** the monastic order (saṃgha), the **upādhyāya instructs** it in the texts.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Namokar Mantra" (and the broader pañca-parameṣṭhī material):** Upādhyāya = the fourth salutation; "preceptors of less advanced ascetics."

**Signal 2 — Jainsite "Pancha Parmeshthi" (Jain institutional):** Upādhyāya = a sādhu who has acquired complete knowledge of the āgamas and philosophical systems and teaches them; assigned **25 qualities** (representing the 25 scriptures studied — in one reckoning 11 Aṅga Āgamas + 12 Upāṅga + 2 further texts). Distinguished from the ācārya (36 qualities, order-head).

**Signal 3 — JCGB / JainGPT study materials:** The 25 qualities of an upādhyāya are tied to knowledge of the 11 Aṅgas and 14 Pūrvas (the scriptural divisions); confirms the scripture-teaching role across Digambara reckonings.

**Independence:** Signal 1 (encyclopedic), Signal 2 (Jain educational site), Signal 3 (study syllabus) agree on role, position, and the 25-quality count. Confidence medium: the exact enumeration of the 25 varies by source/sect; not fixed from a single critical edition.

## Position and qualities in the pañca-parameṣṭhī

| being | qualities | role |
|---|---|---|
| arihant | 12 | embodied omniscient |
| siddha | 8 | bodiless liberated soul |
| ācārya | 36 | head of the order; models + enforces conduct |
| **upādhyāya** | **25** | **teaches the āgamas** |
| sādhu | 27 | observes the mahāvratas |
| **total** | **108** | (= the number of beads on a Jain *mālā*) |

The 12 + 8 + 36 + 25 + 27 = **108** sum is itself doctrinally significant: a Jain rosary (*mālā*) has 108 beads, one per quality of the five supreme beings — so a full circuit of the beads salutes the entire pañca-parameṣṭhī attribute-by-attribute.

## ācārya vs. upādhyāya — leader vs. teacher

Both are senior monks, both still non-liberated, both at high gunasthāna. The distinction is functional, not a difference in liberation-status: the ācārya holds *authority* (ordains, governs the saṃgha, enforces discipline — acarya.md); the upādhyāya holds *learning* (transmits the canonical texts). A community needs both — one to keep the conduct, one to keep the knowledge. This pairing mirrors the broader Jain pairing of *cāritra* (conduct) and *jñāna* (knowledge) within the ratnatraya (Ch 07).

## Not a god

As with every parameṣṭhī, veneration of the upādhyāya is aspirational, not petitionary (see namokara.md): one bows to the *quality of being a scripture-keeper*, not to a deity who answers prayers.
