## Gloss / Divergence map

**Aruṇa** (Sanskrit *aruṇa*, "red, ruddy, dawn-coloured") is the **charioteer of [[surya|Sūrya]]**, the sun-god, and the **personification of the reddish glow of dawn** that heralds sunrise. The name names the phenomenon: the ruddy sky just before the sun appears *is* Aruṇa, seated at the front of the solar chariot. Brahmā appointed him charioteer "to shield all living beings from [the sun's] terrible heat" — so his cosmic function is to **moderate Sūrya's rays**, easing the world into daylight. Sūrya's chariot is drawn by **seven horses** (the seven Vedic metres / spectrum of light) with Aruṇa at the reins.

**Parentage and the egg-myth.** Aruṇa is the son of the sage **Kaśyapa** and **Vinatā**, and the **elder brother of [[garuda|Garuḍa]]**. Both were born from eggs; impatient Vinatā **broke one egg prematurely**, and the half-formed Aruṇa emerged with **only an upper body, no legs/lower half**. Angered, he **cursed his mother to 500 years of servitude** to her co-wife Kadrū (the serpent-mother) — bondage from which Garuḍa, hatching later from the second egg, would eventually free her (his amṛta-quest). Aruṇa's own sons, with his consort Śyenī, are **Sampāti and Jaṭāyu**, the mighty vultures of the Rāmāyaṇa.

**Place in the corpus.** Aruṇa anchors into the sun-deity cluster around [[surya]] (he is Sūrya's own charioteer/dawn) and the bird-attendant cluster (sibling of [[garuda]], Viṣṇu's mount) — a single myth threading the solar and Vaiṣṇava iconographies together.
