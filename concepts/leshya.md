## Gloss / Divergence map

Leśyā is **the karmic coloring of the soul** — the soul's psychic-physical complexion resulting from its association with karma-matter. It functions like a crystal: just as a crystal takes on the color of its surroundings, the soul's leśyā reflects the qualities (color, taste, smell, touch) of the karma-matter presently bonded to it. Leśyā is simultaneously a psychic state (a pattern of mental dispositions) and a physical indicator (the coloring of karma-matter associated with the soul). (Source: Wikipedia "Lesya," citing Kristi Wiley.)

**Six leśyās** — ranging from most inauspicious (black) to most auspicious (white):

| leśyā | Sanskrit | color | moral quality | path |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | kṛṣṇa | black | violent, uncompassionate, cruel | inauspicious → hell |
| 2 | nīla | blue | proud, lazy, deceitful | inauspicious → animal/plant |
| 3 | kāpota | grey | sad, jealous, unstable | inauspicious → lower human |
| 4 | tejo | red | discriminating, generous, benevolent | auspicious → human |
| 5 | padma | yellow | forgiving, austere, calm | auspicious → celestial |
| 6 | śukla | white | pure, meditative, liberated | auspicious → highest heaven or liberation |

**Primary textual source:** Uttarādhyayana Sūtra, Chapter 34 — a Śvetāmbara canonical Āgama text. The six leśyās and their moral/rebirth correlations are fully elaborated there. This is distinct from the Tattvārthasūtra, which is the anchor text for most other concepts in this project. The Tattvārthasūtra references karma-types and their effects but does not provide the leśyā colour-scheme in this form.

**Scholars cited:** Paul Dundas (dating/transmission), Kristi Wiley (color-passion relationship), Hermann Jacobi (translations). (Wikipedia "Lesya.")

**Guṇasthāna correlation** (from search-result aggregation; not in the fetched Wikipedia article):
- Kṛṣṇa/nīla/kāpota: correlate broadly with guṇasthānas 1–3 (states of false belief, vice, violence)
- Tejo/padma: correlate with practice stages (guṇasthānas 4–12)
- Śukla: correlates with guṇasthānas 13–14 (the kevalin's state; perfectly pure leśyā or its complete transcendence)

**Note on "contested" readings:** The question of whether leśyā is primarily psychic (a mental state that generates karmic color) or primarily physical (the color of karma-matter projected on the soul) is present in the tradition but Wikipedia does not flag it as a sharp scholarly dispute. Most sources treat it as both simultaneously.
