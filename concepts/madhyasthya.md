## Gloss

**Mādhyasthya** (Sanskrit, "equanimity, neutrality, middle-standing"; also *mādhyastha*) is the **fourth and culminating right sentiment (bhāvanā)** of **Tattvārtha Sūtra 7.11**: *maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni ca…* It is **tolerance — or detached unconcern — toward the insolent and ill-behaved** (*avineya*, "the unteachable / undisciplined," named in the verse).

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* glosses mādhyasthya as **"freedom from attachment and repulsion based on desire and aversion."** Its specific object is the person who is uncivil, cruel, or beyond correction: rather than meeting them with anger or hatred (which would bind karma through the passions, *kaṣāya*), the practitioner holds an even mind — neither approving nor reacting. Mādhyasthya is thus a guard on the soul against the *rāga-dveṣa* (attraction-aversion) that the incorrigible most provoke; it is internal non-attachment in its hardest case.

**Crucial distinction.** Mādhyasthya is *not* approval of bad conduct or callous indifference to suffering — the afflicted are the object of kāruṇya, not mādhyasthya. It is specifically the refusal to let the ill-behaved disturb one's equanimity or provoke ill-will.

**Cross-tradition note (parallel, not identity).** Mādhyasthya stands in the same structural slot as Buddhist **upekkhā** (equanimity, the fourth brahmavihāra) and the Gītā's **samatva** — all equanimity-ideals that guard against reactivity. But upekkhā is "rooted in insight" into *anattā*, samatva grounds detached action, and mādhyasthya guards a vow against passion-driven karmic bondage. Parallel, not identity; each near-enemy is indifference, which all three traditions are careful to exclude.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **TS 7.11 — directly fetched** (Jainworld translation): "tolerance toward the insolent and ill-behaved" as the fourth right sentiment; verse object *avineya* (the undisciplined/unteachable).
2. **Wikipedia, "Maitrī"** (search) + classical-commentary gloss: mādhyastha = "tolerance or unconcern… freedom from attachment and repulsion based on desire and aversion"; identified as the vow of internal non-attachment.
3. **Jainism Simplified, Ch.13 (Bhavanas)**: independent exposition of mādhyastha as equanimity/indifference toward the ill-behaved.

Three signals converge on definition, the *avineya* object, and the source-verse. Confidence medium: verse fetched; Sarvārthasiddhi gloss via secondary quotation.
