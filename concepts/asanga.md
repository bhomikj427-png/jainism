## Gloss / Divergence map

**Asaṅga** (fl. ~4th c. CE, traditionally ~300–370; by tradition born in Puruṣapura / Gandhāra) is, with his half-brother **Vasubandhu**, one of the two founders of the **Yogācāra** ("yoga-practice") school of Mahāyāna Buddhism — also called **Vijñānavāda** ("consciousness-doctrine") or **Cittamātra** ("mind-only"). Where Vasubandhu is the school's sharpest dialectician, Asaṅga is its **systematic architect**: the encyclopedic builder of its meditation-grounded scholastic framework.

**Role relative to Vasubandhu.** Tradition holds that Asaṅga, already a Mahāyānist, **converted his half-brother Vasubandhu** (then a Sarvāstivāda-Sautrāntika Abhidharmika) to the Great Vehicle. So the corpus's two Yogācāra anchor-authors are siblings: Asaṅga the elder founder/systematizer, Vasubandhu the younger formalizer of its key verse treatises (*Viṃśatikā*, *Triṃśikā*).

**The Maitreya question (flagged, not resolved).** Asaṅga is said to have ascended to **Tuṣita Heaven** to receive teachings from the bodhisattva **Maitreya** (or a human teacher "Maitreyanātha"). A set of foundational verse-texts — the "Five Treatises of Maitreya" (e.g. *Mahāyānasūtrālaṃkāra*, *Madhyāntavibhāga*) — is attributed sometimes to Maitreya *through* Asaṅga, sometimes to Asaṅga himself; the **Chinese and Tibetan traditions disagree** on the division. Modern scholarship is divided on whether Maitreyanātha was a historical teacher or a visionary/literary device.

**Key doctrines he systematized:** the **ālaya-vijñāna** (storehouse-consciousness) as the substratum of karmic continuity; the **trisvabhāva** (three natures); and **vijñaptimātra** (cognition-/appearance-only) — the same cluster Vasubandhu later distilled. The *Abhidharma-samuccaya* recasts Abhidharma categories on Mahāyāna foundations.
