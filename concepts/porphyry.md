## Gloss / Divergence map

**Porphyry** (Porphyrios of Tyre, **c. 234–305 CE**) was the devoted student of **[[plotinus|Plotinus]]** and the figure who made Plotinus' thought transmissible. Born in Tyre (Phoenicia), he studied under Plotinus in Rome (263–269 CE); toward the end of his life (~301 CE) he **edited Plotinus' writings into the [[neoplatonism|Enneads]] — "six books of nine treatises each" — prefaced with his *Life of Plotinus*.** Without Porphyry's editorial act there would be no organised Plotinus; he is the conduit through which Neoplatonism reached posterity.

**The Isagoge — the bridge to medieval logic.** Porphyry's most consequential work is the **Isagoge** ("Introduction"), a short primer on Aristotle's *Categories*. It "served as a standard introductory text in Byzantium, the Arabic world and in the Latin West through Boethius' translations" — "the standard textbook on logic throughout the Middle Ages." It analyses the **five predicables** (genus, species, differentia, property, accident) and, in a famous opening, **poses but declines to answer the problem of universals**: do genera and species *subsist* in reality, or are they mere concepts? — the question that became the central controversy of medieval scholasticism. This is the **Platonic-Forms question** ([[plato-forms]]) handed forward in logical dress.

**Harmonizer, polemicist, ascetic.** Philosophically Porphyry sought to **reconcile Aristotle with Platonism** (reading the *Categories* as about *linguistic expressions*, not ontology, so as to preserve Platonic metaphysics). He also wrote the 15-book **Against the Christians** (so threatening it was later burned by Christian authorities) and, like the Pythagoreans, defended **vegetarianism** in *On Abstinence from Animal Food*.
