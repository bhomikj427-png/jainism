## Gloss / Divergence map

**Epicurus** (341–270 BCE) inherited the atoms-and-void framework of Leucippus and Democritus and modified it on two decisive points: he gave atoms **weight** and he introduced the **swerve** (Latin *clinamen*, coined by Lucretius from *clīnāre*, "to incline"). Reality is exhausted by two principles — **atoms** (discrete, solid, indivisible particles below the threshold of perception) and **void** (empty space, which alone permits motion). As with Democritus, atoms are indivisible, eternal, and **qualitatively neutral**: colour, taste and the other sensory qualities are "epiphenomena of atomic combinations," not intrinsic properties; atoms differ only in shape and size (never large enough to be seen). Epicurus added the doctrine of **minimal parts** — an atom is built of a finite number of minimal expanses, distinct from a mathematical point — and (per SEP) held that the range of atomic sizes is limited, unlike Democritus's atoms of indefinitely many sizes.

**Weight — the addition to Democritus.** Democritus explained all motion by previous collisions plus inertia; Aristotle objected that this never explains why atoms move *at all*. Epicurus answered by positing **weight** as a fundamental atomic property: atoms have a natural motion in one direction — "downward" — even though the infinite void has no bottom. All atoms fall through the void at the same, very high, constant speed.

**The swerve (*clinamen* / *parenklisis*) — the signature innovation.** If all atoms fall straight down in parallel at equal speed, they would "fall straight down through the depths of the void, like drops of rain, and no collision would occur" (Lucretius). Epicurus held that atoms **swerve** — "a minute veering" at "no determinate time or place," uncaused and random. The swerve does two jobs:
- **Cosmological:** it reintroduces the collisions without which no compound body could ever form.
- **Ethical:** it "break[s] the bonds of fate" — by injecting genuine indeterminacy into the universe it leaves room for human freedom, against the strict predestination Epicurus rejected (he said it would be "better to believe in the old myths" than to be a slave to the determinism of the physicists).

**Attestation caution.** The swerve is **not found in Epicurus's own surviving writings**; it is reported by later sources — Lucretius's *De rerum natura* (Book II) and Cicero. The downward-weight doctrine is in the *Letter to Herodotus*. This second-hand transmission of the most famous Epicurean doctrine is the main reason the reading stays at medium confidence.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (SEP, "Epicurus"): atoms + void as exhaustive principles; indivisibility/eternity; atoms qualitatively neutral with sensory qualities as epiphenomena; minimal parts vs mathematical minima; weight and "downward" motion in a bottomless void; the swerve as "minute veering" at no determinate time/place, motivated by anti-determinism and the need for collisions; explicitly notes the swerve is not in Epicurus's surviving works. **Signal 2** (IEP, "Epicurus"): independently states weight is Epicurus's *addition beyond Democritus*, answering Aristotle's "why do atoms move?" objection; atoms swerve "at random times"; the swerve enables both collisions and the breaking of "the bonds of fate"; Democritus deterministic vs Epicurus indeterminist. **Signal 3** (Wikipedia, "Clinamen"): the Latin term *clinamen* is Lucretius's coinage from *clīnāre*; quotes Lucretius on the rain-drop image and the deflection "at a quite uncertain time and in uncertain places"; the two problems solved are collisions and free will.

The three are independent (a scholarly encyclopedia, a teaching encyclopedia, a reference article quoting the primary Lucretius text) and converge on every structural point. Medium, not high: Epicurus's primary texts (Letter to Herodotus; Lucretius II) were not fetched verbatim this session; the Greek term *parenklisis* is the term standardly attributed to Epicurus in the literature but was not confirmed from a fetched source, so it is given only parenthetically.
