## Gloss / Divergence map

**Asatkāryavāda** (a-sat = non-existent; kārya = effect): the causal theory that **the effect does NOT pre-exist in its material cause** — it is a genuinely new product (*ārambha*, beginning). Also called **ārambhavāda** ("beginning-theory"). The school: Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika.

The core claim: before the pot is made, there is no pot — not even a latent pot — in the clay. Production brings something new into existence. The clay does not "contain" the pot; the potter's efficient causation, acting on the clay (material cause), brings forth a genuinely novel entity.

**Why not pre-existence?** Nyāya's argument: if the pot already existed in the clay, there would be no point in making it — you could just "uncover" it; but clearly the potter *produces* something that was not there before. Production must involve genuine novelty.

**Three essential conditions for causation** (Nyāya theory): (a) antecedence (the cause precedes the effect), (b) invariability (whenever the cause is present, the effect follows), (c) unconditionality (no other factor prevents the effect). This three-condition theory requires the effect to be a new item brought into existence by the conjunction of causes.

**Contrast with satkāryavāda (Sāṃkhya-Yoga-Vedānta)**:

| feature | asatkāryavāda (Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika) | satkāryavāda (Sāṃkhya-Vedānta) |
|---|---|---|
| pre-existence | effect does NOT pre-exist | effect pre-exists in cause |
| production | genuine new creation (ārambha) | manifestation of the latent |
| pot/clay example | pot is new, not latent in clay | pot was already in clay, latently |
| causation model | efficient cause brings novelty | material cause reveals latent content |

**Note on Buddhist and Jain theories**: Buddhist *pratītyasamutpāda* is neither satkāryavāda nor asatkāryavāda — the Buddha explicitly rejected both the "world exists" (satavāda) and "world does not exist" (asatavāda) positions; dependent-arising is specifically designed to avoid this dispute. Jain soteriology does not have a single position in this debate (the Jain *sat* definition acknowledges both origination and cessation alongside permanence).
