## Gloss / Divergence map

Karma in Hindu/Vedic tradition means **action and its consequence** — a moral-causal law linking intentional acts across lifetimes. It is emphatically NOT fine physical matter. The key contrast with Jain karma (karma.md) is ontological: Jain karma is explicit physical substance; Hindu karma is a moral force, residual impression, or law of cosmic consequence.

**Historical development of Hindu karma:**

| period | dominant meaning | primary texts |
|---|---|---|
| Early Vedic (~1500–700 BCE) | ritual action (*karman* = the sacrifice and its cosmic fruit) | Ṛg Veda, Brāhmaṇas |
| Upaniṣadic (~700–300 BCE) | any action (physical/mental/verbal) and its moral consequence across lives | Bṛhadāraṇyaka, Chāndogya Upaniṣads |
| Bhagavad Gītā (~3rd–2nd c. BCE or later) | dharmic action without attachment to results; karma-yoga as a liberation path | Bhagavad Gītā (Chs. 3–5) |

**The Bhagavad Gītā's karma-yoga:** Kṛṣṇa teaches Arjuna to act according to *dharma* (duty/right order) without attachment to the fruits (*niṣkāma karma* — desireless action). Acting with ego-attachment produces binding karma; acting desirelessly, karma does not bind. The mechanism is psychological/intentional, not physical. (Source: search-result aggregation, grokipedia, hinduwebsite.)

**Saṃskāra** (residual impression): past karma leaves *saṃskāras* — subtle impressions on the soul/subtle body that condition future tendencies and experiences. These are not physical particles; they are latent dispositions. This is the closest Hindu analogue to Jain karma-matter, but they remain structurally different (intentional impressions vs. physical particles).

**What generates karma (Hindu):** Both action *and* intention — the Gītā and Upaniṣads emphasise intention as the decisive factor; accidental action generates less karma than deliberate action. This intentionality-focus is absent in Jain karma theory, where yoga (activity) itself opens the soul to influx, regardless of intention (intention = kaṣāya determines *how much* karma sticks, not whether karma flows at all).

## Critical comparison: Hindu karma vs. Jain karma

| dimension | Hindu karma | Jain karma |
|---|---|---|
| Ontological status | moral law / residual impression (*saṃskāra*) | fine physical matter (*sūkṣma pudgala*) |
| Mechanism | action → consequence via moral-causal law; no physical particles | fine particles flow into soul via yoga, adhere via kaṣāya |
| Role of intention | central (intentional action is karma-generating) | secondary (kaṣāya determines bondage; yoga causes influx regardless) |
| Can karma be removed by knowledge? | yes (in Advaita, jñāna dissolves karma) | no (karma must be physically shed via tapas/nirjarā) |
| Liberation mechanism | realisation (jñāna) OR desireless action (niṣkāma karma-yoga) | complete physical removal of karma matter |
