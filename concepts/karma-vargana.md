## Gloss / Divergence map

A *varganā* is a **class of pudgala (matter) aggregates** defined by fineness of particle — all particles of the same fineness-class constitute one varganā. Kārmaṇa-varganā (karma-class matter) is the **finest of the eight varganā types** — the pudgala so subtle that it constitutes karma-matter when it adheres to the soul (jīva).

**The eight varganās** (listed from most subtle to most gross; source: search-result aggregation from hinduwebsite.com, jainsite.com):

| # | varganā | Sanskrit | bodily function |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Kārmaṇa | kārmaṇa-varganā | forms the karma-body — the fine matter that bonds to the soul in bandha |
| 2 | Taijas | taijas-varganā | forms the tejas body — heat/energy body; provides digestive fire and aura |
| 3 | Āhāraka | āhāraka-varganā | forms the āhāraka body — subtle projection used by advanced monks to receive knowledge |
| 4 | Vaikrīya | vaikrīya-varganā | forms the vaikrīya body — transformable body of celestial/infernal beings |
| 5 | Bhāṣā | bhāṣā-varganā | matter constituting speech/language |
| 6 | Manas | mano-varganā | matter constituting thought/mind |
| 7 | Śvāsocchvāsa | śvāsocchvāsa-varganā | matter constituting breath |
| 8 | Audārika | audārika-varganā | forms the gross body of humans and animals (the visible physical body) |

**Kārmaṇa-varganā specifically:**
- The finest grade of pudgala in the cosmos
- A "cluster of innumerable kārmaṇa particles" (*kārmaṇa-varganā*) is what the soul attracts when it acts with passion (*kaṣāya*) — search-result aggregation
- When these particles adhere to the soul (via the yoga+kaṣāya mechanism), they become *karma* — i.e., karma IS kārmaṇa-varganā bonded to jīva
- These particles are the "fine" (*sūkṣma*) skandha type mentioned in karma.md and skandha.md

**Bridging physics and soteriology:** The varganā scheme connects Jain physics (the gradations of pudgala fineness) to Jain soteriology (karma as a specific fine-matter class). It explains HOW karma is physical: it is the finest class of aggregated pudgala that has a specific affinity for the soul when passions are present.

**Textual source note:** The formal 8-varganā system is elaborated in the Digambara *Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama* (an ancient Jain canonical work) and its massive commentary *Dhavala* by Vīrasena (~9th c. CE), and in Nemicandra's *Dravyasaṃgraha* (~10th c. CE). The Tattvārthasūtra mentions kārmaṇa pudgala (karma-matter) but does not systematise the 8-varganā hierarchy explicitly — this is later Digambara technical physics. The scheme is not in the Śvetāmbara Āgamic canon in this form.
