## Gloss / Divergence map

Brahmacarya is **celibacy** — the fourth of the five great vows (*mahāvratas*), added by Mahāvīra to Pārśvanātha's original four vows. It addresses sexual desire as a form of passion (*kaṣāya*) that generates karma and prevents liberation.

**TS 7.7 Sanskrit:** *strīrāgakathāśravaṇatanmanoharāṃganirīkṣaṇapūrvaratānusmaraṇavṛṣyeṣṭarasasvaśarīrasaṃskāratyāgāḥ pañca* — "Renunciation of listening to stories that incite attachment for women, looking at beautiful forms of women, recalling former sexual pleasures, delicious food stimulating amorous desire, and body adornment constitutes the five observances." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**The five observances (*bhāvanās*) for brahmacarya — all are tyāga (renunciations):**

| # | Sanskrit | English | what it addresses |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | strīrāgakathā-śravaṇa-tyāga | renouncing stories inciting attachment for women | arousal through narrative imagination |
| 2 | tanmanoharāṃga-nirīkṣaṇa-tyāga | renouncing sight of beautiful women | arousal through visual contact |
| 3 | pūrvarata-anusmaraṇa-tyāga | renouncing recollection of past sexual pleasures | arousal through memory |
| 4 | vṛṣya-iṣṭa-rasa-tyāga | renouncing stimulating food (aphrodisiac foods, rich flavours) | arousal through diet |
| 5 | sva-śarīra-saṃskāra-tyāga | renouncing body adornment | arousal through vanity/attraction |

**Note on gender framing:** TS 7.7 is formulated from the perspective of male ascetics (the default subject of Jain canonical literature). The equivalent observances for female ascetics substitute men for women. Both traditions (Digambara and Śvetāmbara) include female monastic lineages with the same brahmacarya requirements.

**Structural logic — preventing desire at the source:** The five observances do not address the act of sex itself (that is simply prohibited) but address the *inputs* and *triggers* that lead desire to arise: narrative, visual, memorial, dietary, and vain stimuli. The strategy is upstream prevention — eliminating what feeds the kaṣāya before it can develop.

**Brahmacarya and Mahāvīra:** Pārśvanātha's four vows (ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, aparigraha) did not separately enumerate brahmacarya; celibacy may have been included under aparigraha. Mahāvīra added it explicitly as the fourth distinct vow, recognising sexual desire as a powerful and independent kaṣāya requiring its own explicit framework.

**Laypeople's minor vow (*aṇuvrata*):** Fidelity within marriage; abstaining from relations with anyone other than one's spouse.
