## Gloss / Divergence map

Manas is the **mind** or **mind-faculty** — a term shared across major Indian philosophical traditions, with divergent ontological statuses and functional roles in each. It is the most common false cognate in Indian philosophy: four traditions use the same word, but they do not agree on what kind of thing manas is, whether it is part of the soul, part of matter, or a separate organ.

**Cross-tradition comparison table**

| tradition | what manas is | ontological status | function |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jain** | *mano-yoga* — mind-activity as one of the three yogas (kāya/vāk/manas) | not a sense organ (*anindriya*); a mode of soul-activity (yoga) | the mind-channel of karmic influx; inner faculty that can apprehend all object types — not limited to one sensory domain |
| **Buddhist (early)** | the 6th sense-base (*manāyatana*); one of three mind-terms alongside citta and vijñāna | conditioned, impermanent; not a substance but a process | thinking faculty linked to volition; "calculates and examines"; mind-door for dharmas as objects |
| **Buddhist (Yogācāra)** | manas-vijñāna = the **7th consciousness** (of eight) | conditioned process; characterized by self-referential clinging | clings to ālaya-vijñāna as if it were a permanent self — the psychological root of ego-illusion (*asmimāna*) |
| **Sāṃkhya** | one component of the inner organ (*antaḥkaraṇa*) alongside buddhi (intellect) and ahaṃkāra (ego) | **material** (part of evolving prakṛti, not puruṣa) | receives sensory inputs from the five cognitive senses; passes them to ahaṃkāra and buddhi for determination and judgment |
| **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika** | an atomic, internal sense organ (*indriya*) | a dravya (substance); one paramāṇu-sized, invisible organ | internal mediator between ātman and the external sense organs; one-at-a-time contact explains why we can focus attention |

**The critical false-cognate cases**

- **Sāṃkhya manas** is *material* (part of prakṛti) — yet it is the faculty of mind. Buddhist *citta* is consciousness (processual). These are not the same kind of entity despite both being called "mind." The Sāṃkhya manas functions because pure puruṣa-consciousness illuminates it; it does not itself cognize.

- **Yogācāra manas** (7th consciousness) is specifically the self-clinging faculty that mistakes the ālaya-vijñāna for a permanent self. This is far more specific than the general thinking-faculty sense of manas in early Buddhism or Sāṃkhya.

- **Jain mano-yoga** is not a sense organ at all: the Jain text notes manas is *anindriya* (non-indriya). It is the mind-channel of the three yogas, and as yoga (activity), it opens the soul to karmic influx — not a distinct entity but a mode of activity.

**The Nyāya atomicity argument**

Nyāya argues manas must be atomic (one-per-person, partless) because consciousness is serial, not parallel — we attend to one thing at a time. If manas had spatial extension and could contact multiple sense organs simultaneously, simultaneous awareness of all senses should follow. Since it does not, manas must be atomic and contact one sense organ at a time. This is the only atomic mind in the traditions covered.
