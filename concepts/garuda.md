## Gloss / Divergence map

**Garuḍa** is the eagle-being who is the **vāhana (mount) and banner-emblem of [[vishnu|Viṣṇu]]** — "primarily depicted as the mount (*vāhana*) of the Hindu god Vishnu," and he "appears on the god's banner." He is the **king of the birds**, "he who has beautiful feathers," a protector "ever vigilant" with the power to travel anywhere swiftly. His iconography is **part-eagle, part-man**: a golden body, red wings, white face, with the torso and arms of a man and the wings, beak, and talons of an eagle/vulture.

**Parentage and the amṛta-quest.** Garuḍa is the son of the sage **Kaśyapa** and **Vinatā**, and the **younger brother of [[aruna|Aruṇa]]**, the charioteer of the sun. His mother's quarrel with her co-wife **Kadrū** (mother of the **nāgas**/serpents) made Vinatā the serpents' slave; to ransom her, the nāgas demanded the **amṛta** (the nectar of immortality). In his most celebrated deed Garuḍa **storms heaven, defeats the gods including Indra, and seizes the nectar-vessel** — whereupon he becomes the eternal **enemy of all serpents**. Symbolically he "represents birth and heaven," associated with the **sun and fire**, set against the serpents' association with death and the underworld.

**Reach beyond Hinduism.** Garuḍa also figures in **Buddhist** mythology (the garuḍas as a class of great birds, perennial foes of the nāgas) and in **Jainism**, and is a **national symbol of India, Indonesia, and Thailand** — but no dedicated nodes for those exist, so the cross-tradition reach is noted in prose.

**Place in the corpus.** Garuḍa is the Vaiṣṇava counterpart to [[nandi|Nandi]] in the Śaiva household: each is the great deity's animal vāhana and emblem, integral to the cult around its god.
