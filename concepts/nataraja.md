## Gloss / Divergence map

**Naṭarāja** (Sanskrit *naṭa* "dance" + *rāja* "lord/king") is **Śiva as Lord of the Dance** — the iconographic form depicting his **Ānanda Tāṇḍava**, the cosmic "dance of bliss" that simultaneously creates and dissolves the universe. The mature form crystallised in **Cōḻa bronze** (10th–11th c.), cast by lost-wax; stone reliefs appear earlier at Ellora/Elephanta/Badami (~6th c.) and Pallava bronzes (7th–9th c.). The principal cult-site is the **Naṭarāja temple at Chidambaram** (Tamil Nadu).

**The five cosmic acts (pañcakṛtya).** Śaiva Siddhānta reads the icon as the embodiment of Śiva's five functions, **not** four — this is the doctrinally load-bearing point: **creation, preservation, destruction, concealment** (*tirobhāva* / māyā, the obscuring of souls), and **grace** (*anugraha*, liberation). The whole image is a soteriological diagram, not merely a cosmology.

**Iconography (each element doctrinal):**

| element | meaning |
|---|---|
| ring of fire (*prabhāmaṇḍala*) | the cosmos / cyclic creation-destruction |
| upper-right hand: *ḍamaru* (drum) | sound, rhythm, time — creation |
| upper-left hand: *agni* (fire) | destruction |
| lower-right hand: *abhaya mudrā* | "fear not" — protection/grace |
| raised foot | release / liberation (the soul's refuge) |
| dwarf *Apasmāra* underfoot | spiritual ignorance, trampled |

**The physics-conflation — the prime-directive flag.** In 2004 India gave **CERN** a 2 m Naṭarāja with a plaque quoting Fritjof Capra: "the metaphor of the cosmic dance ... unifies ancient mythology, religious art and modern physics." This is exactly the failure mode §0 exists to prevent. Naṭarāja's "dance" is a **Śaiva Siddhānta soteriological icon** (the pañcakṛtya, the freeing of souls from māyā); it is **not** a depiction of subatomic pair-creation/annihilation, and reading quantum field theory into it is a 20th-century analogy imposed from outside, not a meaning the Āgamas or Tamil hymns carry. The corpus therefore draws an explicit `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` edge, drawn dotted, that the map must teach.
