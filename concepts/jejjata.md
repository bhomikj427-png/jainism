## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jejjaṭa** (also **Jajjaṭa**) is the author of the ***Nirantarapadavyākhyā*** — "continuous word-by-word explanation" — one of the **earliest extant commentaries on the Carakasaṃhitā**, and separately of what is called **the oldest known commentary on Suśruta**. He is the bottom layer of the Āyurvedic commentarial tradition this corpus has been building downward into, and he is the first figure in it whose work has largely **not survived**.

**His date is contested, and every reading depends on a single claim about his teacher:**

| reading | who holds it (source) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **9th century A.D.** | *History of Indian Medicine and Ayurveda*, ch. 9 — "a pupil of Vāgbhaṭa," with Ḍalhaṇa and Candraṭa quoting him | a late Vāgbhaṭa, and a long gap before Cakrapāṇi (1040–70) |
| **7th–8th century A.D.** — "**most reliable authorities**" | *eJournal of Indian Medicine*, via Wisdomlib: "due to his possible tutorship with Vāgbhaṭa, whose treatises probably date from the seventh century" | a 7th-c. Vāgbhaṭa, and the discipleship held as *possible* rather than established |
| **6th century C.E.** | reported in the secondary literature without argument | an early Vāgbhaṭa or no Vāgbhaṭa at all |

Note what the three rows have in common: **they are not three datings of Jejjaṭa. They are three datings of Vāgbhaṭa, with Jejjaṭa attached.** The chain is *Jejjaṭa was Vāgbhaṭa's pupil → therefore Jejjaṭa is a generation after Vāgbhaṭa → therefore date Vāgbhaṭa and you have dated Jejjaṭa.* Move the anchor and the whole spread moves with it, which is exactly what has happened across three centuries of proposals.

### Why that anchor cannot bear the weight

[[vagbhata]] records that "Vāgbhaṭa" is **a name attached to a compendium before it is attached to a biography** — the *Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha*'s own colophon has one Vāgbhaṭa explaining that he bears his **grandfather's** name. So the tradition itself asserts at least two men of the name, one generation apart.

Then Jejjaṭa's dating chain reads: *fix a contested figure's date, then hang a second figure on a discipleship to him.* Both ends are soft. And one source consulted also notes an "**Abhinava Jejjaṭa of Kerala**" — a *second* figure of **this** name too, in a 2022 journal article.

So the corpus is looking at a dating argument in which **both** parties may be composite. This node does not attempt to resolve it; it records the shape of the problem, which is the honest output. It is worth setting beside [[arunadatta]], written earlier in this batch, where Gode found **three** men named Aruṇadatta and the popular literature had merged them into one polymath. **Namesake-collapse is not an occasional accident in this tradition — it is the normal condition of its early commentators**, and any date built on "X was the pupil of Y" inherits both men's identity problems.

### What survives, and what Ḍalhaṇa was carrying

The *Nirantarapadavyākhyā* is **fragmentary**: large stretches are missing — *Cikitsā* 5.71 through 23.160, *Kalpa* 1–5, and parts of *Siddhi*. What exists comes partly through **Haridatta's revised edition**, copied and published from a Madras Government Oriental Library manuscript. The Suśruta commentary is worse off: it is known chiefly because **[[dalhana|Ḍalhaṇa]] quotes it**, and so does **Candraṭa**, Vāgbhaṭa's grandson.

**This closes a hole the corpus has been carrying.** [[dalhana]] records that the *Nibandhasaṅgraha* is a "collection of compilations" preserving "the views of a large number of authors and commentators… who lived before Ḍalhaṇa" — and states as an unfilled gap that "**no source consulted names which earlier commentators the *Nibandhasaṅgraha* preserves.**" Jejjaṭa is one of them, by name, and [[gayadasa|Gayadāsa]] is another. The compilation's contents are no longer entirely anonymous.

That gives the corpus a genuinely three-layer view of one text's afterlife:

| layer | on Suśruta | condition |
|---|---|---|
| earliest | **Jejjaṭa** — the oldest known commentary | **lost**, surviving in quotation |
| middle | [[gayadasa|Gayadāsa]], *Nyāyacandrikā* | **partly extant** (the *Nidānasthāna*) |
| latest | [[dalhana|Ḍalhaṇa]], *Nibandhasaṅgraha* | **complete**, and the vehicle by which the other two partly survive |

The commentary that survives is the one that quoted the others. **Compilation is a survival strategy**, and Ḍalhaṇa's reputation as a mere compiler looks different once one notices that his compiling is the reason two earlier commentators are known at all.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — *History of Indian Medicine and Ayurveda* ch. 9 (a book-length treatment giving a table of Caraka commentators with dates); the ***eJournal of Indian Medicine***, quoted through Wisdomlib (an academic Āyurvedic-history journal, and the source of the "most reliable authorities" formulation that **disagrees** with the book); and search-level secondary summaries carrying a third date. They disagree, which is the finding.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor, and here it is the *object*: Jejjaṭa is the earliest layer of it for two of the three saṃhitās, and he is known largely through the layer above him.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — the practical Sanskrit-English dictionary has only "Jejjaṭa (जेज्जट). — Name of an author on medicine," which is a fair measure of how thin the record is. The Devanagari is taken from that entry; the *Jejjaṭa*/*Jajjaṭa* doublet is recorded as an orthographic variant of one name, not as two men.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/`: [[dalhana]] had an **explicitly recorded hole** — which commentators the *Nibandhasaṅgraha* preserves — and [[vagbhata]] had an explicitly recorded identity problem. This node fills the first and is undermined by the second, and both connections came from the sweep rather than from any source.
5. **Provenance** — moderate. The strongest item is the *eJournal of Indian Medicine*'s characterisation, and it reaches this file **through an aggregator's quotation rather than from the journal itself**. No manuscript catalogue entry was located, which is a real gap for a figure whose text survives in identified manuscripts.

**CONTESTED**, and on the axis that matters: the proposed dates span the 6th to the 9th century, and the disagreement is not about evidence for Jejjaṭa but about **which Vāgbhaṭa he is a generation after** — a question [[vagbhata]] independently records as unsettled. Confidence **medium**: the divergence is well attested from three directions and the surviving-text situation is specific (named missing chapter-ranges, a named revision, a named library). It is **not** high, because no work of Jejjaṭa's was read, no manuscript catalogue was consulted, and the "pupil of Vāgbhaṭa" claim on which every dating rests is reported by every source and argued by none.
