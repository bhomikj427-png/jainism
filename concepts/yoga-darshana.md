## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Yoga darśana** is the classical school founded on **Patañjali's Yoga Sūtra** (~350–450 CE; often dated ~400 CE), **196 sūtras** in **four pādas**: *Samādhi* (states of mind), *Sādhana* (the practical path), *Vibhūti* (the powers arising from practice), and *Kaivalya* (liberation). Its defining sūtra is **YS 1.2 — *yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ*** ("yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind-stuff"); the analysis of the five vṛttis is treated in [[citta-vritti]].

**Yoga as "Sāṃkhya with God" (*seśvara* Sāṃkhya).** Yoga shares Sāṃkhya's dualist metaphysics wholesale — plural inactive **puruṣa**, one active **prakṛti** of three guṇas, and the goal of **kaivalya** (the witness-consciousness isolated from material entanglement; puruṣa recognised as always-already free, having only *seemed* bound through *avidyā*). What Yoga adds is **Īśvara**, a special, never-bound puruṣa, and a **practical methodology** where Sāṃkhya gives only theory. Hence Yoga is the praxis-and-theistic counterpart to the Sāṃkhya of [[samkhya-karika]]: same map, an added deity and a road to walk it.

**Aṣṭāṅga — the eight limbs (YS 2.29).** The path proceeds from outer to inner: **yama** (restraints), **niyama** (observances), **āsana** (posture), **prāṇāyāma** (breath-control), **pratyāhāra** (sense-withdrawal), **dhāraṇā** (concentration), **dhyāna** (meditation), **samādhi** (absorption). The five **yamas** are *ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha*; the five **niyamas** are *śauca, santoṣa, tapas, svādhyāya, īśvara-praṇidhāna*. The final three limbs (dhāraṇā + dhyāna + samādhi) jointly constitute **saṃyama**, the integrated meditative discipline. The preparatory **kriyā-yoga** is *tapas + svādhyāya + īśvara-praṇidhāna* (YS 2.1); bondage is rooted in the five **kleśas**, with *avidyā* as the chief.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali"): date ~350–450 CE; 196 sūtras / 4 pādas (named); YS 1.2 definition quoted; Yoga as practical counterpart of Sāṃkhya sharing puruṣa/prakṛti and kaivalya, adding Īśvara → "seśvara Sāṃkhya"; the eight limbs listed; kriyā-yoga (tapas/svādhyāya/īśvara-praṇidhāna); kleśas with avidyā as root. **Signal 2** (Wikipedia, "Ashtanga (eight limbs of yoga)"): the eight limbs introduced at YS 2.29; the five yamas (ahiṃsā/satya/asteya/brahmacarya/aparigraha) and five niyamas (śauca/santoṣa/tapas/svādhyāya/īśvara-praṇidhāna); dhāraṇā+dhyāna+samādhi = saṃyama. The two articles are independent in scope (system overview vs the aṣṭāṅga in detail) and agree on every point of overlap.

Medium, not high: the Yoga Sūtra text was **not fetched verbatim** beyond the YS 1.2 phrase (already verified in [[citta-vritti]]); the eight-limb list, yama/niyama members, and dating come from encyclopedia articles rather than a critical edition (e.g. the Bryant or Āraṇya translations were not opened). The five-yama = five-Jain-mahāvrata correspondence is a genuine *terminological* identity, **not** a doctrinal one — see the NOT-equivalent links.
