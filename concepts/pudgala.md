## Gloss / Divergence map

Pudgala is **matter** — the one ajīva (non-soul) dravya that has sensory form. All other ajīva dravyas (dharma, adharma, ākāśa, kāla) are without sensory qualities; pudgala alone is *mūrta* (having form), in direct contrast to jīva which is *amūrta* (formless).

**TS 5.5 Sanskrit:** *rūpiṇaḥ pudgalāḥ* — "Things which have form constitute the matter (pudgala)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass)

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* (via Vijay K. Jain 2018) glosses *rūpa* (form): "The effect of the aggregation of colour, shape, etc., is form… colour is inseparably connected with [taste and smell]." The four sensory qualities that define pudgala:

| quality | Sanskrit |
|---|---|
| touch | sparśa |
| taste | rasa |
| smell | gandha |
| colour / visual form | varṇa |

Every pudgala — from the ultimate paramāṇu to the largest aggregate — has all four qualities (paramāṇu has one each of touch, taste, smell, colour; skandhas can have more). This is a key contrast with Vaiśeṣika, where only earth-atoms have all four; water has three, fire two, air one.

**Two divisions of pudgala** (TS 5.25, per search-result aggregation; verse not directly fetched):
- **paramāṇu** — the ultimate, indivisible unit (see `paramanu.md`)
- **skandha** — any aggregate of two or more paramāṇus (see `skandha.md`)

**Etymology** (reported in multiple web sources; not from a directly fetched critical-edition commentary): *pud* = to combine/accumulate; *gala* = to dissociate/dissolve. Pudgala is thus "that which undergoes modification by combination and dissociation" — a dynamic substance, not inert.
