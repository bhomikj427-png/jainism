## Gloss / Divergence map

***Sphoṭa*** — from *√sphuṭ*, "to burst open" — is the grammarians' answer to a problem about how a sequence of sounds can carry a single meaning. Abhyankar's definition, at first hand:

> "name given to the **radical *śabda* which communicates the meaning** to the hearers, as different from *dhvani* or the sound in ordinary experience"

The problem it solves is real and not contrived. A word is uttered over time, one sound after another; by the time the last is heard the first has ceased. **No single moment of the utterance contains the word.** Yet meaning arrives whole. The grammarians' answer: what actually bears meaning is not the succession of noises (***dhvani***) but a **single indivisible linguistic entity** that the noises *manifest* — as [[patanjali-grammarian|Patañjali]] puts it, "the sound as it initially **breaks out into the open**." The noise is the occasion; the sphoṭa is the word.

### ⚠ T2: who originated it — four answers, and they are answering different questions

The batch queued this expecting a simple yes/no on Bhartṛhari. What the sources give is a **four-way split that dissolves once you ask what exactly is being originated.**

| candidate | who says so (source) | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-Pāṇinian: Sphoṭāyana** | **IEP**: "The term '*sphoṭa*' dates back to Pāṇini's reference to '*sphoṭāyana*'." **Haradatta** (*Padamañjarī* on A 6.1.123): Sphoṭāyana "was the first advocate of the Sphoṭa theory" | ⚠ **Rejected.** See below — this is an argument from a proper name |
| **The Prātiśākhyas** | Implied by the term's appearance in Prātiśākhya *commentaries* | ⚠ **Rejected, and instructively.** See below |
| **Patañjali** | **SEP**: Patañjali "introduced the term *sphoṭa* for the **first time**," answering Kātyāyana's *avasthitā varṇāḥ*. **IEP**: "it was Patañjali who **explicitly discusses** *sphoṭa* in his *Mahābhāṣya*" | ✅ **For the term and the distinction** |
| **Bhartṛhari** | **Abhyankar**: the grammarians "who followed Pāṇini and who were **headed by Bhartṛhari**… introduced, by way of deduction from Pāṇini's grammar, an important theory" | ✅ **For the doctrine** |

⚠ **The resolution, which no single source states and which the corpus assembles from four:**

- **The *name* "Sphoṭāyana"** occurs in Pāṇini and establishes nothing.
- **The *term* *sphoṭa* and the *sphoṭa/dhvani* distinction** are Patañjali's, in the [[mahabhashya|Mahābhāṣya]], mid-2nd c. BCE.
- **The *doctrine*** — sphoṭa as an indivisible meaning-bearer and eventually as the nature of reality — is [[bhartrhari|Bhartṛhari]]'s. He also **generalised** it: Wikipedia notes he extended it "to **each** element of the utterance, *varṇa*, *pada*, and *vākya*," **unlike Patañjali's narrower application.**

**So the answer to "did Bhartṛhari invent sphoṭa?" is: he did not invent the term, and he did invent the theory.** The batch's initial framing — invention vs inheritance — was the wrong question.

### ⚠ Why it *looks* older than it is — two mechanisms, both caught

**(1) A doctrine read out of a proper name.** Pāṇini's rule A 6.1.123 (*avaṅ sphoṭāyanasya*) names a grammarian called **Sphoṭāyana**. Abhyankar's verdict is unusually blunt:

> "It is doubtful whether this Sphoṭa theory was **advocated before Pāṇini**. The word [Sphoṭāyana] has been put by Pāṇini in the rule… **only incidentally** and, in fact, **nothing can be definitely deduced from it**, although Haradatta says that [Sphoṭāyana] was the originator of the [sphoṭavāda]."

And the giveaway is in Abhyankar's other entry, which preserves the tradition's own reasoning: Haradatta holds that Sphoṭāyana was the first advocate of the theory "**and hence he was nicknamed** Sphoṭāyana." **The inference runs backwards from the name to the doctrine** — the man is supposed to have been *called* Sphoṭāyana *because* he taught sphoṭa. It is a folk etymology doing the work of evidence, and IEP repeats it as fact.

**(2) A term retrojected by commentators.** This is the subtler mechanism and the more useful finding. Abhyankar:

> "The word [*sphoṭa*] is **not actually found in the Prātiśākhya works**. However, **commentators on the Prātiśākhya works have introduced it in their explanations** of the texts which describe… production of sound" (comm. on Ṛk-Prātiśākhya XIII.4, Taittirīya-Prātiśākhya II.1).

**The Prātiśākhyas are older than Pāṇini and do not contain the word. Their commentaries do.** Anyone reading those texts the way the tradition reads them — *with* commentary, as a single object ([[vyakarana]]'s *munitraya* sense-2) — will find sphoṭa in the oldest phonetic literature and conclude it is primordial. **The antiquity is an artefact of the reading apparatus.**

⚠ **This is the batch's recurring mechanism in its most consequential form.** Elsewhere it filled a blank about a *person* ([[panini]], [[katyayana]]) or supplied a *definition* ([[karaka]], [[astadhyayi]]). Here it manufactures the apparent **antiquity of a doctrine**, which is the thing the doctrine's authority most depends on. And note the asymmetry the corpus should keep: **[[astadhyayi]] recorded that this mechanism can produce perfectly good analysis (the pratyāhāra account is correct). Here it produces a false history.** The mechanism is neutral; only checking tells you which case you have.

### The later literature: defended and attacked, and by whom

Abhyankar's *Sphoṭavāda* entry gives the shape of the debate in one line — these are treatises

> "written by the **Vaiyākaraṇas who defend and establish** the theory of Sphoṭa **and by the Naiyāyikas who criticise** the theory."

**Sphoṭa is the doctrine that divides the grammarians from the logicians.** Named participants: **Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa** (author of the *Vaiyākaraṇabhūṣaṇa*) and **Nāgeśa** (18th c.) on the grammarians' side; **[[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]]** wrote a *Sphoṭasiddhi*, as did one Bharata Miśra; **Kṛṣṇaśāstri Ārāḍe**, "a sound modern scholar of Vyākaraṇa **and Nyāya**," wrote on it in early-19th-century Benares.

⚠ **A precision that a coarse reading would lose.** SEP records that **Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa and Nāgeśabhaṭṭa "rejected Bhartṛhari's unitary model"** — while Abhyankar lists both as authors of works **defending** sphoṭa. Both are right: they defend *sphoṭa* and reject *Bhartṛhari's akhaṇḍa (partless) version* of it. **"The grammarians hold the sphoṭa theory" is true and nearly useless**; the tradition's own internal disagreement is about how many kinds of sphoṭa there are and whether the sentence-sphoṭa is genuinely partless. Abhyankar's entry duly enumerates several kinds (*varṇa-*, *pada-*, *vākya-sphoṭa*, and the *akhaṇḍa* variants).

⚠ **And [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana]]'s presence here is a corpus finding.** `mandana-mishra.md` contains no mention of sphoṭa or of grammar; the corpus held him as a Mīmāṃsaka-turned-Advaitin and rival of [[shankara]], with no indication that he wrote a monograph on the grammarians' central doctrine. **A node the corpus thought it knew turns out to have a second career.** Recorded as a follow-up rather than written up here on one line of a dictionary.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Abhyankar, *A Dictionary of Sanskrit Grammar*** (**read at first hand**): the definition; the *dhvani* contrast; the Vākyapadīya citation; the Sphoṭāyana rejection *and* Haradatta's nickname-reasoning; the Prātiśākhya retrojection with exact citations (R.Pr. XIII.4, T.Pr. II.1); the kinds of sphoṭa; the *Sphoṭavāda* genre split between defending grammarians and criticising Naiyāyikas; Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa, Nāgeśa, Maṇḍana Miśra, Bharata Miśra, Kṛṣṇaśāstri Ārāḍe.
2. **SEP, "Language and Testimony in Classical Indian Philosophy"** (fetched, independent, scholarly): Patañjali's first use; the *sphoṭa*/*dhvani* formulation ("the real sound (*śabda*) is thus the *sphoṭa*… and the quality… is part of *dhvani*"); Kātyāyana's *avasthitā varṇāḥ* as the prompt; Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa and Nāgeśabhaṭṭa against Bhartṛhari's unitary model.
3. **IEP, "Bhartrihari"** (fetched, independent): "the term '*sphoṭa*' dates back to Pāṇini's reference to '*sphoṭāyana*'" — ⚠ **counted here as the claim under test, not as corroboration**, since Abhyankar shows what that reference is worth; and, usefully, IEP's own concession that Patañjali discussed it explicitly.
4. **Wikipedia, "Bhartṛhari"** (fetched, independent): the extension to *varṇa*/*pada*/*vākya* against Patañjali's narrower use — the one source that states what Bhartṛhari actually *added*.
5. **Lexicon/morphology** (§4 signal 3): *√sphuṭ*, "to burst"; the metaphor is of the meaning *bursting open* in the hearer, which is exactly Patañjali's "breaks out into the open" and Bhartṛhari's *pratibhā*, the "flash." **The etymology constrains the reading and rules out the common rendering "sphoṭa = the eternal word" as a later metaphysical addition** rather than what the word says.

**CONTESTED** — four sources give four origins, and the disagreement is the finding. But note the shape: this is **not** an unresolvable split. **Two of the four candidates are positively refuted** on evidence Abhyankar supplies, and the remaining two turn out to be answering different questions. Confidence **medium**: the reconstruction is well-supported and the refutations are specific, but no primary text — Mahābhāṣya, Vākyapadīya, *Sphoṭasiddhi* — was read in edition or translation, and the whole account is therefore mediated.
