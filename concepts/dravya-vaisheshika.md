## Gloss / Divergence map

In Vaiśeṣika, *dravya* (substance) is **the substratum of qualities and actions** — the independent, self-subsisting entity that is the material cause of composite things. Kaṇāda's definition (reported in search-result aggregation): "dravya is an entity that possesses qualities (*guṇa*) and actions (*karma*) and is the inherent cause (*samavāyi-kāraṇa*)."

The Vaiśeṣika system posits **nine dravyas**:

| # | Sanskrit | English | atomic? | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | pṛthivī | earth | yes | four qualities: touch+taste+colour+smell |
| 2 | āp | water | yes | three qualities: touch+taste+colour |
| 3 | tejas | fire | yes | two qualities: touch+colour |
| 4 | vāyu | air | yes | one quality: touch |
| 5 | ākāśa | ether/space | no | omnipresent; medium of sound (*śabda*) |
| 6 | kāla | time | no | omnipresent; enables sequence |
| 7 | dik | direction/space | no | omnipresent; enables location |
| 8 | ātman | soul/self | no | infinite, multiple ātmans (not monist); substrate of consciousness |
| 9 | manas | mind | yes (atomic) | internal sense organ; one per soul; mediates sensory-to-conscious cognition |

(Source: search-result aggregation via Britannica, New World Encyclopedia, Wikipedia "Vaisheshika.")

**What Vaiśeṣika has that Jain lacks:** *manas* (mind as a separate atomic substance) and *dik* (direction as a substance separate from ākāśa).

**What Jain has that Vaiśeṣika lacks:** *dharma* and *adharma* (the passive media of motion and rest — the Jain system's most distinctive metaphysical invention, with no Vaiśeṣika equivalent).

**Atomic vs. non-atomic:** The four physical element-substances (pṛthivī/āp/tejas/vāyu) and manas are atomic (have paramāṇus as their ultimate units). Ākāśa, kāla, dik, and ātman are eternal non-atomic substances (omnipresent or infinite in extent).

**Definition comparison:**

| tradition | definition of dravya |
|---|---|
| Vaiśeṣika (Kaṇāda) | entity possessing qualities (*guṇa*) and actions (*karma*); inherent cause |
| Jain (TS 5.30/5.38) | entity with origination/decay/permanence (*utpāda/vyaya/dhrauvya*); has qualities (*guṇa*) and modes (*paryāya*) |

Both use the same Sanskrit term; both require a substance to have *guṇa*; but Vaiśeṣika adds *karma* (action) as a separate category from dravya, while Jain folds process-change into the substance's own paryāya (modes).
