## Gloss / Divergence map

**Cattāri ariyasaccāni** (Pali, "four noble truths"; Sanskrit: *catvāri āryasatyāni*): the **structural foundation of the Buddhist path**, formulated as the first teaching of the Buddha at Isipatana (Deer Park, Sarnath) after his awakening. Not a creed to believe, but a **diagnostic-therapeutic framework** — each truth prescribes an action: the first is to be *fully understood* (pariññeyya), the second *abandoned* (pahātabba), the third *realized* (sacchikātabba), the fourth *developed* (bhāvetabba).

**The four truths:**

| truth | Pali term | definition | action required |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | *dukkha-sacca* (truth of stress/suffering) | Birth, aging, death, sorrow, lamentation, pain, distress, despair, and not getting what is wanted; the five clinging-aggregates (*pañcupādānakkhandhā*) | To be *fully understood* (*pariññeyya*) |
| Second | *samudaya-sacca* (truth of origination) | Taṇhā (craving) that makes for further becoming — in three types: kāma-taṇhā (sensual), bhava-taṇhā (existence), vibhavataṇhā (non-existence) | To be *abandoned* (*pahātabba*) |
| Third | *nirodha-sacca* (truth of cessation) | The complete remainderless fading and cessation, renunciation, relinquishment, release, and letting go of that craving | To be *directly realized* (*sacchikātabba*) — this is nibbāna |
| Fourth | *magga-sacca* (truth of the path) | The Noble Eightfold Path (*aṭṭhaṅgika-magga*): right view, right resolve, right speech, right action, right livelihood, right effort, right mindfulness, right concentration | To be *developed / cultivated* (*bhāvetabba*) |

**The twelve aspects (tīraṇa-parivatta):** Each truth is known in three successive rounds:
1. *Sacca-ñāṇa* — direct knowledge of *what* the truth is (e.g., "this is suffering")
2. *Kicca-ñāṇa* — knowledge of the *task* it entails (e.g., "suffering is to be fully understood")
3. *Kata-ñāṇa* — knowledge that the task *has been completed* (e.g., "suffering has been fully understood")

A fully awakened arhat (*arahant*) has completed all twelve aspects. The Buddha declares this in SN 56.11: it was upon completing all three rounds of all four truths that his "vision and knowledge arose: unshakeable is my deliverance of mind. This is my last birth."

**Medical-diagnostic analogy** (used in commentarial tradition): First truth = disease; second truth = cause of disease; third truth = state of health (cure possible); fourth truth = treatment. This analogy, while useful pedagogically, should not be over-extended — the "disease" is not physical and the "cure" is non-instrumental (no agent does nibbāna; it arises when craving ceases).

**Cross-tradition comparison:**

| tradition | diagnostic framework | first cause of suffering | resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buddhist | four noble truths: dukkha → taṇhā → nirodha → magga | taṇhā (craving) | eightfold path → nibbāna |
| Jain | seven tattvas: jīva + āsrava + bandha → saṃvara + nirjarā + mokṣa | āsrava (karmic influx) via kaṣāya (passions) | saṃvara + nirjarā → mokṣa |
| Advaita Vedānta | no equivalent sequential structure; problem is avidyā throughout | avidyā (ignorance of brahman's sole reality) | jñāna (discrimination-knowledge) → mokṣa |
| Sāṃkhya-Yoga | three kinds of suffering (ādhyātmika / ādhibhautika / ādhidaivika) | puruṣa's mis-identification with prakṛti | viveka-khyāti (discrimination) → kaivalya |

The four noble truths are *unique* in their explicit action-prescription per truth and in grounding the problem in craving (*taṇhā*) rather than metaphysical ignorance. The Jain framework maps structurally (problem → cause → cessation-possible → path) but differs on both cause and mechanism.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **SN 56.11 (*Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta*)** — Access to Insight (Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu tr.): the canonical first-sermon text. Defines each truth directly. The three-round (parivatta) analysis is explicitly stated: *"As long, bhikkhus, as my vision and knowledge of these four noble truths in their three phases and twelve aspects..."* Primary canonical source.

2. **MN 141 (*Saccavibhaṅga Sutta*)** — Access to Insight (Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu tr.): Sāriputta's detailed scholastic analysis of each noble truth, including a full enumeration of the path factors. Independently sourced; confirms SN 56.11 content.

3. **Bhikkhu Bodhi, *In the Buddha's Words* (Wisdom Publications, 2005)**, pp. 75–78 (and *Connected Discourses*, pp. 1843–47): widely cited scholarly translation establishing the four-action prescription (pariññeyya / pahātabba / sacchikātabba / bhāvetabba) as canonical structure.

Independence: SN 56.11 + MN 141 are independent canonical sources (different nikāyas); Bhikkhu Bodhi is an independent scholarly authority. Signals converge. Confidence capped at medium because Bodhi's translation and the twelve-aspects analysis are cited via secondary summaries, not the printed page.
