## Gloss / Divergence map

**Sureśvara** (fl. 8th–9th c. CE — see the dating tension below) is a **direct disciple of [[shankara|Śaṅkara]]** and the most prolific writer among his immediate pupils: the tradition's **Vārttikakāra**, "meticulously and critically examining Śaṅkara's work." Tradition holds that Śaṅkara "entrusted to Sureśvara his first monastic institution, the Sringeri Sharada Peetham." His school of Advaita exegesis is the **Vārttika-prasthāna** — faithful line-by-line commentary on Śaṅkara's own bhāṣyas — one of the sub-traditions (alongside Padmapāda's) through which Advaita was transmitted.

**Works (both signals converge):** the **Naiṣkarmyasiddhi** ("The Attainment of Actionlessness"), an independent 423-verse treatise in four chapters distilling Advaita — its third chapter treats the locus and object of ignorance and analyses the mahāvākya *tat tvam asi*; plus massive **vārttikas** on Śaṅkara's Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Taittirīya Upaniṣad bhāṣyas, and minor works (Pañcīkaraṇa-vārttika, a Dakṣiṇāmūrti-stotra commentary, Manasollāsa).

**His signature doctrine — the locus of avidyā.** Where does ignorance *reside*? All parties agree Brahman is ignorance's **object** (*viṣaya*); the dispute is its **locus** (*āśraya*). [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]] (and later the Bhāmatī line) placed it in the individual **jīva**; Sureśvara held that "avidyā regarding brahman is located in **brahman**" itself, "in accordance with the Bhāṣya." He likewise held liberating knowledge to be **directly** liberating — meditation "at best a useful aid" — against Maṇḍana's requirement of meditative realisation. These two oppositions are the standing argument against identifying the two men (next paragraph).

**The Maṇḍana=Sureśvara identity question (genuinely contested; record, don't resolve).** The hagiographies say Maṇḍana Miśra, defeated in debate by Śaṅkara, converted and *became* Sureśvara. Scholarship splits: **Kuppuswami Sastri** argues they are distinct (the doctrinal differences above being the evidence — a convert who then contradicts his own earlier positions in print is possible but strained); **R. Balasubramanian disagrees**, holding "there is no conclusive evidence available to prove" their separateness. The corpus's [[mandana-mishra]] node independently records the same question as doubtful — the two files agree the identification is legend-borne and unsettled.

**A dating tension flagged honestly:** Wikipedia's own dating band for Sureśvara ("800–900 CE, a 9th-century philosopher") sits awkwardly with his direct-disciple status under a Śaṅkara now standardly dated c. 700–750 (see [[shankara]]); other reference streams simply say "8th century." The discrepancy is a symptom of how soft all these dates are, not a resolvable fact from these sources.
