## Gloss

*Parokṣajñāna* (indirect/mediated knowledge) is one of the two primary categories of Jain knowledge, comprising the first two of the five knowledge-types: *matijñāna* (sensory) and *śrutajñāna* (scriptural). Knowledge is *parokṣa* when the soul does not apprehend its object directly but requires an intermediary — the sense organs, the mind, or language and symbol. The remaining three types (avadhi, manaḥparyāya, kevala) are *pratyakṣa* (direct): the soul apprehends them without any intermediary. This division is the structural backbone of Jain epistemology.

## The parokṣa/pratyakṣa division

| Knowledge type | Direct/Indirect | Reason | Objects |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Matijñāna** | Parokṣa | Sense organs + manas mediate | All 6 substances, partial modes |
| **Śrutajñāna** | Parokṣa | Language/symbol mediates | All 6 substances, partial modes |
| **Avadhijñāna** | Pratyakṣa | Soul directly apprehends | Physical substances at a distance; limited range |
| **Manaḥparyāyajñāna** | Pratyakṣa | Soul directly apprehends another's mind | Another's mental states; limited range |
| **Kevalajñāna** | Pratyakṣa | Soul's unobstructed direct awareness | All substances, all modes, all three times |

The principle: *pratyakṣa* = the soul's direct, unmediated awareness; *parokṣa* = awareness arriving through an instrument or medium.

## The inversion: Jain vs. other traditions

This Jain usage directly inverts the common-sense assignment used by Nyāya and Dignāga:

| Tradition | Sensory perception classified as |
|---|---|
| **Jain** | Parokṣa (indirect — sense organ mediates) |
| **Nyāya** | Pratyakṣa (direct — sense-object contact is directness) |
| **Buddhist (Dignāga)** | Pratyakṣa (direct — non-conceptual, pre-verbal sensation) |

For Jain epistemology, "direct" means the soul itself directly apprehends — not through any physical or mental instrument. Clairvoyance and omniscience meet that criterion; sensory perception does not. This is a substantive ontological commitment about what counts as the subject of knowledge (the soul, not the sense organs).
