## Gloss

The "figurative" or "teleological" standpoint — the first of the seven nayas (partial valid perspectives) in Jain epistemology. Naigama-naya takes into account the **purpose or intention of something not yet accomplished**: the object described is understood in terms of its future goal, not its current state. It is the broadest naya, simultaneously considering both universal (general) and particular (specific) aspects of a thing. In the hierarchy of seven nayas, each succeeding one is "purer" (more restricted in scope) than the one before — naigama is the most inclusive standpoint.

## TS 1.33 (directly fetched) — the seven nayas

**Sanskrit:** *naigamasaṃgrahavyavahārarjusūtraśabdasamabhirūḍhaivaṃbhūtā nayāḥ* ॥ १.३३ ॥
**Translation:** "The standpoints are: figurative (naigama), generic (saṃgraha), systematic (vyavahāra), straight (ṛjusūtra), verbal (śabda), conventional (samabhirūḍha), and specific (evaṃbhūta)."

## Definition of naigama

**Pūjyapāda, Sarvārthasiddhi:** Naigama "takes into account the purpose or intention of something which is not accomplished." It refers to things by their intended outcome even when that outcome is not yet actual.

**Classic example:** A man is seen carrying an axe. Asked where he is going, he answers: "I am going to fetch a wooden measure (*prastha*)." The prastha does not yet exist — it will be carved from wood he intends to cut. The naigama standpoint accepts this description as valid because the purpose is genuine even if the object is only intended.

A second example: someone preparing ingredients is described as "cooking" although the food is not yet cooked.

## Position in the seven-naya system

| # | naya | type | focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **naigama** | **dravyārthika** | **purpose/intention — both universal and particular simultaneously** |
| 2 | saṃgraha | dravyārthika | generic/universal — groups all things under a common category |
| 3 | vyavahāra | dravyārthika | systematic/conventional — practical distinctions within the generic |
| 4 | ṛjusūtra | paryāyārthika | present moment — only current modes, no past/future |
| 5 | śabda | paryāyārthika | verbal — word-meaning as currently conventional |
| 6 | samabhirūḍha | paryāyārthika | etymological — meaning restricted to original root |
| 7 | evaṃbhūta | paryāyārthika | specific — word applies only when object is actively performing its named function |

Nayas 1–3 are dravyārthika (substance-aspect): they emphasise the permanent identity of a thing across its modifications. Nayas 4–7 are paryāyārthika (mode-aspect): they emphasise the momentary or specific state. Naigama sits at the intersection — Pūjyapāda classifies it as primarily dravyārthika, but Prabhācandra (~10th c.) notes it uniquely treats both *dharma* (universal attribute) and *dharmī* (the particular bearer) simultaneously, with one as principal (*pradhāna*) and the other as secondary (*guṇa*).

## Why naigama matters for anekāntavāda

Naigama illustrates that ordinary language regularly ascribes to things properties they have not yet actualised. This is not error — it is a legitimate *partial* perspective. Anekāntavāda's claim that all perspectives are partially valid and no single one is absolute is exemplified by naigama: the prastha-statement is true from the naigama standpoint even though it would be rejected as false from the evaṃbhūta standpoint (the specific naya, which would insist the prastha does not yet exist). Neither is the full picture; both are valid naya-assertions.
