## Gloss / Divergence map

**Balarāma** — also **Haladhara**, **Halāyudha** (both from *hala*, the **plough**), **Baladeva**, **Balabhadra**, **Saṃkarṣaṇa** — is [[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]]'s elder brother, a **farmer's god** who fights with a plough and a mace, and the figure on whom **two traditions reach opposite verdicts**. This node exists mainly for that disagreement, which is one of the sharpest in the corpus.

### Who he is in the Hindu tradition

He is "mostly described as an incarnation of **[[shesha|Śeṣa]]**, the serpent associated with Viṣṇu," while "some Vaiṣṇava traditions regard him as the **eighth avatar of Viṣṇu**," and the 12th-century *Gīta Govinda* placed him as the **ninth of ten** principal avatāras. In the *Mahābhārata* he "taught both **Duryodhana** of the Kauravas and **Bhīma** of the Pāṇḍavas the art of war with a mace," **remained neutral** in the war, and returned only to watch his two pupils fight. At Puri he stands as **Balabhadra** in the Jagannāth triad, with Jagannāth (Kṛṣṇa) and their sister Subhadrā.

**The daśāvatāra slot he occupies is contested by design.** [[avatara-vedanta]] already records the ninth position as "**Buddha/Balarāma**" — different traditions fill it differently, and the corpus notes what the choice *does*:

| ninth avatāra | effect |
|---|---|
| **the Buddha** | an act of **assimilation** — a rival tradition's founder absorbed into the Vaiṣṇava sequence. "Buddhism does not reciprocate" ([[avatara-vedanta]]) |
| **Balarāma** | an act of **consolidation** — the slot stays inside the Kṛṣṇa cycle, and the list gains no outsider |

So the variation is not a scribal accident: **the ninth slot is where the daśāvatāra list decides whether to expand outward or close inward**, and the two answers are still in circulation.

### The Jain inversion — the node's central finding

Jainism does not reject Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma; it **enrolls them**, in its own scheme of the ***triṣaṣṭi-śalākāpuruṣa***, the **63 illustrious persons** of each half-cycle ([[utsarpini-avasarpini]]): 24 [[tirthankara|tīrthaṅkaras]], 12 *cakravartins*, and nine each of **Baladeva**, **Vāsudeva (Nārāyaṇa)** and **Prati-vāsudeva** — the last a triad repeated nine times, in which a hero, his elder brother, and their adversary recur. **Kṛṣṇa is the ninth Vāsudeva; Balarāma the ninth Baladeva**, in Hemacandra's 12th-century *Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra*.

And then the verdicts diverge completely:

> **"Baladevas are gentle heroes and devout laymen, who attain liberation** corresponding to Tīrthaṅkaras. **Vāsudevas** are also devout Jain laymen and ultimately attain liberation, **but are first reborn in hell because of their violent actions.**"

| | Hindu | Jain |
|---|---|---|
| **Kṛṣṇa** | the supreme *avatāra*, God himself in the [[gita|Gītā]] | the ninth **Vāsudeva** — a devout layman who **goes to hell first**, because he killed |
| **Balarāma** | elder brother; a *partial* or disputed avatāra, ninth at best | the ninth **Baladeva** — a gentle hero who **attains liberation directly** |

**The two traditions rank the same pair of brothers in opposite order, and for exactly the reason each tradition is about.** For Vaiṣṇavism the criterion is *who is the descent of Viṣṇu*, and Kṛṣṇa wins outright. For Jainism the criterion is [[ahimsa|*ahiṃsā*]], and Kṛṣṇa's violence is a debit that must be paid in a hell-birth before liberation, while the brother who did not kill is liberated without the detour.

This is worth stating carefully, because it is easy to get wrong in both directions:

- It is **not** a Jain polemic against Kṛṣṇa. He is a *śalākāpuruṣa*, an illustrious person, a devout layman, and **he does attain liberation**. Jainism honours him and then applies its own accounting to him.
- It is **not** a mere difference of emphasis. The Jain scheme is **structurally committed** to the result: in a system where [[karma|karma is matter]] adhering by [[kashaya|passion]] and violent action, no amount of righteousness in the cause can prevent the bond. **The hell-birth is not a judgement about Kṛṣṇa; it is an entailment of the physics.** Compare the Gītā's answer to the same problem — Arjuna may kill without binding if he acts without attachment to the fruit — and the contrast becomes exact: **the Gītā locates the bondage in the intention; Jainism locates it in the act.**

That single disagreement about where karma attaches is the difference between a scripture that can command a warrior to fight and a scripture that cannot.

The Jain triad structure adds one further point the corpus should hold: the *Baladeva* is defined by **upholding non-violence while his brother does the killing**. The pair is not hero-and-sidekick but a **division of moral labour** — and the tradition rewards the half that abstains. Compare [[rama]], whom Jainism likewise reworks (Jain retellings make him a Baladeva too, and give the killing of Rāvaṇa to Lakṣmaṇa).

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Balarama"** (fetched): all five names with the *hala* etymology for Haladhara/Halāyudha; the **Śeṣa**-incarnation identification as the majority view against the eighth-avatāra minority view; the *Gīta Govinda*'s ninth-of-ten placement; the mace-teaching of Duryodhana and Bhīma and his **neutrality** in the war; plough and *gadā*; the agricultural association; the **Jagannāth triad** at Puri with Subhadrā; and the Jain placement as one of nine **Baladevas** among the *śalākāpuruṣas*, with Kṛṣṇa as *Vāsudeva*.
2. **Search aggregation on the Jain side** (Wikipedia's "Śalākāpuruṣa"; *Encyclopedia MDPI*; wisdomlib's introduction to Hemacandra's *Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra* vol. 3; "Rama in Jainism"): the **63 = 24 + 12 + 9 + 9 + 9** breakdown; the *Baladeva*/*Vāsudeva*/*Prati-vāsudeva* triad repeated nine times; **Kṛṣṇa as the ninth Vāsudeva and Balarāma as the ninth Baladeva**; **Hemacandra's 12th-century** epic as the source text; and the decisive doctrinal statement quoted above — Baladevas attain liberation, **Vāsudevas are first reborn in hell for their violent actions.**
3. **Corpus cross-check** ([[avatara-vedanta]], [[krishna]], [[shesha]], [[ahimsa]], [[karma]], [[utsarpini-avasarpini]], [[tirthankara]]): [[avatara-vedanta]] independently records the ninth slot as "**Buddha/Balarāma**" and already flags the Buddha-inclusion as assimilative and unreciprocated — written in an earlier batch, from a different source-set, and it is what turns the Balarāma variant from a curiosity into a structural observation about how the list grows. [[shesha]] supplies the incarnation claim's other end; [[karma]] and [[ahimsa]] supply the mechanism that makes the Jain verdict an entailment rather than an opinion.

**CONTESTED** — on his avatāra status (Śeṣa-incarnation vs eighth vs ninth avatāra, with the ninth slot itself disputed between him and the Buddha) and, far more substantially, between the two traditions' rankings. Confidence **medium**: signal 1 is a single encyclopedia article; signal 2 is an aggregation and **Hemacandra's text was not read** in Helen Johnson's translation, though that translation is exactly what would settle the Jain material; and no Purāṇic passage was fetched for the Hindu side. The central quotation about Baladevas and Vāsudevas is **single-sourced** and carries the node's main finding — the specific thing to verify on any upgrade.
