## Gloss

***Īryāpathika-āsrava*** ("influx along the path of movement") is the Jain doctrine of **karmic influx that does not bind** — the influx that occurs in a being who still acts but no longer has passions. It is the technical answer to the question that any karma-as-matter theory must face: *if activity itself draws karma in, how can an omniscient being who still walks, speaks and teaches avoid re-accumulating what it has just destroyed?*

The root sūtra, **fetched directly** with Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*:

> ***sakaṣāyākaṣāyayoḥ sāmparāyikaryāpathayoḥ*** — TS 6.4
> "Activity with-passions and without-passions causes influx of karmas, those that extend transmigration — *sāmparāyika* — and those that shorten transmigration — *īryāpatha* — respectively."

Pūjyapāda localises both precisely on the [[gunasthana|guṇasthāna]] ladder:

> "Influx of *sāmparāyika* karmas takes place in case of persons with-passions and who are wrong-believers… **from the first stage up to the tenth stage** of spiritual development."
> "Influx of the *īryāpatha* karmas takes place in case of saints free from passions, **from the eleventh stage till the thirteenth stage**. The *īryāpatha* karmas **do not have the power to bind with the soul**."

### The mechanism, stated exactly

[[asrava|Āsrava]] has two independent factors, and the doctrine falls straight out of separating them:

| | *yoga* (activity of body, speech, mind) | *kaṣāya* (passion) | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| guṇasthāna **1–10** | present | present | ***sāmparāyika*** — genuine [[bandha|bondage]]; saṃsāra extended |
| guṇasthāna **11–13** | present | **absent** | ***īryāpathika*** — influx occurs and **does not stick** |
| guṇasthāna **14** (*ayoga-kevalin*) | **absent** | absent | **no influx at all** |

**Yoga opens the channel; kaṣāya is the adhesive.** Remove the adhesive and matter still enters but slides off — the tradition's image is water on a lotus leaf, and the standing analogy in the karma literature is a dry ball thrown at a wall. This node exists because that mechanism is *stated* in [[asrava]] but was, until this batch, **not sourced from the sūtra** — TS 6.4 is what turns it from commentarial report into a text-grounded claim.

### What actually gets bound, and for how long

The technical description is startlingly specific. The karma bound in *īryāpathika* influx is **a single species — *sāta-vedanīya***, the pleasure-producing [[aghati-karma|aghāti]] karma — and it is "**bound in one instant and experienced in the next**," with none of the duration-bondage (*sthiti-bandha*) that mundane souls incur. So this is not "less bondage"; it is a **different kind of event**: an influx with *prakṛti* and *pradeśa* aspects but effectively **no duration and no intensity**, which is why the sūtra can say it "shortens transmigration" rather than extending it.

That the residue is *sāta*-vedanīya and not *asāta* is itself doctrinally loaded: a passionless being's remaining karmic traffic is, by construction, the pleasant kind.

### The awkward stage: eleven

Guṇasthāna 11 is *upaśānta-moha*, where the passions are **suppressed, not destroyed** — the sources are explicit that this is "only suppression or subsidence (and not annihilation) of the Karma," and it is the stage from which a soul can fall back. Yet TS 6.4's commentary already counts influx there as *īryāpathika*.

The corpus records this rather than smoothing it, because it fixes what *akaṣāya* means in the sūtra: **not "has eradicated passion" but "is not currently acting out of passion."** The criterion is operational — what is driving this act — not biographical. That reading is also what makes stage 11's reversibility intelligible: the suppressed passion can resurface, and the influx immediately becomes *sāmparāyika* again.

### Why the doctrine is structurally necessary

Without *īryāpathika* influx, Jainism would face a dilemma with no good horn:

- If activity always binds, then the *sayoga-kevalin* ([[arihant]]) rebinds karma with every sermon, and **omniscience would be self-undermining** — the teaching that liberates others would re-enslave the teacher.
- If the omniscient being does not act at all, there is **no Tīrthaṅkara preaching**, and the whole institution of the *tīrtha* (the ford, the community) has no cause.

The doctrine buys both: the arihant acts, teaches, and accumulates nothing that lasts. The [[aghati-karma|aghāti]] karmas already in place run their course; nothing new adheres. And at guṇasthāna 14 — the *ayoga-kevalin* stage, whose duration is fixed at **the time taken to utter five short syllables** — activity itself ceases, the remaining aghāti karmas are "simultaneously exhausted," and the soul becomes a [[siddha]].

This is a good place to notice how *un*-mystical the machinery is. The tradition does not say the enlightened being is beyond karma; it says the enlightened being **fails to satisfy one of the two necessary conditions for adhesion**. That is a mechanical answer to a soteriological problem, and it is characteristic of Jainism against its neighbours: compare [[jivanmukti]], where Advaita handles the same "why does the liberated body persist?" question with *prārabdha* karma — momentum already released, exhausting itself like a potter's wheel after the hand is lifted. **Same problem, two different mechanisms**: Jainism denies new adhesion, Advaita denies new relevance.

Independently corroborates signal 1's 11–13 window from the *ladder's* side rather than the *influx's* side.

**CONVERGED.** A directly-fetched root sūtra with its classical commentary, an independent encyclopedia statement of the same division, and an independent guṇasthāna account that localises the same window from the other direction. Confidence **medium** rather than high because the most interesting claims — *sāta-vedanīya* as the bound species, the one-instant bind-and-experience structure, the absence of *sthiti-bandha* — rest on signal 3 alone, whose provenance is mixed; and because the *sāmparāyika* subdivisions of TS 6.5 were not fetched.
