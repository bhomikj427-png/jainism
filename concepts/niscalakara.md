## Gloss / Divergence map

**Niścalakara** (Bengal, 12th century) wrote the ***Ratnaprabhā***, a commentary on [[cakrapanidatta|Cakrapāṇidatta]]'s *Cikitsāsaṃgraha* (the *Cakradatta*). He entered this corpus long before he had a node: **four concept files already run on his testimony** — `candrata.md`, `tisata.md`, `haricandra.md` and `jejjata.md` — and Ch 35 cites him. **He is the corpus's single most load-bearing Āyurvedic witness, and until now he was only a name inside other people's arguments.**

### ⭐ What he is *for*: a commentary that is a bibliography

Meulenbeld's judgement is the whole point of the node:

> "The former's ***Ratnaprabhā* is a very rich mine of information on the subject.**"

The "subject" is **what Cakrapāṇidatta read**. The *Ratnaprabhā* identifies roughly **fifty** works and authors that Cakrapāṇi drew on, among them **Agniveśa, Ātreya, Bhaluki, Bhela, Bhoja, Bindusāra, Candraṭa, Caraka, the *Cikitsākalikā*, Dṛḍhabala, Hārīta, Jatūkarṇa, Kharanāda, Kṛṣṇātreya, Kṣārapāṇi, Mādhavakara, Nāgārjuna, the *Navanītaka*, Parāśara, Ravigupta, the *Siddhasāra*, the *Siddhayoga*, Suśruta, Tīsaṭa, Vāgbhaṭa, Vṛddhasuśruta, Vṛddhavāgbhaṭa, Vṛddhavideha, the *Yogaśata*** — many of them works that **do not survive**.

⚠⚠ **This is the same finding the corpus made in a completely different literature in this same batch.** `vedanta-desika.md` records that **five lost Śrīvaiṣṇava and Vedāntic works survive only in Veṅkaṭanātha's quotations.** Here a Bengali physician performs the identical service for Āyurveda. **Two traditions, two centuries apart, no contact — and in both the historical record of what an earlier author read is preserved by a later commentator who was not trying to write history.** The corpus reached both halves in Batch 46 from unrelated sources.

**And note that this is the machinery behind `tisata.md`'s dating.** Tīsaṭa's terminus ad quem is that Cakrapāṇi used the *Cikitsākalikā* — a fact known **because Niścalakara listed it**. The general observation and the specific use are the same act.

### The divergence I — his date, and a chain that hangs on it

| dating | who | basis |
|---|---|---|
| **first half of the 12th century** | **D. Ch. Bhattacharyya** | reported by Meulenbeld as the working figure |
| effectively **c. 1150–1200** | **Meulenbeld's own usage** — he uses Niścalakara's acquaintance with Mādhava's *Dravyaguṇa* to establish "that it was written **before 1150–1200**" | an operating window, not a stated conclusion |

⚠ **These do not sit comfortably together**, and this file does not pretend otherwise: a scholar who reports "first half of the twelfth century" and then works with a *before 1150–1200* bracket is either hedging Bhattacharyya's figure or quietly widening it. **Which, these passages do not say.** Recorded as an unresolved tension in the source rather than averaged into a date.

⚠⚠ **And the stakes are not confined to him.** Meulenbeld states plainly that **Vijayarakṣita's date "ultimately depends on the period of time that elapsed between his death and the composition of Niścalakara's *Ratnaprabhā*."** Vijayarakṣita — author of the *Madhukośa*, the standard commentary on the *Mādhavanidāna*, and himself a witness in other chains — **is dated through this man.** The received datings of Vijayarakṣita span two centuries and are a fair sample of the field's condition:

| Vijayarakṣita | proposed by |
|---|---|
| **c. 1100** | follows from D. Ch. Bhattacharyya's dating of Niścalakara |
| **c. 1200**, later revised to the reign of Viśvarūpasena or Keśavasena | **P. V. Sharma** (two published positions) |
| **c. 1240** | **A. F. R. Hoernle** — "on insufficient grounds, which were refuted by D. Ch. Bhattacharyya" |
| **second half of the 13th c.** | **G. Halder**, who makes him a daughter's son of Keśavasena |
| **14th or 15th century** | **J. Jolly** |

**Five scholars, four centuries, one commentator's death as the hinge.** ⚠ Note that **P. V. Sharma holds two positions at different times** — the same pattern `madhva.md` records for B. N. K. Sharma, who published one date in 1962 and another in 2000. **This is now the corpus's second case of a leading Indologist changing a date in print without the earlier figure leaving circulation.**

### The divergence II — was he Vijayarakṣita's pupil?

He names **Vijayarakṣita as an *āyurvedaguru*** in the *Ratnaprabhā*'s introductory verses, and says he undertook the commentary because the interpretation of the *Cakradatta* had been obscured **since that teacher's death**.

| reading | who | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Niścalakara was **one of Vijayarakṣita's pupils** | **D. Ch. Bhattacharyya**, whose "main argument is the mention of Vijayarakṣita as an *āyurvedaguru*" | a short interval, and Vijayarakṣita c. 1100 |
| No — the verses mean he wrote "**after a considerable interval of time** had passed since Vijayarakṣita's death," during which bad explanations accumulated | **P. V. Sharma** | a long interval, and a later Vijayarakṣita |
| Sharma's reading is not what the words say | **Meulenbeld**: "Niścala's own words, however, **do not hint at Vijayarakṣita's death as an event that occurred a long time ago**," but only that his commentary will restore to the *Cakradatta* the splendour (*tvis*) lost through bad interpretations (*kubodha*) since the *guru* passed away | the short interval, on the text rather than on the inference |

**Meulenbeld reads the verse and finds it does not carry the weight Sharma puts on it.** ⚠ But note what he does *not* say: he does not endorse the discipleship either. *Āyurvedaguru* is a title of respect that a whole region's physicians could use.

⚠⚠ **And Niścalakara has a blank of his own.** He says he will restore the text by consulting other treatises and **by having recourse to the words of his *guru*** (*gurukti*) — and **"he does not inform his readers who his guru was, although the latter is repeatedly quoted and referred to."**

**That is the second unnamed authority-figure this batch has found in Āyurvedic prefatory verses.** `tisata.md`: the *Cikitsākalikā*'s opening verse **praises Tīsaṭa's father and does not name him**, and colophon-writers later filled the blank with "Vāgbhaṭa." Here a commentator quotes his teacher repeatedly and never names him. **Two authors, two centuries apart, both leave the crucial name out — and in the first case we can watch the tradition invent one.** P. V. Sharma reads Niścala's *guruparamparā* and *gurusampradāya* as the tradition established by Vijayarakṣita; **that is a plausible filling of the blank and it is exactly the operation `tisata.md` warns about.** Not adopted.

### The divergence III — Niścalakara as textual critic

The corpus already holds his best piece of work, in `haricandra.md`: some held that Bhaṭṭārakahariścandra also wrote a commentary on the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya*; **Niścalakara refuses the ascription by observing that [[vagbhata|Vāgbhaṭa]] *quotes* Haricandra**, so Haricandra cannot be commenting on him. **That is a chronological argument from citation direction, made by a 12th-century commentator** — the same method Meulenbeld uses eight centuries later, and the reason `haricandra.md` calls it "the medieval commentators doing textual criticism."

His critical habits are consistent:
- He and Śivadāsasena "**are sometimes critical of Haricandra's opinions**" — a tradition that argues with its authorities rather than only praising them.
- He records **a variant of *Cikitsā* 13.69ab "preferred by the Kāśmīras"** — regional recensions, noticed and attributed.
- He "**does not acknowledge a separate chapter on *daha***" in the *Cakradatta*, where the *Mādhavanidāna* and *Siddhayoga* have one — a witness to the state of the text he had.
- He brackets **Haricandra and Jejjaṭa** together "more than once," saying they agree on many points though not all.
- He refers "very frequently" to **Jatūkarṇa's treatise**, and quotes twenty-one verses of **Kauśika** on the influence of *nakṣatra*s.

⚠ **Note what follows for the corpus's use of him.** A witness who registers regional variants, disputes ascriptions, disagrees with his authorities and reports what a chapter-list did *not* contain is a **careful** witness — which is why four earlier nodes could safely lean on him. **His reliability is not assumed here; it is visible in what he does.**

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — G. Jan Meulenbeld, *A History of Indian Medical Literature*, vol. IIA — read at first hand** from the full OCR, archive.org `Meulenbeld-HIML`, re-downloaded this batch by the method recorded in `candrata.md`. Effectively the sole source: the ~50-source census of the *Ratnaprabhā*, the Bengal origin, the *āyurvedaguru* verses and the *tvis*/*kubodha* reading, the unnamed *guru*, the D. Ch. Bhattacharyya / P. V. Sharma dispute with Meulenbeld's own adjudication of the words, the Vijayarakṣita dating table (Bhattacharyya, Sharma twice, Hoernle, Halder, Jolly), the Rakṣitapādaḥ identification with the *pāṇḍuroga* 35–40 evidence, the *Dravyaguṇa* window, the *daha* chapter, the Kāśmīra variant, and the Jatūkarṇa and Kauśika citations.

⚠ **Single-source dependence, stated as in `tisata.md`.** This file is one scholar deep. What mitigates it here is unusually strong: **Meulenbeld is reporting a dispute in which he sides with one party against another and gives his textual reason**, and he lists five mutually incompatible datings for Vijayarakṣita rather than one. **The reader can see the disagreement, which is what §4 asks for even when the disagreement arrives through a single reporter.**

**Signal 2 — corpus-internal, and the real check.** `haricandra.md` (his refutation of the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* ascription, his criticisms of Haricandra's opinions, the Haricandra–Jejjaṭa bracketing — **all written in Batch 45 from a different section of the same work, before this node existed**), `candrata.md` and `tisata.md` (the terminus ad quem he supplies), `jejjata.md`, `cakrapanidatta.md`, `dalhana.md`. **Four nodes were already resting on him; none of them contradicts what the *Ratnaprabhā* section says, which is the closest thing to internal corroboration available for a single-source file.**

⚠ **A name-identification flagged, not made.** Meulenbeld's authority here is **"D. Ch. Bhattacharyya."** `udayana.md` and `gangesha.md` both name a **"D. C. Bhattacharya"** — author of *History of Navya-Nyāya in Mithilā*, whose Gaṅgeśa dating SEP says "has not been corrected to date." **These may well be one scholar** — a Bengali philologist working on both Navya-Nyāya and Bengali medical literature would be entirely ordinary. **This file does not assert it.** Batch 46 has logged **four** name-collisions already (Vācaspati Miśra I/II, two Harirāmas, two Vyāsatīrthas, the Jñānottama question), and the discipline that produced them applies to modern scholars too. **Recorded as a lead: if it is one man, the corpus is leaning on the same philologist in two unrelated literatures, and that is worth knowing.**

**Why contested.** His own date is given two incompatible ways within one source; whether he was Vijayarakṣita's pupil is a live dispute between two named scholars with Meulenbeld dissenting from one of them; and he never names the *guru* he repeatedly quotes. **Why medium.** The reference work read at first hand, with the disputes visible and attributed, and four earlier corpus nodes consistent with it. **Not high**: **the *Ratnaprabhā* was not read** in any form — every quotation of Niścala's own words here is Meulenbeld's paraphrase or translation; D. Ch. Bhattacharyya, P. V. Sharma, Hoernle, Halder and Jolly are all reached only through him; and the corpus has **no node for Vijayarakṣita or the *Madhukośa***, though this file shows he is a hinge. `term_devanagari` is **omitted** rather than guessed (§3).
