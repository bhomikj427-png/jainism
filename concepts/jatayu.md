## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jaṭāyu** is a demi-god in the form of a vulture (*gṛdhrarāja*, "king of vultures") in the Rāmāyaṇa, the younger son of [[aruna|Aruṇa]] (the dawn, [[surya|Sūrya]]'s charioteer) and younger brother of [[sampati|Sampāti]]. An old friend of King Daśaratha, he appears in the Araṇya-Kāṇḍa: when Rāvaṇa abducts Sītā and carries her south through the sky, the aged Jaṭāyu intercepts him and fights to free her. Rāvaṇa severs his wings and Jaṭāyu falls mortally wounded; Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa find the dying bird, who reveals that Sītā was carried **southward** before expiring. Rāma performs his funeral rites. Jaṭāyu is framed as a model of self-sacrificing dharma — a creature who dies in another's service.

**Lineage variant (recorded, not reconciled).** Encyclopedic sources flatten the genealogy to "Aruṇa and his consort Śyenī" as Jaṭāyu's parents. The Vālmīki text itself (Araṇya-Kāṇḍa 14) instead has Jaṭāyu call himself a *descendant of Śyenī* within the longer Kaśyapa → Tāmrā → Vinatā avian line, with Aruṇa as father — i.e. Śyenī as a more distant ancestress, not the immediate mother. Both claims are preserved.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Araṇya-Kāṇḍa Sarga 14** (wisdomlib translation of the primary text — highest weight): Jaṭāyu names his own lineage and his elder brother Sampāti; "born of Aruṇa."
2. **"Jatayu," Wikipedia** (citing Mani, *Puranic Encyclopedia*) — independent of the translation: younger son of Aruṇa, brother of Sampāti, friend of Daśaratha, the wing-severing fight with Rāvaṇa, and Rāma's funeral rites.
3. **wisdomlib essay on the animal kingdom in the epics** — a separate distillation corroborating the narrative.

Core narrative (the doomed fight to save Sītā, the southward revelation, the funeral rites) is stable across a primary translation and an encyclopedia-grade source → **converged**. Held at **medium**: mythological detail leans partly on derivative devotional retellings, and a genuine genealogical variant (mother vs. distant ancestress Śyenī) exists.
