## Gloss / Divergence map

In Jain ontology, karma is **fine physical matter** (sūkṣma pudgala) — the "fine" skandha type — that flows into the soul (jīva), adheres to it, and conditions all of the soul's future states: its embodiment, cognition, lifespan, and experienced pleasure/pain. Karma is not an abstract moral law or residual intention; it is literally a class of infinitesimally fine particles.

**The bondage process — five causes (TS 8.1):**

Sanskrit: *mithyādarśanāviratipramādakaṣāyayogā bandhahetavaḥ* (Vijay K. Jain 2018, directly fetched)

1. *mithyā-darśana* — wrong belief/view
2. *avirati* — non-abstinence
3. *pramāda* — negligence/carelessness
4. *kaṣāya* — passions (anger, pride, deceit, greed)
5. *yoga* — activity of mind, speech, and body

Pūjyapāda notes these operate differently by guṇasthāna stage: all five in wrong-believers; only yoga remains in advanced ascetics. These five causes open the soul to *āsrava* (influx of karma particles) which then bind (*bandha*).

**The four aspects of karma bondage (TS 8.3):**

Sanskrit: *prakṛtisthityanubhavapradeśāstadvidhayaḥ* (Vijay K. Jain 2018, directly fetched; note: *anubhava* = *anubhāga*, variant spellings in use across editions)

1. *prakṛti* — nature/species: which karma type is bonded; yoga causes nature-bondage
2. *sthiti* — duration: how long the karma persists; kaṣāya causes duration-bondage
3. *anubhava/anubhāga* — fruition/intensity: how strongly experienced when it ripens; kaṣāya also causes fruition-bondage
4. *pradeśa* — quantity: how many karma particles; yoga causes quantity-bondage

**Eight main karma types** (from Bhagavatī Sūtra classification tradition; this is the standard Jain scheme, not from TS itself):

*Ghātīya* (destructive — directly obstructs the soul's natural capacities):
| karma | Sanskrit | what it obscures |
|---|---|---|
| Knowledge-obscuring | jñānāvaraṇīya | infinite knowledge |
| Perception-obscuring | darśanāvaraṇīya | right insight/faith |
| Deluding | mohanīya | right belief and conduct |
| Obstructive | antarāya | virtuous capacities |

*Aghātīya* (non-destructive — conditions empirical existence):
| karma | Sanskrit | what it determines |
|---|---|---|
| Feeling-producing | vedanīya | pleasure/pain experience |
| Lifespan-determining | āyuh | type and duration of embodiment |
| Body-making | nāma | physical form and individuality |
| Status-determining | gotra | social position |

(Source: Deepak Bagadia, 2016, citing Bhagavatī Sūtra; confirmed in outline by Wikipedia "Karma in Jainism.")

**The soteriological arc** (five of the seven tattvas = fundamental categories):
*Āsrava* (influx) → *Bandha* (bondage) → *Saṃvara* (stoppage/blocking influx) → *Nirjarā* (erosion/shedding existing karma) → *Mokṣa* (liberation: all karma shed, jīva free)

At *kevala-jñāna* (omniscience), the four ghātīya karmas dissolve. At physical death thereafter, the four aghātīya karmas dissolve. The liberated soul (*siddha*) is entirely karma-free.

**Critical distinction — Jain karma vs. karma in other traditions:**
- **Hindu/Vedic karma**: primarily the law of intentional action and moral consequence; residual impressions (*saṃskāra*) condition future experience; not explicitly physical particles
- **Buddhist karma**: *cetanā* (intentional action) and its mental traces; the mechanism is intentional, not material; no fine-particle physics
- **Jain karma**: explicitly physical fine matter that literally flows into, adheres to, and is shed from the soul; the materialist account is unique to Jainism among major Indian traditions
