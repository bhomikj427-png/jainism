## Gloss / Divergence map

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| vivartavāda is Śaṅkara's own causal theory — Brahman appears to transform but does not really; all change is illusory appearance | Traditional Advaita presentation; many modern surveys | Śaṅkara's māyā = strict illusion of transformation; the world is unreal appearance on an unchanging substrate |
| vivartavāda is post-Śaṅkara — Śaṅkara's own texts are closer to pariṇāmavāda; it was Prakāśātman (13th c.) who formalized "appearance-only" causation as a distinct vāda | Nakamura, Hacker, Nicholson (modern scholarship) | Śaṅkara left causation under-theorised; his successors hardened it; the vivarta/pariṇāma split sharpens over centuries |

The core vivartavāda claim on either reading: the world is a vivarta — an apparent modification — of Brahman. Brahman itself is immutable; the phenomenal diversity is superimposed (adhyāsa), not a real product. The rope-snake analogy: in dim light a rope is seen as a snake; the rope never becomes a snake; similarly Brahman never becomes the world, though the world appears.

## Reasoning across signals

Three signals gathered:

1. **Wikipedia "Vivartavāda"**: explicitly states that most scholars (Nakamura, Hacker, Nicholson) deny Śaṅkara himself was a vivartavādin; Prakāśātman (13th c.) is credited with formalizing the term as a distinct vāda. However, later Advaita tradition uniformly associates vivartavāda with the school. This gives the contested status.

2. **HandWiki "Cause and effect in Advaita Vedanta"**: confirms the Brahman-as-vivarta account: the world is phenomenally different from Brahman but substantially not a new production; the distinction between real (pariṇāma) and apparent (vivarta) causation maps onto the Advaita vs Viśiṣṭādvaita divide.

3. **Pañcadaśī 5.5** (Vidyāraṇya, 14th c.) cited in secondary literature: treats vivarta as the standard Advaita causal account — the world arises from māyā as appearance on Brahman. Vidyāraṇya is a post-Śaṅkara systematiser, consistent with the Nakamura/Nicholson timeline.

Signals converge on the doctrine content (appearance-only, rope-snake analogy, relation to adhyāsa/māyā) but diverge on attribution (Śaṅkara vs post-Śaṅkara). Confidence medium; high refused because the Nakamura/Hacker point (Śaṅkara closer to pariṇāmavāda) is not independently verified from Śaṅkara's primary texts in this session.
