## Gloss / Divergence map

Sallekhanā (also *santhārā*, *samādhi-maraṇa*, *samlehnā*) is the Jain vow of **voluntarily and gradually emaciating both body and passions as death approaches** — a deliberate, fully-conscious withdrawal from food and drink undertaken when death is certain and near (terminal illness, extreme old age, unavoidable calamity), so that the final departure from the body is made in equanimity rather than in attachment or fear. The word splits as *sat* ("rightly/well") + *lekhanā* ("thinning, scouring out"): a *scouring-out* that is simultaneously physical (*kāya-sallekhanā* — the body) and spiritual (*kaṣāya-sallekhanā* — the passions). The internal scouring is the point; the bodily wasting is its outward correlate.

**TS 7.22, Sanskrit + English fetched directly (Vijay K. Jain 2018):**
> *māraṇāntikī sallekhanāṃ joṣitā*
> "The householder adopts, with a sense of contentment (*joṣitā*), the practice of dispassionately abandoning his body — *sallekhanā* — at the end of his life (*māraṇāntikī*)."

Two things are doctrinally load-bearing. (1) It is the *last item* in the chapter on the layperson's vows — the culminating, terminal observance, not one of the recurring five aṇuvratas. (2) The qualifier *joṣitā* ("with contentment / willingly relished") is doing the central ethical work: the practice is valid *only* when undertaken in calm detachment, free of *rāga* (attachment), *dveṣa* (aversion), and the desire to die.

**The contested question — is sallekhanā a form of suicide?** This is where the divergence lives:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Sallekhanā is **not** suicide; it is the spiritual perfection of a death already arriving — passion-free, gradual, reversible, publicly counselled | Jain tradition (Digambara & Śvetāmbara); *Tattvārtha Sūtra* commentators; Jain ethicists (e.g. Tukol, S. Settar) | suicide (*ātma-ghāta*) is driven by passion and uses violent means; sallekhanā removes exactly those — so the two are categorically different acts, not degrees of one |
| Sallekhanā is **a religiously-framed self-killing** and, where coerced or glorified, a social-harm and rights problem | *Nikhil Soni v. Union of India*, Rajasthan High Court (Aug 2015) — ruled it punishable under IPC §§306/309 (abetment of / attempt to suicide) | the deliberate intention to end one's own life, however calm, falls under the legal definition of suicide; the state may regulate it |
| Sallekhanā is **protected religious practice**, not suicide | Supreme Court of India (Aug 31, 2015) — *stayed* the High Court ban, restoring the practice under Arts. 25 (freedom of religion) and 21 | a centuries-old, scripturally-grounded, voluntary spiritual rite is shielded from the suicide statutes |

So the *finding is the disagreement*: within Jainism the categorisation is settled (it is the opposite of suicide); in Indian constitutional law it was litigated and reversed within months. The dispute turns entirely on whether "intent + calm + gradualness + certainty of imminent death" changes the *kind* of act, or only its manner.

**The Jain distinction, made precise:**

| feature | suicide (*ātma-ghāta*) | sallekhanā |
|---|---|---|
| driving state | passion — grief, anger, fear, delusion (*kaṣāya*) | passionless equanimity (*joṣitā*, *samādhi*) |
| means | violent, sudden — poison, weapon, drowning | gradual reduction of food/water; no instrument |
| timing | any time; flees a bearable life | only when death is *already* certain and near |
| reversibility | irreversible by design | may be set aside if the precipitating condition lifts |
| oversight | private, concealed | public vow, ascetic counsel, witnessed |
| aim | to *escape* | to *not flee* — to meet death without attachment |

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain 2018 (Motilal Banarsidass):** TS 7.22 Sanskrit + English fetched directly from WisdomLib. Critical-edition provenance for the verse and its placement as the terminal lay vow.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Sallekhana":** independent of the TS edition. Supplies the *kāya/kaṣāya* twofold division, the suicide-distinction argument, the eligibility conditions (voluntary, publicly declared, ascetically counselled, no chemicals/tools), and the full 2015 *Nikhil Soni* litigation including the Supreme Court stay.

**Independence & status:** Signal 1 (verse) and Signal 2 (doctrine + legal history) are genuinely independent and converge on *what the practice is*. They are marked **contested** not because the sources disagree on the description, but because the *evaluative* question — is it suicide? — is a live, externally-litigated dispute that the divergence table exists to map. Confidence medium: TS 7.22 verse confirmed directly; the Digambara/Śvetāmbara agreement and the legal record are from the reference relay, not primary court documents.
