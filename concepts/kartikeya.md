## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kārttikeya** — also **Skanda**, **Subrahmaṇya**, and (in Tamil) **Murukaṉ** — is the Hindu **god of war** and commander of the divine army, the second son of **Śiva** and **Pārvatī** and brother of **Gaṇeśa**. He completes the Śiva–Pārvatī household alongside his elephant-headed brother.

**Birth myth (multiple, openly variant).** The accounts do not agree — itself worth recording rather than smoothing over. In the common *Skanda Purāṇa* version, **six sparks** from Śiva are nursed by the six **Kṛttikā** maidens (the Pleiades) and fused by Pārvatī into one **six-headed** child — hence "Kārttikeya," "of the Kṛttikās." Alternative versions route Śiva's seed through **Agni** and the **Ganges** (Gaṅgā). He is created for one purpose: to slay the asura **Tārakāsura**, whom Brahmā's boon made killable only by a son of Śiva.

**Iconography.** He rides a **peacock** (Paravāṇi) and wields the **vel**, a spear that is "the embodiment of the power of Śakti." Early coins (1st–2nd c. CE) show one or six heads; the six-headed form standardised after the **Gupta** era — under whose emperors (Kumāragupta I, Skandagupta) his cult peaked.

**The North→South shift.** After the 7th c. his prominence waned in North India and intensified in the **Tamil South**, where the indigenous **Murukaṉ** — "favoured god of the Tamils" in Sangam literature (3rd–5th c. CE) — merged with the Vedic Skanda. He is patron of the Tamil language.
