## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Abhidharmakośa** ("Treasury of Abhidharma") of **Vasubandhu** (4th–5th century CE) is the most influential single compendium of [[abhidharma]] dharma-theory — the **primary-text anchor** behind the abhidharma concept file. It comes in two layers:

- the **Abhidharmakośa-kārikā** — the *root text*: **eight chapters, ~600 verses (kārikās)**, which systematically summarise the **Vaibhāṣika** (Kashmiri **Sarvāstivāda**) Abhidharma;
- the **Abhidharmakośa-bhāṣya** — Vasubandhu's own prose *auto-commentary*, which expounds the verses but repeatedly **critiques the Vaibhāṣika positions from a Sautrāntika perspective** (sometimes also defending Vaibhāṣika against Sautrāntika). This double stance — presenting orthodoxy in verse, then problematising it in prose — is what made the work both the standard textbook and a provocation; **Saṃghabhadra** wrote a lengthy defence of Sarvāstivāda orthodoxy in reply, and **Yaśomitra** a sub-commentary (*Sphuṭārthā*).

**The 75 dharmas.** The Kośa gives the classic **Sarvāstivāda catalogue of 75 dharma-types** (the count cited in [[abhidharma]]), grouped into matter (rūpa), mind (citta), mental factors (caitta), factors dissociated from mind (cittaviprayukta-saṃskāra), and the unconditioned (asaṃskṛta). The eight chapters run: (1) elements/dhātu, (2) faculties/indriya, (3) world/cosmology (with dependent origination), (4) **karma**, (5) defilements/kleśa, (6) the path and the noble persons, (7) knowledges/jñāna, (8) meditative attainments/samāpatti.

**The ninth chapter (Pudgala-pratiṣedha).** The Bhāṣya adds a ninth prose section with no corresponding verses, a sustained **refutation of the "person" (pudgala)** as a real entity — directed against the Pudgalavāda (Vātsīputrīya) Buddhists. It is a *locus classicus* of the Buddhist no-self argument (see [[anatta-buddhist]]).

**Transmission.** The Kośa became "the main source of Abhidharma … for later Mahāyāna Buddhists." It survives in Sanskrit and in **Chinese** (Paramārtha, 564–567; Xuanzang, 651–654) and **Tibetan** translations, and remains a core monastic-curriculum text in Tibetan Buddhism. (Vasubandhu is traditionally said to have later co-founded **Yogācāra** with his brother Asaṅga — but that is a separate phase of his work, not the Kośa.)

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Abhidharmakośa-bhāṣya"): Vasubandhu, 4th/5th c.; ~600 verses in 8 chapters; auto-commentary that presents Sarvāstivādin Abhidharma while frequently criticising it from a Sautrāntika standpoint; 75 factors in five groups; the eight chapter-topics; a ninth prose section refuting a permanent self; wide authority in India, Tibet, East Asia; Chinese and Tibetan translations. **Signal 2** (Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Abhidharmakośa"): same dating; the kārikā (8 ch., ~600 vv., Vaibhāṣika/Sarvāstivāda) vs the bhāṣya (prose, nine chapters, ninth refuting pudgala, Sautrāntika critique); 75 dharmas; the same chapter list; Yaśomitra's commentary and Saṃghabhadra's defence; Paramārtha (564–567) and Xuanzang (651–654) Chinese translations + Tibetan transmission.

The two are independent (a reference encyclopedia and a Buddhist-studies wiki) and agree on dating, the two-layer kārikā/bhāṣya structure, the verse count, the school positioning (Vaibhāṣika presented, Sautrāntika critique), the 75-dharma count, the chapter sequence, the ninth-chapter pudgala refutation, and the transmission history; a search aggregation independently confirmed the kārikā-vs-bhāṣya split and the ninth chapter. **Medium, not high**: the kārikās themselves were **not fetched verbatim**; Vasubandhu's exact dates and the "one Vasubandhu vs. two Vasubandhus" question remain scholarly disputes; the 75-dharma content is cross-checked against the already-written [[abhidharma]] rather than read off the text directly.
