## Gloss / Divergence map

In Advaita Vedānta, ātman is **the individual self** — but crucially, it is a self that is ultimately **identical** with Brahman (the universal consciousness, the sole ultimate reality). The apparent individuality is a product of *avidyā* (ignorance); the liberation (*mokṣa*) Advaita prescribes is the direct realisation of this identity.

**The core assertion:** *jīvātman* (the individual experiencing self) is "ultimately pure awareness, non-different from ātman/Brahman" (Wikipedia "Advaita Vedānta"). The plurality of selves we observe — the apparent multiplicity of persons — is not ultimately real; only Brahman, the One, is real.

**Key Upaniṣadic mahāvākyas (great sayings) that assert this:**

| saying | Upaniṣad | Sanskrit | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| *tat tvam asi* | Chāndogya Up. 6.8.7 | "That art thou" | the individual soul (tvam) = that Ultimate (tat = Brahman) |
| *ahaṃ brahmāsmi* | Bṛhadāraṇyaka Up. 1.4.10 | "I am Brahman" | direct first-person assertion of identity |
| *prajñānam brahma* | Aitareya Up. 3.3 | "Consciousness is Brahman" | Brahman identified as pure consciousness |
| *ayam ātmā brahma* | Māṇḍūkya Up. 1.2 | "This self is Brahman" | the particular self = the universal absolute |

**Brahman** is *nirguṇa* (without qualities) — changeless, unlimited, without multiplicity. The world's apparent diversity arises through *māyā* (cosmic illusion) and *avidyā* (individual ignorance). Neither is ultimately real.

**Śaṅkara** (~8th c. CE) is the systematic architect of Advaita Vedānta: liberation requires no accumulation or removal of karma (contra Jain soteriology) but only *jñāna* (knowledge/realisation) of the pre-existing ātman-Brahman identity.

**Internal Vedānta differences** (all use the same Upaniṣadic texts, read differently):

| school | position | key thinker |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita | ātman = Brahman, no distinction; apparent plurality is māyā | Śaṅkara (~8th c.) |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita | souls are real but are modes/attributes *of* Brahman; qualified non-duality | Rāmānuja (~11th c.) |
| Dvaita | souls and Brahman are eternally, absolutely distinct | Madhva (~13th c.) |

## The Advaita ātman vs. the Jain jīva — the key structural difference

| dimension | Advaita ātman | Jain jīva |
|---|---|---|
| Plurality | apparent only; ultimately one Brahman | real and permanent; infinitely many distinct jīvas |
| Individuality after liberation | dissolved — the individual merges into Brahman | preserved — the siddha is an eternally distinct individual at lokāgra |
| Relation to matter | the physical is ultimately illusory (māyā) | the physical (pudgala) is equally real as jīva |
| Path to liberation | *jñāna* (realisation of identity) — no karma-removal needed | complete physical removal of all karma matter |
| World's reality | not ultimately real (māyā) | fully real; six dravyas are all equally real |

The Jain tradition was explicitly aware of and rejected the monist reading. Anekāntavāda's pluralism (many real substances) is structurally incompatible with Advaita's one-substance monism.
