## Gloss / Divergence map

The Greek *atomos* ("uncuttable," from *a-* [not] + *tomos* [cut]) is the **smallest, indivisible, eternal particle of matter** proposed by Leucippus and Democritus in the 5th century BCE. The universe, on this account, consists of exactly two things: **atoms** and **void** (*kenon*). Atoms move in the void and combine to form all macroscopic things.

**Key properties of the Democritean atom:**
- **Solid and indivisible** — no internal structure, no further division possible
- **Eternal** — uncreated, indestructible; atoms neither come into being nor pass away
- **Qualitatively neutral** — atoms have NO intrinsic sensory qualities (no color, taste, smell, heat); they differ only in **shape** (*schēma*), **arrangement** (*taxis*), and **position** (*thesis*). All apparent sensory qualities (hot/cold, color, etc.) are the *result* of atomic combinations — not properties of atoms themselves
- **Infinite variety** — atoms come in infinitely many shapes (hooks, hollows, spheres, etc.) to account for all material diversity
- **A-priori argument:** division cannot proceed to infinity; therefore there must be ultimate, uncuttable units — the same infinite-regress argument used by Jain and Vaiśeṣika thinkers

(Source: search-result aggregation citing Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, philosophy.institute; Democritus fragments via secondary sources.)

## Structural comparison: Greek / Jain / Vaiśeṣika atoms

| dimension | Greek (Democritus) | Jain paramāṇu | Vaiśeṣika paramāṇu |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic sensory qualities? | NO — qualitatively neutral; only shape/size differ | YES — all have touch+taste+smell+colour (one each) | YES — tiered by type (earth=4, water=3, fire=2, air=1) |
| How many kinds? | one kind (all atoms are qualitatively uniform) | one kind | four kinds (element-types) |
| Combination mechanism | mechanical collision/entanglement in the void | snigdha/rūkṣa affinity (≥2-degree difference) | adṛṣṭa (unseen force) + God/Īśvara |
| Void/space? | YES — void is essential (atoms move in void) | NO — ākāśa enables location; dharma enables motion; no void (loka is full) | YES — ākāśa (ether) as medium; no explicit "void" |
| Soteriological role? | none — pure materialist physics | yes — karma-matter is the finest skandha | limited — linked to ritual/liberation via adṛṣṭa |
| God? | none | none | yes (later Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, Udayana) |
| A-priori argument? | yes — infinite regress impossible | yes — same structure | yes — same structure |

**The qualitative-neutrality of Greek atoms is the decisive difference** from Indian paramāṇu theories. Democritus explains apparent qualities by *combinations and arrangements* of qualitatively neutral atoms — a position closer to modern physics. Jain and Vaiśeṣika atoms carry their qualities intrinsically — a realist position.

**Independence of Greek and Indian atomism:** The scholarly consensus is that Greek and Indian atomic theories developed independently (search-result aggregation citing Tablan PhilArchive; arxiv.org paper by Roopa Narayan). The anecdote that Democritus visited India and learned from the Gymnosophists is not well-attested in reliable ancient sources. Structural similarities (infinite-regress argument, eternal indivisible units) may reflect parallel philosophical reasoning rather than direct influence.
