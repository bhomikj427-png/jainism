## Gloss / Divergence map

**Theurgy** (*theourgia*, "divine work" / "god-working") is **salvation by ritual means** — the late-Neoplatonic doctrine, systematized by **[[iamblichus|Iamblichus]]**, that the soul ascends to the divine not by contemplation alone but through **revealed ritual acts** (prayer, offering, sacrifice, symbolic *sunthēmata*). Its rationale is anthropological: because the human soul has **descended fully** into the body (with "no superior part remaining in the intelligible"), it cannot raise itself by intellect — it needs the gods to "bestow their presence." Crucially, ritual does **not compel** the impassible gods (that would be mere magic); correct practice makes the soul *apt to receive* (*epitēdeiotēs*) the divine influx. Drawing on the **Chaldean Oracles**, theurgy reordered Neoplatonism: theology now serves ritual praxis rather than contemplation serving as the primary ascent.

**The break it marks (recorded as the defining contrast).** Against **[[plotinus|Plotinus]]** and Porphyry — for whom the intellect's silent ascesis suffices — Iamblichus held contemplation *insufficient*. This is the contemplation-vs-ritual divide that splits Late Antique Neoplatonism; **[[proclus|Proclus]]** and the Athenian school took the theurgic side. (Theurgy is *analogous* to but **not identical with** ritual-efficacy doctrines in other traditions — the comparison is structural, not an equation.)
