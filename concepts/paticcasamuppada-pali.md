## Gloss / Divergence map

**Paṭicca-samuppāda** (Pali, "dependent origination / dependent co-arising"; Sanskrit cognate: *pratītyasamutpāda*): the Theravāda Pali account of **how suffering arises and how it ceases** through a chain of 12 conditional links (*nidānas*). The doctrine is not merely a cosmological theory but the explanatory mechanism connecting the second noble truth (taṇhā as the origin of dukkha) and the third noble truth (nibbāna as the cessation of the chain).

**The conditional formula (Pali canonical statement, SN 12.1):**
> *"Imasmiṃ sati idaṃ hoti; imassuppādā idaṃ uppajjati. Imasmiṃ asati idaṃ na hoti; imassa nirodhā idaṃ nirujjhati."*
> "When this exists, that comes to be; with the arising of this, that arises. When this does not exist, that does not come to be; with the ceasing of this, that ceases."

This formula has **two directions** — the *samudaya-vāra* (arising sequence, binding to saṃsāra) and the *nirodha-vāra* (cessation sequence, liberation to nibbāna). Both are stated in SN 12.1; the cessation sequence is the structural *definition* of nibbāna.

**The twelve nidānas (Pali terms):**

| # | Pali nidāna | English | three-lifetime position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **avijjā** | ignorance (of the four noble truths, of anattā) | past life |
| 2 | **saṅkhāra** | volitional formations (kammically active mental factors) | past life |
| 3 | **viññāṇa** | consciousness (rebirth-linking consciousness) | present life |
| 4 | **nāmarūpa** | name-and-form (psycho-physical organism taking shape) | present life |
| 5 | **saḷāyatana** | six sense bases (eye/ear/nose/tongue/body/mind) | present life |
| 6 | **phassa** | contact (sense organ + sense object + consciousness) | present life |
| 7 | **vedanā** | feeling-tone (pleasant / unpleasant / neutral) | present life |
| 8 | **taṇhā** | craving (kāma- / bhava- / vibhava-taṇhā) | present life |
| 9 | **upādāna** | clinging/grasping (to sensuality, views, rules, self-doctrine) | present life |
| 10 | **bhava** | becoming (the process of existence that generates a next life) | present life |
| 11 | **jāti** | birth (into a new existence; the fresh arising of the five khandhas) | future life |
| 12 | **jarāmaraṇa** | aging-and-death (plus sorrow, lamentation, pain, grief, despair) | future life |

**The three-lifetime structure (dominant Theravāda reading — Buddhaghosa, *Visuddhimagga* Ch. 17):**
Links 1-2 are "past cause"; links 3-10 are "present result + present cause"; links 11-12 are "future result." This makes the chain a description of *how kamma in one life produces rebirth in another*. Avijjā + saṅkhāra (past) produce viññāṇa through vedanā (present result); taṇhā + upādāna + bhava (present cause) produce jāti + jarāmaraṇa (future).

**Internal Theravāda debate:** Some modern scholars and monastics (Nāṇavīra Thera, Bhikkhu Bodhi acknowledges both readings) interpret the chain as describing moment-to-moment arising within a single life, not three literal lifetimes. The three-lifetime reading is the Abhidhamma orthodoxy; the moment-to-moment reading is a minority position grounded in certain sutta passages.

**Cessation chain (nirodha-vāra) — how liberation works:**
avijjā ceases → saṅkhāra cease → viññāṇa ceases → ... → taṇhā ceases → upādāna ceases → bhava ceases → jāti ceases → jarāmaraṇa ceases. The cessation of avijjā is not a mere cognitive insight but the **eradication of ignorance through the Noble Eightfold Path** (*maggaṅga*), which directly cuts the chain at its root.

**Theravāda vs. Madhyamaka (Mahāyāna) on paṭicca-samuppāda:**

| dimension | Theravāda | Mahāyāna/Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna MMK) |
|---|---|---|
| What the chain describes | How ignorance generates actual rebirth across three real lifetimes | How all phenomena arise dependently — no three-lifetime literalism required; the chain shows *śūnyatā* (emptiness of inherent existence) |
| Nibbāna vs. saṃsāra | Genuinely distinct; cessation of the chain IS nibbāna; the two are different | From ultimate standpoint, saṃsāra = nirvāṇa (MMK 25.19-20); the distinction is conventional |
| Role of avijjā | Ignorance of the four noble truths and anattā; a psychological-epistemic factor | Ignorance of śūnyatā; a deeper metaphysical misapprehension |
| Status of nidānas | Real psychological/kamma processes that cause rebirth | Dependently arisen themselves; no intrinsic nature |

This file covers the Pali-canonical Theravāda reading. The broader cross-school Sanskrit treatment is in `pratityasamutpada.md`.
