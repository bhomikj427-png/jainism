## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pañcamahābhūta** (pañca = five; mahā = great; bhūta = that which exists): the five great elements that constitute the material world in Hindu and Āyurvedic thought. Sanskrit names:

| element | Sanskrit | associated sensory quality |
|---|---|---|
| ether/space | ākāśa | sound (*śabda*) |
| air/wind | vāyu | touch (*sparśa*) |
| fire | agni / tejas | sight/form (*rūpa*) |
| water | ap / jala | taste (*rasa*) |
| earth | pṛthvī | smell (*gandha*) |

**Hindu origin sequence.** The Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.1–2.5 gives a sequential emergence: *"From this very self did ākāśa come into being; from ākāśa, vāyu; from vāyu, agni; from agni, ap; from ap, pṛthvī."* In Sāṃkhya, the five arise from **tanmātrās** (subtle, undifferentiated essences: sound/touch/form/taste/smell), which in turn evolve from **ahaṃkāra** (ego/self-principle) out of **prakṛti**. The mahābhūtas are the grossest level of prakṛti's evolution.

**Key cross-tradition divergence — how many elements?**

| tradition | number of "great elements" | key difference |
|---|---|---|
| Hindu (Sāṃkhya, Vaiśeṣika, Āyurveda) | 5 | ākāśa (space) is primary |
| Buddhist (Abhidharma) | 4 | ākāśa is *secondary/derived* (*upādā-rūpa*); earth/water/fire/air are primary (Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta, MN 140) |
| Greek (Empedocles, Aristotle) | 4 | fire/air/water/earth; no space element; different philosophical function |
| Jain | different framework | 5 body-types (earth-body, water-body, fire-body, air-body, plant-body jīvas) ≠ mahābhūtas; jīvas inhabiting elements, not elements as substances |

The Buddhist four-element system (MN 140: *Dhātuvibhaṅga Sutta* — "element-analysis discourse") uses earth/water/fire/air as *characteristics of matter* (hardness/fluidity/heat/motion), not as substances in the Sāṃkhya sense.

**Not to be conflated with atomism**: the mahābhūtas are the *types* of matter, not fundamental indivisible units. The Vaiśeṣika paramāṇu (atom) is a separate, lower-level concept: paramāṇus come in four kinds (one per mahābhūta, excluding ākāśa which is non-atomic in Vaiśeṣika).
