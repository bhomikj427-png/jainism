## Gloss

The *Viṣṇusahasranāma* ("Thousand Names of Viṣṇu") is a Sanskrit devotional hymn (*stotra*) of 108 verses containing 1,000 names of Viṣṇu. It appears in the *Anuśāsanaparva* of the Mahābhārata (chapter 149, verses 14–120), where the dying patriarch Bhīṣma, lying on a bed of arrows at Kurukṣetra, recites the names in response to Yudhiṣṭhira's question about the highest refuge. It is the most widely recited of all Vaiṣṇava hymns; the term "the Sahasranāma" in Hindu usage typically refers specifically to this text, not to the many other deity-sahasranāmas.

**Structure**: 108 verses of names + a *phalāśruti* (fruit-of-recitation) section enumerating soteriological benefits. Each name is a grammatical compound functioning as an epithet identifying one attribute, function, or cosmic role of Viṣṇu. Examples bearing philosophical weight: *Dharmadhyakṣa* (overseer of dharma), *Bhavanah* (cause of all existence), *Vidhātā* (dispenser of karma-fruits), *Apramattaḥ* (the vigilant).

**Commentarial tradition and inter-school dispute**: Multiple traditions produced commentaries using the same text as a proof-base for incompatible metaphysics:

| commentator | school | reading |
|---|---|---|
| Śaṅkarācārya (~8th c.) | Advaita Vedānta | Viṣṇu = Brahman itself (nirguna); names referencing Śiva (e.g., name #27, #114) are *non-different* qualities — a Smārta non-sectarian synthesis |
| Parāśara Bhaṭṭar (~12th c.) | Viśiṣṭādvaita | each name describes a quality of the Supreme Person who has souls and matter as his body; explicitly Vaiṣṇava-sectarian |
| Vidyādhirāja Tīrtha | Dvaita | names affirm Viṣṇu's utter distinctness from jīvas and matter; five eternal differences intact |
| Baladeva Vidyābhūṣaṇa | Acintya-bhedābheda | names establish simultaneous difference-and-non-difference between Viṣṇu and everything else |

The text's **cross-sectarian reach** is unusual: Śaṅkara's commentary made it acceptable to Smārtas who revere Śiva equally; Vaiṣṇavas regard it as their supreme stotra; Sikh communities include a version in the *Sundar Gutka*.
