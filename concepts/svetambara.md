## Gloss

*Śvetāmbara* ("white-clad," *śveta* = white + *ambara* = garment) is the second major Jain sect, defined against the Digambara ("sky-clad") on a cluster of points that all trace back to **whether the monastic vow of non-possession requires literal nudity**. Śvetāmbara monks and nuns wear **simple white robes**, holding that minimal clothing does not compromise renunciation. From this flow the sect's other characteristic positions: **women *can* attain liberation in a female body** (*strī-mokṣa* affirmed), the **scriptural Āgama canon is preserved and authoritative** (fixed at councils, not lost), an **omniscient being still takes food** (*kevali-bhukti* affirmed), and the **19th tīrthaṅkara, Mallinātha, was a woman** — Mallī Devī — held up as the doctrinal proof that female liberation is possible. The Śvetāmbara is the larger sect in northern and western India (Gujarat, Rajasthan).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Digambara" (comparison table) + Śvetāmbara material:** White garments permitted; women can achieve liberation; the Āgama canon preserved; Mallinātha female. These are the mirror-image of the five Digambara positions (digambara.md).

**Signal 2 — Historical sources (Britannica, First-Council material):** Sthūlabhadra, leader of the monks who stayed in Magadha during the famine, permitted white robes amid hardship and presided over the **First Council at Pāṭaliputra (~300 BCE)**, which organized the canon into **12 Aṅgas**; a later redaction at **Valabhī (~5th c. CE, under Devarddhi-gaṇi)** fixed the surviving Śvetāmbara canon (traditionally 45 texts). The Digambaras reject this canon as a post-loss reconstruction.

**Signal 3 — Gender-studies / Jainpedia sources:** Confirm *strī-mukti* affirmation as the Śvetāmbara hallmark, and Mallī/Mallinātha as the test case — female for Śvetāmbara, male for Digambara.

**Independence:** Encyclopedic, historical, and gender-studies sources converge. Confidence medium: as with digambara.md, the schism's *cause and direction* are contested (each sect claims the other split off), and the canon-history is traditional reconstruction.

## The five differences, from the Śvetāmbara side

| issue | Śvetāmbara | Digambara |
|---|---|---|
| monastic dress | **white robes** permitted | nudity for the advanced muni |
| women's liberation (*strī-mokṣa*) | **affirmed** — women liberate in this birth | denied — must be reborn male |
| scriptural canon | **Āgamas preserved** (councils; Valabhī redaction) | original Pūrvas lost; this canon rejected |
| omniscient's eating (*kevali-bhukti*) | a kevalin **still eats** | a kevalin does not eat |
| Mallinātha (19th tīrthaṅkara) | **female** (Mallī Devī) | male |

The internal logic mirrors the Digambara one but starts from a *softer* reading of aparigraha: if minimal clothing is a *protection of equanimity* rather than a *possession in the binding sense*, then robes are permissible, a clothed woman can complete the path, and the doctrine of a still-eating kevalin is unproblematic. The single fork — *is a garment a binding possession?* — propagates into all five differences.

## Two further Śvetāmbara sub-divisions (orientation only)

Within Śvetāmbara, later movements split over **image-worship**: the **Mūrtipūjaka** (idol-worshipping, temple-based) majority, versus the **Sthānakavāsī** and **Terāpanthī** reform movements (~15th–18th c.) that reject image-worship and emphasize the mouth-cloth (*muhpattī*) and aniconic meditation. These are noted here only to place Śvetāmbara on the map; they are not separate concept files in this batch.
