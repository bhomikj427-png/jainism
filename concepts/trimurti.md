## Gloss

*Trimūrti* ("three-formed") is the Purāṇic framework assigning the three cosmic functions of creation, maintenance, and dissolution to three distinct deities: **Brahmā** (creation), **Viṣṇu** (preservation), **Śiva** (dissolution). Viṣṇu Purāṇa 1.2.66 is among the earliest clear textual articulations of this triad; the Maitri Upaniṣad and Kūrma Purāṇa develop the concept further. Historian A.L. Basham notes that Brahmā is rarely accorded supreme status comparable to Viṣṇu or Śiva — the Trimūrti is a **later theological synthesis** (Purāṇic period, 4th–12th c. CE), not an archaic Vedic doctrine.

**The key structural point**: the Trimūrti formula provides a *functional* division (creation/preservation/dissolution), not a metaphysical hierarchy. But each major devotional school reads the formula through its own sectarian lens — and most do not accept it as their primary framework:

| tradition | stance on Trimūrti |
|---|---|
| Vaiṣṇavism | *generally rejects* the Trimūrti — Viṣṇu is the Supreme, Brahmā and Śiva are subordinate or derivative |
| Śaivism | *reinterprets* — Śiva performs all five cosmic actions (creation/preservation/dissolution + concealment + grace); Brahmā and Viṣṇu are modes of Śiva |
| Śāktism | *feminises* — the three functions belong to the three goddess forms of Mahādevī: Mahāsarasvatī / Mahālakṣmī / Mahākālī |
| Smārtism (Śaṅkara) | *integrates* — the pañcāyatana pūjā treats Brahmā/Viṣṇu/Śiva (+ Devī + Sūrya) as equally valid names for the one nirguṇa Brahman |

**The Tridevi is the Trimūrti's feminine counterpart**: Sarasvatī (creation/knowledge) / Lakṣmī (preservation/prosperity) / Pārvatī-Śakti (dissolution/power) mirrors the male triad one-to-one.

**Conflation alert — Trimūrti ≠ the Trinity of Christianity:** The surface parallel (three persons, one divine reality) has attracted comparison, but the structural logic differs: the Christian Trinity is an eternal intra-divine relation (not functional roles), and the three persons are co-equal and of the same substance, whereas the Trimūrti assigns distinct cosmic *tasks* to three gods whose hierarchy is itself contested.
