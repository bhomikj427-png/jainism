## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śaivism** (*Śaivadharma*, *Śaivasampradāya*): the major Hindu religious tradition that holds **Śiva** as the supreme being (Paramātman / Paramaśiva). Approximately 385 million adherents; the tradition's doctrinal diversity is arguably wider than Vaiṣṇavism's, ranging from **strict dualism** (Śaiva Siddhānta) to **radical monism** (Kashmir Śaivism). Three realities are common to most schools: **pati** (Śiva, lord), **paśu** (the individual soul, "bound one"), and **pāśa** (the bond — karma, māyā, āṇava-mala / primal ignorance).

**Major philosophical schools:**

| school | metaphysical position | key figure / period | distinctive feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Śaiva Siddhānta | realistic dualism — Śiva and souls remain permanently distinct | Meykaṇṭār (~13th c.); Āgamic sources from ~5th c. | Tamil devotional canon (Tirumurai); liberation = Śiva's grace (*śaktipāta*), not identity |
| Kashmir Śaivism (Trika / Pratyabhijñā) | absolute monism — all is Śiva's self-recognition (*pratyabhijñā*) | Abhinavagupta (~950–1016 CE), Utpaladeva | ātman IS Śiva; liberation = *pratyabhijñā* (recognising one's own Śiva-nature); Śakti is Śiva's creative power |
| Vīra Śaivism / Liṅgāyat | qualified monism with anti-caste bhakti | Basavaṇṇa (1131–1167 CE); Karnataka | personal liṅga worn on body; rejection of Brahmin temple ritual; egalitarian ethics |
| Nāth / Siddha tradition | heterodox yoga-asceticism | Gorakhnāth (~11th–12th c.) | kuṇḍalinī yoga; non-sectarian; bridges Śaiva and Buddhist Vajrayāna |

**Śakti and pariṇāmavāda.** Śaiva Siddhānta and Kashmir Śaivism both treat **Śakti** (Śiva's divine power) as real and active — NOT as illusion (*māyā*) in the Advaita sense. In Śaiva Siddhānta, Śakti transforms (*pariṇāmavāda*) into the world through Śiva's will: the world's evolution is a real unfolding from a divine cause. This is the **pariṇāmavāda** reading of satkāryavāda — effects are real transformations, not superimpositions. Kashmir Śaivism takes a different route: the world is Śiva's *Ābhāsa* (luminous self-manifestation), neither illusory nor independently real.

**Contrast with Advaita Vedānta.** The standard Advaita māyāvāda (world as superimposition on brahman) is explicitly rejected by both Śaiva Siddhānta (world is too real for māyā) and Kashmir Śaivism (Śiva's Śakti is real, dynamic power — not māyā as ignorance). This parallels the Vaiṣṇava critique: Rāmānuja and Madhva reject Advaita for the same basic reason. The Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava objections converge against Advaita's attribute-less, action-less brahman.

**Contrast with Vaishnavism.** Structurally parallel: both have (a) a supreme personal God, (b) devotional (bhakti) and/or āgamic traditions, (c) sampradāya lineages, (d) a spectrum of dualist to monist Vedānta readings. The decisive difference is the supreme deity (Śiva / Viṣṇu), the role of **Śakti** (more central in Śaivism — Śiva and Śakti are inseparable; Vaishnavism treats Lakṣmī as consort, not co-equal creative power), and Śaivism's deeper entanglement with Tantra.
