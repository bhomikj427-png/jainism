## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jñāna-mārga** ("path of knowledge"; also *jñāna-yoga*): liberation through direct, transformative knowledge (*jñāna*) of the identity of ātman and Brahman. One of the three classical Hindu paths to *mokṣa* (alongside *bhakti-mārga* and *karma-mārga*); most closely identified with Advaita Vedānta.

The path is not merely intellectual study but a three-stage meditative method rooted in Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4 (attributed to Yājñavalkya):
1. **Śravaṇa** (hearing) — receiving the Upaniṣadic teaching (*tat tvam asi*) from a qualified teacher
2. **Manana** (reflection) — sustained personal contemplation to remove intellectual doubts
3. **Nididhyāsana** (meditation) — deepening realization until the non-dual truth is directly seen

**BG 4.38** (Krishna to Arjuna): *nahi jñānena sadṛśaṃ pavitramīha vidyate... ātmani tu tat svayaṃ yoga-saṃsiddhaḥ kālena ātmani vindati* — "There is nothing as pure as knowledge in this world... he who is perfected in yoga finds that in due time in himself" (Wikipedia, citing BG 4.38).

**Contrast with the other two paths:**

| path | means | primary school | suited to |
|---|---|---|---|
| jñāna-mārga | direct knowledge of ātman=Brahman | Advaita Vedānta | philosophically oriented; "formless reality" seekers |
| bhakti-mārga | loving devotion to a personal deity | Viśiṣṭādvaita / Dvaita | devotional practitioners |
| karma-mārga | right action without attachment to results | BG framework (all schools) | those engaged in worldly life |

BG describes jñāna-mārga as "the most difficult, slow, and confusing" path — suited to those who can engage with formless, attribute-less Brahman rather than a personal God.
