## Gloss / Divergence map

**Prabhākara** (Prabhākara Miśra; fl. **~6th–7th c. CE**) is the founder of the **Prābhākara** (Gurumata, "the Guru's school") sub-school of Pūrva Mīmāṃsā — the rival of [[kumarila-bhatta|Kumārila]]'s Bhāṭṭa school. His principal work is the **Bṛhatī**, a commentary on **Śabara's Śābarabhāṣya** (itself on Jaimini's [[mimamsa-sutra|Mīmāṃsāsūtra]]); Śālikanātha later commented on it in the *Ṛjuvimalā*. He shares the anchor-role over [[mimamsa-pramana]] with Kumārila.

**Chronology disputed.** Tradition makes Prabhākara **Kumārila's student** (nicknamed "Guru" for his brilliance), but several scholars (e.g. Gaṅgānātha Jhā) hold the reverse — that "the Prābhākara school is older and seems to be nearer to the spirit of the original Mīmāṃsā," and that Prabhākara "was a more original thinker than Kumārila." The teacher/student order is therefore *unsettled* and recorded here as contested, not resolved.

**The three Bhāṭṭa/Prābhākara splits (the substance of the rivalry):**

| issue | Prabhākara (Prābhākara) | Kumārila (Bhāṭṭa) |
|---|---|---|
| **sentence-meaning** | *anvitābhidhāna* — words denote meaning only as *already syntactically connected* | *abhihitānvaya* — word-meanings are denoted separately, then *combined* into sentence-meaning |
| **theory of error** | *akhyātivāda* (a-khyāti / vivekākhyāti) — there is *no positive erroneous cognition*; "error" is mere non-apprehension of a difference between two real contents | (Bhāṭṭa) *viparīta-khyāti* — error is a positive mis-relating of one thing as another |
| **pramāṇas** | accepts **five** | accepts six (adds *abhāva*, non-apprehension) |

Prabhākara's **akhyātivāda** is the more radical: strictly, *there is no error* — what looks like illusion is the failure to notice that two separately-valid cognitions (e.g. "this" and "remembered silver") have not been discriminated. This dovetails with Mīmāṃsā's commitment to the intrinsic reliability of cognition.
