## Gloss

*Kundakunda* (Ācārya Kundakunda) is the most influential **Digambara** philosopher-monk and mystic — in the Digambara tradition he is revered third in authority, "right after Mahāvīra and [his disciple] Indrabhūti Gautama," and is invoked in the Digambara *maṅgala* (auspicious opening). His historical identity is genuinely uncertain: traditional sources date him to the **2nd–3rd century CE**, while modern scholarship ranges from a single 8th-century author to a "collective author" whose corpus accreted across centuries (~350–1150 CE). His enduring contribution is the **two-standpoints framework** — *niścaya-naya* (the absolute/transcendental viewpoint) and *vyavahāra-naya* (the conventional/empirical viewpoint) — and its application to the soul: from the *niścaya* standpoint the soul (*jīva*) is *always* pure, blissful, omniscient, and untouched by karma; bondage and delusion belong only to the *vyavahāra* standpoint. His four principal works each carry the title *-sāra* ("essence"): **Samayasāra** (439 vv.), **Pravacanasāra** (275 vv.), **Niyamasāra** (187 vv.), **Pañcāstikāyasāra** (153 vv.).

## Why Kundakunda matters for the corpus

Kundakunda is the hinge between **two layers the corpus has otherwise kept separate**:
- the **Umāsvāti / Tattvārtha-Sūtra layer** — systematic, realist, pan-sectarian metaphysics (the six dravyas, the seven tattvas, karma physics);
- the **mystical / soul-centred layer** — the inward turn to the pure self.

The Tattvārtha Sūtra describes the soul *as it is entangled* (jīva bound by karma, climbing the gunasthāna ladder). Kundakunda's Samayasāra describes the soul *as it really is* (already pure, the entanglement being a vyavahāra-level appearance). These are not contradictory; they are the **same soul from two standpoints** — which is exactly the niścaya/vyavāhara device (nishchaya-vyavahara.md). This two-truths structure is what later made Kundakunda the doctrinal anchor of Digambara mysticism, and it invites (with care) comparison to two-truths schemes elsewhere — Buddhist *saṃvṛti/paramārtha*, Advaita *vyāvahārika/pāramārthika* — comparisons drawn and bounded in nishchaya-vyavahara.md.
