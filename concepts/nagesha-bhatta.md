## Gloss / Divergence map

**Nāgeśa Bhaṭṭa** (also **Nāgojī Bhaṭṭa**; fl. c. 1700, early 18th century) is where the Pāṇinian tradition stops. Abhyankar's phrasing is the tell: "Patañjali and other reputed writers on Pāṇini's grammar **right on upto Nāgeśa of the 18th century**" — the dictionary uses him as the terminus of a list that starts with Patañjali. Deshpande calls him "**the last luminary in the tradition of Pāṇinian grammar**."

He is a Maharashtrian brāhmaṇa — reported from **Tasgaon**, Satara district — who studied at **Benares** and lived mostly at **Śṛṅgaverapura** near Allahabad under the patronage of **Rāma of the Bisena family**, a local chieftain. He is **tier 5** of the stack [[mahabhashya]] records, and his pupil **Vaidyanātha Pāyaguṇḍe** promptly built a **sixth**.

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐ The batch's third instance of one pathology, and the first where the mechanism is fully reconstructable.** Hindupedia's entry says Bhaṭṭoji "was his grandfather." Three other sources say Nāgeśa was **the pupil of Haridīkṣita, who was Bhaṭṭoji's grandson**. The two are incompatible, and the second **explains** the first: strip the teacher out of the sentence *"Nāgeśabhaṭṭa was a pupil of Haridīkṣita, the grandson of Bhaṭṭojidīkṣita"* and the trailing appositive re-attaches to Nāgeśa, yielding exactly Hindupedia's claim. **A relative clause changing which noun it modifies turns a teaching lineage into a blood one.**

⚠ **Checked properly, because the alternative was to blame the wrong party.** A search summary produced the false genealogy first, and the obvious inference was that the *summariser* had misparsed. Fetching Hindupedia directly showed that **the page itself asserts it** — so the error is in a published source, not in the retrieval layer. ⭐ **The check mattered and reversed the attribution.**

⚠ **And Hindupedia's own entry carries the material that sits badly with its claim** — it gives Nāgeśa's parents as **Śiva Bhaṭṭa and Satī**, a father who appears in no account as Bhaṭṭoji's son. **This is Batch 47's Vararuci finding again**: there, Abhyankar's own entry supplied the evidence refuting the claim its opening sentence called "very likely," and the corpus concluded the pathology "is a property of the **entry as a unit of writing**, not of crowd-sourcing." The corpus has now seen it in a single-author scholarly dictionary (Batch 47) and in a crowd-sourced encyclopedia (here). **The generalisation holds across both source types, which is what it predicted.** ⚠ The corpus does **not** assert that Śiva Bhaṭṭa was not Bhaṭṭoji's son — it has not read a genealogy of Bhaṭṭoji's family — only that no source consulted connects them, and that three do connect Nāgeśa to Bhaṭṭoji **through Haridīkṣita instead**.

**⭐ What he did to the Vārttika — and why it is this corpus's business.** [[katyayana]] already records the consequence at first hand from Abhyankar: Nāgeśa "appears to have **divided Vārttikas into two classes**," and on the narrow definition "many of the Vārttikas given in the Mahābhāṣya as explaining and commenting upon the Sūtras **will not strictly be termed as Vārttikas**." The received count falls from around **5,000 to about 1,400**. ⭐ **An 18th-century grammarian redefined a category and thereby changed how much of a 3rd-century-BCE author survives.** The number in every later handbook is downstream of a definition, not of a manuscript — and this is the same shape as [[astadhyayi]]'s five sūtra counts, where "the text is stable; its **boundary** is a judgement."

**⭐ The gloss that proves too much, seen from his end.** Nāgeśa comments on [[kaiyata|Kaiyaṭa's]] ranking maxim (*Uddyota* on M.Bh., MLBD ed. I: 217) and rationalises it: ***uttarottarasya bahulakṣyadarśitvāt*** — the later authority ranks higher because he "has seen a wider range of the target language." Deshpande's objection is that this "does not explain why this process must end with Patañjali." **The irony is sharpest at Nāgeśa himself.** By his own stated reason, the man who has read sixteen centuries more usage than Patañjali should outrank him — and Nāgeśa instead calls Patañjali ***bhagavān*** while naming Pāṇini plainly and Kātyāyana merely as *vārttikakṛt*. **He supplies an empirical justification for a hierarchy and then defers to it on theological terms.** ⚠ Deshpande notes Nāgeśa knows *all three* religious motifs — the sages as Munis, Pāṇini receiving the akṣarasamāmnāya from Śiva, and Patañjali as an incarnation of Śeṣa — where [[kaiyata|Kaiyaṭa]] knew only the last and Haradatta only the middle. **By tier 5 the myths have merged; the ranking they produced has not been re-examined.**

**Where he breaks with the tradition's philosophy.** SEP records that **Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa and Nāgeśabhaṭṭa rejected [[bhartrhari|Bhartṛhari's]] unitary sentence-meaning model** — already noted in that node as evidence "the tradition did not simply absorb him." Nāgeśa nonetheless wrote a ***Sphoṭavāda*** and the ***Vaiyākaraṇasiddhāntamañjūṣā*** on the philosophy of grammar. ⚠ **The corpus has read neither**, and does not here characterise *how* he rejected the unitary model. Recorded as a gap.

**The sixth tier, verified at first hand.** Abhyankar's entry for **Vaidyanātha Pāyaguṇḍe** — "one of the **chief pupils of Nāgeśa**," who "prepared a line of pupils at Varanasi" — lists his commentaries: the *Bhāvaprakāśikā* on the **Bṛhacchabdenduśekhara**, the *Cidasthimālā* on the **Laghuśabdenduśekhara**, the *Kāśikā* (or *Gadā*) on the **Paribhāṣenduśekhara**, and the *Prabhā* on Bhaṭṭoji's *Śabdakaustubha*. ⭐ **Four of Nāgeśa's works acquire commentaries from one pupil in one generation** — and this list independently confirms LinguIndic's account of Nāgeśa's output, from a source that never mentions Nāgeśa's works directly. **Two sources, neither citing the other, meeting on the same four titles.** ⚠ Note also that Vaidyanātha's *Kāśikā* is one of the **name-collisions [[kasika]] flags**.

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** Abhyankar (first hand; the terminus phrasing, the Vārttika redefinition, Vaidyanātha's commentaries), Deshpande 2018 (first hand; the *Uddyota* gloss with Sanskrit, the honorifics, the three motifs), LinguIndic (biography, teacher, the three work-domains), Hindupedia (patronage, parents — **and one refuted claim**), SEP (the Bhartṛhari rejection). Genuinely independent, and two of them converge on the works-list without contact. Confidence **medium**: no work of Nāgeśa's was read in the original or in translation, and the dates rest on a floruit.
