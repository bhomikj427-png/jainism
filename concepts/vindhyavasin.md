## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vindhyavāsin** (also *Vindhyavāsa*; fl. c. 4th c. CE) is an early-classical **Sāṃkhya teacher** known almost entirely **at second hand** — he left **no surviving work**. By report he was a **pupil of Vārṣagaṇya** (Vṛṣagaṇa) and a **reformer of the Sāṃkhya system** (revising the *Ṣaṣṭitantra* line older versions of which were ascribed to Kapila / Pañcaśikha). His fame rests on a **debate**: per Paramārtha's *Life of Vasubandhu*, he went to Ayodhyā and **defeated Vasubandhu's Buddhist teacher** (Buddhamitra, or Manoratha per Kuei-chi), under a king named Vikramāditya (often identified with Candragupta II). The Yogācārin **[[vasubandhu|Vasubandhu]]**, on returning, wrote the now-lost ***Paramārthasaptati*** to refute Vindhyavāsin's *Sāṃkhyaśāstra* — so the figure survives mainly in the record of his Buddhist opponents.

**Note (against the batch assumption).** A common modern guess links Vindhyavāsin to the disputed Yoga**bhāṣya** authorship; **no source consulted here supports that** — it is not asserted. (See [[vyasa-yogabhasya|vyasa-yogabhasya]] for the actual Yogabhāṣya-authorship debate.)

**Contested data:** the king's identity (Vikramāditya = Candragupta II? Skandagupta?), the exact dates, and the relation to **Īśvarakṛṣṇa** (a recent thesis uses Vindhyavāsin to fix the *Sāṃkhyakārikā*'s earliest date) all remain open.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Paramārthasaptati"** (from Paramārtha's *Life of Vasubandhu*) — Vindhyavāsa the Sāṃkhya adherent challenges the Buddhists at Ayodhyā; Buddhamitra loses; Vasubandhu writes the Paramārthasaptati against his *Sāṃkhyaśāstra*.
2. **Encyclopedia of Indian Philosophies, Vol. 4 (Sāṃkhya)** (Frauwallner's reconstruction, via search) — Vindhyavāsin a pupil of Vārṣagaṇya who "revised the developing system further," and the Ṣaṣṭitantra attribution.

The two agree on the core (Sāṃkhya reformer, Vārṣagaṇya's pupil, the Vasubandhu-circle debate) → **converged** on identity. Confidence **low**: **weak independence** (both ultimately lean on Paramārtha's hostile biography + Frauwallner's reconstruction), **no extant work**, and the dates/king genuinely contested.
