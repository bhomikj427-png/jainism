## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bhāmaha** is the author of the ***Manoramā***, "the popular *Manoramā* commentary" on [[prakrta-prakasha|*Light on Prakrit*]] (Ollett) — the commentary **E. B. Cowell printed alongside the text in 1854**, and therefore the form in which the corpus's oldest Prākṛt grammar reached European scholarship. He names his author in his own opening verse. ⭐ **This node exists because Batch 50 opened Cowell 1854 and found a collision waiting in the title page.**

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐⭐ The batch's methodological result: this corpus's own rule predicted the specialist's verdict, in a case it had not yet seen.** Batch 49 closed by sharpening the collision rule to: **distrust identifications that rest on the name *alone*.** Applied here before Ollett was read, that rule says: a grammarian's commentary and a poetician's treatise share nothing but the word *Bhāmaha*, so the identification is unsupported. Ollett — reading a literature this corpus has not — reaches the same place. ⭐ **The rule was formulated from grammatical evidence in one batch and correctly anticipated a Prākṛtist's judgement in the next.** That is the strongest validation the method in §4/§5 has had, and it is recorded as such rather than as a coincidence.

**⚠⚠ And the corpus accepts Ollett's conclusion while explicitly declining one of his two reasons.** His supporting argument is that Abhinavagupta, who did know *Light*, does not appear to know the *Manoramā* — so its author was probably not a famous poetician Abhinavagupta would have recognised. ⭐ **Batch 49 established, from Ajotikar, Ajotikar & Scharf, that an argument from a commentator's silence is worth zero**: "subcommentaries as a rule specifically mention only a small proportion of the words in the base text… one cannot assume that silence… indicates support." ⚠ **The two cases are not identical** — theirs concerns a subcommentary's silence about a *reading*, Ollett's an author's silence about a *work* — but they are the same family of inference and carry the same weakness, and Ollett leans on the form more than once (he also notes that Vīrasena and Jinasena "do not seem to have been aware of" *Light*). ⭐⭐ **So this node holds the split on ground (a) and marks ground (b) as not load-bearing.** The corpus is not correcting its best source; it is declining to inherit a weak half of a right answer — which is exactly what §4 means by "take the term, leave the editorializing."

**⭐⭐⭐ *Manoramā* is a genre label, and this is the seventh instance of Batch 49's fifth generator.** Batch 49 identified *Nyāsa* (5 systems) and *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* (4) as **genre-label collisions** — titles so generic that they name a shelf rather than a book. ***Manoramā*** ("the delightful one") is another, and **Abhyankar alone supplies three more of them**:

| *Manoramā* | on what | whose |
|---|---|---|
| **this one** | the *Prākṛtaprakāśa* | **Bhāmaha** |
| a commentary "named *Manoramā* on the *Kātantradhātuvṛtti*" | [[durgasimha\|Durgasiṃha]]'s Kātantra dhātu-vṛtti | Rāmanātha, ⚠ and separately "a grammarian of the Kātantra school who lived in the **fifteenth century**" — **Abhyankar prints both** |
| ***Prauḍhamanoramā*** | Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita's own *Siddhāntakaumudī* | Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita |
| ***Bālamanoramā*** | the *Siddhāntakaumudī* | Vāsudeva Dīkṣita |

⭐ **Four works, three śāstric neighbourhoods, one word.** And note what the last two do: *Prauḍha-* ("mature") and *Bāla-* ("child's") are **disambiguating prefixes bolted onto a title that had stopped disambiguating**. ⭐⭐ **The tradition solved the collision the way a filesystem does, and only after the collision had already happened.**

**⭐ What Bhāmaha himself says, read at first hand.** Cowell's translation of the *Manoramā*'s opening: after a benediction to Gaṇeśa, "**By searching out the things which were to be defined in these Sūtras of Prakrit definitions, composed by Vararuci, Bhāmaha, having attained their meaning, has made a clear and yet concise commentary.**" ⭐⭐ **Two facts in one sentence**: the commentator **names his author as Vararuci** — so the attribution is not merely, as [[vararuci]] records from Abhyankar, the work of "later writers," it is inside the commentary that carries the text; and he describes his own method as *recovering the intention* of sūtras whose objects had to be **searched out**, which is a commentator conceding that the bare sūtras were already opaque to him.

**⚠ The lost section, and why it is the sharpest instance of a finding this corpus already has.** Cowell on Section XII: "**In consequence of the loss of Bhāmaha's commentary on this Section, many of Vararuci's Sūtras are obscure and corrupt**… **I have not attempted a translation of this Section.**" ⭐⭐⭐ **A printed Sanskrit text of some thirty sūtras, in the hands of the man who edited the book, untranslatable because one commentary was missing.** Ch 45 §1.2 records Ajotikar, Ajotikar & Scharf's claim that commentaries are the *older and better* evidence because "manuscripts in India last no more than about five hundred years"; that is a claim about **transmission**. This is the same claim about **intelligibility**, demonstrated rather than asserted, and demonstrated by failure. ⚠ And it is the load-bearing section: [[sauraseni|Śaurasenī]] is declared the base of both other dialect sections.

**⚠ Two further collisions this node opens and does not resolve.** Ollett names **"Appayya Dīkṣita III"** as the commentator on Trivikramadeva's Prākṛt grammar — the corpus's [[appayya-dikshita]] is the Advaita/Śivādvaita author of the *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*, and **Ollett's numeral is itself the evidence that the tradition had already needed to number them.** And Abhinavagupta "seems to have been more familiar with a lost work called *Prākṛtadīpikā* and **Utpaladeva's** commentary thereon" — the corpus's [[utpaladeva]] is the Pratyabhijñā philosopher, Abhinavagupta's grand-teacher, so this is *either* a genuine and interesting extra work by him *or* a third man. ⭐ **Flagged, not decided**; no source read addresses either.

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** Three genuinely independent signals, and they are independent in the strong sense: **Cowell 1854** (critical edition with translation, read at first hand — the *Manoramā*'s own maṅgala verse, the lost Section XII, the six-manuscript collation); **Ollett 2017** (a modern peer-reviewed open-access monograph on Prākṛt, read at first hand — the identity judgement, the recension point, the Abhinavagupta dating); and **Abhyankar 1961** (read at first hand, for the other three *Manoramā*s). ⚠ They are 163 and 56 years apart and share no editorial descent. **CONTESTED** — because the identity question has a specialist's stated *doubt* rather than a demonstration on either side — confidence **medium**: the *Manoramā*'s existence, authorship, coverage and textual role are secure; **the man is not**, and neither is his century.
