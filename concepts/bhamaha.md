## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bhāmaha** is the author of the ***Manoramā***, "the popular *Manoramā* commentary" on [[prakrta-prakasha|*Light on Prakrit*]] (Ollett) — the commentary **E. B. Cowell printed alongside the text in 1854**, and therefore the form in which the corpus's oldest Prākṛt grammar reached European scholarship. He names his author in his own opening verse. ⭐ **This node exists because Batch 50 opened Cowell 1854 and found a collision waiting in the title page.**

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐⭐ The batch's methodological result: this corpus's own rule predicted the specialist's verdict, in a case it had not yet seen.** Batch 49 closed by sharpening the collision rule to: **distrust identifications that rest on the name *alone*.** Applied here before Ollett was read, that rule says: a grammarian's commentary and a poetician's treatise share nothing but the word *Bhāmaha*, so the identification is unsupported. Ollett — reading a literature this corpus has not — reaches the same place. ⭐ **The rule was formulated from grammatical evidence in one batch and correctly anticipated a Prākṛtist's judgement in the next.** That is the strongest validation the method in §4/§5 has had, and it is recorded as such rather than as a coincidence.

**⚠⚠ The corpus declines one of Ollett's reasons — and then has to narrow its own refusal.** His supporting argument is that Abhinavagupta, who did know *Light*, does not appear to know the *Manoramā* — so its author was probably not a famous poetician Abhinavagupta would have recognised. ⭐ **Batch 49 established, from Ajotikar, Ajotikar & Scharf, that an argument from a commentator's silence is worth zero**: "subcommentaries as a rule specifically mention only a small proportion of the words in the base text… one cannot assume that silence… indicates support." ⚠ The two cases are not identical — theirs concerns a subcommentary's silence about a *reading*, Ollett's an author's silence about a *work* — but they are the same family of inference, and Ollett leans on the form more than once (Vīrasena and Jinasena "do not seem to have been aware of" *Light*). **So the node holds the split on ground (a) and marks ground (b) as not load-bearing.**

⭐⭐ **But the refusal has to be aimed more carefully than it first was.** Ollett uses Abhinavagupta for **two different things**, and only one is an argument from silence: (i) that Abhinavagupta does not *mention* the *Manoramā* — silence, worth little; and (ii) that Abhinavagupta **positively describes** a Vararuci "excluding regional languages such as Śaurasenī," which is a **testimony about the text's contents**, not an absence. ⚠ **This node's first draft lumped (ii) in with (i) and discounted both.** ⭐ **Corrected: (ii) is a dated external witness and it is load-bearing** — it is what overturns Cowell on the missing chapter (see below and [[sauraseni]]). **The rule against arguments from silence is a scalpel, not a broom**, and using it as a broom cost this node a real fact for several hours.

⭐ **And there is a second, independent authority for the split that the node did not have when it was written.** Ollett, n. 35: "See the introduction to **Ghosh's edition of the *Prākṛtakalpataru* (pp. xvii–xviii) for further arguments against the identification of Bhāmaha with the Kashmiri poetician.**" ⚠ **Not read** — but it means the split is not one specialist's stated doubt. ⚠ **Stated precisely, because the first draft of this sentence was muddled**: there is still **no positive case for the identification** anywhere the corpus has looked — that row of the table stands. What is new is that there is a **published *negative* case**, argued in print before Ollett, which the corpus has **not** opened. ⭐ **The question is better-evidenced than the node knew, on the side the node had already taken** — which is the more dangerous direction to be under-informed in.

**⭐⭐⭐ *Manoramā* is a genre label, and this is the seventh instance of Batch 49's fifth generator.** Batch 49 identified *Nyāsa* (5 systems) and *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* (4) as **genre-label collisions** — titles so generic that they name a shelf rather than a book. ***Manoramā*** ("the delightful one") is another, and **Abhyankar alone supplies three more of them**:

| *Manoramā* | on what | whose |
|---|---|---|
| **this one** | the *Prākṛtaprakāśa* | **Bhāmaha** |
| a commentary "named *Manoramā* on the *Kātantradhātuvṛtti*" | [[durgasimha\|Durgasiṃha]]'s Kātantra dhātu-vṛtti | Rāmanātha, ⚠ and separately "a grammarian of the Kātantra school who lived in the **fifteenth century**" — **Abhyankar prints both** |
| ***Prauḍhamanoramā*** | Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita's own *Siddhāntakaumudī* | Bhaṭṭoji Dīkṣita |
| ***Bālamanoramā*** | the *Siddhāntakaumudī* | Vāsudeva Dīkṣita |

⭐ **Four works, three śāstric neighbourhoods, one word.** And note what the last two do: *Prauḍha-* ("mature") and *Bāla-* ("child's") are **disambiguating prefixes bolted onto a title that had stopped disambiguating**. ⭐⭐ **The tradition solved the collision the way a filesystem does, and only after the collision had already happened.**

**⭐ What Bhāmaha himself says, read at first hand.** Cowell's translation of the *Manoramā*'s opening: after a benediction to Gaṇeśa, "**By searching out the things which were to be defined in these Sūtras of Prakrit definitions, composed by Vararuci, Bhāmaha, having attained their meaning, has made a clear and yet concise commentary.**" ⭐⭐ **Two facts in one sentence**: the commentator **names his author as Vararuci** — so the attribution is not merely, as [[vararuci]] records from Abhyankar, the work of "later writers," it is inside the commentary that carries the text; and he describes his own method as *recovering the intention* of sūtras whose objects had to be **searched out**, which is a commentator conceding that the bare sūtras were already opaque to him.

**⚠⚠ The missing section — and a correction this node received from [[sauraseni]] a few hours after it was committed.**

Cowell on Section XII: "**In consequence of the loss of Bhāmaha's commentary on this Section, many of Vararuci's Sūtras are obscure and corrupt**… **I have not attempted a translation of this Section.**" This node was first written taking that at face value, and drew from it what looked like a sharp result: a printed Sanskrit text of some thirty sūtras, in the hands of the man who edited the book, **untranslatable because one commentary was missing** — Ch 45 §1.2's claim about commentarial mediation, demonstrated by failure rather than asserted.

⭐⭐⭐ **The demonstration was real; the premise was not.** Ollett (n. 34): "**The version of *Light* known to Abhinavagupta did not define Śaurasenī; Bhāmaha's commentary also does not extend to the chapter on Śaurasenī.**" The absence is a **terminus, not a loss** — the chapter had not yet been added when Bhāmaha wrote. And Ollett is not arguing from the absence: Abhinavagupta, in the eleventh century, describes a Vararuci that **excludes** "regional languages such as Śaurasenī." ⚠ **A dated external witness beats an inference from a gap, and this node took the inference.**

⭐⭐ **What survives the correction, and what does not.** *Not* surviving: the claim that Bhāmaha's work is incomplete, and the Ch 45 illustration built on it — **Cowell's failure to translate Section XII shows the sūtras were hard, not that a commentary had perished.** Surviving, and better: Bhāmaha's coverage is now **evidence** — his commentary's stopping-point is a **terminus ante quem for the expansion of the text**, which makes him a witness in the argument rather than a casualty of it. ⭐ **The node gained a fact by losing a finding.**

⚠ **And the error has a name in this corpus already.** Batch 49's [[candra-vyakarana]] caught Abhyankar reading **a transmission accident as an authorial choice**. This is the mirror: **a redactional terminus read as a transmission accident** — and it was made here, by this corpus, in the same batch that recorded the original. ⭐ **Both errors sit at the end of a text, because that is where "absent" and "lost" are hardest to tell apart, and where material is most likely to have been added.** Left visible rather than tidied away.

**⚠ Two further collisions this node opens and does not resolve.** Ollett names **"Appayya Dīkṣita III"** as the commentator on Trivikramadeva's Prākṛt grammar — the corpus's [[appayya-dikshita]] is the Advaita/Śivādvaita author of the *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*, and **Ollett's numeral is itself the evidence that the tradition had already needed to number them.** And Abhinavagupta "seems to have been more familiar with a lost work called *Prākṛtadīpikā* and **Utpaladeva's** commentary thereon" — the corpus's [[utpaladeva]] is the Pratyabhijñā philosopher, Abhinavagupta's grand-teacher, so this is *either* a genuine and interesting extra work by him *or* a third man. ⭐ **Flagged, not decided**; no source read addresses either.

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** Three genuinely independent signals, and they are independent in the strong sense: **Cowell 1854** (critical edition with translation, read at first hand — the *Manoramā*'s own maṅgala verse, the lost Section XII, the six-manuscript collation); **Ollett 2017** (a modern peer-reviewed open-access monograph on Prākṛt, read at first hand — the identity judgement, the recension point, the Abhinavagupta dating); and **Abhyankar 1961** (read at first hand, for the other three *Manoramā*s). ⚠ They are 163 and 56 years apart and share no editorial descent. **CONTESTED** — because the identity question has a specialist's stated *doubt* rather than a demonstration on either side — confidence **medium**: the *Manoramā*'s existence, authorship, coverage and textual role are secure; **the man is not**, and neither is his century.
