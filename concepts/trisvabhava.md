## Gloss

The three natures (*svabhāvas*) that together constitute every moment of experience. **Parikalpita** (imaginary / constructed): the superimposed duality of subject and object — a grammatical subject "grasping" a substantive object that is never actually there. **Paratantra** (dependent / other-dependent): the causal stream of representations (*vijñaptis*) arising by dependent origination — what genuinely exists, but not as it appears. **Pariniṣpanna** (consummate / perfected): the paratantra seen correctly — the emptiness of the parikalpita overlay within the dependent stream. Liberation = recognizing parikalpita as unfounded within paratantra; what remains is pariniṣpanna.

The magician-elephant analogy (*Trisvabhāvanirdeśa* stanzas 27-28): the elephant conjured by the mantra does not exist (parikalpita); the visual percept does exist (paratantra); the non-existence of the elephant in the percept is the consummate (pariniṣpanna). Stanzas 20-21 establish that paratantra and pariniṣpanna are **not different in characteristic** — they are the same stream, misread vs. correctly seen, not two separate ontological levels.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Garfield translation, *Asian Philosophy* 7.2, 1997): translation directly from Tibetan (Sanskrit of Trisvabhāvanirdeśa is lost); stanzas 1-3 for definitions, 20-21 for non-difference, 27-28 for elephant simile — authoritative Tibetan transmission. **Signal 2** (Enc. of Buddhism): cites *Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra* Ch.6 and *Madhyāntavibhāga* Chs.1+3 as canonical sources predating the Trisvabhāvanirdeśa — independently grounds the doctrine in sūtra literature. Two genuinely independent traditions (Vasubandhu treatise vs. canonical sūtra). Medium confidence because both signals remain within the Yogācāra tradition; Madhyamaka commentary perspective not independently verified.
