## Gloss

Austerity / disciplined self-restraint — from Sanskrit *tap* ("to heat, to burn") — a practice of deliberate bodily and mental discipline aimed at burning away karma, purifying the practitioner, or cultivating spiritual energy. Tapas is shared vocabulary across Jain, Hindu, and Buddhist traditions but its metaphysical grounding differs radically: Jains treat it as a literal karma-destruction mechanism (fine pudgala particles shed from the soul); Hindus treat it as spiritual fire purifying the subtle body and awakening divine energy; Buddhists rejected extreme mortification (the Middle Way) while retaining moderate disciplinary practices under reinterpreted categories.

## Jain tapa — TS 9.19–9.20 (directly fetched)

Jain tapa has dual karma-management functions: **samvara** (stopping new karma influx) and **nirjara** (shedding accumulated karma). It is the central mechanism of avipāka-nirjara (active karma-eradication, as opposed to natural karmic expiry).

### Six external austerities (bahya-tapa) — TS 9.19

**Sanskrit:** *anaśanāvamaudarya vṛttiparisaṃkhyāna rasaparityāga viviktaśayyāsana kāyakleśā bāhyaṃ tapaḥ*
**Translation:** "The six kinds of external austerities are: fasting (anaśana), reduced diet (avamaudarya), special restrictions for begging food (vṛttiparisaṃkhyāna), giving up stimulating food (rasaparityāga), lonely habitation (viviktaśayyāsana), and mortification of the body (kāyakleśa)."

| external tapa | Sanskrit | Pūjyapāda's gloss |
|---|---|---|
| fasting | anaśana | destroys attachment and karma through self-control |
| reduced diet | avamaudarya | develops vigilance and contentment |
| food restrictions | vṛtti-parisaṃkhyāna | limits begging locations to overcome desire |
| renouncing delicacies | rasa-parityāga | curbs sensory excitement (ghee, milk, curds etc.) |
| lonely habitation | vivikta-śayyāsana | maintains celibacy and meditation undisturbed |
| bodily mortification | kāya-kleśa | sun exposure, sleeping outdoors — cultivates patience |

Pūjyapāda: "External because they depend on environment and are visible to others."

### Six internal austerities (abhyantara-tapa) — TS 9.20

**Sanskrit:** *prāyaścittavinayavaiyāvṛttya svādhyāya vyutsargadhyānānyuttaram*
**Translation:** "The six internal austerities are: expiation (prāyaścitta), reverence (vinaya), service (vaiyāvṛttya), study (svādhyāya), renunciation (vyutsarga), and meditation (dhyāna)."

| internal tapa | Sanskrit | Pūjyapāda's gloss |
|---|---|---|
| expiation | prāyaścitta | "removal of faults due to negligence" |
| reverence | vinaya | "veneration to the holy personages" |
| service | vaiyāvṛttya | "help rendered to the saints in difficulty" |
| study | svādhyāya | "contemplation of knowledge; giving up sloth" |
| renunciation | vyutsarga | "giving up the attitude of 'I' and 'mine'" |
| meditation | dhyāna | "checking the ramblings of the mind" |

Pūjyapāda: "Internal as the mind is restrained or subdued through these."

## Cross-tradition comparison

| feature | Jain tapa | Hindu tapas | Buddhist (Middle Way) |
|---|---|---|---|
| metaphysical ground | karma-particles (pudgala) literally burned off the soul | spiritual fire (agni metaphor) purifying subtle body; divine energy activated | rejected: extreme mortification doesn't produce liberation; creates suffering |
| primary function | nirjara (active karma-eradication) + samvara (influx stoppage) | purification of body/mind; awakening kuṇḍalinī/prāṇa; merit accumulation | reinterpreted as meditative self-discipline (sīla + samādhi); dhutanga as optional moderate asceticism |
| textual authority | TS 9.19–9.20 (6+6 classification); Ācārāṅga Sūtra | Muṇḍaka Upanishad ("By Tapas Self is grasped"); Yoga Sūtra II.32 (tapas as niyama) | Pāli Vinaya (rejection of extreme asceticism); Dhutanga texts (13 optional practices) |
| Buddha's history | — | — | Buddha tried Jain/Śramaṇa-style extreme fasting before rejecting it; Middle Way arose from this rejection |
| Patañjali's Yoga | shares vocabulary but different: Yoga tapas purifies body/organs | "a means for perfection of the body and organs through the lessening of impurities" (YS II.43) | — |
| cosmic dimension | none (tapas is soteriological, not cosmological) | Vedic: Prajāpati created the world through tapas; creative cosmic heat | none |
