## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kālī** (Sanskrit *kālī*, feminine of *kāla* — "time" **and** "black/dark"; "the dark one," "she who is time/death") is the fiercest, darkest form of the Hindu Goddess (**Devī / Śakti / Mahādevī**) and the first of the ten **Mahāvidyās**. She personifies **power over time and death** — the devouring, dissolving aspect of cosmic energy.

**Emergence and the Raktabīja battle.** Her first major narrative appearance is the *Devī Māhātmya* (~6th c. CE), where she **springs from Durgā's brow** as wrath made incarnate. In the Śumbha-Niśumbha episode she defeats the demon **Raktabīja** — who clones himself from every drop of his blood that touches the ground — by drinking his blood before it can fall.

**Iconography as theology.** Dark-skinned, lolling red tongue, a **garland of severed heads** (the fifty/108 Sanskrit phonemes — cosmic sound), a **skirt of severed arms**, standing upon the supine **Śiva**. The standard symbolic reading: the severed heads/ego destroyed by knowledge; her nakedness = freedom from *māyā*; her **blackness = nirguṇa**, the formless transcendent reality beyond all qualities.

**The destroyer-who-is-mother paradox.** Despite the terrifying form, Kālī is in devotion a **liberating mother (Kālī Mā)** — the Bengali Śākta poets Rāmprasād Sen (1718–1775) and Kamalākānta granted her childlike-devotion *bhakti* in which the fearsome goddess grants *mokṣa*. Fierceness and maternal grace are one: she who devours time also frees from it.

**Śiva-on-the-ground motif.** Kālī rampaging uncontrollably is halted only when Śiva lies beneath her feet and she, recognising him, stills. In Tantra this encodes **Śiva (static consciousness, puruṣa) ↔ Kālī (active energy, prakṛti)**: energy is blind without consciousness as its ground; consciousness is inert without energy to act.
