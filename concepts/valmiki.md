## Gloss

**Vālmīki** is the legendary sage honoured as the **ādikavi** ("first poet") — the author of the **Rāmāyaṇa**, which is correspondingly the **ādikāvya** ("first poem"). His name is traditionally derived from *valmīka* ("anthill"): after years of motionless meditation, anthills are said to have grown over his body.

**The origin-of-poetry legend.** Tradition makes Vālmīki the inventor of the **śloka** (the *anuṣṭubh* couplet that structures classical Sanskrit verse). Witnessing a hunter shoot one of a mating pair of *krauñca* (crane/curlew) birds, the grieving sage's spontaneous curse fell into a perfectly metrical verse — grief (*śoka*) becoming *śloka*. At Brahmā's command he then composed the whole Rāmāyaṇa in this new measure.

**The redemption legend.** A widely-told (Purāṇic/later) narrative says Vālmīki was first a forest robber — abandoned as a child, raised by hunters, turned dacoit (popularly named **Ratnākara**) — who, redirected by sages and given a mantra, meditated until transformed into the *ādikavi*. The story is a paradigmatic **bhakti** motif: even a murderer is redeemed by repeating the divine name.

**A character in his own epic.** In the Rāmāyaṇa's Uttara Kāṇḍa, the exiled **Sītā** takes refuge in Vālmīki's hermitage, where her twin sons **Lava and Kuśa** are born and become his first disciples — the boys to whom he teaches, and who recite, the Rāmāyaṇa. As with Vyāsa, the author writes himself into the story he composes.

**Historicity caution.** Dating is impossible to fix (textual stages span roughly the 8th–4th c. BCE with growth to ~3rd c. CE); the redemption-and-anthill legend is later (Skanda Purāṇa and other Purāṇic sources), not part of the oldest stratum. "Vālmīki" names a tradition's founding-poet figure more than a securely datable individual.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia, "Valmiki":** *Ādi Kavi* / *Ādi Kāvya* titles; Rāmāyaṇa of ~24,000 ślokas; the *krauñca*-bird legend producing the first *śloka* "out of sorrow"; the robber-transformation legend (Skanda Purāṇa names him Lohajaṅgha) with the anthill (*valmīka*) etymology; Sītā's refuge and the raising/teaching of Lava and Kuśa in the Uttara Kāṇḍa; dating uncertainty (8th–4th c. BCE core to ~3rd c. CE) and acknowledged legendary/historical ambiguity.

**Signal 2 — WisdomLib, "Valmiki":** independently gives *Ādikavi*; invention of the *śloka* metre; the *Krauñca*-bird curse falling into the *anuṣṭubh* metre "at the command of Brahman"; the welding of scattered Rāma songs into one poem; the abandoned-child-turned-dacoit legend with the anthill (*valmika*) name-origin; sheltering Sītā and raising Lava and Kuśa.

**Independence:** an encyclopedia article and a Sanskrit-reference compendium converging on the titles, the metre-origin legend, the redemption legend, the etymology, and his in-story role. Confidence medium: the Rāmāyaṇa's frame verses and the Skanda Purāṇa were not directly fetched; the "Ratnākara" name is popular tradition (the fetched sources give "Lohajaṅgha"/unnamed robber), so it is flagged as such.
