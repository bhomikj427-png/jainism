## Gloss / Divergence map

**Nāthamuni** is the **first** of the *munitraya*, the three ācāryas of Śrī Vaiṣṇavism — Nāthamuni → [[yamunacarya|Yāmuna]] (his grandson) → [[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]]. He did two things, and **neither of them survives in his own hand**:

1. He **compiled the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham***, the four thousand Tamil verses of the twelve Āḻvārs, and set them to music for temple recitation. This is the act that makes Śrī Vaiṣṇavism what it is — **a Sanskrit philosophical tradition with a Tamil devotional canon inside it.** Everything `lakshmi.md`, `bhakti.md` and `vishishtadvaita.md` record about the school's devotional character descends from a compilation, not a doctrine.
2. He wrote the ***Nyāyatattva*** and the ***Yogarahasya***. **Both are lost.**

He is therefore that recurring figure in this corpus: **an author known entirely through the people who quote him.** Compare [[gayadasa|Gayadāsa]], surviving in one section of one manuscript yet demonstrably a whole-text commentator because [[dalhana|Ḍalhaṇa]] quotes him throughout. The Śrī Vaiṣṇava case is the same evidential shape in a different literature.

### Divergence 1 — the dates are not merely uncertain; they are impossible

| reading | source | lifespan implied |
|---|---|---|
| **823–951 CE** | "generally considered"; carried by Wikipedia and the handbook literature | **128 years** |
| **582–922 CE** | reported as an alternative dating | **340 years** |
| **born after 907, fl. 10th c.** | reported as a scholarly view | plausible |

Wikipedia states plainly that the traditional claim of a **400+ year lifespan is implausible**. That is the right verdict, and it makes this file's real finding visible only when the lineage is looked at whole:

| ācārya | conventional dates | span |
|---|---|---|
| Nāthamuni | 823–951 | **128 years** |
| Yāmuna | 918–1038 (also 917–1042) | **120 / 125 years** |
| Rāmānuja | 1017–1137 | **120 years** |

⚠ **Three consecutive ācāryas, each assigned a span no human reaches.** One such figure is a hagiographic flourish; three in a row, in one lineage, is a **convention** — the *munitraya*'s dates are a system of stitching, not a record. `yamunacarya.md` raised this on two data points; the third confirms it, and it retrospectively supports Sydnor's verdict in `ramanuja.md` that reconstructing these lives is "nearly impossible." **What survives the observation:** the 9th–12th century placement and the *order*, which are constrained by the citation record. What does not: any endpoint.

### Divergence 2 — how the Prabandham was recovered

The tradition: Nāthamuni heard only **ten** (or eleven) hymns of Nammāḻvār at Kumbakonam; went to Tirukkurugūr; chanted the associated verses **12,000 times** beneath a sacred tree; whereupon **Nammāḻvār appeared and gave him all four thousand** compositions of the twelve Āḻvārs.

This file records that as **what the tradition says about its own canon's origin**, not as an event. Its structure is worth noting exactly because it is so explicit: the account concedes that **the corpus was lost and that only a fragment was in circulation**, and supplies a supernatural mechanism for the remainder. Stripped of the miracle, what remains is a real and ordinary claim — *one man assembled a scattered oral corpus, and the tradition could not say from where.* That is a **text-critical statement dressed as a vision**, and it is the most useful thing in the legend.

### What the *Nyāyatattva* argued — recovered from its citations

The lost work is being reconstructed from quotation in **Yāmuna (10th c.), Rāmānuja (12th c.), Sudarśanasūri (13th c.) and Veṅkaṭanātha / Vedānta Deśika (13th–14th c.)**. Prof. P. T. G. Y. Sampathkumaracharya (National Sanskrit University, Tirupati) reports that **Vedānta Deśika cites the *Nyāyatattva* forty-four times across his works.**

Its subject is **Īśvara as cause of the world**, and its thesis is that God is *abhinna-nimittopādāna-kāraṇa* — **the non-different instrumental *and* material cause** — against the Naiyāyika position that keeps the two causes separate and leaves God merely instrumental. Deśika's *Nyāyasiddhāñjana*, in the *Īśvara-pariccheda*, quotes Nāthamuni on agency: *kartṛ-karaṇa-karmāṇīti trīṇi kāraṇāni kriyāviśiṣṭāni*.

Two things follow, and both matter to the corpus:

- **The title is not an attack.** *Nyāyatattva* — "the truth of Nyāya" — **appropriates the Naiyāyika logical apparatus and rejects only its conclusion.** Compare `appayya-dikshita.md`'s method warning, from the other direction: there, a title named for its target concealed the real opponent; here, a title naming a school conceals that the school's *method* is being adopted.
- **The material-cause thesis is the seed of the school.** God as material cause is exactly what makes Viśiṣṭādvaita a *pariṇāma* Vedānta — see [[parinamavada]], which `vishishtadvaita.md` already carries as Rāmānuja's causal theory. On this evidence the position is in the lineage **two generations before Rāmānuja**, in a work no longer extant. That is a real gain: it moves the doctrine's floor earlier, and it does so on citation evidence rather than on tradition's say-so.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Prof. P. T. G. Y. Sampathkumaracharya, National Sanskrit University, Tirupati**, in *Hitaya* (ISSN 2581-7787). A named academic at a Sanskrit university, writing on the specific question: the *Nyāyatattva*'s subject, the *abhinna-nimittopādāna-kāraṇa* thesis against the Naiyāyikas, the count of forty-four Deśika citations, and the *Īśvara-pariccheda* fragment in Devanagari. **Signal 2 — Wikipedia, "Nathamuni"**: three datings and the explicit "400+ years is implausible"; the Prabandham recovery narrative with the ten hymns, Tirukkurugūr, the 12,000 recitations and Nammāḻvār's appearance; the two lost works; Yāmuna as grandson; cites T. Rajagopala Chariar. **Signal 3 — search-level aggregation**: the *munitraya* framing; the reconstruction programme naming Yāmuna, Rāmānuja, Sudarśanasūri and Veṅkaṭanātha as the citing authors; Yāmuna as 917–1042 (a **fourth** span for him, 125 years, which is itself part of the finding). **Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `yamunacarya.md`, written this batch from a 1972 critical edition, independently gives Nāthamuni as grandfather and as compiler of the Āḻvārs' sayings — from the *Āgamaprāmāṇya* translation's own footnote 304.

**Independence.** Signals 1 and 2 are independent in author, platform and purpose, and signal 1 is the stronger on everything doctrinal. Signal 4 is the useful check: it comes from a printed 1972 edition's footnotes rather than the web layer, and it agrees.

⚠ **A dead lead, logged with its cause.** A conference abstract — "On the Lost *Nyāyatattva* of Nāthamuni: A Reconstruction of Its Reception in Later Works," ECSAS 2025 — appeared in search results with a URL that **now serves Polish-language cryptocurrency-casino spam**. The paper is real and is the obvious upgrade path; the domain is not to be cited or re-fetched. Recorded because a plausible-looking academic URL that has been repurposed is a failure mode worth naming.

**Why contested.** The dates are not merely disputed but **arithmetically impossible on two of three readings**, and the corpus's own canon-origin account is a miracle narrative. **Why medium.** The doctrinal core rests on a named academic reporting verbatim fragments with a citation count. **Not high**: the *Nyāyatattva* **does not exist to be read**; everything about it is at second or third hand; signal 1 was not peer-reviewed so far as could be established; and the Prabandham compilation itself was not examined.
