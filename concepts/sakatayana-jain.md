## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pālyakīrti Śākaṭāyana** (c. 814–867) was a Jain ācārya at the Rāṣṭrakūṭa court of **Amoghavarṣa I**, after whom he named the *Amoghavṛtti*, his own vast commentary on his own grammar. He is two things at once, and the corpus reaches him from both directions:

1. ⭐ **A grammarian** — author of the *Śabdānuśāsana* (also called the *Śākaṭāyana-vyākaraṇa*), one of the eight post-Pāṇinian "systems," "revered by both [Jain sects] as a great grammarian, **a Jaina Pāṇini as it were**" (Jaini).
2. ⭐⭐⭐ **The only surviving author of a lost Jain sect.** His *Strīnirvāṇaprakaraṇa* and *Kevalibhuktiprakaraṇa*, each with his own autocommentary, are — on Jaini's account — "**the only two extant texts of the Yāpanīya school**." **The entire surviving literature of a whole Jain sect is four works by one grammarian.**

⚠ **He is not the ancient Śākaṭāyana** whom Pāṇini and Yāska name; that is [[sakatayana]], and the two are separated by roughly a millennium and by religion. **The typed split is drawn deliberately** (§8), because the name is a patronymic and merging them would enact the confusion the map exists to teach.

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐ What this node does to a node the corpus already holds

[[digambara]] lists, as defining Digambara positions, that **women cannot attain liberation in a female body** (*strī-mokṣa* denied) and that **an omniscient being no longer takes food** (*kevali-bhukti* denied). ⭐⭐ **Śākaṭāyana wrote one treatise against each of those two propositions, and those two treatises are the whole surviving literature of his sect.** The corpus had the doctrines and did not have the man who argued against them.

⚠ And [[digambara]] is right about who the opponent *was expected* to be and wrong about who it turned out to be. Jaini: "One would expect his opponents to be the **Śvetāmbaras**, who have traditionally held that view. Yet the earliest extant work dedicated to a systematic refutation of the Digambara position **does not originate in the Śvetāmbara camp**." ⭐ **The first sustained defence of women's capacity for liberation in Indian religious literature comes from the sect that no longer exists.**

⚠⚠ **A structural hole this node exposes and does not fill: the corpus has [[digambara]] and [[svetambara]] and no node for the Yāpanīyas.** That is exactly the shape of the gap [[candragomin]] exposed when it found the corpus had `madhyamaka`, `nagarjuna` and `yogacara` and no Candrakīrti. Recorded, not filled — §8 scope-lock.

### 2. ⭐⭐ The evidence for his sect is a colophon, and two disciplines converge on it without knowing they do

Jaini, following **Upadhye 1974**: in the colophons at the end of each *pāda* Śākaṭāyana is called "**Śrutakevalideśīya-ācārya**," and that title is what places him among the Karnataka Yāpanīyas.

⭐⭐⭐ **Abhyankar's grammatical dictionary independently records the same title from the other side**, defining it as "a term of a **very great honour** given to such Jain monks as have **almost attained perfection**," and noting that "the term is used in connection with **Pālyakīrti Śākaṭāyana, the Jain grammarian**." ⚠ **Abhyankar's headword is OCR-lost in the scan the corpus reads; Jaini supplies the word.** Two specialists in different fields — a Sanskrit grammarian's lexicographer and a Jainologist — attach the same honorific to the same man, neither citing the other, and **each supplies what the other's evidence lacks**. §4 signal 1 at its strongest.

⭐ Note what the title itself says: *śruta-kevalī* is the highest attainment available through **scripture** rather than through direct omniscience — literally "a kevalin by way of what is heard," and *-deśīya* means "approximating to." [[shruta-jnana]] and [[kevala-jnana]] are the two ends this compound joins. ⭐ **A grammarian's honorific is a claim about how far textual knowledge can take you.** ⚠ **The corpus's reading of the compound, not a source's; §3 makes etymology a clue, not a verdict.**

### 3. ⭐⭐ A date fixed four different ways, and they agree

- **Patron**: contemporary of **Amoghavarṣa I** (c. 814–877); "after whom he named his commentary… the *Amoghavṛtti*."
- **Lower limit, internal**: the *Svopajñavṛtti* quotes **Dharmakīrti's *Pramāṇavārttika*** — 7th century.
- **Upper limit, external**: the 10th-century **Digambara** ācārya **Prabhācandra** quotes *Strīnirvāṇaprakaraṇa* v. 30 in his *Nyāyakumudacandra*.
- Abhyankar independently: "ninth century… Rāṣṭrakūṭa king Amoghavarṣa."

⭐ **This is the best-dated person in this batch, and the reason is worth naming: he is dated by a quotation from a Buddhist logician, a citation by a doctrinal enemy, and a book named after a king.** ⚠ **Note the collision hazard**: Abhyankar also names a **Prabhācandra** as author of the *Nyāsa* on the *Amoghavṛtti* — a commentator *within* Śākaṭāyana's own school. **Whether Jaini's Digambara Prabhācandra (c. 980–1065) and Abhyankar's Śākaṭāyana-commentator are one man is not established here**, and the corpus flags it rather than assuming either way.

### 4. ⚠⚠ The §0 guard, and it comes from inside the source

There is an obvious and wrong thing to say about this node: that a 9th-century Jain monk was an early feminist, or that his sect represents a suppressed egalitarian India. ⭐ **The book that supplies these facts warns against exactly that, in its own Foreword.** **Robert P. Goldman** describes how Orientalist romanticism, "picked up by exponents of the so-called Hindu Renaissance," was "developed into **a theory of ancient India as a place of social and gender equality**" that later foreign rule had supposedly spoiled.

⚠ And the argument itself does not have the shape a modern reader wants. On Jaini's account Śākaṭāyana's move is technical: he "**rejected the very idea of distinguishing libido (*veda*) along the lines of biological gender**," and works within the shared Jain framework in which sexuality of every kind is eliminated at the ninth [[gunasthana|guṇasthāna]] anyway. ⭐ **It is an argument about karmic categories, made to win a scholastic dispute, and it is not less interesting for that.**

⭐⭐ **This is Batch 50's finding 7 arriving a second time and from a different direction** — the same §0 failure mode (**inflation into modern *politics***), again aimed at Jainism, again warned against by the specialist supplying the evidence. **Two batches, two independent specialists, one unnamed failure mode.** The charter question Batch 50 left for the user is now carried by two cases rather than one.

### 5. ⭐ The transmission, which is a small miracle and should be stated as one

The two verse-texts (**46 and 37** verses on Jaini's count of the edition; he prints **49** for the first, against Jambūvijaya's 54, differing over five *saṅgraha-āryā*s he treats as quotations) were first published by **Muni Jinavijayaji in 1924**. The **autocommentaries** were unknown until **Muni Puṇyavijayaji found, in 1963, a single palm-leaf manuscript** of them — ⚠ **in the Śāntinātha *Śvetāmbara* Jaina temple library of Khambhat, Gujarat.** Muni **Jambūvijayaji** collated it against three others (two of verses only, one a fragment) for the critical edition of **1971**.

⭐⭐ **So: the only autograph commentary of the only surviving author of an extinct sect survived in one manuscript, in the library of one of the two sects that outlived it, and was found in 1963.** ⚠ And Jaini notes the verses "**were not numbered at all**" in the manuscripts except one incomplete copy — **the verse-numbering of this text is an editor's construction.** ⭐ **The second time in this batch that a verse number turns out to be an editorial artefact** — see [[candra-vyakarana]] on the *Vākyapadīya*.

### 6. The grammar, and the one link between his two halves that the corpus can point at but not yet read

Abhyankar's dictionary uses the *Śabdānuśāsana* as a **working reference throughout** — "Śāk. IV.3.55," "Śāk. I.1.46" — for shared technical vocabulary across the **Śākaṭāyana, Jainendra, Hemacandra and Cāndra** systems (*sakarmaka*; the term for a conflict of rules; the first-person term; and Śākaṭāyana's definition of *karmadhāraya*). It has **four chapters arranged by topic**, its own **Paribhāṣāpāṭha** (98 maxims, in [[paribhasha]]'s census), a *Nyāsa* by **Prabhācandra** on the *Amoghavṛtti*, a *Cintāmaṇi* by **Yakṣavarman**, and a further gloss by **Ajitasena** (12th c.).

⭐⭐ **And Goldman points at the seam**: he directs the reader to "the extensive **linguistic argumentation** on this point in Chapter II (#95–141)." ⚠⚠ **Those sections were not read**, and the corpus therefore **does not assert** that the grammarian's technique is at work in the theology — only that a specialist editor describes fifty consecutive sections of the theological treatise as linguistic argument, and that the man who wrote them wrote a grammar. **It is the sharpest open question this node produces and it is one read away.**

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Padmanabh S. Jaini, *Gender and Salvation* (UC Press, 1991)** — read at first hand, in the open-access full text: the Introduction, the sect history, the manuscript history, the dating, and the editorial notes to Chapter II. ⭐ **It contains a complete published English translation of the *Strīnirvāṇaprakaraṇa* with its autocommentary — a primary text reachable under §1 — and this node did not read the translation itself, only the apparatus around it.** **Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand, entirely independent and from another discipline. **Search-level corroboration** for the Amoghavarṣa dating and the four-adhyāya structure. ⚠ **Jaini is the single source for everything Yāpanīya here**, and he is reporting Premi, Birwe and Upadhye rather than arguing from inscriptions himself — so the sect material is **one scholar deep and two removes from its evidence**, which is why this is `medium` and not `high`.

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. Contested on the sect (two sects assign it to each other; the works themselves are silent), on the date of the sect's end (⚠ **the same book gives two**), and on whether the grammarian's method reaches into the theology. Firm: the man, his date, his four works, and the fact that they are the sect's whole surviving output.
