## Gloss / Divergence map

**Maitreya Rakṣita** (12th c., eastern India) wrote the ***Tantrapradīpa***, a commentary on [[jinendrabuddhi|Jinendrabuddhi's]] *[[nyasa-vyakarana|Nyāsa]]*, which is a commentary on the [[kasika|Kāśikā]], which is a commentary on the [[astadhyayi|Aṣṭādhyāyī]]. ⭐ **He is the fourth tier of a five-tier stack** — and a fifth exists, because Abhyankar records a commentary **on the *Tantrapradīpa*.**

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐ A religion inferred from a name — the second such inference this corpus has caught, and the source flags it himself

Abhyankar, s.v.: "**As it appears from the name Maitreya Rakṣita he appears to have been a Buddhist grammarian.**" ⚠ **That is the entire argument.** *Maitreya* is the name of the future Buddha; *rakṣita* ("protected") is a common Buddhist monastic ending. The inference is not unreasonable. **It is also the only thing offered.**

⭐⭐ **Batch 50 caught the identical move**: Cowell's **Kaccāyana** inference, "made on the name alone," which the corpus's own Batch-49 rule discounted. ⭐ **This is the second instance, in a different reference work, and it is aimed at something larger — not an identity but a religious affiliation.** ⚠ **The corpus does not deny that he was a Buddhist**; the affiliation is plausible and is repeated downstream. It records that **the evidence consulted for it is a name**, and that Abhyankar hedges twice in one sentence ("as it appears… he appears").

⭐⭐⭐ **And the same entry supplies the objection, which no source draws.** Abhyankar says that later grammarians call him "**Rakṣita** alone" or "**Maitreya**," and "**very rarely** by the name Maitreya Rakṣita." ⚠ **So the compound — the whole basis of the inference — is the form the tradition least used.** Taken singly, *Maitreya* alone and *Rakṣita* alone are each far weaker evidence than the compound. *(The corpus's inference from two sentences of one source; no source makes it.)*

⭐⭐⭐⭐ **And the corpus has the pattern already, twice, on the other side of the desk.** [[haradatta]] reads a **doctrine** out of the proper name *Sphoṭāyana*; [[jinendrabuddhi]] reads a **biography** out of the proper name *Śalātura*; and [[paribhasha]] records that the technique — *jñāpaka*, reading a convention out of a text's incidental features — is **named, licensed and institutionalised** in the tradition. ⭐ **Here the same move is made by a twentieth-century lexicographer, about a religion, and marked as an inference rather than a finding.** ⚠ **The corpus does not treat that as a fault** — Abhyankar hedges it explicitly and the reading is a good one. It records that **its own catalogue of the tradition's habits turned out to describe its own principal modern source**, and that the corpus had been repeating the conclusion without the hedge.

⚠ **Independent support does exist and is thin**: a search-level summary calls him "a Buddhist writer" — ⚠ **in wording that matches Abhyankar's *Dhātupradīpa* entry almost verbatim**, i.e. it is very probably Abhyankar again, the trap [[vyadi]] documented. **Kanjilal's 2007 edition description does not state his religion at all.**

### 2. ⭐⭐ The five-tier stack, and the corpus holds four of the tiers

| tier | text | node |
|---|---|---|
| sūtra | *Aṣṭādhyāyī* | [[astadhyayi]] |
| vṛtti | *Kāśikā* (Jayāditya & Vāmana, c. 650) | [[kasika]] |
| pañjikā | *Nyāsa* / *Kāśikāvivaraṇapañjikā* (Jinendrabuddhi, 8th–9th c.) | [[nyasa-vyakarana]] |
| ṭīkā | ***Tantrapradīpa*** (Maitreya Rakṣita, 12th c.) | **this node** |
| ⭐ a fifth | a commentary **on the *Tantrapradīpa*** — Abhyankar has an entry for it | ⚠ **no node; the name is OCR-lost in the scan** |

⭐⭐ **And the stack is regional and confessional as well as textual.** Jayāditya, Jinendrabuddhi ("the **eastern school** of Pāṇini's grammar," per Abhyankar) and Maitreya Rakṣita ("the **Bengal** school," per Kanjilal) are a **single local lineage running from the 7th century to the 12th**, and at least two of its three named members are held to have been Buddhists. ⚠ **This corpus has never drawn the eastern school as a lineage**; [[jinendrabuddhi]] noted the phrase "eastern school" as "a regional division of the Pāṇinian tradition the corpus has not otherwise recorded." ⭐ **This node is the other end of it.**

⭐ **Compare [[haradatta]]**, "a well-known **southern** scholar of grammar" who "has drawn considerably from Nyāsa in his *Padamañjarī*" (Abhyankar, in the same entry). **Two 11th–12th-century commentators, one eastern and one southern, both working on the same *Nyāsa*** — which is [[haradatta]]'s own finding that "Pāṇinian grammar turns out to have regions," here with the map filled in from the other side.

### 3. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ A source caught being out of date rather than wrong — and it is a new category for a caution the corpus already had

Abhyankar (1961): the *Tantrapradīpa* is "available only in a **fragmentary** manuscript form today," though "**profusely quoted** by prominent grammarians after him."

**In 2007 it was published in full.** *Tantrapradīpa of Maitreyarakṣita (A Treatise on Kāśikā and Nyāsa)*, ed. **Kanjilal**, **Sanskrit Pustak Bhandar, Kolkata, 2007**, 267 pp., Sanskrit and English — from a manuscript **"hitherto unpublished," found in the Sāhitya Sabhā collection at Coochbehar**, running to **over 220 folios**.

⭐⭐ **Batch 47 issued a standing caution that "read at first hand" and "specialist" are strong marks for what a discipline knows *internally* and weak for anything depending on outside scholarship that has moved — "chronology above all."** ⭐⭐⭐ **This extends it: a reference work's statements about *what survives* are exactly as perishable as its statements about dates, and for the same reason — a manuscript can be found.** ⚠ Abhyankar is not wrong; **he is forty-six years old**, and the corpus was about to repeat him as a present-tense fact. **[[jinendrabuddhi]] already carries the "fragmentary state" clause and is corrected this batch.**

### 4. ⭐ A text known through its citers — the batch's second, eighteen centuries apart

"Profusely quoted by prominent grammarians after him," and available in fragments. ⭐ **That is the same shape as [[vyadi]]'s lost *Saṅgraha*, at the other end of the tradition**: a work whose existence is attested by the people who used it. ⚠ **And the difference is the finding.** Vyāḍi's book is gone; Maitreya Rakṣita's was in a north-Bengal collection the whole time. **The corpus should not treat "known through citations" as a synonym for "lost."**

### 5. The other works, and a date-anchor the corpus can check

- ***Dhātupradīpa*** — on verbal forms. ⭐⭐ **The date-anchor**: Abhyankar records that it "is quoted by **Śaraṇadeva**, who was a **contemporary** of Maitreya Rakṣita, in his ***Durghaṭavṛtti* on P. II.4.52**." ⚠⚠ **The corpus has no node for Śaraṇadeva or the *Durghaṭavṛtti***, and Abhyankar's abbreviation list treats *Durgh. Vṛ.* as a standing citation source (the corpus met it three times in this scan). **A real gap, named and not filled** (§8 scope-lock).
- **A short gloss on the *[[mahabhashya|Mahābhāṣya]]*** — Abhyankar names it separately, and places it among the explanatory glosses of which the prominent authors' "fragmentary works exist in a manuscript form."
- ⭐ He **quotes Candra** — i.e. the [[candra-vyakarana|Cāndra-vyākaraṇa]] — **in the *Tantrapradīpa*** (search-level). ⚠ Not verified in a read source, but it is a checkable claim about a printed text, and if true it is a **Buddhist Pāṇinian citing the Buddhist non-Pāṇinian system** inside a commentary on a Buddhist's commentary.

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand — five separate entries, and the source of everything substantive here **including the two contradictory dates and the name-based inference**. **Kanjilal 2007**, reached through its publisher's bibliographic description — ⚠ **a bookseller's page, not a scholarly one**, used only for the edition's existence, editor, publisher, year, extent, manuscript provenance, and the 1075–1175 and "Bengal school" statements it attributes to the edition's own introduction. ⚠⚠ **The one search-level "Buddhist writer" corroboration is very probably Abhyankar recycled** and is not counted.

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. ⚠ **This node is close to single-source.** Abhyankar contradicts himself on the date and infers the religion from the name; the only other witness is a publisher's blurb. Not lower than medium because the **bibliographic** facts — the works, the commentarial position, the 2007 edition — are firm and mutually corroborating, and because the node's principal finding (that Abhyankar's "fragmentary" is out of date) is established **by the existence of the edition**, which is not a matter of opinion.
