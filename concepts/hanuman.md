## Gloss / Divergence map

**Hanumān** (also Hanumat; epithets **Vāyu-putra**/Pavana-suta "son of the wind," **Mahāvīra**, Bajraṅgbalī) is the divine **vānara** (monkey-being) who is the supreme devotee and servant of **Rāma**. Son of the wind-god **Vāyu** and the apsarā/vānara-princess **Añjanā**, he inherits flight, shape-shifting, and superhuman strength, and holds **chiranjīvī** (deathless) status.

**Role in the Rāmāyaṇa.** Hanumān is the engine of the Sundara Kāṇḍa: he **leaps the ocean to Laṅkā**, finds the captive **Sītā** and reassures her with Rāma's ring, **burns Laṅkā** with his flaming tail, and in the war **carries the whole Himālayan herb-mountain (the saṃjīvanī)** to revive the fallen **Lakṣmaṇa**. These feats establish him as the paradigm of devoted, selfless action in a lord's service.

**The bhakti paradigm — dāsya-bhāva.** Hanumān is the canonical model of **dāsya-bhāva**: love-as-servitude, the devotee who finds his whole identity in serving the personal God. His often-quoted self-definition — "When I forget who I am, I serve You; when I know who I am, I am You, and You are I" — captures the bhakti tension between difference (servant/master) and non-difference. He is thus the emotional anchor of Rāma-bhakti, raised to a directly-worshipped deity especially through **Tulsīdās** (*Rāmcaritmānas*, *Hanumān Cālīsā*, ~16th c.).

**Cross-epic and cross-sectarian threads.** In the *Mahābhārata* he meets **Bhīma** — his half-brother, also a son of Vāyu — and humbles the proud warrior (who cannot lift Hanumān's tail), and he rides on **Arjuna**'s chariot-banner. In a Purāṇic strand he is born **through an aspect of Śiva (Rudra)** — so some traditions class him as an *avatāra of Śiva* even as he serves the Viṣṇu-avatāra Rāma, a rare bridge across the Vaiṣṇava/Śaiva divide.
