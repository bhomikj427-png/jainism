## Gloss

The **two-standpoint scheme** of Kundakunda's *Samayasāra* (Prakrit, ~415–439 gāthās; Kundakunda ~1st c. BCE–2nd c. CE; the standard commentary is Amṛtacandra's *Ātmakhyāti*, ~10th c.). It distinguishes:

- **niścaya-naya** (the definitive / transcendental / "real" standpoint; also *śuddha-naya*, the pure standpoint): from here the soul (*jīva*) is intrinsically pure consciousness, neither the agent nor the enjoyer of karma, untouched by karmic matter. The niścaya-naya describes things as they ultimately are — it is *bhūtārtha*, "expressing the real."
- **vyavahāra-naya** (the conventional / empirical standpoint): from here the embodied soul *appears* bound to karma — agent and sufferer, entangled in saṃsāra through its interaction with matter. Samayasāra gāthā 11 declares the vyavahāra-naya *abhūtārtha* ("not expressing the real," untrue in a certain respect), while one who knows the soul through the *bhūtārtha* (niścaya) standpoint is the *samyagdṛṣṭi* (right-seer).

**The asymmetry is deliberate and soteriological.** Unlike a neutral pair of viewpoints, Kundakunda *ranks* them: vyavahāra is a teaching device (*upāya*) — true enough for the unliberated, who must be addressed in karma-talk — but the goal is to transcend it and rest in the niścaya realization of the pure self (the *samayasāra*, "essence of the self"). Vyavahāra is the ladder; niścaya is the roof.

**This is NOT the same axis as the seven-naya schema.** The standard seven nayas (naigama … evaṃbhūta, TS 1.33) include a *vyavahāra-naya* meaning "the practical-classification standpoint" — one partial perspective among seven, none privileged. Kundakunda's niścaya/vyavahāra is a higher-order, two-fold, *hierarchical* division specifically about the soul–karma relation. **A recognised scholarly tension follows** (Springer, *J. Indian Philosophy* 2025): the equal co-validity of all nayas demanded by anekāntavāda sits uneasily with Kundakunda's ranking of niścaya *above* vyavahāra. Reconciling the two — whether Kundakunda subordinates anekāntavāda or merely applies it asymmetrically — is an open question, not a settled identity.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Arihanta Institute, on the Samayasāra): niścaya = pure soul free of karma / "not the agent of karma"; vyavahāra = soul entangled with karma; liberation = "shifting from the mundane perspective to realization of the soul's pure nature" (the ladder reading). **Signal 2** (JainGPT *Samayasāra* summary): the same dual perspective; "the ultimate aim is to transcend the Vyavahara Naya and realize the Niscaya Naya … the ultimate truth, the samayasāra." **Signal 3** (translation/commentary of gāthā 11–12, yashkjain): vyavahāra-naya "called untrue in a certain aspect," śuddha-naya relates to "the pure soul's actual nature"; samyagdṛṣṭi follows śuddha-naya. Convergent across three sources of different provenance. Medium confidence: the Prakrit critical text of gāthā 11 (the *bhūtārtha*/*abhūtārtha* wording) was confirmed via English translation/commentary, not fetched from a critical edition; the anekāntavāda-tension claim rests on a paywalled abstract (read by title + summary), not the full article.
