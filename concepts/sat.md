## Gloss / Divergence map

*Sat* (being/existence) in Jain ontology is not a simple predicate ("it exists") but a **triadic structure**: everything that genuinely exists has origination, destruction, and permanence simultaneously. This is the ontological foundation on which the entire Jain dravya/guṇa/paryāya system and anekāntavāda rest.

**TS 5.30 Sanskrit:** *utpādavyayadhrauvyayuktaṃ sat* — "Existence (*sat*) is with origination (*utpāda*), destruction (*vyaya*), and permanence (*dhrauvya*)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (via Vijay K. Jain 2018) — the three components:

| component | Sanskrit | definition | maps to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Origination | utpāda | "attainment of new states each instant, without the substance losing its essential nature" | new paryāya (mode) arising |
| Destruction | vyaya | "loss of former states; the substance transitions through configurations" | old paryāya ceasing |
| Permanence | dhrauvya | "continuity of the inherent nature and quality of substance across all transformations" | guṇas and dravya persisting |

**Pūjyapāda's clay analogy:** "The clay continues to exist in all states — the lump, the pitcher, and in the broken parts." The pitcher-form arises (*utpāda*), the lump-form ceases (*vyaya*), but the clay's essential nature (*dhrauvya*) persists throughout. Every moment, everywhere, in every existent, all three occur simultaneously.

**This is not a contradiction** — Jain ontology holds all three together without resolving them into one:
- A substance is **permanent** (*nitya*) with respect to its guṇas and dravya — dhrauvya
- A substance is **impermanent** (*anitya*) with respect to its paryāyas — utpāda/vyaya
- Both are true **simultaneously** — this is why anekāntavāda asserts "a substance is both permanent and impermanent" without this being a paradox

**Connection to prior concepts:**
- Directly unpacks the dravya.md definition (TS 5.30 cross-referenced in dravya.md)
- Is the ontological grounding of the paryāya/guṇa distinction (paryāya = the utpāda/vyaya side; guṇa = the dhrauvya side)
- Is the Jain metaphysical basis for anekāntavāda's claim that "the same substance is both permanent and impermanent from different standpoints"

## Three-way comparison: Jain sat / Buddhist anicca / Vedānta sat

| tradition | position on existence | key contrast |
|---|---|---|
| **Jain** | sat = utpāda + vyaya + dhrauvya simultaneously | permanent in substance, impermanent in modes; change and stability co-real |
| **Buddhist** | *anicca* (impermanence): all conditioned dharmas arise and cease; no dhrauvya (no enduring substance) | process without substrate; what Jainism calls dhrauvya is denied |
| **Advaita Vedānta** | Brahman is *sat* (pure being) without origination or destruction; only dhrauvya; utpāda/vyaya are in the realm of māyā | permanence without real change; what Jainism calls utpāda/vyaya are denied as ultimately real |

The Jain three-fold sat is the deliberate philosophical middle: neither pure permanence (Advaita) nor pure impermanence (Buddhism), but a synthesis in which both are real.
