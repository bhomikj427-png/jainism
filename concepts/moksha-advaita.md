## Gloss

In Advaita Vedānta, *mokṣa* (liberation) is the direct recognition that the individual self (*ātman*) has always been identical with the absolute (*Brahman*). Bondage (*saṃsāra*) is not a real condition but a product of *avidyā* (ignorance); liberation is not an event in time but the immediate collapse of ignorance when self-knowledge (*ātma-jñāna*) arises. The liberated state is not a destination reached after death — Advaita's signature claim is *jīvanmukti* (liberation while still living in the body). The apparent individuality of the self was never real; it was superimposed (*adhyāsa*) on Brahman by māyā.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vivekacūḍāmaṇi (traditionally ascribed to Śaṅkara, ~8th c. CE; cited via Wikipedia):** "By reflection, reasoning and instructions of teachers, the truth is known — not by ablutions, not by donations, nor by breath control exercises." Confirms that jñāna (knowledge/discrimination) is the *sole* proximate cause of liberation in Advaita; ritual and austerity only prepare the mind. (Attribution caveat: modern scholarship — e.g. Michael Comans — judges Śaṅkara's authorship of this text "most probably erroneous" on stylistic grounds. The verse still represents standard Advaita doctrine, so the *claim* stands even if the author does not.)

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Moksha" (broad coverage, cites scholarly sources):** Confirms jivanmukti/videhamukti distinction, the role of avidyā removal, and the intra-Vedanta disagreement (Advaita dissolves individuality; Viśiṣṭādvaita retains it; Dvaita keeps soul-God distinction). Source covers academic positions (Eliade, Deutsch cited elsewhere in the article).

**Signal 3 — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 1.4.10 / Chāndogya 6.8.7 (mahāvākyas — already documented in atman-vedanta.md):** "Aham Brahmāsmi" and "Tat tvam asi" are the primary Upanisadic grounds for ātman-Brahman identity. Not re-fetched here; cross-reference from existing file.

**Independence:** Signals 1 and 2 draw on different aspects of the tradition; the Upanisadic mahāvākyas are a third strand. Confidence medium because Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtrabhāṣya (the canonical commentary) was not directly fetched.

## Three-tradition liberation comparison

| Feature | Advaita mokṣa | Jain mokṣa | Buddhist nirvāṇa |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cause of bondage** | Ignorance (avidyā) / māyā | Karma-matter binding jīva | Craving (taṇhā) / ignorance |
| **Means of liberation** | Self-knowledge (jñāna) | Right faith + knowledge + conduct | Eightfold path; insight (vipassanā) |
| **Status of individual soul** | Dissolves into Brahman (was never separate) | Remains individual at siddha-śilā | No permanent individual soul (anattā) |
| **Liberated state** | Identity with Brahman; sat-cit-ānanda | Pure consciousness at universe-summit | Cessation; unborn, unbecome |
| **Liberation while alive** | Yes — jivanmukti | No — siddhahood requires body-shedding | Arhatship = liberation while living |
| **Creator God** | Brahman = ultimate reality; not a personal creator | Rejected entirely | No creator God |
| **Karma** | Moral law; not physical particles | Fine physical matter that accretes to jīva | Intentional mental formations; not particles |

## Intra-Vedanta split on liberation

| School | What survives at mokṣa |
|---|---|
| **Advaita** (Śaṅkara) | Nothing survives — individuality was always māyā |
| **Viśiṣṭādvaita** (Rāmānuja) | Jīva retains individuality in loving union with God |
| **Dvaita** (Madhva) | Soul and God permanently distinct; eternal blissful proximity |

Advaita's position is the most radical: individuality is not transcended but recognised as never having existed. This is why Śaṅkara's Advaita is often labelled illusionism (*vivartavāda*) by critics.
