## Gloss

The **Rāmāyaṇa** ("Rāma's journey/course") is the shorter of the two Sanskrit *itihāsa* epics, traditionally ascribed to the sage **Vālmīki** and celebrated as the *ādikāvya* — the "first poem," the foundational work of Sanskrit *kāvya*. It narrates the life of **Rāma**, prince of Ayodhyā: his fourteen-year exile (engineered by his stepmother Kaikeyī), the abduction of his wife **Sītā** by the demon-king **Rāvaṇa** of Laṅkā, the war to recover her (aided by Hanumān and the monkey army), and Rāma's return and coronation.

**Structure:** ~**24,000 ślokas** in **7 *kāṇḍas*** (books): Bāla, Ayodhyā, Araṇya, Kiṣkindhā, Sundara, Yuddha, Uttara. It is about a quarter the length of the Mahābhārata.

**Textual layering.** The core is Books 2–6, dated roughly **5th–4th c. BCE** with growth to ~3rd c. CE. The first book (**Bāla Kāṇḍa**) and last (**Uttara Kāṇḍa**) are widely held to be **later interpolations** — absent from the oldest manuscripts and differing in language and rhetoric. This matters theologically: Rāma's explicit identification as the **seventh *avatāra* of Viṣṇu** is concentrated in exactly these framing books, so the fully divine Rāma is in part a later devotional overlay on an older heroic narrative.

**Theme:** the epic is the paradigmatic Hindu treatment of *dharma* through character-ideals — Rāma as ideal son/king, Sītā as ideal wife, Bharata as ideal brother — and a major source for *bhakti* (Rāma-devotion). Like the Mahābhārata it teaches *dharma* by story rather than by revelation: it is *smṛti*, not *śruti*.
