## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śiva** (Sanskrit *śiva*, "auspicious, gracious, benign") is one of the principal deities of Hinduism and the supreme being of **Śaivism**. The name itself is a euphemism that grew out of the fierce Vedic storm-god **Rudra**: the Śatarudrīya and later texts address the dangerous Rudra as *śiva* ("the kindly one"), and over time Śiva-Rudra coalesced into a single supreme deity who is at once destroyer, regenerator, and the still ground of consciousness.

**Role in the Trimūrti.** In the Purāṇic triad Śiva is the **destroyer/dissolver** (saṃhāra), paired with Brahmā (creator) and Viṣṇu (preserver). His "destruction" is cosmic re-absorption that enables renewal, not mere annihilation — Naṭarāja, the lord of the cosmic dance, embodies the cycle of creation and dissolution.

**Key epithets:** *Mahādeva* ("great god"), *Rudra* (the fierce Vedic antecedent), *Naṭarāja* ("lord of dance"), *Bhairava* ("the terrible," fierce tantric form), *Paramaśiva* (the absolute, in Kashmir Śaivism). Śiva is worshipped chiefly through the aniconic **liṅga**, and is inseparable from **Śakti** (his consort and creative power, as Pārvatī / Durgā / Kālī) — in Śaivism Śiva-and-Śakti are a non-dual pair, unlike the consort-relation of Viṣṇu and Lakṣmī.

**Ontological status — incompatible school readings** (parallel to the Viṣṇu/Brahman dispute):

| school | Śiva ↔ ultimate reality |
|---|---|
| Śaiva Siddhānta (dualist) | Śiva (pati) is eternally distinct from souls (paśu); liberation is Śiva's grace, never identity |
| Kashmir Śaivism (monist) | Śiva IS the sole reality; the self's liberation is *pratyabhijñā* — recognising one is Śiva |
| Advaita / Smārta | Śiva is the saguṇa face of the nirguṇa Brahman; one of five worshippable forms (pañcāyatana) |

**The critical NOT-equivalent:** personal Śiva (saguṇa, with Śakti, mythic agent) ≠ the impersonal nirguṇa Brahman of Advaita. Vaiṣṇavas and Śaivas further disagree over whether Śiva or Viṣṇu is ultimate — the two supreme-deity claims cannot both be final.
