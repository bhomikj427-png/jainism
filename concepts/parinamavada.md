## Gloss

Pariṇāmavāda: the theory that causes really transform into effects. The effect is substantially identical with the cause (satkāryavāda — the effect pre-exists latently in the cause) but phenomenally distinguishable; manifestation is a genuine unfolding, not a mere appearance. Three variants:

| variant | school | locus of transformation |
|---|---|---|
| Satkāryavāda / Prakṛti-pariṇāma | Sāṃkhya | Prakṛti (primordial matter) really evolves into 23 tattvas; Puruṣa is unchanging witness |
| Brahma-pariṇāma | Viśiṣṭādvaita | Brahman (with cit/acit as its modes) really transforms; the world is Brahman's body in genuine change |
| Śakti-pariṇāma / Prakṛti-pariṇāma | Śaiva Siddhānta | Śakti (divine energy) really unfolds into the world |

Contrast with vivartavāda (Advaita): vivarta = apparent transformation only; pariṇāma = real transformation. Both accept satkāryavāda, but diverge on whether the manifested effect is ontologically real or illusory.

Brahmasūtra 2.2.26: *nābhāva upalabdheḥ* — "non-existence cannot arise from existence" — is read by Rāmānuja as ruling out the Advaita vivarta (appearance-from-nothing reading) and mandating a pariṇāma interpretation.

## Reasoning across signals

Three independent signals:

1. **HandWiki "Parināma-vāda (Hindu thought)"**: defines pariṇāmavāda as real-transformation theory; explicitly lists Sāṃkhya + Viśiṣṭādvaita + Śaiva Siddhānta as the three variants; distinguishes it from vivartavāda. Independent from Wikipedia.

2. **Wikipedia "Vivartavāda"**: from the contrast side, confirms that pariṇāmavāda = "world is a real transformation (pariṇāma) of Brahman," attributed to most Vedānta sub-schools except Advaita; also notes Nicholson's point that Śaṅkara himself was closer to pariṇāmavāda.

3. **Encyclopedia MDPI "Pariṇāma-vāda"**: cites Brahmasūtra 2.2.26 (*nābhāva upalabdheḥ*) and Pañcadaśī 5.5 as primary-text reference points; confirms Sāṃkhya satkāryavāda as the foundational causal model.

Signals converge on doctrine and school attribution. Confidence medium; high refused because Rāmānuja's Śrī Bhāṣya and Sāṃkhyakārikā primary texts not fetched independently in this session.
