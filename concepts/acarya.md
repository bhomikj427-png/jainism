## Gloss

*Ācārya* (literally "one who teaches by example") is the third of the five supreme beings (*pañca-parameṣṭhī*) revered in Jain practice: arihant, siddha, **ācārya**, upādhyāya, sādhu. The ācārya is the head of a Jain monastic order (*saṃgha*), holding the highest position among living, non-liberated practitioners. Unlike the arihant and siddha (who are already liberated and beyond all karma-bondage), the ācārya is still on the path — they are a monk at a high gunasthāna, fully observing the mahāvratas, and taking responsibility for the monastic community. Their veneration (third line of the Namaskar Mantra: *Namo Āyariyāṇam*) is aspirational, not petitionary.

## Pañca-parameṣṭhī hierarchy

| Rank | Being | Status | Venerable because |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Arihant** | Liberated (body remains) | Omniscient; still active in the world |
| 2 | **Siddha** | Fully liberated (bodiless) | All karma shed; at siddha-śilā |
| 3 | **Ācārya** | Non-liberated monk, path-leader | Models right conduct for the community |
| 4 | **Upādhyāya** | Non-liberated monk, teacher | Teaches the āgamas (canonical texts) |
| 5 | **Sādhu/Sādhvī** | Non-liberated monk/nun | Observes the five mahāvratas |

The first two (arihant, siddha) cannot be petitioned — they are beyond all agency in the world. The last three (ācārya, upādhyāya, sādhu) are living practitioners; venerating them is venerating the path itself.

## Ācārya's qualifications

The ācārya must possess and model the five mahāvratas in full: ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha. In addition, they must be capable of guiding others — the *caudaha guṇa* (fourteen qualities) of an ācārya include mastery of the āgamas, correct conduct, ability to ordain, and protecting the monastic order. This institutional role completes the picture of how the tīrthaṅkara's founding of the four-fold community (*catur-vidha-saṃgha*: monks, nuns, lay-men, lay-women) is perpetuated.

## Not a creator or a god

Like the arihant and siddha, the ācārya is not a deity, not a creator, and not a figure to petition for worldly favours. Jain worship of the pañca-parameṣṭhī is consistently described in the tradition as a cultivation of qualities in the worshipper, not a transaction with a divine agent.
