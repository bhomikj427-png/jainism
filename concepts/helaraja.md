## Gloss / Divergence map

**Helārāja** (Kashmir, second half of the **10th century**) is the commentator through whom [[bhartrhari|Bhartṛhari]]'s third *kāṇḍa* is read — by the tradition, by modern scholarship, and by this corpus. IEP calls him Bhartṛhari's "first major commentator." His ***Prakīrṇaprakāśa*** (also *Prakīrṇakaprakāśa*; the name proper is *Prakāśa*) is printed alongside the verses in the standard editions of [[vakyapadiya|kāṇḍa III]].

⚠ **He wrote on all three kāṇḍas and only the third survives** — "Helārāja has composed commentary on all the three cantos of Vākyapadīya, but his *Prakīrṇaprakāśa*, the commentary on the third canto alone is extant now." **The corpus's most load-bearing commentator is himself two-thirds lost**, which is the same transmission pattern [[vakyapadiya]] records for Bhartṛhari's own *Mahābhāṣyadīpikā* (one manuscript, fifty-five sūtras).

## Reasoning across signals

**T3 — CONFIRMED, and more strongly than the queue expected.** The test asked whether Helārāja is reachable at first hand, or whether the corpus's `avacchedaka`→grammar edge rests on Matilal alone. The queue warned he might go **blocked** (Abhyankar has two hits, both bare). He is not blocked: **Charles Li's Cambridge PhD (2017) is an open-access critical edition *and* annotated translation** of the *Dravyasamuddeśa* with Helārāja's commentary, CC BY-NC-SA, read at first hand here. There is also a 1963 Deccan College critical edition by **K. A. Subramania Iyer** (kāṇḍa III part 1) and a *Journal of Indian Philosophy* article by **Vergiani** on his citation practice. ⭐ **This is a D1 result** (`DRIFT.md`, "distance from primary sources"): the corpus now reaches Helārāja through a critical edition rather than through a single secondary citation.

**⭐⭐ And reading him at first hand immediately qualifies an edge the corpus had already drawn.** Li, §2 ("Helārāja on *dravya*: an all-encompassing doctrine"):

> At the beginning of the *Dravyasamuddeśa*, Bhartṛhari presents a list of **five synonyms** of the word *dravya*. **Whatever his own intention was, Helārāja interprets each of the words to refer to a specific school of thought**: Ātmādvaita, Buddhism, Sattādvaita, Sāṃkhya, and Cārvāka.

The decisive detail is chronological. Li cites **Bronkhorst**: Advaita was "**conspicuously absent** in listings of philosophical schools during Bhartṛhari's time" — "and yet here, in Helārāja's 10th-century commentary, it is mentioned **twice**." Li's judgement: the list "does not correspond to any common list of philosophical schools… the choices Helārāja makes are **very idiosyncratic**, and give us a sense of which philosophical opponents **he** was most concerned with."

**Bhartṛhari wrote a list of synonyms. Helārāja read a map of tenth-century philosophy out of it.** ⭐ And Li notes the aim is not polemical but **absorptive**: "unlike many other doxographies, Helārāja is not so much concerned with **refuting** rival doctrines as with showing that **all of them can be subsumed** by the kind of non-dual philosophy that Bhartṛhari proposes."

**⚠ The consequence for this corpus, stated precisely and not overstated.** [[avacchedaka]] rests its central historical claim — that Navya-Nyāya's signature device has **grammatical** ancestry — on Matilal citing *Vākyapadīya* III, "Adhikaraṇādhikāra," vv. 2–3 **with Helārāja's commentary**, for the pervasive (*abhivyāpaka*) / partitive (*aikadeśika*) locus distinction. That claim is now known to pass through a commentator who **demonstrably reads later doctrine back into these verses**. This does **not** refute the edge: the distinction may be in Bhartṛhari's verses themselves, Matilal is a careful reader, and Li's demonstration concerns the *Dravyasamuddeśa*, **not** the *Adhikaraṇasamuddeśa* — Li's thesis does not treat that chapter at all, and the corpus checked. But the edge should now carry the caveat that **the corpus has not seen the verses apart from the commentary**, and the terminology it cites is quoted from a page where verse and gloss are printed together.

**⭐ This is [[sphota]]'s Batch 47 finding, in a second literature.** There the corpus concluded that *sphoṭa*'s apparent antiquity "is an **artefact of the reading apparatus**": the word is absent from the Prātiśākhyas and present in their commentaries, so anyone reading them as the tradition does — root plus commentary, as one object — finds the doctrine in the oldest phonetic literature. **The same mechanism operates here**, and this time the corpus catches it on **its own** citation practice rather than someone else's. [[vakyapadiya]] had already written the shape of the problem without the evidence — "Bhartṛhari is read through Helārāja, as Gaṅgeśa is through the Dīdhiti." **The prediction was right, and it now has a case.**

⚠ **Batch 47's corollary applies and must be applied.** That batch corrected itself on exactly this point: the mechanism is **not inherently corrupting — it is inherently *invisible*.** Haradatta reading sphoṭa out of the *name* Sphoṭāyana produced false history; the tradition supplying a definition for *pratyāhāra* produced a perfectly good analysis. So Helārāja's reading is not thereby *wrong* — Li treats him as "**highly influential in shaping the interpretation of the text by later authors**," which is a statement about influence, not about error. **Noticing that it operated is the job; assuming the result is false is not.**

**Follow-up #5 from Batch 47, partially closed as a by-product.** That follow-up asked: *is the Vṛtti on kāṇḍas I–II by Bhartṛhari?* Two answers surfaced. **Abhyankar** (first hand) attributes the commentary on kāṇḍas I and II to **Puṇyarāja**, adding "Some scholars hold the view that the commentary on the **first kāṇḍa was written by Bhartṛhari himself**." The wisdomlib essay names the modern challenger: "The authorship of this commentary has been recently questioned by scholars like **Ashok Aklujkar**," and reports Puṇyarāja as a Kashmiri of c. 800 CE whose kāṇḍa-I commentary "has many gaps in it and hence cannot be taken as complete," while the kāṇḍa-II *Ṭīkā* "is available in full." ⚠ **The question is not settled** — it now has a name attached and a bibliography to chase. Carried, narrowed.

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** Three signals, genuinely independent: **Abhyankar** (specialist, thin but confirming the commentary's object and title), the **wisdomlib *Vākyapadīya* essay** (biography, dates, Puṇyarāja), and **Li 2017** (critical edition, first hand, the doxography finding). They agree on the 10th century and on the *Prakāśa*'s object; they do not overlap in content. Confidence **medium**, not high: no verse of the *Prakāśa* was read in the original here beyond Li's framing of §2, and the biography rests on a single website.
