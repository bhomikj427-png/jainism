## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Samudra-Manthana** ("churning of the ocean") is the cosmogonic episode in which the *devas* and *asuras*, having agreed to a temporary truce, churn the ocean of milk (*kṣīra-sāgara*) to extract the *amṛta*, the nectar of immortality. **Mount Mandara** is the churning-rod, the serpent **[[vasuki|Vāsuki]]** the cord wound round it, and Viṣṇu's tortoise avatāra **[[kurma|Kūrma]]** the pivot that bears the mountain on its back; the two sides pull the serpent back and forth (asuras at the hood, devas at the tail). A succession of *ratnas* (treasures) surfaces — among them **[[lakshmi|Lakṣmī/Śrī]]**, the wish-cow [[kamadhenu|Kāmadhenu]], the wish-tree [[kalpavriksha|Kalpavṛkṣa]], the moon, the horse Uccaiḥśravas, the elephant Airāvata, the gem Kaustubha, the goddess Vāruṇī, and finally the physician-god **[[dhanvantari|Dhanvantari]]** bearing the **[[amrita|amṛta]]**. The deadly *hālāhala* poison also rises and is swallowed by Śiva (hence *Nīlakaṇṭha*, "blue-throated"). Viṣṇu as the enchantress Mohinī then tricks the asuras out of the nectar, distributing it to the devas alone.

**Divergence (recorded, not reconciled):**

| point | reading | source |
|---|---|---|
| the *ratna* list | the set and order of treasures **varies by text** — Mahābhārata, Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bhāgavata, and Rāmāyaṇa each differ | Wikipedia (explicit); MBh Ādi Parva |
| amṛta spilling at four sites (Kumbha Melā basis) | **absent** from the ancient Purāṇas — a later theological overlay, not in the source episode | Wikipedia (explicit) |
| comparative frame | Dumézil read it as a reflex of a proto-Indo-European "ambrosia cycle"; Oosten noted Norse parallels (sacred drink, sea, serpent, god-conflict) — **structural comparison, not identity** | Wikipedia (Dumézil/Oosten) |

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva, Āstika sub-parva (Ganguli tr., via Wikisource)** — the anchor textual source: "the gods and the Asuras made Mandara their churning staff" and "Vasuki the cord," with "The Asuras held Vasuki by the hood and the gods by the tail"; the tortoise-king holds the mountain on his back; the moon, Lakṣmī, wine, the white steed, the gem Kaustubha, Dhanvantari "with the white vessel of nectar," Airāvata, and the poison emerge.
2. **Wikipedia, "Samudra Manthana"** (Purāṇa-based, esp. Viṣṇu Purāṇa) — independently corroborates apparatus (Mandara / Vāsuki / Kūrma), the hālāhala-and-Śiva episode, the full *ratna* list, Dhanvantari + amṛta, and Mohinī; flags the text-to-text variance and the Kumbha-Melā anachronism.

A critical-translation source and an encyclopedic Purāṇic summary agree on apparatus, products, and sequence → **converged**. Confidence **medium**: the episode is one narrative tradition with many retellings (weak independence between Purāṇic versions), and the *ratna* enumeration is text-dependent.
