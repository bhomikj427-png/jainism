## Gloss

"Yoga is the cessation of the modifications of the mind" — *yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ* (YS 1.2). Patañjali defines Yoga entirely through this: the discipline of stilling the citta's vṛttis.

**Citta** in Sāṃkhya-Yoga is the mind-organ — a material modification of *prakṛti* (not a separate immaterial substance). It is illuminated by *puruṣa* (pure witness-consciousness) but is NOT itself conscious. When the citta's vṛttis cease, puruṣa no longer misidentifies with the mind; it rests in its own nature (*sva-rūpa*) — liberation.

**Five types of vṛtti** (YS 1.5-1.11):

| # | Sanskrit | nature | gloss |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | pramāṇa | valid cognition | perception, inference, testimony (YS 1.7) — the same three sources Nyāya accepts at minimum |
| 2 | viparyaya | false cognition / error | knowledge that contradicts actual form of the object (YS 1.8) |
| 3 | vikalpa | conceptual construction | verbal cognition not based on a corresponding object — "the son of a barren woman" (YS 1.9) |
| 4 | nidrā | deep dreamless sleep | a vṛtti characterised by the absence of a content (YS 1.10) |
| 5 | smṛti | memory | retention of experienced objects without their reappearance (YS 1.11) |

Goal (*nirodha*): all five types of vṛtti are stilled. This is NOT suppression of consciousness — puruṣa remains; what ceases is the misidentification of puruṣa with the citta's fluctuating activity.

**Vikalpa** (vṛtti #3) is the key epistemological term: conceptual construction without a real referent. This is the same concept Dignāga targets in apoha theory (his *kalpanā* = the conceptual overlay that pure perception does not contain) — independently converged on from opposite directions (Yoga: "still it"; Buddhist: "it has no real referent, so inference is its proper domain").
