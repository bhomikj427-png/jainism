## Gloss / Divergence map

Nirjarā is **karma dissociation** — the shedding of karma particles already bonded to the soul (jīva). Where saṃvara blocks new karma from entering, nirjarā removes what has already accumulated. Together they are the two active purification processes of Jain soteriology.

**TS 8.23 Sanskrit:** *tataś ca nirjarā* — "After fruition (*anubhava*), the shedding — nirjarā — of karmas takes place." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, p. 314.)

Pūjyapāda's analogy (ibid.): karmas naturally fall away after delivering their effects, like food that decays after serving its purpose.

**Two kinds of nirjarā:**

| kind | Sanskrit | mechanism | spiritual value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural ripening | sāvipāka (or vipākajā) | karma runs its assigned duration and falls away on its own | passive; happens to all beings; does not accelerate liberation |
| Accelerated shedding | avipāka (or avipākajā) | karma is forced to ripen early through austerity (*tapas*) before it would naturally mature | active; the practitioner's tool; shortens the path to mokṣa |

**TS 9.3** (reported in commentary): *tapasā nirjarā ca* — "and nirjarā through austerity." Tapas (austerity) is the instrument for avipāka nirjarā. TS Chapter 9 enumerates twelve types of tapas divided into external (*bāhya*) and internal (*abhyantara*) austerities; full verse-text for the twelve not fetched this run.

**Position in the soteriology:**
āsrava → bandha → saṃvara (stops new karma) → **nirjarā** (removes old karma) → mokṣa

Nirjarā is what makes liberation actually achievable, not merely theoretically stoppable: even with saṃvara in place, the soul carries accumulated karma from beginningless past lives; nirjarā through tapas burns through this accumulated stock.
