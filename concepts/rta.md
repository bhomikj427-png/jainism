## Gloss / Divergence map

**Ṛta** (Sanskrit, "that which is properly joined; fixed order; truth"): the Vedic concept of **cosmic, moral, and ritual order** — the underlying principle of rightness that governs the movements of the heavens, the behaviour of deities and humans, and the efficacy of sacrifice. Ṛta is not a deity but an impersonal overarching principle: *the gods themselves obey ṛta and are judged by it*, rather than commanding it. This makes ṛta structurally unique in ancient Indian religion — a normative order prior to and above the gods.

**Etymology:** Proto-Indo-European *h₂r-tós* ("properly joined, right, true"; cognate with Latin *artus*, English "art," Greek *arariskō*). Sanskrit root *ṛ-* (to go/move) + -*ta* (past passive participle suffix) = "what has moved [in its proper course]." The root conveys both physical regularity (the sun moves as it should) and normative rightness (action is ṛta when properly done).

**Three-domain scope:**

| domain | what ṛta governs | example |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmic / physical | Natural regularities: sunrise and sunset, seasons, rain, the movements of celestial bodies | The sun rises and sets; Varuṇa's "fixed paths" of the night sky |
| Moral / ethical | The principle of honesty and righteousness in human conduct; cosmic truth | Oath-keeping, right action; *anṛta* (falsehood/unrighteousness) is the violation |
| Ritual | The proper performance of Vedic sacrifice; the correct order of sacred acts | Correctly ordered yajña generates ṛta; misperformed ritual is against ṛta |

**Varuṇa and Mitra as guardians of ṛta:** The Ādityas — especially Varuṇa and Mitra — are the primary divine upholders of ṛta. Varuṇa is described as the "friend" and "charioteer" of ṛta, its celestial enforcer who punishes oath-breakers and violations of cosmic order (*Ṛgveda* passim). Crucially, Varuṇa does not *create* ṛta but guards it — ṛta transcends even the gods. (~390 occurrences in the Ṛgveda, the highest frequency of any abstract principle.)

**Ṛta and satya (truth):** In the Ṛgveda, ṛta and satya (Sanskrit "truth; what is") frequently appear together as a cognate pair. Ṛta is the active cosmic process; satya is the static truth-quality. The pair jointly express the dual Vedic concern with how things *are* (satya) and how they *move/function correctly* (ṛta). This is the closest Vedic analogue to the later Greek pairing of *logos* (rational structure) and *alētheia* (truth).

**Transition from ṛta to dharma:** By the late Vedic and Upaniṣadic period, ṛta recedes and **dharma** takes its place as the central normative concept. Dharma is ṛta's "particularized manifestation" in duty, social obligation, and religious law — more prescriptive and context-sensitive than ṛta's cosmic sweep. The transition marks a shift from Vedic ritualism (right cosmic order) to the classical Dharmaśāstra tradition (right social conduct).

**Cross-cultural comparison:**

| tradition | cosmic order principle | personal / impersonal | moral force? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vedic | ṛta | impersonal (gods obey it, cannot override it) | yes — anṛta (untruth) violates it |
| Iranian/Avestan | *aṣ̌a* (Avestan cognate of ṛta) | impersonal; Ahura Mazdā embodies it | yes — opposition between aṣ̌a (order) and *druj* (lie) |
| Greek | *moira* (fate/allotment) / *logos* (rational structure) | impersonal | partially — moira is fate; logos is rational order |
| Chinese | *Dao* (Way) | impersonal | yes — the Dao as the Way of proper conduct |
| Buddhist | *dhamma* (natural law sense) | impersonal cosmic law | partially — natural regularities + ethical conditionality |

Note: these are *structural* comparisons, not identities. Ṛta and Dao share impersonality and scope across cosmic/moral domains; ṛta and *aṣ̌a* share PIE etymology and dual cosmic/ethical function. But they are not the same concept, and the absence of a cosmic redemption story (Zoroastrianism) or a metaphysical substrate (Advaita brahman) makes each distinct.
