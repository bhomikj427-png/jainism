## Gloss / Divergence map

**Ammonius Saccas** (c. 175 – 243 CE, Alexandria) was the teacher of [[plotinus|Plotinus]], who studied under him for ~11 years and is reported to have said on meeting him, "this is the man I was looking for." He is conventionally called the **founder of Neoplatonism** — yet almost nothing of his actual doctrine survives. Longinus reports he **wrote nothing**; Porphyry reports a **pledge of silence** among his students (Plotinus, Erennius, Origen) not to disclose his teaching. His philosophy is therefore known only by hazy attribution — at most the soul's immateriality and a program of reconciling Plato with Aristotle.

**Why this node is `blocked`.** The historicity is real (he existed; he taught Plotinus), but the *doctrine* is unattested and the *person* is irreducibly contested, with no critical-edition modern treatment (and no SEP entry) found within a bounded search:

| contested point | the split |
|---|---|
| religion | Eusebius & Jerome: Christian to the end · Porphyry: born Christian, reverted to paganism |
| identity | a single man · OR the modern "two Ammonii" hypothesis — a pagan Neoplatonic teacher distinct from a Christian Ammonius of Alexandria who wrote biblical works |
| doctrine | largely unknown / unrecorded |

The "two Ammonii" point is a conflation the map should teach in prose: Ammonius Saccas the Neoplatonist ≠ the Christian Ammonius of Alexandria (no second node).

## Reasoning across signals

The ancient testimonies *are* independent — Porphyry vs Eusebius/Jerome — and they **conflict**, which is the point. But the only modern sources secured are tertiary (Wikipedia, Britannica), which rework the same Porphyry/Eusebius testimonia; there is no SEP entry and no critical-edition study to anchor a gloss of his *philosophy*. By the §4 standard this fails ≥2 genuinely independent critical sources on doctrine → **blocked**, confidence **low**. The biographical facts (teacher of Plotinus, wrote nothing, pledge of silence) are recorded; the doctrine is left unglossed by design.
