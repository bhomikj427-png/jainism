## Gloss / Divergence map

Mokṣa is **liberation** — the complete annihilation of all karma from the soul (jīva), ending the cycle of rebirth (saṃsāra). The *Tattvārthasūtra*'s final chapter defines it directly:

**TS 10.2**: "Owing to the absence of the cause of bondage and with the functioning of the dissociation of karmas, the annihilation of all karmas is liberation." (Cited in Wikipedia "Mokṣa (Jainism)," sourcing the TS.)

The TS is itself sometimes called the *Mokṣaśāstra* (Scripture on Liberation) — mokṣa is the entire text's telos.

**The liberated soul — siddha:** At mokṣa the soul attains its "true and pristine nature": four infinite qualities (*ananta-catuṣṭaya*), traditionally enumerated:

| quality | Sanskrit | description |
|---|---|---|
| Infinite knowledge | ananta-jñāna | complete, unobstructed omniscience |
| Infinite perception/faith | ananta-darśana | unlimited insight/apprehension |
| Infinite bliss | ananta-sukha | unobstructed bliss (no vedanīya karma remains) |
| Infinite energy | ananta-vīrya | unlimited capacity (no antarāya karma remains) |

(Source: Wikipedia "Mokṣa (Jainism)," citing *Puruṣārthasiddhyupāya*; the four-infinite formula is standard across Jain sources.)

**Where the siddha goes — lokāgra:** Upon final liberation (physical death of the last body after gaining *kevala-jñāna*), the soul rises automatically to *Siddhashila* — the summit (*agra*) of the Jain cosmos (*loka*) — and remains there eternally. The upward motion is natural: freed from karma's downward-binding weight, the intrinsically weightless soul rises like a flame. (Wikipedia "Mokṣa (Jainism).")

**The process leading to mokṣa** (completing the soteriology arc from karma.md):
1. *Saṃvara* — stopping new āsrava (karmic influx) by ethical restraint
2. *Nirjarā* — actively shedding existing karma through austerity (*tapas*)
3. At complete karma annihilation: *kevala-jñāna* (omniscience) → followed by *mokṣa* at death
4. The soul then dwells at lokāgra as a *siddha* forever

**The three jewels (*tri-ratna*)** as the path: Right faith (*samyak-darśana*) + Right knowledge (*samyak-jñāna*) + Right conduct (*samyak-cāritra*) — the TS's own summary of the path to liberation. TS 1.1: *samyag-darśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* ("Right faith, knowledge, and conduct together are the path to liberation").

**Critical distinction — Jain mokṣa vs. Advaita Vedānta mokṣa:**
In Advaita Vedānta, mokṣa = recognition that individual ātman is identical to universal Brahman; individual identity merges into the absolute. In Jainism, each siddha remains a **distinct individual consciousness** at lokāgra — infinite in knowledge but not merged with any other soul or universal Being. The plurality of ātmans is permanent; there is no universal Ātman to merge into. This structural distinction follows directly from the jīva.md pluralism.
