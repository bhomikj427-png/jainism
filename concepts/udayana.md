## Gloss / Divergence map

**Udayana** (Udayanācārya) is the culminating figure of **old Nyāya** — the last great systematiser before Gaṅgeśa's Navya-Nyāya, and the man Gaṅgeśa himself calls simply **"Ācāryaḥ," the Master**. The corpus has been citing him since its Vaiśeṣika layer was written: `jati.md` runs on his exclusion criteria for universals and names the *Kiraṇāvalī* as a source text; `paramanu-vaisheshika.md` credits him with making **Īśvara** the director of atomic combination; `prashastapada.md` and `democritus-atom.md` both invoke him. He had no node.

He wrote the most sustained argument for God in Indian philosophy (*Nyāyakusumāñjali*), the definitive Nyāya refutation of Buddhist no-self (*Ātmatattvaviveka*), and — on the traditional account — the definitions that [[sriharsa|Śrīharṣa]] spent a book demolishing.

He is **contested on his date**, and the contest is unusually instructive: **the single piece of evidence that appeared to settle it turns out to rest on one manuscript.**

### The divergence — a self-dated work that may not be self-dated

Udayana's short *Lakṣaṇāvalī* carries a verse stating that it was composed in **Śaka 906 = 984/985 CE**. Self-dating is vanishingly rare in Sanskrit philosophical literature; the verse is famous precisely because it appeared to fix, from the inside, a chronology that everything else in Nyāya hangs on.

| reading | who holds it (source) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 975–1050 CE**, on the *Lakṣaṇāvalī* verse; "this discovery settled scholarly controversies" | Wikipedia; "accepted by most scholars" (Bhadra) | Taking a colophon-style date at face value — and Udayana ends the 10th century |
| **c. 1025–1100**, or (as reported elsewhere) **the 12th century** | **D. C. Bhattacharyya**, reported by Bhadra 2014 and by the secondary literature | That the verse is unreliable, and Udayana moves **a century or more later** |
| **the question is open** | this file | Both — the verse's textual status is a manuscript question no source consulted here resolves |

**Bhattacharyya's case is not an assertion but four converging arguments**, and it is worth setting out because it is a model of the method §4 prescribes:

1. **Manuscript witness.** The dating verse "occurs only in one manuscript" of the *Lakṣaṇāvalī*. A reading with a single witness is a *variant*, not a datum.
2. **A named opponent with an external date.** Udayana "mentioned clearly the name of the great Buddhist scholar **Jñānaśrī** and refuted his views." Jñānaśrī visited Tibet in **1038–41** — after Udayana is supposed to have died.
3. **His teacher.** Udayana's preceptor **Śrīvatsa** belonged to approximately **1000–1050**, which a 984 composition makes impossible.
4. **An emendation that fits.** Read *tarkāvarāṅka* for *tarkāmvarāṅka* and the verse gives **Śaka 976 = 1054 CE** — the same verse, one syllable different, a full seventy years later, and consistent with (2) and (3).

⚠ **A character reference for the dissenting scholar, added in Batch 46.** When `gangesha.md` was written, **SEP's Gaṅgeśa entry** turned out to rest its own chronology on the **same D. C. Bhattacharya** — giving "1300 and 1350 for his period of flourishing—**from evidence that has not been corrected to date**." The corpus reached this scholar twice, from two nodes, in two batches, by unrelated routes; and a specialist encyclopedia says his dating work in this exact field has stood. That does **not** make his Udayana redating correct — but it does mean the four arguments below come from someone whose chronology the field has otherwise not needed to revise, and they cannot be waved off as a lone dissent.

⚠ **A shared village, from the Gaṅgeśa side.** Wikipedia's Gaṅgeśa article places Gaṅgeśa at **Karion**, twelve miles (19 km) south-east of Darbhanga, and identifies it as **Udayana's own village**. Recorded as the encyclopedia's claim; two men three centuries apart from one village is the kind of detail a local tradition produces as easily as a fact does.

⚠ **Two things this file will not do.** It will not adopt "984" as settled, because argument 1 alone disqualifies it as *settled*. And it will not adopt Bhattacharyya's conclusion either, because **the two secondary reports of that conclusion disagree**: one gives "1025–1100," Bhadra gives "the twelfth century." The evidence Bhadra himself lists — Jñānaśrī 1038–41, Śrīvatsa 1000–50, an emended 1054, a debate at 1070–80 — points to the **later eleventh** century, not the twelfth. Where a secondary source's stated conclusion does not follow from the evidence it reports, the corpus records the evidence.

### The Śrīharṣa link — and an unplanned cross-check inside this batch

Bhattacharyya's fourth argument is a synchronism, and it lands on a node written **earlier in this same batch**. The commentator **Cāṇḍupaṇḍita** attests that **Śrīharṣa's father was defeated in debate by Udayana**; since Śrīharṣa flourished under the Gāhaḍavāla kings Govindacandra and Vijayacandra, the debate is placed at **1070–80**.

`sriharsa.md`, written hours earlier from an unrelated source (Wikipedia's Śrīharṣa article, which knows nothing of Udayana's dating problem), independently records that **Śrīharṣa's father was Śrīhira, a poet at the court of Vijayacandra**. Two routes — a Naiṣadha commentator quoted in a dating argument, and a modern encyclopedia article about a different man — converge on the same father, the same court, the same generation.

That convergence does real work. It supplies the **motive** the corpus could not previously see: on this tradition, `sriharsa.md`'s observation that Udayana is Śrīharṣa's "principal named opponent" and that the *Khaṇḍana* is "shaped by the target it attacks" is not merely doctrinal choice — **it is a son answering his father's defeat.**

⚠ **Held at arm's length, twice over.** Cāṇḍupaṇḍita's report is a commentator's anecdote about a debate, of the same genre as the *digvijaya* legends this corpus discounts elsewhere (`shankara.md`, `appayya-dikshita.md`'s Tātācārya stories). Its evidential value here is **not** that the debate happened but that a 13th–14th-c. commentator believed the two men were contemporaries. And there is a **reign-date problem left open**: the sources place Śrīharṣa "under Govindacandra and Vijayacandra (1104–54)" while also dating the *Naiṣadhacarita* to **1174 under Jayacandra** — a span the secondary reporting does not reconcile. **Recorded as an open question, not smoothed.**

### The works

Seven treatises, given by Bhadra in composition order:

| work | what it does |
|---|---|
| ***Lakṣaṇamālā*** | manual defining Nyāya's sixteen and Vaiśeṣika's six categories |
| ***Lakṣaṇāvalī*** | the same, restricted to the Vaiśeṣika categories — **and the carrier of the disputed date** |
| ***Ātmatattvaviveka*** | four chapters refuting every theory that denies a permanent soul |
| ***Nyāyakusumāñjali*** | the theistic masterwork |
| ***Nyāyapariśiṣṭa*** | on the fallacies of the *Nyāyasūtra* |
| ***Nyāyavārttikatātparyapariśuddhi*** | commentary on [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]]'s *Tātparyaṭīkā* |
| ***Kiraṇāvalī*** | commentary on [[prashastapada|Praśastapāda]] |

⚠ **Note the collision this creates.** *Lakṣaṇāvalī* is the work `sriharsa.md` records SEP calling Udayana's "Garland of Definitions," the principal target of the *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya*. **The book Śrīharṣa attacks and the book that dates its author are the same book.**

### The Nyāyakusumāñjali

Nine arguments for Īśvara, of which the corpus already carries two in other files:

- **from effect** (the world is a product and needs an efficient cause), **from combination** (atoms are inert; their motion requires intelligence — this is the argument `paramanu-vaisheshika.md` records), **from support** (the world's maintenance needs guidance), **from word** (the representational power of words needs a cause), **from faith** and **from scripture** (the infallible Veda requires a divine author), **from precepts** (moral law requires a lawgiver), **from numerical specialty**, and **from the unforeseen** (*adṛṣṭa* / karma requires intelligent administration — the point `paramanu-vaisheshika.md` records as the *adṛṣṭa*-to-Īśvara move).

⚠ Note the shape of the fifth and sixth: the Veda is infallible **because** God authored it, and God exists **because** the Veda says so. Whether Udayana's own text runs these as one circular pair or as two distinct arguments at different levels **cannot be settled from a nine-item summary**, and is not adjudicated here.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Sri Ramen Bhadra, "Nyāyakusumāñjali of Udayana (a study)" (2014)**, via Wisdomlib. An extended academic study, named author, and the only source consulted that treats the dating **as a problem with an apparatus**: the single-manuscript objection, the Jñānaśrī and Śrīvatsa synchronisms, the *tarkāmvarāṅka*/*tarkāvarāṅka* emendation, and the Cāṇḍupaṇḍita testimony. **Signal 2 — Wikipedia, "Udayana"**: the seven works in order with descriptions; the nine Nyāyakusumāñjali arguments; Gaṅgeśa's "Ācāryaḥ"; the Dharmakīrti/Jñānaśrī engagement; D. C. Bhattacharya's remark that from the 12th c. Udayana was "the greatest target of all scholars of the opposing camps." **Signal 3 — search-level aggregation**: independently gives the single-manuscript objection and reports Bhattacharya's conclusion as "1025–1100." **Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `jati.md`, `paramanu-vaisheshika.md`, `prashastapada.md` and `democritus-atom.md`, all written batches earlier, on the universals criteria, the Īśvara-directs-atoms doctrine and the *Kiraṇāvalī*; and `sriharsa.md`, written this batch, on Śrīhira and the *Lakṣaṇāvalī* as the *Khaṇḍana*'s target.

⚠ **An internal contradiction in signal 2, worth naming.** Wikipedia presents Śaka 906 as having "settled scholarly controversies" about Udayana's date **and**, in the same article, cites **D. C. Bhattacharya** approvingly on Udayana's reputation — the same scholar whose whole argument is that Śaka 906 settles nothing. The article carries both without noticing.

**Independence.** Signals 1 and 2 are independent in authorship and kind (a 2014 study vs an encyclopedia article) and **disagree**, which is the finding. Signal 3 corroborates signal 1's manuscript point from a separate route. Signal 4 is the strongest check available: `sriharsa.md`'s Śrīhira detail was written before this node existed, from a source with no stake in Udayana's chronology.

**Why contested.** The date splits by up to a century and a half; the decisive evidence is a single-witness reading; and the two reports of the dissenting scholar's conclusion do not match each other. **Why medium.** The dispute is mapped with each argument attributed and the emendation given in its actual form. **Not high**: **no work of Udayana's was read** — not the *Lakṣaṇāvalī* verse in any edition, not the *Nyāyakusumāñjali*; D. C. Bhattacharyya is reached only through two secondary reports; the *Kiraṇāvalī* material in `jati.md` was likewise never read at first hand; and the manuscript question is left exactly where the sources leave it.
