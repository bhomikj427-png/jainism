## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pārvatī** (Sanskrit *pārvatī*, "she of the mountain," from *parvata* "mountain" — she is the daughter of **Himavān**, the personified Himālaya) is the principal benign form of the Hindu Goddess (**Devī / Śakti**) and the consort of **Śiva**. Where Kālī and Durgā are her fierce, battle-going aspects, Pārvatī herself is the **gentle, nurturing** face: love, beauty, devotion, fertility, and motherhood. Her gentle sub-forms include **Gaurī** ("the fair/golden one"), **Umā**, and **Annapūrṇā** (goddess of nourishment).

**The consort relation (and its theological reach).** Pārvatī is the reincarnation of **Satī**, Śiva's first wife; through prolonged austerity (*tapas*) she wins the ascetic Śiva to marriage — the narrative of Kālidāsa's *Kumārasambhava*. Philosophically she is Śiva's **Śakti**: the activating creative power without which Śiva is inert ("Śiva without Śakti is a corpse [*śava*]" — the common Tantric pun). She is the mother of **Gaṇeśa** and **Kārttikeya/Skanda**.

**Status — consort vs. supreme, the same fork as Śiva/Viṣṇu:**

| frame | Pārvatī's status |
|---|---|
| Popular / Purāṇic (Śaiva) | Śiva's devoted wife; the divine-couple complementarity (masculine puruṣa-pole / feminine prakṛti-pole) |
| Kashmir Śaivism (Trika) | Pārvatī = Śiva's *vimarśa* (self-reflective power); non-dual with Śiva, not a separate person |
| Śāktism | a benign aspect of the **Mahādevī**, who is the supreme reality (Brahman); Śiva is then subordinate to / embedded in the Goddess |

**NOT a fertility-goddess survival only.** The earliest reference, **Umā Haimavatī** in the Kena Upaniṣad (mid-1st millennium BCE), already casts the goddess as the one who *reveals Brahman* to the gods — a knowledge-bestowing role, not merely a mother/consort. The full Pārvatī profile is Purāṇic (4th–13th c.), but the figure is not a late invention.
