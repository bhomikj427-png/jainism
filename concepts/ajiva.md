## Gloss / Divergence map

Ajīva is **non-soul**: the collective term for everything that lacks the defining mark of jīva (upayoga — consciousness). The *Tattvārthasūtra* uses it at two levels:

**As tattva** (TS 1.4): one of the seven fundamental categories (*tattvas*) — jīva, **ajīva**, āsrava, bandha, saṃvara, nirjarā, mokṣa. At this level it covers everything that is not a conscious soul.

**As dravya-class** (TS 5.1, with TS 5.39): the five non-soul *dravyas* (substances) collectively:

| Sanskrit | name | nature | astikāya? |
|---|---|---|---|
| pudgala | matter | has colour/taste/smell/touch; locus of paramāṇus and skandhas | yes |
| dharma | medium of motion | passive enabling condition for movement (not moral dharma) | yes |
| adharma | medium of rest | passive enabling condition for rest (unique to Jain ontology) | yes |
| ākāśa | space | provides locus for all other dravyas | yes |
| kāla | time | enables sequence/change; one atom per space-point, non-combining | no |

**TS 5.1 Sanskrit:** *ajīvakāyā dharmādharmākāśapudgalāḥ* — "The non-soul substances (bodies) — ajīvakāya — are the medium of motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma), the space (ākāśa) and the matter (pudgala)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass)

Kāla is listed separately (TS 5.39: "Time also is a substance") because it has no multiple space-points and thus no *kāya* (extension). Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* (via Vijay K. Jain 2018) explains: "Time is of the extent of one atom and pervades one space-point only."

**Dharma and adharma as motion-media** are ontologically distinctive to Jainism — no other major Indian school posits separate eternal substances enabling motion and rest. They are passive conditions (*nimitta-kāraṇa*), not active causes: like water enabling a fish to swim, not pushing it.

The negative definition — "the characteristic mark (lakṣaṇa) of jīva is absent" (Pūjyapāda, via Vijay K. Jain 2018) — is the only definition needed because jīva is defined first by upayoga; everything without upayoga is ajīva.
