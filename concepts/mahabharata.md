## Gloss

The **Mahābhārata** is the longer of the two Sanskrit *itihāsa* ("thus it was," i.e. history/legend) epics, narrating the dynastic struggle between two branches of the Kuru family — the five **Pāṇḍavas** and the hundred **Kauravas** — culminating in the eighteen-day Kurukṣetra war. Traditionally ascribed to the sage **Vyāsa** (Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana), who is himself a character within it, the epic is in fact a layered redaction compiled over roughly **400 BCE – 400 CE**, reaching its received form by the early Gupta period. Tradition recognises three growth stages:

| stage | verses | reciter |
|---|---|---|
| *Jaya* ("victory") | 8,800 | Vyāsa |
| *Bhārata* | 24,000 | Vaiśaṃpāyana |
| *Mahābhārata* | ~100,000 | Ugraśravas Sauti |

The received text has **~100,000 ślokas** in **18 parvas** (books) plus a *khila* (the *Harivaṃśa* appendix) — about four times the length of the *Rāmāyaṇa* and roughly ten times the *Iliad* and *Odyssey* combined.

**Why it matters for this project:** the Mahābhārata is the textual *container* for two nodes already in the corpus. The **Bhagavad Gītā** sits inside the *Bhīṣma Parva* (Book 6, chs. 25–42), and the **Viṣṇu-sahasranāma** inside the *Anuśāsana Parva* (Book 13). The longest book, the *Śānti Parva* (Book 12), is Bhīṣma's didactic instruction to Yudhiṣṭhira on *dharma*, kingship, and the four *puruṣārthas* (goals of life) — making the epic as much a *dharmaśāstra*-style teaching vehicle as a narrative.

**Genre caution.** Indian tradition calls it the "fifth Veda" and classes it as *smṛti* ("remembered," human-authored) — categorically distinct from *śruti* (the Vedas, "heard"/revealed). Calling the Mahābhārata a "Veda" is an honorific about authority and scope, **not** a claim that it is *śruti*. It is *itihāsa* + *kāvya* (epic poetry), not revelation.
