## Gloss / Divergence map

***Dhātu*** ("that which sustains / holds together," from √*dhā*, to place, support) is Āyurveda's term for the **seven bodily tissues** — the *sapta-dhātu* — that food becomes. Where [[tridosha|tridoṣa]] answers *what regulates the body*, dhātu answers *what the body is made of and how it is built*, and the two together carry almost all of classical Āyurvedic physiology. This node closes the gap Ch 23 named as "a hole in the chapter."

### The seven, in order

The order is not a list but a **production sequence**: each dhātu is formed *from* its predecessor.

| # | dhātu | conventional rendering | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ***rasa*** | plasma / lymph / chyle | "the substratum formed just after the digestion of food. The main function of this Dhātu is nourishment" |
| 2 | ***rakta*** | blood | "formed from the former Dhātu, Rasa Dhātu" |
| 3 | ***māṃsa*** | muscle | "the main function of covering the bones" |
| 4 | ***medas*** | fat / adipose | |
| 5 | ***asthi*** | bone | |
| 6 | ***majjā*** | marrow (bone **and** spinal) | |
| 7 | ***śukra*** | reproductive tissue / semen | the terminal dhātu of the chain |
| (8) | ***ojas*** | vigour, vitality | "known as the **eighth Dhātu, or Mahādhātu** (superior, or great dhātu)" — the refined residue left once the seventh is fully nourished; classical statements make it the seat of immunity, strength and clarity |

⚠️ **The English renderings above are conventional, not equivalences.** *Rasa* is not "lymph," *śukra* is not "semen" simpliciter, and *majjā* covers what modern anatomy separates into two tissues. The corpus uses them as labels and does not license inference from them (§0).

### *Dhātv-agni*: the mechanism

Transformation is driven by **[[agni-ayurveda|agni]]**, and the Caraka tradition splits agni into **thirteen**: one *jāṭharāgni* (digestive), **five *bhūtāgni*** — *pārthiva, āpya, taijasa, vāyavya, nābhasa*, one per [[pancha-mahabhuta|mahābhūta]] — and **seven *dhātv-agni***, one resident in each tissue (*rasāgni, raktāgni, māṃsāgni, medo-agni, asthy-agni, majjāgni, śukrāgni*). Each dhātv-agni cooks the incoming material into its own tissue and passes the remainder on.

The regulatory claim is explicit and quantitative in form:

> "The small portions of agni are present in each dhātu. **The increase or decrease of these agni leads to depletion or overproduction of dhātu respectively**" [A. Hṛ. Sūtra Sthāna 11/24]

and agni is made the condition of everything downstream:

> "Sustenance of life, complexion, strength, health, nourishment, luster, **ojas**, teja and prāṇa depend on the status of agni" [Ca. Sa. Cikitsā Sthāna 15/3]

Note the **inverse relation** in the first quotation — more tissue-fire yields *less* tissue, because the fire consumes what it processes. That is a real structural claim about a regulated system, and it is the sort of thing worth recording precisely *because* it is easy to garble into a vague "fire is good."

### Three competing models of how tissues are nourished

The most interesting thing about dhātu theory is that classical Āyurveda **did not settle on one mechanism**. Three *dhātu-poṣaṇa-nyāya*s ("maxims of tissue-nourishment") are transmitted, credited in the standard account to **Cakrapāṇidatta**, Caraka's commentator. They are genuinely different theories, and the tradition uses them as complementary rather than adjudicating between them:

| nyāya | image | claim | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***kṣīra-dadhi-nyāya*** — the milk-and-curd maxim | milk → curd → buttermilk → butter → ghee → *ghṛta-manda* | **total conversion** (*sarvātma-pariṇāma*): each dhātu is *entirely* converted into the next | a strictly serial pipeline: nothing reaches dhātu 5 that was not first dhātu 4. Explains sequence; struggles to explain why all seven persist at once |
| ***kedārī-kulyā-nyāya*** — the field-and-canal maxim | irrigation channels feeding successive paddy fields | **transmission**: one nutrient stream is carried through channels and fills each tissue in turn | a distribution network — vessels, a pump, an order of supply. Explains simultaneity and locality; makes the *channels* (*srotas*) the thing that can fail |
| ***khale-kapota-nyāya*** — the threshing-floor-and-pigeons maxim | pigeons flying from one granary to their own homes, each taking its own grain | **selection**: each dhātv-agni **draws from a common nutrient pool exactly what its own tissue requires** | a demand-driven model: no queue at all, and tissue failure is a failure of *selection*, not of supply |

The corpus records this as **contested-by-design**: three incompatible mechanisms held simultaneously, each doing explanatory work the others cannot. That is a real feature of the tradition's method, and it is closer to how a mature descriptive science tolerates multiple models than to a doctrine with one answer.

### A §0 warning taken from the source itself

The Caraka-Saṃhitā-Online page consulted here states, in the same paragraph as its sourced classification, that jāṭharāgni "is responsible for all amylolytic, proteolytic and lipolytic enzymes secreted by various exocrine glands." **That is a modern identification, not a classical claim, and the corpus does not adopt it.** The site is used for its verse citations and its thirteen-fold division; its enzyme gloss is left where it was found. This is exactly the failure mode §4 warns about — a source can be reliable for a term and unreliable in the next clause — and it is worth flagging here because *dhātu* and *dhātv-agni* are among the most aggressively over-translated concepts in the whole corpus. The honest statement is that Āyurveda has **a sequential, fire-driven, tissue-specific theory of bodily construction from food**, which is a substantive proto-physiology, and that saying so does not require it to be biochemistry.

### ⚠ Contested from Batch 43: three rival mechanisms, or four cooperating ones?

This file's central finding — that classical Āyurveda transmits **incompatible** nourishment mechanisms simultaneously, contested *by design* — was challenged by evidence gathered for [[cakrapanidatta]]. A peer-reviewed review (Sharma et al., *IJRAR* 2024), read with its endnotes, reports **four** nyāyas rather than three (adding *eka-kāla dhātu-poṣaṇa*, credited to **Aruṇadatta** on the *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya*), and states the opposite relation between them: "the Dhātu Poṣaṇa Nyāya function as a cohesive group… they do not conflict or interfere with one another," with *eka-kāla* as the frame in which the other three run at once.

Two further facts bear on it. (a) The per-nyāya citations are to **different** authorities — Cakrapāṇi on Caraka for *kṣīra-dadhi* and *khale-kapota*, **Suśruta himself** for *kedārī-kulyā*, Aruṇadatta for *eka-kāla* — so the set is assembled across three commentarial traditions. (b) The **enumeration as a numbered set of theories** is footnoted not to a classical text but to a 2017 *Kriyā Śārīra* textbook, i.e. it is a modern pedagogical taxonomy laid over genuinely classical images.

The corpus does **not** retire the finding above, and does not adopt the new one either. The disagreement turns on a question neither source asks: whether the classical texts ever offered these as *alternatives*, or whether the rivalry is an artefact of the same modern scheme that numbered them. Resolving it requires reading the Āyurvedadīpikā at Ci. 15:20 directly — recorded as this node's named upgrade path.

3. **Search aggregation across the Āyurvedic academic press** (*Journal of Ayurveda and Integrated Medical Sciences*, "A comprehensive guide to Dhātuposhana Nyāyas" and "Physiological study of Kedārī Kulyā Nyāya"; a Zenodo record on *Dhātu Poṣaṇa Nyāya*; a ResearchGate item on *Kṣīra-dadhi Nyāya*): all three nyāyas by name with their images and claims, and the *dhātv-agni*-driven sequence.
4. **easyayurveda.com** (fetched, practitioner-facing): the attribution of the three-nyāya scheme to **Cakrapāṇidatta's** Caraka commentary; *kṣīra-dadhi* as *sarvātma-pariṇāma* ("total bio-conversion"); the canal-irrigation and the selective-drawing formulations. **Explicitly checked and found lacking** shloka citations and any attribution to Suśruta, Vāgbhaṭa or Ḍalhaṇa — so the Cakrapāṇidatta attribution rests on one derivative source and is reported as such.
5. **Lexicon/morphology** (§4 signal 3): *dhātu* from √*dhā* "to place, support," hence "constituent, layer, that which sustains" — the same word doing service in Sanskrit grammar for a **verbal root** and in Buddhist Abhidharma for an **element/sphere**. The shared word is not a shared concept, and the map records that split.

**CONTESTED** — but of an unusual kind: not a scholarly disagreement about what the texts say, rather **the tradition itself transmitting three mechanisms for one process**. Converged on the seven dhātus, their order, ojas as the eighth, and the thirteen-agni scheme. Confidence **medium**: signal 1 carries real verse references but was read through a summary rather than in an edition; the nyāya attribution rests on signal 4 alone; and neither the Caraka nor the Suśruta passage on the *sapta-dhātu* themselves (as opposed to on agni) was fetched. The **upadhātus** (secondary tissues) and the exact *dhātu-mala* correspondences are **not covered here** — see [[mala]].
