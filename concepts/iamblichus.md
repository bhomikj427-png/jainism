## Gloss / Divergence map

**Iamblichus** of Chalcis (c. 242 – c. 325 CE) was a Syrian Neoplatonist, pupil of [[porphyry|Porphyry]], who founded an influential school at Apamea. His signature move was to subordinate philosophical contemplation to **theurgy** (*theourgia*, "godlike work") — ritual practice (prayer, sacrifice, divination) understood as divine action that lets the embodied soul receive grace and ascend. Against the intellectualism of Plotinus and Porphyry, he held that contemplation alone cannot save, because the human soul is **fully descended** into body (rejecting Plotinus's "undescended soul," the doctrine that part of the soul remains always in the intelligible). His major surviving work, the *Reply to Porphyry* (*De Mysteriis*), written in the voice of the Egyptian priest "Abamon," defends pagan ritual against Porphyry's over-confident rationalism. He distinguished three disciplines — philosophy, theology, theurgy — and decisively shaped later Neoplatonism (Syrianus, Proclus).

**Honest contestation.** Whether Iamblichus *originated* the henad doctrine (via his "law of mean terms") or whether it belongs to Syrianus is unresolved in the sources — see [[proclus-henad]]. Recorded as contested, not asserted.
