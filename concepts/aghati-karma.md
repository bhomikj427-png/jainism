## Gloss

***Aghāti-karma*** ("non-destructive karma") is the second family of the eight karma species ([[karma-prakriti]]): the four that **do not obscure any intrinsic power of the soul** and instead fix the *conditions of embodiment* — how long you live, in what kind of body, in what social station, and with what balance of pleasure and pain. The classical formulation is exact about the contrast: the aghātiyā karmas "do not obscure the real attributes of the soul, but affect only the external environments of the soul and pertain to the body," and they are "responsible for the reborn soul's physical and mental circumstances, longevity, spiritual potential and experience of pleasant and unpleasant sensations."

This is the family that makes Jain soteriology **two-staged rather than one-staged**, and that is the whole reason the category needs its own name.

### The four, with their sūtras

| karma | what it fixes | sub-types | Tattvārtha sūtra |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***vedanīya*** — feeling-producing | pleasure and pain as *sensation* | **2** — *sāta* ("causes a feeling of pleasure, created, e.g. by licking something sweet") and *asāta* ("causes the feeling of pain, created, e.g. if one is hurt by a sword") | TS 8.8 |
| ***āyuḥ*** — life-determining | the length of *this* life and the realm it is lived in | **4** — *naraka* (infernal), *tiryañc* (plant and animal), *manuṣya* (human), *deva* (celestial) | **TS 8.10**, fetched: *nārakatairyagyonamānuṣadaivāni* |
| ***nāma*** — physique-making / name | body, form, sense-class, gait, symmetry, colour, and much else — *and* Tīrthaṅkarahood | **42** in the sūtra; **93** in the later systematised list (see below) | **TS 8.11**, fetched: the long compound running *gati-jāti-śarīra-…-yaśaḥkīrti-setarāṇi tīrthakaratvaṃ ca* |
| ***gotra*** — status-determining | the family one is born into | **2** — **TS 8.12**, fetched: *uccairnīcaiśca*; Pūjyapāda glosses high status as birth "in a high or noble family of great respectability and prestige" and low status as birth "in a low-status family, lacking in prestige and respectability" | TS 8.12 |

### The divergence the fetched sūtras expose: 42 vs 93

The standard modern statement of Jain karma theory gives **148 *uttara-prakṛtis*** (sub-types): 5 *jñānāvaraṇa* + 9 *darśanāvaraṇa* + 2 *vedanīya* + 28 *mohanīya* + 4 *āyuḥ* + **93 *nāma*** + 2 *gotra* + 5 *antarāya*.

But **TS 8.11 as fetched enumerates 42** for *nāma*: 21 primary subdivisions (*gati, jāti, śarīra, aṅgopāṅga, nirmāṇa, bandhana, saṃghāta, saṃsthāna, saṃhanana, sparśa, rasa, gandha, varṇa, ānupūrvya, agurulaghu, upaghāta, paraghāta, ātapa, udyota, ucchvāsa, vihāyogati*), 20 more as **ten paired opposites** (*pratyeka-śarīra / trasa / subhaga / susvara / śubha / sūkṣma / paryāpti / sthira / ādeya / yaśaḥkīrti*, each with *setara*, "and their opposites"), and **1 more: *tīrthakaratva*** — Tīrthaṅkarahood itself as a species of body-making karma.

Every other count the corpus has checked against the sūtra matches the systematised list — *vedanīya* 2, *āyuḥ* 4, *gotra* 2, *mohanīya* 28 ([[mohaniya]], TS 8.9 fetched), *jñānāvaraṇa* 5 (TS 8.6). **The entire 148-vs-sūtra gap is inside *nāma*, and it is 93 − 42 = 51.** The corpus does **not** call one figure right: 42 is what the root sūtra as translated enumerates, 93 is what the later karma-grantha systematisation reaches by expanding the compound categories (e.g. *gati* into its four, *jāti* into its five, *śarīra* into its five, the colours into five, the tastes into five, and so on). The point to hold is structural, and it is a §0 point: **the numbers most often quoted for Jain karma theory are the commentarial tradition's, not the root text's.**

### Why this family, and not the *ghāti* family, is the interesting one

Destroying the four *ghāti* karmas produces **omniscience**, not liberation. The four *aghāti* karmas survive that event and keep the body running. Hence the two-tier holy-being scheme the corpus already records:

- an **[[arihant]]** (*sayoga-kevalin*) has destroyed all four *ghāti* karmas — [[kevala-jnana|kevala-jñāna]] has dawned — but still carries *vedanīya*, *āyuḥ*, *nāma* and *gotra*, so still has a body, a lifespan, sensations and a social identity, and still teaches;
- a **[[siddha]]** is what remains when the *aghāti* karmas are exhausted at death: "as soon as the aghātiyā karmas are exhausted" the soul is liberated.

So the *aghāti* karmas are precisely **the difference between the omniscient teacher and the liberated soul** — the residue that keeps an enlightened being in the world long enough to be of use to it. A tradition without this category would have to make omniscience and liberation the same event, and would lose its account of why a Tīrthaṅkara preaches at all.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:

1. ***Tīrthaṅkara-nāma-karma* is a karma.** Being a ford-maker "procures the position of a ford-maker" and sits inside the body-making karma, one item among forty-two. The highest office in Jainism is, technically, **a species of bondage** — favourable, still *pudgala*, still to be shed. This is a sharper claim than the usual "even good karma binds," because it locates the tradition's own supreme figure inside the machinery it is trying to dismantle. Compare [[punya]].
2. **The *aghāti* karmas cannot be attacked by knowledge.** The *ghāti* karmas fall to insight; the *aghāti* karmas fall only when they run out. This is why the last stages of the [[gunasthana|guṇasthāna]] ladder are not about seeing more clearly but about **stopping activity** — and why the residual influx of an omniscient being needs its own doctrine (see [[iryapathika-asrava]]).
