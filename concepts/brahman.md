## Gloss / Divergence map

In Advaita Vedānta, Brahman is **the sole ultimate reality** — the one, non-dual, absolute ground of all existence, from which everything else is an appearance. Brahman is not a God among others; it is the one and only ultimately real thing.

**Sat-Cit-Ānanda** — the essential nature of Brahman (not its attributes but its very being):
- *Sat*: pure being/existence — Brahman is; it cannot not-be
- *Cit*: pure consciousness — Brahman is awareness itself
- *Ānanda*: pure bliss — Brahman's nature is uncaused, unconditional bliss

(Source: search-result aggregation, shankaracharya.org, Quora scholarly thread.)

**Nirguṇa vs. Saguṇa Brahman:**
- *Nirguṇa* ("without qualities"): Brahman as-it-is — formless, impersonal, beyond time, space, and causality; what Śaṅkara calls the ultimate referent of the mahāvākyas
- *Saguṇa* (= Īśvara, "with qualities"): Brahman as perceived from the standpoint of devotion — personal God who creates, sustains, destroys; the same Brahman, viewed through the devotee's frame

Śaṅkara holds that nirguṇa Brahman is the higher truth; saguṇa/Īśvara is a pragmatic lower-truth appearance for those not yet ready for full non-dual realisation.

**A three-way ontological map** (a useful summary from search-result scholarship):

> "Vedānta recognises substances but not process. Buddhism recognises process but not substance. **Jainism pays equal attention to both substance (*dravya*) and process (*paryāya*).**" (Reported via jainworld.com scholarly essay; not independently verified to a named author this run.)

| tradition | ontological stance | ultimate reality |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita Vedānta | one substance; process is māyā | Brahman (Sat-Cit-Ānanda) |
| Theravāda Buddhism | process only; no enduring substance | conditioned arising (pratītyasamutpāda); nirvāṇa = unconditioned |
| Jainism | multiple substances (6 dravyas) + real processes (paryāya) | no single ultimate; all six dravyas co-equally real |

**Brahman vs. Śūnyatā (Buddhist emptiness):**
- Advaita: affirms an eternal, positive, conscious ultimate (Brahman = Sat-Cit-Ānanda)
- Buddhism: śūnyatā = all dharmas are empty of inherent existence; no positive eternal substrate; the ultimate is unconditioned emptiness
- These are structurally opposite: Advaita fills the ultimate positively; Buddhism empties it of substance
