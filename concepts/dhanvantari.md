## Gloss / Divergence map

**Dhanvantari** is the **physician of the gods** and the presiding deity of **Āyurveda** (Indian medicine). He is the figure who **rises last from the [[samudra-manthana|Samudra-Manthana]]**, bearing the pot of **[[amrita|amṛta]]** — the moment that precipitates the deva–asura struggle for the nectar. He is reckoned an **avatāra of [[vishnu|Viṣṇu]]**; iconographically four-armed, holding conch, discus, the amṛta-pot, and medicinal herbs (or a leech). A parallel tradition gives him an **earthly incarnation as a king of Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)**, ancestor of Divodāsa, who is named in the Suśruta Saṃhitā tradition as a founder of surgery — linking the churning-born god to the historical transmission of Āyurveda.

**Variant (recorded, not reconciled).** Sources differ on the celestial-vs-earthly Dhanvantari relation: whether the Kāśī king *is* the descended deity or a separate Dhanvantari in his lineage; the Suśruta frame names Divodāsa-Dhanvantari as the teacher of surgery.

Two sources agree on the signature churning-emergence and the physician role → **converged**, confidence **medium** (the divine-vs-mortal Dhanvantari relation and the Āyurvedic lineage details are tradition-dependent).
