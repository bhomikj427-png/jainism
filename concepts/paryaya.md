## Gloss / Divergence map

Paryāya is **mode/modification** — the transient, ever-changing aspect of a substance (dravya), in contrast to the permanent *guṇa* (quality). Every dravya has both: guṇas are what it always is; paryāyas are what it is now, briefly, before changing.

**TS 5.38 Sanskrit:** *guṇaparyāyavat dravyam* — "That which has qualities (guṇa) and modes (paryāya) is a substance (dravya)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (via Vijay K. Jain 2018):
- **Guṇa** exhibits *anvaya* (association/continuity) with the substance — permanent, inseparable
- **Paryāya** exhibits *vyatireka* (distinction/discontinuity) — represents modification of the substance; arises and ceases

**Examples from Pūjyapāda:**
- *Paryāyas of soul*: "knowledge of the pitcher, knowledge of the cloth, anger, pride" — each is a momentary cognitive or affective mode of the jīva
- *Paryāyas of matter*: "intense or mild odour, colour variations" — a piece of wood burning changes its paryāyas (colour, smell) while remaining pudgala
- *Guṇas of soul*: knowledge-capacity itself (the permanent attribute; specific instances of knowing are paryāyas)

**The dravya/guṇa/paryāya triad** — these three are not separate entities but three analytical aspects of any single existing substance:
- *Dravya* = the substance as such (the persisting substrate)
- *Guṇa* = the permanent, inalienable qualities that constitute its essential nature
- *Paryāya* = the transient modifications that arise and pass moment by moment

**Connection to TS 5.30** (the existence/sat definition — fetched for dravya.md): *utpāda-vyaya-dhrauvya-yuktam sat* — "existence is with origination, decay, and permanence." This maps directly onto the triad: **origination** = new paryāya arising; **decay** = old paryāya ceasing; **permanence** = dravya and its guṇas continuing. Every moment in the universe is paryāya-change on a guṇa-dravya background.

**Connection to anekāntavāda and the seven nayas:**
The seven nayas split into *dravyārthika* (substance-focused) and *paryāyārthika* (mode-focused) nayas precisely because of this distinction:
- *Dravyārthika nayas* (saṃgraha, vyavahāra, naigama) foreground the guṇa/dravya dimension — what is permanent
- *Paryāyārthika nayas* (ṛjusūtra, śabda, samabhirūḍha, evaṃbhūta) foreground the paryāya dimension — what is momentary/specific

Anekāntavāda's claim that a substance is both permanent and impermanent is grounded here: permanent with respect to guṇa/dravya; impermanent with respect to paryāya. Neither standpoint alone captures the whole.

**Connection to Buddhist pratītyasamutpāda**: Buddhism's analysis focuses on process (paryāya-like change with no underlying dravya-substrate). Jainism's distinctive claim is that paryāya-change *requires* a dravya-guṇa substrate — change without a substance is incoherent. This is the anti-Buddhist realist move in Jain metaphysics.
