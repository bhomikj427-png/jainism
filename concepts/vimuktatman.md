## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vimuktātman** is the author of the ***Iṣṭasiddhi***, one of the **four traditional *siddhi*s** of Advaita Vedānta — the set being [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]]'s *Brahmasiddhi*, [[sureshvara|Sureśvara]]'s *Naiṣkarmyasiddhi*, this, and [[madhusudana-sarasvati|Madhusūdana Sarasvatī]]'s *Advaitasiddhi*. **All four authors are now in this corpus.** For him, "absolute Reality is **pure intuitive consciousness**"; the *Iṣṭasiddhi* is remembered for an "exhaustive analysis of the theories of error" mounted as a prelude to establishing the world's inexplicability, and for a doctrine of the ***pañcamaprakāra-avidyā-nivṛtti***, a "fifth mode" of the removal of nescience.

⚠ **He was queued as a *test*, not as a fact.** Batch 45 listed him as "Ānandabodha's alleged teacher — **single-sourced**, so writing him is also the test." `anandabodha.md` recorded that web aggregation makes Ānandabodha his disciple while **Dasgupta, the one scholarly history consulted, does not** — and declined to assert it. **The test has now been run. The claim does not survive.**

### ⭐ The test result: the discipleship fails on every available dating

To be a discipleship, Vimuktātman must precede Ānandabodha by roughly one generation. Here is what the sources actually give:

| Vimuktātman | source |
|---|---|
| **10th century**, "younger contemporary of Sarvajñātman" | advaita-vedanta.org, *Post-Śaṅkaran Authors* |
| **10th century**, grouped with Prakāśātman and Sarvajñātman | Wikipedia, *History of Advaita Vedanta* — **its chronological list** |
| "last quarter of the tenth and the first quarter of the eleventh century"; **c. 1050** | search-level aggregation |
| **c. 1200 CE** | Wikipedia, *History of Advaita Vedanta* — **its prose**, citing Nakamura 1950 and Dasgupta 1955 |

| Ānandabodha | source |
|---|---|
| **11th–12th c.**, with Tripathi's *c.* 1200 in a footnote | Dasgupta, via `anandabodha.md` |
| **13th century** | advaita-vedanta.org, *Post-Śaṅkaran Authors* |

**Now cross them:**

- Vimuktātman 10th c. + Ānandabodha 11th–12th c. → a gap of **one to two centuries**. Not a discipleship.
- Vimuktātman 10th c. + Ānandabodha 13th c. → a gap of **three centuries**. Not a discipleship. ⚠ **And these two datings come from the same page of the same site** — the one that the aggregation layer credits with the claim.
- Vimuktātman *c.* 1200 + Ānandabodha 11th–12th c. → **the relation inverts**: the alleged teacher becomes the later man.

**There is no combination of the available dates on which "Ānandabodha was Vimuktātman's disciple" works.** ⚠ This is a **negative** result and is stated as one: it shows the claim is unsupported and chronologically obstructed by every source consulted, **not** that some source refutes it. **`anandabodha.md` was right to decline it, and now has a reason rather than a caution.**

⚠ **A methodological note worth keeping.** The claim reached the corpus through *search-level aggregation* that named advaita-vedanta.org among its sources. **Read directly, that site's own page says no such thing** — it gives Vimuktātman no *guru* and no pupils, and puts the two men three centuries apart. **An aggregator's attribution to a site is not the site's claim**, and checking the named source was enough to dissolve it.

### The divergence II — a source that contradicts itself in one article

⚠ Wikipedia's *History of Advaita Vedanta* gives **"c. 1200 CE"** for Vimuktātman in its prose and lists him under **"Prakāsātman, Vimuktātman, Sarvajñātman (10th century)"** in its own chronology — **a two-century contradiction inside one article.**

**This is the third time in Batch 46 that the encyclopedia layer has been caught contradicting itself:** `gangesha.md` (13th c. in one Wikipedia article, 14th in another, with a fossilised 12th-c. mirror still live); `jayatirtha.md` (a hagiography attributed to a "direct disciple" born sixty years after its subject died); and now this. **The pattern is not that these sources are unreliable in detail but that they are unreliable *as a set of witnesses*: they do not check themselves across articles, so sampling several of them is not corroboration.** That is the same finding `gangesha.md` states about date-strata, arrived at by a different route.

### What is solid

**Not the biography — the book.** Where the dates and the discipleship dissolve, the textual record is firm and verifiable:

> ***Iṣṭa-Siddhi of Vimuktātman, with extracts from the Vivaraṇa of Jñānottama***, edited by **Mysore Hiriyanna**, **Gaekwad's Oriental Series No. LXV**, Baroda Oriental Institute, **1933**, 754 pp.

Metadata read directly from the archive.org record. **M. Hiriyanna is a scholar of the first rank and Gaekwad's Oriental Series is a critical-edition series with apparatus** — this is the strongest provenance available for anything in this file, and it is provenance for the *text*, not the man.

⚠ **The edition was pursued and could not be read.** Its OCR was downloaded and inspected: unusable garbled Devanāgarī, no Latin characters, so not even the editor's English introduction is recoverable from the scan. **The two questions this file leaves open — Vimuktātman's date, and whether Jñānottama is Citsukha's teacher — are almost certainly answered on a page that exists, is public, and cannot be read by these means.** Recorded as a stopped line of enquiry with its cause (§8), not as a gap.

⚠⚠ **And the commentator's name closes a circuit inside this corpus.** The *Vivaraṇa* on the *Iṣṭasiddhi* is by **Jñānottama**. `citsukha.md` — written in an earlier batch, **converged**, with **Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum*** confirming the lineage — records that [[citsukha|Citsukha]] (*c.* 1220) was the **pupil of Gauḍeśvarācārya, also called Jñānottama**.

**If that is one man, then Citsukha's own teacher wrote the standard commentary on this book** — and the *Iṣṭasiddhi* sits directly upstream of the corpus's whole Advaita-dialectic cluster (`citsukha`, `sriharsa`, `anandabodha`). ⚠ **"If" is doing real work.** This batch has already logged **three** name-collisions — Vācaspati Miśra I/II, two Harirāma Tarkavāgīśas, two Vyāsatīrthas — and the lesson recorded there applies here: *in this literature, "same name" is weak evidence of "same person."* **The identification is flagged as a lead to check in Hiriyanna's introduction, not asserted.**

### His position in the school

Wikipedia: "Vimuktātman's school of thought was eventually **replaced by [[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]]'s Vivaraṇa school**." ⚠ Note the irony in the vocabulary, which no source remarks on: the *Iṣṭasiddhi*'s own commentary is a *Vivaraṇa*, and the school that displaced him is *the* Vivaraṇa school — named for Prakāśātman's *Vivaraṇa* on [[padmapada|Padmapāda]]'s *Pañcapādikā*. **Two different *vivaraṇa*s; the word is a genre-name, not a school-name, except when it is.** Recorded so the corpus does not merge them.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — archive.org metadata for the Hiriyanna edition, read directly.** The only hard, checkable datum in the file: title, editor, series, publisher, date, extent. It fixes the text and its commentator; it says nothing about the author.

**Signal 2 — advaita-vedanta.org, *Post-Śaṅkaran Authors*, read directly rather than through aggregation.** Vimuktātman 10th c., younger contemporary of Sarvajñātman, **no guru and no pupils named**; Ānandabodha 13th c.; Citsukha 13th c. with **Jñānottama** as guru; Prakāśātman as founder of the Vivaraṇa sub-tradition. ⚠ **This is the site the aggregation layer credited with the discipleship claim, and it does not make it.**

**Signal 3 — Wikipedia, *History of Advaita Vedanta*.** "c. 1200 CE"; "pure intuitive consciousness"; the four traditional *siddhi*s; the replacement by the Vivaraṇa school; a chronological list that **contradicts its own prose**; and the citation of **Nakamura 1950** and **Dasgupta 1955**.

**Signal 4 — search-level aggregation.** "c. 1050 AD"; "flourished between the last quarter of the tenth and the first quarter of the eleventh century"; the *pañcamaprakāra-avidyā-nivṛtti*; the "deep and exhaustive analysis of the theories of error."

**Signal 5 — corpus-internal, and the reason this node was worth writing.** `anandabodha.md` (the discipleship claim, already declined, now disposed of), `citsukha.md` (Jñānottama = Gauḍeśvarācārya, on Aufrecht's authority), `sarvajnatman.md` (which **independently** records Wikipedia listing him "with Prakāśātman and Vimuktātman" in the 10th c.), `prakasatman.md`, `mandana-mishra.md`, `sureshvara.md`, `madhusudana-sarasvati.md`.

**Independence.** Signals 2, 3 and 4 disagree about the date by up to **two and a half centuries** and are not independent in the way that matters — signal 4 is aggregation over signals like 2 and 3. What is genuinely independent is signal 1 (a 1933 critical edition's catalogue record) and signal 5 (`citsukha.md`, written batches ago from Dasgupta, Hindupedia and Aufrecht, with no knowledge of the *Iṣṭasiddhi*). **The Jñānottama convergence is the only thing in this file that two unrelated evidential routes both support** — and it is still only a name-match, so it is flagged rather than asserted.

**Why contested.** The date splits across 10th c. / c. 1050 / c. 1200, with one source holding two of those positions at once; the discipleship that motivated the node fails on every dating; and the one promising identification is a bare name-match in a literature where this batch has already found three name-collisions. **Why medium.** The text, its editor and its series are verified from a catalogue record; the negative result is solid because it was reached by reading the named source directly. **Not high**: **the *Iṣṭasiddhi* was not read**, in Sanskrit or otherwise, though **Hiriyanna's 1933 edition is freely available and was located** (see below); Nakamura and Dasgupta 1955 are known only as Wikipedia's citations; the doctrinal statements — "pure intuitive consciousness," the *pañcamaprakāra*, the theories of error — are all one-line reports from non-specialist sources; and `term_devanagari` is **omitted** rather than guessed (§3).
