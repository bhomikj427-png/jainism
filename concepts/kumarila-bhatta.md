## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** (fl. **~7th c. CE; ~660 CE**) is the founder of the **Bhāṭṭa** sub-school of Pūrva Mīmāṃsā — "those Mīmāṃsakas who take their bearings from Kumārila are styled **Bhāṭṭas** (followers of Bhaṭṭa)." He is one of the two great commentators (with [[prabhakara]]) who built on **Śabara's Śābarabhāṣya**; his three works — the **Ślokavārttika** ("critical commentary in verse"), **Tantravārttika**, and **Ṭupṭīkā** — are subcommentaries on Śabara's commentary on Jaimini's [[mimamsa-sutra|Mīmāṃsāsūtra]]. He is the author-anchor behind the epistemology file [[mimamsa-pramana]].

**Svataḥ-prāmāṇya — intrinsic validity (his signature thesis).** Kumārila's central epistemological doctrine, introduced in the Ślokavārttika, is **svataḥ prāmāṇya**: a cognition is **valid intrinsically**, by its own arising, and is to be accepted as true *unless and until* it is defeated — because "any demonstration must itself presuppose reliable ways of knowing," so validity cannot be conferred from outside without regress. He deploys this to argue that "a commitment to the uniquely authoritative status of the Vedas [is] rationally held."

**Defence of the authorless Veda.** Kumārila is the great systematic defender of **apauruṣeyatva**: "the absence of an author would safeguard the Veda against all reproach" — an eternal, unauthored Veda needs no fallible (human or divine) source to validate it, dovetailing with svataḥ-prāmāṇya.

**The Buddhist duel.** Kumārila mounted "sophisticated attacks on Buddhist epistemology, particularly against **Dignāga**," defending Vedic realism against Buddhist idealism and *apoha* semantics — a confrontation often credited (probably over-credited) with contributing to Buddhism's decline in India. The doctrinal contrast is the inverse of Dignāga's: intrinsic-validity realism vs constructed, exclusion-based cognition (see [[dignaga-pramana]]).

**Bhāṭṭa vs Prābhākara.** The split from [[prabhakara]] is partly semantic: Bhāṭṭas hold **abhihitānvaya** (sentence-meaning is *built up* from the separately-denoted meanings of individual words), Prābhākaras **anvitābhidhāna** (words denote meaning only as already *syntactically connected*).
