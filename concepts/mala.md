## Gloss

***Mala*** — from √*mṛj*, "that which is to be cleaned or eliminated" — is Āyurveda's category for **the waste side of bodily transformation**: the by-products thrown off at every stage where food is turned into tissue. It is the necessary complement of [[dhatu]]: the same *dhātv-agni* that produces a refined tissue also produces a residue, and classical Āyurveda tracks both.

The category divides in two:

| class | members |
|---|---|
| ***śārīrika mala*** — the gross body-wastes, the **tri-mala** | ***purīṣa*** (faeces) · ***mūtra*** (urine) · ***sveda*** (sweat) |
| ***dhātu-mala*** (*kiṭṭa*) — the residue thrown off by each of the seven tissues | one per dhātu, listed below |

### The dhātu-malas — and the doctrine hiding in the first two rows

| dhātu | its mala |
|---|---|
| **rasa** | ***kapha*** |
| **rakta** | ***pitta*** |
| māṃsa | *kha-mala* — the excreta of the orifices (ear, nose) |
| medas | *sveda* (sweat) |
| asthi | *keśa* and *loma* — scalp and body hair |
| majjā | *akṣi-tvac-viṭ-sneha* — the unctuousness of eye, skin and stool |
| śukra | (not specified in the source consulted) |

**Two of the three doṣas appear here as tissue wastes.** *Kapha* is the residue of *rasa*; *pitta* is the residue of *rakta*. This is not a marginal detail — it means that on the *dhātu-mala* analysis, the [[tridosha|tridoṣa]] that regulate the body are *simultaneously* products of the body's own metabolism, and the neat popular picture of "three humours governing seven tissues" is, from this angle, the wrong way round. The corpus records this as a **real internal complication** of Āyurvedic physiology, not a contradiction: the classical texts operate both descriptions and do not treat them as rivals. (The third doṣa, *vāta*, has no dhātu-mala row — worth noting rather than explaining away, and a specific thing to check on any upgrade pass.)

### The claim that makes *mala* interesting

The tradition explicitly refuses to treat malas as mere refuse:

> "**A certain amount of waste products is needed for maintaining the proper functioning of the body.**"

Malas *support* (the *dhāraṇa* function) as well as leave. Sweat maintains skin; the unctuous residues keep surfaces working; even faeces and urine have a retentive role while resident. Hence the practical upshot that runs through Āyurvedic clinical reasoning: **both excess and deficiency of a mala are pathological**, and a great deal of diagnosis is mala-examination — *purīṣa-parīkṣā*, *mūtra-parīkṣā* — which is why urine and stool inspection occupy the place in Āyurvedic examination that they do.

This is the point at which the *dhātu* / *mala* pair stops looking like a list and starts looking like a **balance model**: intake is transformed by tissue-specific fires into a refined line and a waste line, and health is the correct *ratio* at every stage rather than the maximisation of one line. Compare — and only compare — the *sāmya* (equilibrium) framing that [[tridosha]] already records.

1. **Caraka Saṃhitā Online, "Mala"** (fetched; the verse-referenced scholarly digitisation): the √*mṛj* etymology and the "formed after digestion and metabolism" definition; the three *śārīrika* malas; **the seven-row dhātu-mala table including kapha for rasa and pitta for rakta**; and the functional statement that a certain quantity of waste is required for proper functioning. This is the node's anchor and the only source consulted that carries the dhātu-mala correspondences.
2. **Search aggregation across the Āyurvedic academic press** (*IJHSR*, "Classical Study on Mala in Ayurveda Science"; ResearchGate, "The Role of Trimala in Ayurveda"; *IJCRT*, "Conceptual Analysis of Purīṣa Parīkṣā"; *JAIMS*): the two-fold **śārīrika mala / dhātu mala** division stated independently; *tri-mala* = *purīṣa*, *mūtra*, *sveda*; seven dhātu-malas corresponding to the *sapta-dhātu*; and the attribution of the health-significance claim to **both Caraka and Suśruta**.
3. **Lexicon/morphology** (§4 signal 3): *mala* in general Sanskrit means dirt, impurity, or moral taint — the same word used for ritual and spiritual defilement across traditions. The Āyurvedic sense is **technical and non-moralised**, which is a genuine narrowing and the reason the cross-tradition link below is typed as a conflation to avoid.
4. **Corpus cross-check** ([[dhatu]], [[tridosha]], [[ayurveda]]): the seven dhātus and the *dhātv-agni* mechanism were established in this batch from a different set of pages, and the mala table indexes onto them one-for-one; [[tridosha]] independently carries kapha and pitta as *doṣas*, which is what makes their appearance here as *malas* a finding rather than a restatement.

**CONVERGED** on the two-fold division, the tri-mala, and the functional (non-refuse) doctrine. Confidence **medium**, capped for a specific reason: the **dhātu-mala table rests on signal 1 alone**. Signal 2 confirms that seven such malas exist and correspond to the seven dhātus, but the summaries consulted did not enumerate them, so the individual correspondences — including the kapha/pitta rows that carry the node's main finding — are **single-sourced**, and the *śukra* row is simply missing.
