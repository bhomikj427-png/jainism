## Gloss / Divergence map

Syādvāda is the **doctrine of conditional predication**: the method of making any philosophical assertion about an object by prefixing it with *syāt* — "in a certain respect / from a certain standpoint." It is the linguistic-logical arm of anekāntavāda. Where anekāntavāda is the metaphysical principle that reality is many-sided, syādvāda is the disciplined procedure for asserting truths about any one side.

**What *syāt* means here:** "in some respect / from a defined standpoint / conditionally" — *not* "perhaps, maybe, probably." The word carries an optative force in general Sanskrit usage, but in Jain technical usage it functions as a standpoint-relativiser. A syāt-prefixed assertion claims truth from a specified naya, not general truth and not uncertain truth. (New World Encyclopedia, accessed June 2026; Deepa Baruah 2017.)

**Definition from Deepa Baruah, *A Study of the Philosophy of Jainism* (WisdomLib, 2017), citing *Syādvādamañjarī* and Prabhācandra:** "When an object which possesses many characters is expressed in a particular form of judgment [prefixed with syāt], the expression is known as syādvāda."

**Samantabhadra, *Āptamīmāṃsā* v. 105** (reported via scholarly aggregation; not directly fetched): "Syādvāda and kevalajñāna are both illuminators of the substances of reality — syādvāda indirectly [through conditioned predication], kevalajñāna directly [through omniscience]." This locates syādvāda epistemologically: it is the finite knower's best available method, structurally analogous to but distinct from omniscience.

**Textual note:** The term *syādvāda* as a named doctrine is not in the TS itself (parallels the anekāntavāda case). The TS 1.6 (*pramāṇanayaiḥ*) uses nayas as instruments, which is the foundation syādvāda systematises. The doctrine as a named, formalised system is attributed to Samantabhadra (~2nd c. CE, dates disputed) and further developed by Siddhasena Divākar and Akalanka (~8th c.).

**What syādvāda is NOT:**
- Not skepticism or relativism. Śaṅkara (Advaita Vedānta) charged that syādvāda collapses all assertions into "maybe" and cannot ground any definite claim. Jain response: each syāt-qualified assertion is definite within its specified standpoint; the system is perspectival, not probabilistic. (New World Encyclopedia.)
- Not the claim that all views are equally valid. A nayābhāsa (distorted standpoint that denies all others) is explicitly invalid.

**Relation to saptabhaṅgī:** Syādvāda provides the one-assertion-at-a-time method; saptabhaṅgī systematises the complete set of possible basic assertions about any subject-predicate pair into seven canonical forms (the "sevenfold predication"). Saptabhaṅgī is the formalisation of syādvāda.
