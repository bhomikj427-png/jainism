## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kapila** is the **legendary founder of the Sāṃkhya darśana** — "regarded the founder of the Samkhya school of Hindu philosophy." Traditionally placed in the **7th–6th c. BCE** (said to "pre-date the Buddha," with Kapilavastu, the Buddha's natal city, named in his honour), he is for most purposes a **semi-mythical sage** rather than a securely historical author: several scholars hold the figure may be largely legendary, and no work demonstrably his survives. He is thus an *anchor by attribution* — the name to which the whole school traces itself — not a verifiable individual.

**The text problem.** Kapila is traditionally said to have "authored an influential sūtra, called *Sāṃkhyasūtra* (also *Kapilasūtra*)," but the extant Sāṃkhyasūtra is a **late work (~14th c. CE)**, far too late to be his. The earliest surviving systematic Sāṃkhya text is **Īśvarakṛṣṇa's [[samkhya-karika]] (~350 CE)** — so Kapila's actual teaching is recoverable only *through* the kārikā and its lineage-claims, never directly. This is exactly the §1 situation: the founder is a name the tradition projects backward, and the doctrine must be read off the surviving edition.

**The teaching lineage.** The Sāṃkhya tradition transmits itself as **Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha → … → Īśvarakṛṣṇa** (the Sāṃkhyakārikā closes by naming this succession). Kapila is credited with originating the system's core: the dualism of plural inactive **puruṣa** and one active three-guṇa **prakṛti**, the evolution of the tattvas, *satkāryavāda*, and *kaivalya* as the goal — and, notably, **classical Sāṃkhya's atheism** (*nirīśvara*).

**The devotional Kapila — a genuine NOT-equivalent.** Multiple Purāṇas (esp. the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa**) recast Kapila as an **incarnation of Viṣṇu**, son of Kardama Prajāpati and Devahūti, who teaches his mother a *theistic, bhakti-inflected* Sāṃkhya. This devotional Kapila is doctrinally at odds with the atheistic Sāṃkhya-founder Kapila: the same name carries an *ātheist system-founder* in the darśana literature and a *Viṣṇu-avatāra preaching God-devotion* in the Purāṇic literature — a divergence the map should flag, not smooth over.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Kapila"** (fetched): founder of the Sāṃkhya school; ~7th–6th c. BCE, pre-Buddha, historicity contested (possibly mythological); credited with the Sāṃkhyasūtra/Kapilasūtra (the later Sāṃkhyakārikā "explained" the system); lineage Kapila → Āsuri → Pañcaśikha; Bhāgavata-Purāṇa Viṣṇu-incarnation as son of Kardama and Devahūti.
2. **World History Encyclopedia, "Kapila"** (fetched, independent): "the founder of the Samkhya school of philosophy of India," dated "at least 100 years before the Buddha," with Kapilavastu named in his honour — independently confirming the founding role and the pre-Buddhist placement.
3. **Corpus ([[samkhya-karika]], [[prakriti-samkhya]], [[purusha-samkhya]], [[guna-samkhya]])**: the doctrine Kapila is credited with founding, and the kārikā's lineage-claim, were recorded earlier from IEP + Wikipedia.

Two independent reference works agree on the founding role and pre-Buddhist dating; the corpus supplies the doctrinal content. Confidence **medium**: no text is actually Kapila's (the Sāṃkhyasūtra is late), his historicity is genuinely contested, and the dates are traditional rather than established — appropriate caution for a legendary founder.
