## Gloss / Divergence map

**Liṅga** (Sanskrit, "sign; mark; symbol; characteristic; evidence"): the primary **aniconic symbol of Śiva** — a cylindrical form (usually stone, often with a rounded top) that represents Śiva's transcendent, formless (*nirguṇa*) aspect. The liṅga does not depict Śiva; it *points to* what cannot be depicted. The term's primary Sanskrit meaning is "sign/mark" (e.g., Upaniṣads use it for "evidence/proof"), not "phallus" — though the creative/generative symbolic dimension is present in the liṅga-yoni iconography.

**Levels of meaning:**

| level | what the liṅga symbolizes | textual basis |
|---|---|---|
| Cosmogonic | Śiva as infinite pillar of light (*jyotirliṅga*) — neither Brahma nor Viṣṇu can find its top or bottom | Śiva Purāṇa (Rudra-saṃhitā): Śiva appears as boundless light to establish his supremacy |
| Metaphysical | The formless (*nirguṇa*) aspect of Śiva — pure consciousness without attributes; the absolute that precedes all form | Śaiva Āgamas; Liṅga Purāṇa |
| Cosmological | Union of Śiva (masculine principle / puruṣa) and Śakti (feminine principle / yoni) generating creation | Śākta-Śaiva synthesis; liṅga in yoni = creative cosmic union |
| Soteriological | The object of worship that leads the devotee to recognize Śiva's formless nature and attain liberation | Śaiva Siddhānta Āgamas; Kashmir Śaivism pratyabhijñā doctrine |
| Personal/devotional | For Liṅgāyats: the *iṣṭaliṅga* worn on the body — Śiva is always present, no temple intermediary required | Basavaṇṇa's Vachanas (12th c. CE) |

**The jyotirliṅga myth (Śiva Purāṇa):** When Brahma and Viṣṇu disputed which was supreme, Śiva appeared as an infinite column of light (*jyoti-liṅga* — "liṅga of light") that had no beginning or end. Brahma flew upward as a goose; Viṣṇu dove downward as a boar; neither reached the end. Śiva revealed himself as the source: the infinite liṅga establishes Śiva as *paramātman* (supreme self) beyond Brahma and Viṣṇu. The 12 *jyotirliṅgas* — sacred sites across India — commemorate this theophany.

**The scholarly debate (aniconic symbol vs. phallic symbol):**
- **Symbolic/cosmological reading** (mainstream Hindu tradition): The liṅga is a *nishkala* (partless, formless) sign pointing to Śiva's nirguṇa (attributeless) aspect; the phallic dimension is a secondary layer in some textual traditions
- **Phallic/generative reading** (some Western scholars, Wendy Doniger et al.): The liṅga-yoni iconography has genuine generative/erotic dimensions in some Purāṇic and Tantric texts; both layers coexist
- **Colonial distortion**: 19th-century British scholars and missionaries imposed Victorian obscenity readings, obscuring the symbolic/cosmological interpretation. This distortion persists in some Western accounts and should be noted as methodologically unreliable
- **Scholarly consensus**: The liṅga carries multiple simultaneous layers; neither "just a phallus" nor "purely abstract symbol" is adequate

**Liṅgāyat / Vīra Śaiva theology (Basavaṇṇa, 12th c. CE, Karnataka):**
The Liṅgāyat reform is one of the most radical applications of liṅga theology in Hindu history:
- Every devotee wears a personal *iṣṭaliṅga* (consecrated liṅga) on the body at all times
- This makes each person's body a temple — no external Brahmin priest or stone temple required
- Liṅgāyats rejected caste hierarchy, temple ritual intermediaries, and the Vedic varṇāśramadharma system
- The iṣṭaliṅga is contemplated as the devotee's own ātman (self) identified with Śiva — an intimate non-dual practice
- Liberation (*mukti*) = the devotee merging with the liṅga; the living body is already divine if properly realized

**Liṅga worship compared to mūrti (iconic image) worship:**
The liṅga is Śaivism's primary *aniconic* symbol — it does not depict Śiva's features (as murti/icon sculpture does). This is significant: aniconic symbols resist anthropomorphic projection; they point to the formless (*arūpa*). Comparable aniconic worship: the Buddhist stūpa (commemorating parinibbāna, not depicting the Buddha), the Jewish/Islamic prohibition on images, the Jain rejection of worshipping liberation-figures as intervening gods.
