## Gloss / Divergence map

Ālayavijñāna is the **storehouse consciousness** — the eighth and foundational of the eight consciousnesses in the Yogācāra school. It functions as the **repository of karmic seeds** (*bīja*) from all past actions, storing subliminal impressions (*vāsanā*) that ripen into future experience in this life and across rebirths.

**Function: basis of the other seven consciousnesses**

The ālaya-vijñāna is the "ground for the arising of all other types of consciousness" — the eye-, ear-, nose-, tongue-, body-, thought- (mano-vijñāna), and self-referential (manas-vijñāna) consciousnesses all arise on this base and deposit impressions back into it. Mipham Rinpoche's image: "an immense ocean" from which the other seven consciousnesses "rise and fall like waves."

**The seed–sprout mechanism**

Experiences deposit *bīja* (seeds) into the ālaya. Seeds ripen (*vipāka*) into new experiences, which deposit new seeds, creating a self-sustaining causal stream. This accounts for: (1) the continuity of consciousness through interruptions such as deep sleep or meditative absorption; (2) the transmission of karmic effects across lifetimes.

**The rebirth-without-self solution**

Ālaya-vijñāna was formulated precisely to solve the puzzle: how can karma produce retribution in a later life if there is no permanent ātman? The answer: the stream of ālaya-vijñāna is *continuously changing* (impermanent moment-to-moment) yet *causally continuous* — the seed planted in one moment ripens into a result in a later moment without requiring any permanent underlying self. The causal chain is dharmic, not substantial. Vasubandhu: this "did away with the necessity of a permanent ātman as the doer and recipient of karma."

**The "hidden ātman" objection**

Critics — including within Buddhist schools — argued that ālaya-vijñāna is simply a disguised ātman. The Yogācāra response: unlike a permanent ātman, ālaya-vijñāna is explicitly impermanent and causally conditioned; it is described as *neutral* (neither positive nor negative), not a self that perceives or enjoys. The Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra uses the term *ādāna-vijñāna* (appropriating consciousness) to emphasize that it grasps a body but does not constitute a fixed self.

**Primary text anchor: Saṃdhinirmocana Sūtra**

Chapters 5 and 6 introduce and discuss ālaya-vijñāna. The original Sanskrit has not survived; the most reliable extant version is Xuanzang's Chinese translation (7th c. CE). Asaṅga's *Yogācārabhūmi* and *Mahāyānasaṃgraha* develop it systematically; Vasubandhu's *Triṃśikā* (Thirty Verses) integrates it into the full eight-consciousness model.
