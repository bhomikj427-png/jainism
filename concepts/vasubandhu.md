## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vasubandhu** (fl. ~4th–5th c. CE, Gupta period; by tradition from Gandhāra/Puruṣapura) is one of the most influential systematic philosophers of Indian Buddhism — the author who bridges **Abhidharma scholasticism** and the **Yogācāra** "consciousness-only" school he helped found. He is counted among the Seventeen Nālandā Masters and revered as a patriarch in East Asian schools.

**The Abhidharmakośa phase.** Vasubandhu first composed the **Abhidharmakośa-kārikā**, a verse compendium of orthodox **Sarvāstivāda-Vaibhāṣika** Abhidharma — and then wrote his own prose **bhāṣya** (auto-commentary) that **critiques** that very system from a more economical **Sautrāntika** standpoint (notably on momentariness and unperceived matter). The "unusual fact" of a root-text defending what its own commentary attacks made his Kashmiri Vaibhāṣika colleagues "livid" — and made the *Kośa* the indispensable summa of Abhidharma debate for both Tibetan and East Asian Buddhism.

**The Yogācāra phase.** Converted to Mahāyāna (by tradition under his half-brother **Asaṅga**), Vasubandhu produced the concise treatises that set the standard for Yogācāra: the **Viṃśatikā** and **Triṃśikā** establishing **vijñaptimātra** ("appearance-/cognition-only"), the **Trisvabhāva-nirdeśa** on the **three natures** (parikalpita / paratantra / pariniṣpanna), and accounts of the **ālaya-vijñāna** (storehouse-consciousness) that carries karmic continuity.

**Two open scholarly questions (flagged, not resolved):**

| question | state of the debate |
|---|---|
| **"Two Vasubandhus"** (Frauwallner, 1950s): one Sautrāntika, one Yogācārin? | No consensus; the theory "holds sway in some areas" but the trend (Lusthaus and others) is **one author whose thought evolved**, not two people. |
| What does **vijñaptimātra** mean? | Contested between an **idealist** reading (only mind exists) and a **phenomenological** one. SEP stresses "appearance-only does **not** mean the non-existence of dharmas" — only their lack of independent nature; Vasubandhu distinguishes conventional appearance, mental causation, and ineffable ultimate. |

His *Vāda-vidhi* is also cited as an early landmark of Indian formal logic — the methodological ground his successor **Dignāga** built on.
