## Gloss

The Stoic *logos* is the active, rational principle that pervades, moves, and structures the entire cosmos. It is identified with God/Zeus and with *pneuma* (fiery breath): "the right reason pervading everything and identical to Zeus" (Chrysippus, cited SEP). Unlike the passive matter (*hylē*) on which it acts, logos is the active principle — yet crucially it is **corporeal**, not immaterial. Stoic radical materialism insists only bodies can act or be acted upon; logos is therefore a body, specifically a fiery intelligent pneuma that holds tensional motion (*tonos*) — simultaneous inward and outward movement — giving objects their qualities and coherence.

Key aspects:
- **Logos = God = Nature = Reason**: these are not different things but different names for the single active cosmic principle.
- **Logos spermatikos**: "seminal reasons" — rational seeds embedded in matter, driving the generation and differentiation of all things.
- **Hegemonikon**: The commanding faculty of the human soul that participates in logos at the highest level of pneumatic activity. Animals have lower-tension pneuma; only humans and gods have logos proper.
- **Ethical consequence**: Living in agreement with logos/nature is the Stoic telos. Individual rationality is a fragment of the cosmic rationality.

## Comparison with Indian pervasive-principle concepts

| feature | logos (Stoic) | brahman (Advaita) | dharma-dravya (Jain) | pratītyasamutpāda (Buddhist) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| material or immaterial | **material** (corporeal fiery pneuma) | immaterial (pure consciousness, sat-cit-ānanda) | material (one of 6 dravyas) | neither — a process, not a substance |
| active or passive | **active** (moves, organises, creates) | nirguna = passive; saguṇa = active | **passive** medium (enables motion, does not cause it) | neither — conditional arising, no agent |
| rational character | explicitly rational (logos = reason) | beyond conceptualisation (nirguṇa is beyond reason) | non-rational (a physical medium) | not rational; impersonal causal law |
| personal | personal (Zeus/God) | impersonal (nirguṇa) / personal (saguṇa) | impersonal | impersonal |
| individual souls | logos pervades all; individual = partial participant | ātman = brahman (non-dual identity) | jīva = distinct substance, independent of dharma-dravya | no permanent soul; momentary consciousness-stream |
| ethical implication | live in accordance with logos/nature | realise identity with brahman | accumulate right conduct to remove karma | follow the Eightfold Path; release craving |

**Critical distinction from brahman**: the most common surface conflation. Logos is **material** and **rational**; brahman (especially nirguṇa Advaita) is **immaterial** and **beyond rationality**. Brahman is one uniform substrate; logos is an active ordering that pervades matter without being reducible to it. The analogy works only at the level of "single cosmic principle" — it breaks at materiality, rationality, and the agent-structure question.

**Critical distinction from dharma-dravya**: dharma-dravya merely enables motion (a passive medium, like water for fish); logos actively creates and governs. The two are not comparable beyond both being physical.

## Reasoning across signals

SEP (Stoicism) is the primary scholarly source: defines logos, identifies it with God/Zeus via Chrysippus, and establishes the corporeal-active/matter-passive distinction. IEP (Stoic Mind) confirms logos as the highest pneumatic activity and the hegemonikon link. Both are independent critical-encyclopedic sources and converge on the key claims. Primary texts (SVF = *Stoicorum Veterum Fragmenta*, von Arnim) not directly fetched; Chrysippus and Cleanthes are preserved only in fragments. Confidence medium — no direct fragment fetch, but SEP/IEP cite the ancient sources.
