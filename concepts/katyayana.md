## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kātyāyana** is the ***vārttikakāra*** — the author of the **Vārttikas**, the terse critical notes on [[panini|Pāṇini]]'s sūtras — and the second member of the ***munitraya*** ([[vyakarana]]). Abhyankar places him "**a few centuries after Pāṇini**," and credits him with the decisive move: "The subject of Sanskrit grammar was **first treated as a science** by the two epoch-making grammarians, first by Kātyāyana… and then by [[patanjali-grammarian|Patañjali]], the exponent of Kātyāyana." Wikipedia independently gives c. 3rd–4th century BCE.

⚠ **His work does not survive as a book.** The Vārttikas are known because the [[mahabhashya|Mahābhāṣya]] quotes and discusses them. **Kātyāyana is a text embedded in his own commentator** — which is why the corpus can say almost nothing about him that does not pass through Patañjali's hands first, and why the *munitraya*'s inverted authority-hierarchy ([[vyakarana]]) has a mundane cause as well as a doctrinal one: the later sage is the only route to the earlier one.

### The divergence 1: how many Vārttikas, and what a Vārttika is

| count | who gives it (source) | what it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| **"well-nigh above 5000,"** including Vārttikas in verse | **Abhyankar** (read at first hand), reporting the received total, on a threefold classification of Vārttikas | The broad definition: any of Patañjali's cited notes explaining or commenting on a sūtra |
| **"about 1400 or so"** | **Abhyankar**, deriving the consequence of **Nāgeśa's** definition (18th c.), who "appears to have divided Vārttikas into two classes" | The narrow definition: on it, "many of the Vārttikas given in the Mahābhāṣya as explaining and commenting upon the Sūtras **will not strictly be termed as Vārttikas**" |
| **1,228 sūtras** commented on | Wikipedia, "Mahābhāṣya" | A different measure entirely — sūtras covered, not notes written |

⚠ **The size of Kātyāyana's corpus is a function of an eighteenth-century definition.** Abhyankar adds the detail that settles it as a real textual fact rather than a scholastic quibble: "There are **some manuscript copies which give this reduced number**." A definitional decision propagated into the manuscript tradition. This is the same shape as [[astadhyayi]]'s sūtra-count problem, one tier up: **a corpus whose boundary is drawn by whoever is defining the genre.**

### The divergence 2: is Kātyāyana Vararuci? — and a source contradicting itself

The identification **Kātyāyana = Vararuci** is standard in reference works. Abhyankar's dictionary contains **both** the endorsement and its refutation, in two different entries, without cross-reference.

| reading | where it appears | what it rests on |
|---|---|---|
| **Yes** — one man, two names | **Abhyankar s.v. Vararuci**, opening: "it is **very likely** that Vararuci was the **individual** name of the scholar, and Kātyāyana his **family** name" | Both names appear "in commentary works in connection with the Vārttikas" |
| **Yes**, with a rider | **Abhyankar s.v. Kātyāyana**: "Some scholars say that Vararuci was also another name given to him, **in which case** the Vārttikakāra Vararuci Kātyāyana has to be looked upon as **different from the subsequent writer named Vararuci**" to whom Prākṛt and Kātantra works are ascribed | Accepting the identification forces you to split *Vararuci* into two men instead |
| **No** — a late conflation | **Abhyankar s.v. Vararuci**, conclusion, from the primary text: "**There is no reference at all in the Mahābhāṣya to Vararuci as a writer of the Vārttikas**; there is only one reference which shows that there was a scholar by name Vararuci known to Patañjali, **but he was a poet**" (M.Bh. on A 4.2.4). Meanwhile the Ślokavārttika-kāra ascribes the Vārttikas to **Kātyāyana** (M.Bh. on A 3.2.3, 3.2.118). Verdict: "**Patañjali does not associate Vararuci with Kātyāyana at all**… it is only the **later writers** who identified" them | Argument from what the earliest witness does and does not say |
| **Yes**, on legendary authority | Wikipedia: "The *Kathāsaritsāgara* mentions Kātyāyana as another name of Vararuci, a **re-incarnation** of Lord Shiva's gaṇa Puṣpadanta" | A story in an 11th-century tale-collection |

⚠ **Two findings here, and the second is the more important.**

**(a) The identification is probably false, and the corpus can say why.** The earliest and best witness — Patañjali, who quotes the Vārttikas constantly — never calls their author Vararuci, and mentions a Vararuci only as a *poet*. There is a *second, later* Vararuci, "a grammarian later than Patañjali," author of a **Prākṛt grammar** and of the fourth adhyāya of the **Kātantra** sūtras. **The likeliest history is that the tradition merged this later Vararuci into Kātyāyana.** And Wikipedia's supporting authority is the *Kathāsaritsāgara* — the very text Abhyankar elsewhere calls "**only legendary** and hence, it has very little historical value" ([[panini]]). **The corpus can turn a source's own verdict on the evidence another source relies on.**

**(b) The specialist lexicon does not check itself across entries.** Batch 46 generalised that encyclopedia sources "are unreliable **as a set of witnesses**, because they do not check themselves across articles." That was said of Wikipedia strata. **It holds here inside a single-author scholarly dictionary**: Abhyankar's *Vararuci* entry supplies the evidence that refutes the claim its own opening sentence calls "very likely," and his *Kātyāyana* entry reports the identification without the refutation. **The pathology is not peculiar to crowd-sourced reference works — it is a property of the entry as a unit of writing.** Recorded as a qualification of the corpus's own Batch 46 finding, not as a hit on Abhyankar, whose *Vararuci* entry is in fact the best piece of argument the batch has met.

### ⚠ A biography deduced from a stylistic remark

Abhyankar: Kātyāyana "is believed to be a **resident of South India** on the strength of the remark *priyataddhitā dākṣiṇātyāḥ* ["southerners are fond of *taddhita* formations"] made by Patañjali in connection with" a statement treated as Kātyāyana's Vārttika. Wikipedia independently reports him "born in the Deccan, Southern India."

**The two sources agree, and the agreement is worth nothing extra** — the second is almost certainly reporting the first's inference. **The whole geography rests on a passing remark about a stylistic preference of southerners**, applied to a line attributed to Kātyāyana. It may well be right; it is not corroborated by anything. This is the batch's recurring mechanism a fourth time ([[panini]], [[astadhyayi]], [[sphota]]): **the record leaves a blank, and an inference from inside the text fills it — after which the inference is reported as a fact.**

### The other Kātyāyanas

Attributions accumulate on the name. Abhyankar: he "is also **believed** to be the author of the **Vājasaneyi Prātiśākhya** and many sūtra works named after him" — note *believed*. Wikipedia adds one of the **Śulbasūtras** (altar geometry) and carries a disambiguation note for a **Buddhist** Kātyāyana. ⚠ **The corpus does not merge these.** A vārttikakāra, a Prātiśākhya author, a geometer and a Buddhist monk sharing a common Brahmin gotra-name is what one would expect *without* any identity between them; this node covers **only the grammarian**.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Abhyankar, *A Dictionary of Sanskrit Grammar*** (**read at first hand**): s.vv. Kātyāyana, Vararuci, Vārttika, plus the Introduction's chronology. Supplies the 5000/1400 count problem and Nāgeśa's redefinition; the South-India inference and its exact basis; and both sides of the Vararuci question with the primary-text argument.
2. **Wikipedia, "Kātyāyana"** (fetched, independent): c. 3rd–4th c. BCE; Deccan origin; the *Kathāsaritsāgara* Vararuci legend; the Śulbasūtra attribution; the Buddhist name-collision.
3. **Wikipedia, "Mahābhāṣya"** (fetched, independent): 1,228 sūtras covered across 85 āhnikas — the independent measure of the Vārttikas' scope.
4. **SEP, "Language and Testimony in Classical Indian Philosophy"** (fetched): Kātyāyana and Patañjali at 200–100 BCE, jointly responsible for "a significant reorganization of Brahmanical views in the face of opposition from Jains and Buddhists," and for the claim that "intelligent use of Sanskrit, backed by explicit understanding of its grammar, leads to prosperity." **Kātyāyana's grammar has an apologetic motive**, which the purely technical sources do not mention.
5. **Internal consistency**: the count-boundary problem matches [[astadhyayi]]; the name-collision matches the five the corpus logged in Batch 46; the "founder known only through a commentator" shape matches [[jejjata]] and [[candrata]] in the Āyurvedic literature.

⚠ **A dating tension left open rather than resolved.** Abhyankar puts Kātyāyana "a few centuries after Pāṇini" and Patañjali in the 2nd c. BCE; Wikipedia puts Kātyāyana at 3rd–4th c. BCE; SEP puts *both* Kātyāyana and Patañjali at 200–100 BCE. If Pāṇini is mid-4th c. BCE ([[panini]]) and Kātyāyana is 3rd–4th c. BCE, then "a few centuries after Pāṇini" fails and the two are nearly contemporary. **The three cannot all be right.** The tension is recorded, not adjudicated — it is downstream of the unresolved Pāṇini date.

**Contested**, on two independent axes (the corpus's extent, and the Vararuci identity). Confidence **medium**: Abhyankar is first-hand and argues from the primary text, but no critical edition of the Vārttikas or the Mahābhāṣya was consulted, and the dating cannot be closed.
