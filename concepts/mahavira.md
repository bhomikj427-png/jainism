## Gloss

*Mahāvīra* ("Great Hero"; birth name **Vardhamāna**) is the **24th and last tīrthaṅkara** of the present cosmic half-cycle and the towering historical figure of Jainism — but, crucially, **not its founder**. He is the *reformer and systematizer* of a tradition already established by Pārśvanātha (the 23rd, ~250 years earlier; parshvanatha.md). Born a Kṣatriya at Kuṇḍagrāma (near Vaiśālī, Bihar) to Siddhārtha and Triśalā, he renounced royal life at 30, practised severe asceticism for 12.5 years, and attained omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*) — becoming a *jina* ("victor"). He preached for some 30 years, founded (or refounded) the **four-fold community (*catur-vidha-saṅgha*: monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen)**, fixed the **five mahāvratas** (adding brahmacarya to Pārśva's four), and attained final liberation (*nirvāṇa*/mokṣa) at Pāvāpurī. His chief disciple, Indrabhūti Gautama, headed the transmission that became the Jain Āgamas.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Mahavira":** 24th and final tīrthaṅkara; "not Jainism's founder but a reformer who revived and systematized" Pārśva's tradition; born Vardhamāna, Kṣatriya, Kuṇḍagrāma; renounced at 30; 12.5 yrs asceticism; kevala-jñāna; five mahāvratas; four-fold saṅgha; nirvāṇa at Pāvāpurī; contemporary of the Buddha (under Bimbisāra/Ajātaśatru); dating contested.

**Signal 2 — Encyclopædia Britannica "Mahavira":** "the last of the 24 Tirthankaras … and the reformer of the Jain monastic community"; traditional c. 599 BCE "as much as 100 years early"; scholars place him in the **second half of the 5th century BCE**, contemporary with the Buddha.

**Independence:** Wikipedia and Britannica are independent and agree on the reformer-not-founder framing, the biography, and the dating debate. Confidence medium: the *dates* are explicitly contested (a documented scholarly disagreement, mapped below — this is a *contested-dating* point inside an otherwise converged figure), and the biographical narrative is hagiographic in its sources.

## The dating disagreement (mapped, not resolved)

| dating | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 599–527 BCE** | Śvetāmbara tradition (Kalpa Sūtra) | Mahāvīra somewhat *older* than the Buddha |
| **c. 615–527 BCE** | Digambara tradition | similar, slightly earlier birth |
| **2nd half of 5th c. BCE** (d. ~425 BCE) | much of modern scholarship (Britannica) | Mahāvīra a near-exact *contemporary* of the Buddha; nirvāṇa ~425 BCE not 527 |

All accounts agree he overlapped with **Gautama Buddha** and **Makkhali Gosāla** (the Ājīvika leader; ajivika.md) in the same Magadhan milieu — the *śramaṇa* (renouncer) ferment of the Gangetic plain. He appears in the Buddhist canon as *Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta* ("the unclothed one of the Jñātṛ clan"), an external attestation that anchors his historicity beyond Jain sources.

## Founder of a religion, or 24th in a line? — keep the frame straight

Outsider summaries routinely call Mahāvīra "the founder of Jainism." The tradition flatly denies this, and the corpus follows the tradition's *own* logic: Jainism's self-understanding is a beginningless succession of 24 tīrthaṅkaras per half-cycle (utsarpini-avasarpini.md), of whom Mahāvīra is merely the *most recent*. Pārśva's pre-existing order — to which Mahāvīra's own parents belonged — is the concrete evidence that he *inherited* rather than *invented*. The honest statement is: **Mahāvīra is the historical reformer who gave Jainism its present shape (five vows, four-fold saṅgha, the codified teaching), and the last jina of this age — not the originator of the faith.**
