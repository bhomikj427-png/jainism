## Gloss

Ākāśāstikāya (space-substance) is one of the six Jain dravyas. Its single defining function is *avagāha* — providing accommodation/room for all other substances to exist. It is formless, motionless, eternal, and itself requires no accommodation. Crucially, ākāśa is the only dravya that extends infinitely beyond the universe: within the universe (*lokākāśa*), all six dravyas co-exist; outside it (*alokākāśa*), only ākāśa exists — a boundless empty void. Jain ākāśa is not an element, not an ether, and does not bear any sensory quality such as sound.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 5.18 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi):** *ākāśasyāvagāhaḥ* — "The function of the space (ākāśa) is to provide accommodation (*avagāha*)." The briefest functional definition in the chapter; commentary by Pūjyapāda emphasises that this accommodation is "common to all substances" and is not physical penetration but an inherent property of space.

**Signal 2 — TS 5.6 (already fetched):** *ā ākāśādekadravyāṇi* — ākāśa is singular (one substance), like dharma and adharma. Confirms the count.

**Signal 3 — Wikipedia, "Ākāśa (Jainism)" (citing Uttaradhyayana Sūtra Chapter 28):** Confirms lokākāśa/alokākāśa distinction; dharma, adharma, jīva, pudgala, and kāla all confined to lokākāśa; alokākāśa perceptible only to omniscients.

**Independence:** Signals 1 and 2 are direct fetches from the same critical edition; Signal 3 is Wikipedia with a different canonical source. Confidence medium.

## Lokākāśa vs. Alokākāśa

| Region | Contents | Size |
|---|---|---|
| **Lokākāśa** (universe-space) | All six dravyas: jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, kāla, ākāśa | Finite (14 rājus tall) |
| **Alokākāśa** (non-universe space) | Ākāśa only — no motion, no rest, no souls, no matter | Infinite in all directions |

The boundary between lokākāśa and alokākāśa is defined by the presence of dharma and adharma: where those two substances end, the universe ends. Motion and rest are impossible in alokākāśa; a liberated soul (*siddha*) rests at the very summit of lokākāśa — not in alokākāśa — because without *adharmāstikāya* there would be nothing to halt it.

## Comparison with other ākāśa concepts

| Tradition | Term | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Jain** | ākāśāstikāya | Pure spatial accommodation; no sensory quality; extends infinitely |
| **Vaiśeṣika** | ākāśa | One of 9 substances; *bears śabda (sound) as a guṇa*; not infinite in the same sense |
| **Hindu (Sāṃkhya/Vedānta)** | ākāśa | Fifth element (*mahābhūta*); medium for sound; product of prakṛti |
| **Buddhist** | ākāsa | Space as absence of obstruction (*ākāsadhātu*); not a substance in the Jain sense |

The Vaiśeṣika ākāśa's role as sound-bearer is rejected in Jain ontology: Jain pudgala (matter) carries sound as a modification, not a separate substance. The conflation of Jain ākāśa with ether/element is the primary confusion to flag.
