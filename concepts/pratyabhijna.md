## Gloss

*Pratyabhijñā* ("Recognition") is the philosophical system of monistic Kashmir Śaivism developed by **Utpaladeva** (c. 925–975 CE) in the *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* (IPK) and elaborated by **Abhinavagupta** (c. 975–1025 CE) in the *IPK Vimarśinī* and *Tantrāloka*. Its central thesis: **everything is Śiva — pure, dynamic, self-aware consciousness — and liberation is the re-cognition (*pratyabhijñā*) of this fact, not the acquisition of new knowledge.**

**Etymology:** *prati-* (back / again) + *abhi-* (toward) + *√jñā* (to know) = "to know again," "direct re-knowing of oneself." The crucial epistemological claim is that recognition is *not* new knowledge but *recovery* of what was always the case. The soul is not ignorant of Śiva in the Advaita sense (where *avidyā* conceals Brahman) — rather, the soul *is* Śiva but has *lost the explicit awareness* of this identity. Removing that forgetfulness IS liberation.

**The IPK's argument:**
1. All experience presupposes a unified self-aware subject (recognizer/agent).
2. No theory that fragments consciousness into momentary events (as Buddhist *kṣaṇavāda* does) can account for *recognition* — recognizing X now as the same X seen before requires a *persisting* subject.
3. The only coherent ultimate subject is Śiva as *vimarśa* (reflexive self-awareness) — consciousness that knows itself in knowing all things.
4. The individual soul is this Śiva-consciousness appearing to be limited; liberation = re-cognition of the non-difference.

**Vimarśa:** Abhinavagupta's key contribution — *vimarśa* is the "I-awareness" (ahaṃ-vimarśa) that is inseparable from *prakāśa* (pure luminous consciousness). Śiva is both light (what illuminates) and reflexive self-recognition (knowing itself as light). Aesthetic experience (*rasa*) is for Abhinavagupta analogous to spiritual realization — both are moments of expanded, undifferentiated awareness.

**Distinction from Advaita Vedānta (crucial):**

| issue | Advaita | Pratyabhijñā |
|---|---|---|
| ultimate reality | nirguṇa Brahman — featureless, without śakti | Śiva — consciousness WITH dynamic power (śakti) |
| status of the world | ultimately unreal (vivartavāda / māyā) | real — Śiva's real self-expression |
| liberation | removal of avidyā via jñāna | positive re-cognition of identity with Śiva |
| method | śravaṇa/manana (hearing/reflection on śruti) | IPK logical argument + contemplation |
| authority | Upaniṣads (Brahmasūtra tradition) | Āgamic revelation + philosophical reasoning |

**Relation to Spanda:** The earlier Spanda system (Vasugupta/Kallata, ~9th c.) introduced Śakti as *spanda* ("cosmic pulsation" — the dynamic throb of consciousness). Pratyabhijñā inherits this dynamism and converts it from a primarily experiential/tantric teaching into a rigorous philosophical argument. Spanda is the experiential-poetic layer; Pratyabhijñā is the epistemological-systematic layer of the same school.
