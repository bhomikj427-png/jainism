## Gloss / Divergence map

**Maṇḍana Miśra** (fl. 9th c. CE) is the philosopher who **bridges Mīmāṃsā and Advaita Vedānta**: a student of the Mīmāṃsaka **[[kumarila-bhatta|Kumārila Bhaṭṭa]]** who then wrote the **Brahmasiddhi** ("establishment of Brahman"), a foundational **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]]** treatise. He defended the *sphoṭa* theory of language (Sphoṭasiddhi) and gave a **distinctive Advaita doctrine** — *avidyā* (ignorance) is *anirvacanīya* (inexpressible) and the individual **jīva is its locus** — which differs from Śaṅkara's account. A scholarly thesis holds that until the 10th c. it was Maṇḍana, not Śaṅkara, who was the dominant Advaitin.

Two questions are genuinely **contested**:

| question | reading | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Is Maṇḍana = **Sureśvara** (Śaṅkara's disciple)? | tradition says Maṇḍana lost the debate to Śaṅkara and became Sureśvara | one person, a conversion narrative — but "authenticity is doubtful"; many scholars hold them **distinct** authors with incompatible doctrines |
| whose Advaita is primary? | the debate-legend subordinates Maṇḍana to Śaṅkara | yet Maṇḍana's locus-of-avidyā doctrine is independent, and he may have been the **more influential** Advaitin for a century |
