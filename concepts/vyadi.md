## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vyāḍi** is the name attached to the ***Saṅgraha***, a grammatical work of "a lac of verses" that **no longer exists**, and to the ***Paribhāṣāsūcana***, a collection of 93 metarules that **does**. He is the earliest named figure in the [[paribhasha|paribhāṣā]] literature, the oldest recorded holder of the position that a word denotes the individual and not the class, and — on the reference works that disambiguate names rather than assume them — **not one man.**

⚠ **The disagreement is not about what he wrote. It is about how many people the name covers, and when any of them lived.**

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **One grammarian**, "a relative and contemporary of [[panini|Pāṇini]]," author of the *Saṅgraha* | **Abhyankar**, s.v. — and the whole traditional account | Vyāḍi is roughly Pāṇini's own generation, and the *Saṅgraha* is nearly as old as the *Aṣṭādhyāyī* |
| The same man, but "**lived after [[katyayana|Kātyāyana]] and before [[patanjali-grammarian\|Patañjali]]**" | ⚠ **Abhyankar again**, s.v. *Paribhāṣāsūcana* — **in the same dictionary** | two or more centuries later than the row above; **the two rows cannot both be true of one man** |
| The *Paribhāṣāsūcana* "from its style and other peculiarities seems to have been written **after the Vārttikas, but before the [[mahabhashya\|Mahābhāṣya]]**" | **Abhyankar**, s.v. *Vyāḍi* — argued, with a text-internal reason: at **Pari. 42 "Vyāḍi clearly refers to the Vārttika of Kātyāyana"** | the *surviving* work is post-Kātyāyana whatever the *lost* one is |
| The independent collection of paribhāṣās is a **response to a need Patañjali's practice created** — "the need for a validation and collection of implicit pāṇinian *paribhāṣā*s **as carried out by authors such as Vyāḍi**" | ⭐ **Candotti & Pontillo 2018** (abstract) | later still: **after** Patañjali, not before him — and the attribution itself is held at arm's length ("**handed down as**… and **attributed to** Vyāḍi") |
| ⭐⭐ **Four different Vyāḍis** — a **poet**, a **grammarian**, a **lexicographer**, and a **medical/alchemical author** — each with his own citations | **Aufrecht, *Catalogus Catalogorum***; and **Monier-Williams**: "Name of various men (especially of a poet, a grammarian, and a lexicographer)" | the single entry in the grammatical dictionary is a **selection**, not the whole record |

⭐ **The corpus's reading of the split is stated in "Reasoning" below and is its own, not a source's.**

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐ The one thing that is certain is a loss — and this corpus has now read the sentence that records it

The *Saṅgraha* is **gone**. Abhyankar, s.v. *Saṅgraha*: "Some quotations only are found from the Saṃgraha in grammar works, but the **work is lost long ago**"; s.v. *Vyāḍi*: "The work is **not available at present**." What survives is a name, a size ("a lac of verses" — 100,000), and citations.

⭐ **And the loss is not merely reported by a modern lexicographer; it is the opening move of the [[vakyapadiya|Vākyapadīya]]'s epilogue**, read at first hand this batch (depth read D-C, see [[candra-vyakarana]] for the full source note). At **VP 2.480** — *saṃgrahe 'stam upāgate*, "**the *Saṅgraha* having gone to its setting**" — the loss of Vyāḍi's book is given as the *condition* that made grammarians "fond of brevity and of little learning"; three verses later, at **2.483**, the *ārṣa* text is "thrown into confusion" *saṃgraha-pratikañcuke*. **The *Saṅgraha* is named twice in ten verses as the thing whose absence defines the crisis.**

⚠⚠ **Two hard limits on that.** First, **§1 forbids this corpus from translating those verses**, and no published English of them was obtainable — both archive.org copies of Iyer's kāṇḍa II are garbled OCR. The Sanskrit is quoted as *wording read*, not as a rendering asserted. Second, ⭐ **Aklujkar holds these ten verses to be a pupil's, not Bhartṛhari's** — so even the witness to the loss is contested at the level of who wrote it.

### 2. ⚠⚠ A reference work contradicting itself, and the corpus's second instance of the same defect in the same book

Abhyankar's dictionary states, **of the same name**, that Vyāḍi was "a relative and **contemporary of Pāṇini**" and that he "**lived after Kātyāyana and before Patañjali**." On the standard chronology those are separated by two centuries or more. **They cannot both hold of one man.**

⭐ **This is the second time this corpus has caught Abhyankar printing two unreconciled notices under one head** — Batch 50 found him giving two irreconcilable accounts of the *Kātantradhātuvṛtti*-*Manoramā*'s author. ⚠ **Neither is carelessness in the ordinary sense.** A dictionary of a tradition records what the tradition says; where the tradition says two things, a scrupulous compiler prints two things. **The defect is that nothing marks them as rivals.**

### 3. ⭐⭐⭐ The corpus's reading: the contradiction is the shape of a collision, and the reference works that disambiguate names already show it

Abhyankar's two datings are **attached to two different works**:
- "contemporary of Pāṇini" travels with the ***Saṅgraha***, and its evidence is the **Dākṣāyaṇa argument** — s.v. *Dākṣāyaṇa*: "the word *dākṣāyaṇa* indicates that Vyāḍi was a descendant of Dakṣa, and, as Pāṇini is called *dākṣīputra*, **critics say that Pāṇini and Vyāḍi were relatives**; cf. *M. Bh.* on P. II.3.66."
- "after Kātyāyana, before Patañjali" travels with the ***Paribhāṣāsūcana***, and its evidence is **internal to that text** — Pari. 42 citing a Vārttika.

⭐ **So the dictionary is not confused about a man; it is running two different arguments about two different books and reporting both under one headword.** And **Aufrecht and Monier-Williams already say so** — four Vyāḍis in the *Catalogus Catalogorum* (poet, grammarian, lexicographer, medical author), "various men" in MW.

⭐⭐ **This is collision candidate #16, and it arrives with the corpus's own machinery already fitted to it.** [[vararuci]] is Abhyankar's own **four-way** disambiguation of another grammarian's name; [[sarvavarman]] is a third. Batch 50's seventh generator — **the name as WARRANT**, a prestigious ancient name invoked to authorise a later work — predicts exactly a pre-Patañjali sage accumulating attributions. ⚠ **But the corpus does not assert that the *Paribhāṣāsūcana* is pseudonymous.** Candotti & Pontillo's hedges ("handed down as," "attributed to") are theirs and are recorded as hedges; the argument itself was **not readable** (see Sources).

⚠ **And a fifth designation the corpus refuses to fold in.** A reference aggregator lists, under the same ASCII lemma, that "**Vyādi is another name for Bhalipā**," one of the eighty-four *siddha*s of the Sahajayāna. ⚠ **That is a different word** — dental *d*, not retroflex *ḍ* — collapsed by transliteration, not by the tradition. ⭐ **A collision generated by a modern search index rather than by an ancient scribe**, and the corpus records it as a warning about its own retrieval method, not as a datum about Vyāḍi.

### 4. ⭐⭐ What Vyāḍi actually holds, and why it is not a small point

Abhyankar: "**Vyāḍi is well-known to have been the oldest exponent of the doctrine that words denote an individual object and not the genus**," with a page reference (pp. 136–8, vol. 7 of the D. E. Society's *Vyākaraṇa-Mahābhāṣya*).

[[mahabhashya]] already carries the other end of this from an independent source: Patañjali records the dispute between **Vyāḍi** — words denote "individual instances" — and **Vājapyāyana** — words denote "generic properties (*ākṛti*)" — and reaches an **inclusive** verdict. ⭐ **So the corpus's earliest named position on the [[jati|universal]] question belongs to a man whose own book is lost, and is preserved only because his opponent's side was preserved with it.** [[nirukta]] records the same axis one discipline over, in Yāska's Śākaṭāyana/Gārgya dispute, and makes the same observation: **the tradition kept the losing argument.**

⚠ Abhyankar also credits Vyāḍi with influence downstream: "**Bhartṛhari, possibly following Yāska and Vyāḍi**, has developed the theory of *dyotaka* as contrasted with *vācaka*." **"Possibly" is his word and is kept.**

### 5. ⭐ He had a school, and it is named in the Mahābhāṣya

Abhyankar, s.v. *Vaiyāḍīya*: "name given to the **pupils and students belonging to the school of Vyāḍi**; cf. *M. Bh.* on P. VI.2.36." ⭐ **That is a stronger fact than most of the biography**, and of a different kind: it is not an attribution of a book to a name but a *derived word for a group of people*, in the *Mahābhāṣya*. **A patronymic can be invented; a school-name in a grammarian's own examples is harder to invent.**

### 6. ⚠ The legend, and where it does and does not reach

Vyāḍi is a character in the ***Kathāsaritsāgara***: in Tawney's translation he and **Indradatta** are cousins, sons of two Brahman brothers of Vetasa, who go looking for a teacher, find the boy who remembers anything heard once — **Vararuci** — and bring him to study under **Varṣa**. Rājaśekhara's ***Kāvyamīmāṃsā*** (c. 900) has him as an *ācārya* and *kavi*, "a famous grammarian… known for his work on grammar named *Saṃgraha*, composed with one lac[h] [of] śloka[s]," examined at **Pāṭaliputra**.

⭐⭐ **The corpus already knows what to do with this text.** [[sarvavarman]] is rated `low` because its only witness is this same tale-collection, and Batch 50 found **Cowell** grounding the Vararuci = Kātyāyana identification on "**universal popular belief**… and the direct testimony of Somadeva" — *i.e.* on the *Kathāsaritsāgara*. **Vyāḍi, Vararuci and Pāṇini are all classmates in one 11th-century story-collection**, and that collection is the evidential circle the corpus has already named.

⭐ **But two things save this node from `sarvavarman`'s rating, and both are worth stating.** First, **the story attributes no grammar to Vyāḍi at all** — Tawney's chapter gives him no work; the *Saṅgraha* attribution comes from elsewhere. Second, **Abhyankar's relative-claim is not from the story either**: it is a philological inference from two patronymics in the *Mahābhāṣya*. ⚠ **Which is itself worth noticing.** Reading a biographical fact out of the incidental forms *dākṣāyaṇa* and *dākṣīputra* is a ***jñāpaka*-shaped move** — and [[paribhasha]] records that **the earliest attested use of the term *jñāpaka*** ("indicator": a feature of the wording taken to imply a convention never stated) **is in Vyāḍi's own *Paribhāṣāsūcana***. ⭐ **The technique by which the tradition establishes who Vyāḍi was is the technique Vyāḍi is credited with naming.** *(The corpus's observation; no source consulted makes it.)*

### 7. The *Paribhāṣāsūcana* is not a lost book, and this matters for the node's confidence

Unlike the *Saṅgraha*, it survives and is **usable**: Abhyankar cites Vyāḍi's paribhāṣās by number throughout the dictionary — Pari. **11, 17, 38, 42, 51, 53, 58, 68** appear in entries the corpus read directly — and prints it as item (i) of his own ***Paribhāṣāsaṃgraha***, "containing **93** Paribhāṣās with a commentary by Vyāḍi." Item (ii) is a **bare text of 140** paribhāṣās "belonging to the **school** of Vyāḍi" — ⭐ **two different sizes under one name, one of them explicitly the school's rather than the man's.** [[paribhasha]]'s census already carries both rows; this node supplies what they mean.

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Genuinely independent:** (a) the ***Vākyapadīya*** itself, read in a scholarly e-text; (b) **Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand; (c) **Aklujkar 1978**; (d) **Candotti & Pontillo 2018** (abstract only); (e) the **Cologne lexica** — Monier-Williams, Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum*, the DDSA dictionary — which are §4 signal 3 and, on the number of Vyāḍis, **disagree with (b)**; (f) **Rājaśekhara**, a poetician, reached through a doctoral thesis.

⚠ **NOT independent, and now provable rather than inferred:** the aggregator entry that repeats Abhyankar's Vyāḍi **credits him by name** — "Wikisource: *A dictionary of Sanskrit grammar* (K. V. Abhyankar)" — and its first sentence is verbatim the scan. ⭐ **Batch 50 inferred the wisdomlib = Abhyankar identity from a collision; this batch has the site's own attribution line. The trap is now documented, not merely suspected.**

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. Medium and not high because the central dispute — how many Vyāḍis, and when — is live in the sources; the *Saṅgraha* is unrecoverable in principle; the one modern study that would date the *Paribhāṣāsūcana* properly was **not readable** beyond its abstract; and **no line of the *Paribhāṣāsūcana* itself has been read**, only Abhyankar's numbered citations of it. Not lower than medium because the load-bearing facts — the lost *Saṅgraha*, the surviving 93 paribhāṣās, the individual-not-genus doctrine, the *Vaiyāḍīya* school-name — are attested across four independent kinds of source.
