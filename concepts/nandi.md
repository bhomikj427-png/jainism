## Gloss / Divergence map

**Nandi** is the **bull** *vāhana* (mount) and gatekeeper (*dvārapāla*) of **Śiva**, guardian of Mount Kailāsa, and **chief of Śiva's gaṇas**. The Sanskrit *nandi* means "joy, happiness, satisfaction." In nearly every Śaiva temple a seated Nandi sculpture sits on the **central axis facing the inner shrine** that holds Śiva's **liṅga**.

**The placement is doctrinal, not decorative.** Nandi facing the liṅga symbolises the **individual soul (jīva) oriented toward the Supreme (Parameśvara)** — the whole sight-line of the temple is a figure of the soul fixed in contemplation of God. The yogic gloss makes Nandi "the mind dedicated to Śiva, the absolute." (The word *jīva* here is Hindu Vedānta usage — to be kept distinct from the Jain *jīva*, a substance-soul; same vocabulary, different metaphysics.)

**Devotional role.** In Śaiva Siddhānta Nandi is reckoned chief among the *siddhas*, said to have received Śiva's and Pārvatī's teaching and transmitted it through eight disciples. Worshippers whisper their wishes into Nandi's right ear, trusting him as the **intermediary** who carries them to Śiva — the same "trusted attendant who conveys the devotee to the god" role Hanumān plays for Rāma.
