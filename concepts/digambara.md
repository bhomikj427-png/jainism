## Gloss

*Digambara* ("sky-clad," *dik* = sky/direction + *ambara* = garment) is one of the two major Jain sects (the other being Śvetāmbara, "white-clad"). Its defining mark is the practice of **monastic nudity**: a fully advanced male ascetic (*muni*) owns *literally nothing*, clothing included, taking the vow of non-possession (*aparigraha*) to its absolute limit — the sky is his only "garment." Beyond dress, the Digambara tradition holds several distinctive doctrinal positions: that **women cannot attain liberation in a female body** (and must first be reborn male — *strī-mokṣa* denied), that the **original scriptural canon (the Pūrvas/Aṅgas) is irretrievably lost** (so the Śvetāmbara Āgamas are rejected as inauthentic), that an **omniscient being (*kevalin*) no longer takes food** (*kevali-bhukti* denied), and that **all 24 tīrthaṅkaras were male** (so the 19th, Mallinātha, was a man, not the woman Mallī Devī of the Śvetāmbara account).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Digambara":** "sky-clad"; senior ascetics practise complete nudity as the ideal of detachment; the five characteristic positions (absolute non-possession, no female liberation, canon lost/Śvetāmbara texts rejected, kevalin doesn't eat, Mahāvīra never married); oldest surviving text the *Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama* (~2nd c. CE, Dharasena); Kundakunda authored *Samayasāra* and *Pravacanasāra*.

**Signal 2 — Britannica "Bhadrabahu I" + multiple history sources:** The schism narrative — Bhadrabāhu predicts a 12-year Magadha famine (~3rd c. BCE), leads ~12,000 monks south to Śravaṇabelagoḷa (Karnataka); those who stayed north under Sthūlabhadra relaxed the nudity rule and adopted white robes, and convened the **First Council at Pāṭaliputra (~300 BCE)** to fix a 12-Aṅga canon — which the returning southern (Digambara) group rejected.

**Signal 3 — Encyclopedia / academic gender-studies sources (MDPI, Encyclopedia.com):** Confirm the *strī-mukti* debate as a *defining* sectarian split, and the contested gender of Mallinātha (Digambara: male; Śvetāmbara: female).

**Independence:** Encyclopedic, historical, and gender-studies sources converge on the same set of differences and the same schism narrative. Confidence medium: the schism *date and cause* are themselves contested between the sects (each claims the other broke away), and the famine-migration story is traditional reconstruction, not contemporary documentation.

## The four/five sectarian differences (Digambara vs. Śvetāmbara)

| issue | Digambara | Śvetāmbara |
|---|---|---|
| monastic dress | **nudity** for the advanced muni (true aparigraha) | **white robes** permitted |
| women's liberation (*strī-mokṣa*) | **denied** — must be reborn male first | **affirmed** — women can liberate in this birth |
| scriptural canon | original Pūrvas **lost**; Śvetāmbara Āgamas rejected | the **Āgama canon preserved** (fixed at councils) |
| omniscient's eating (*kevali-bhukti*) | a kevalin **does not eat** | a kevalin **still takes food** |
| Mallinātha (19th tīrthaṅkara) | **male** (all 24 are male) | **female** (Mallī Devī) |

These are not arbitrary: they radiate from a *single* root commitment — **the rigour of aparigraha**. If true renunciation requires owning *nothing*, then (a) the monk wears nothing, (b) a being who must wear clothes for modesty (in the Digambara view, a woman) cannot complete the path in that body, and (c) the perfected being is beyond even the "possession" of needing food. The Śvetāmbara softening of the nudity rule (traced to the famine hardship) loosens the whole chain.

## The schism, told from both sides

Crucially, **each sect narrates the other as the breakaway.** Digambaras hold that *they* preserve Mahāvīra's original nude, canon-less practice and that the white-robed northerners *innovated* under famine pressure. Śvetāmbaras (per their own sources) attribute the split to a later figure, Śivabhūti (~609 years after Mahāvīra), who reintroduced nudity out of pride. The corpus presents this as a genuine *contested origin*, not a settled fact: what is agreed is the *result* (two sects with the five differences above), not *who left whom*.
