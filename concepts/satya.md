## Gloss / Divergence map

Satya is **truthfulness** — the second of the five great vows (*mahāvratas*). In Jain usage it is defined primarily through its opposite: abstention from *a-satya* (falsehood/non-truth), in keeping with the Jain preference for negative determination.

**TS 7.5 Sanskrit:** *krodhalobhabhīrutvahāsyapratyākhyānānyanuvīcibhāṣaṇaṃ ca pañca* — "Giving up anger, greed, fearfulness, and jest, and speaking only faultless words (*anuvīcibhāṣaṇa*) are the five observances for the vow of truthfulness." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**The five observances (*bhāvanās*) for satya:**

| # | observance | Sanskrit | what it guards against |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Abandon anger | krodha-pratyākhyāna | anger provokes false accusation or harsh speech |
| 2 | Abandon greed | lobha-pratyākhyāna | greed motivates deceptive speech for gain |
| 3 | Abandon fearfulness | bhīrutva-pratyākhyāna | cowardice motivates lying to avoid consequences |
| 4 | Abandon jest/mockery | hāsya-pratyākhyāna | jest can injure through falsehood presented as humour |
| 5 | Speak faultless words | anuvīcibhāṣaṇa | positive injunction: speak only what is true, beneficial, and measured |

Each observance addresses a distinct psychological root of falsehood — the Jain analysis is that lying is almost always motivated by one of these four afflictions.

**The ahiṃsā constraint on satya (from search-result aggregation; not in the fetched verse):** Jain truthfulness is subordinated to ahiṃsā. If telling the truth would cause violence — e.g., revealing to a hunter where a fleeing animal is hiding — that "truth" is *not* satya in the Jain ethical sense. Truth that violates non-violence is functionally a form of violence and must be withheld. This qualification is the primary Jain nuance distinguishing their satya from other truth-doctrines.

**The *satya-mahāvrata* in daily practice:** Jain ascetics recite the satya-vow twice daily during the obligatory *pratikramaṇa* (self-examination ritual). The vow is framed negatively: abstaining from all forms of a-satya (falsehood, half-truth, deceptive speech, injurious speech).

**Laypeople's minor vow (*aṇuvrata*):** Satya's aṇuvrata = not speaking injurious or false words under the influence of attachment/aversion/*moha* (delusion). The threshold is lower than the monk's absolute commitment.
