## Gloss / Divergence map

Abhidharma ("higher Dharma") is **the Buddhist systematic philosophical analysis of experience into its irreducible constituents** — called *dharmas* (Pali: *dhammas*). It constitutes the third "basket" (*piṭaka*) of the Buddhist canon and represents the scholastic apex of Buddhist psychology and metaphysics.

**Core methodology:** Any apparently unified phenomenon (a person, a physical object, a mental state) is analysed into its constituent *dharmas* — momentary, causally efficacious psycho-physical events. The analysis is exhaustive: Theravāda Abhidhamma catalogues **82 types** of possible dhammas; Sarvāstivāda catalogues **75 dharma types**.

**The dharmas of Abhidharma** are:
- *Momentary* — each exists for only a moment then ceases
- *Causally efficacious* — they produce effects on subsequent dharmas
- *Ultimately real* — they constitute ultimate truth (*paramārtha-satya*); wholes (persons, tables) are conventional (*saṃvṛti-satya*) constructs

**The two-truths framework in Abhidharma:**

| level | Sanskrit/Pali | content |
|---|---|---|
| Ultimate truth | *paramārtha-satya* | the stream of discrete dharma-events; what is genuinely, irreducibly real |
| Conventional truth | *saṃvṛti-satya* | persons, wholes, substances — convenient designations over the dharma-stream |

**Sarvāstivāda claim — dharmas exist in all three times:** The Sarvāstivāda school (a major Abhidharma school, Kashmir branch) held that dharmas exist in past, present, and future — hence the name ("all exists"). This was to explain how past karma can bear future fruit. The Madhyamaka (Nāgārjuna) responded that if dharmas existed permanently they would have *svabhāva*, and then śūnyatā would not hold — so Sarvāstivāda conflicts with Madhyamaka's fundamental thesis.

## Abhidharma dharmas vs. Jain dravyas — the structural opposition

This is one of the sharpest metaphysical contrasts in the corpus:

| dimension | Abhidharma dharmas | Jain dravyas |
|---|---|---|
| Duration | momentary — arise and cease within a single moment | eternal — dravyas persist indefinitely; change only in paryāya |
| Substance? | no — dharmas are events, not substances with attributes | yes — dravyas are genuine substances with guṇa and paryāya |
| How many types? | 82 (Theravāda) or 75 (Sarvāstivāda) | 6 (jīva, pudgala, dharma, adharma, ākāśa, kāla) |
| Persons/wholes real? | no — persons are conventional designations over dharma-streams | yes — jīva is a real individual; physical wholes (skandhas) are real aggregates of pudgala |
| Ultimate reality | the dharma-stream itself | the 6 co-equal, co-eternal dravyas |
| Method | analytical reduction to dharmas | ontological mapping of all substances |

The Abhidharma project is to *dissolve* apparently unified substances into streams of events. The Jain project is to *map* the genuine plurality of eternal substances. These are opposite metaphysical programs.

**Note on "dharma" terminology**: The word *dharma* in Abhidharma means "irreducible psycho-physical event" — completely different from the Jain *dharma-dravya* (the medium of motion). Same Sanskrit word, different technical meanings.
