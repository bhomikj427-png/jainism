## Gloss / Divergence map

Dravya is the Jain technical term for **substance** — any ultimate, irreducible existent. The *Tattvārthasūtra* gives two complementary definitions:

1. **Process definition** (TS 5.30, per Vijay K. Jain 2018): "Existence is with origination (utpāda), destruction (vyaya), and permanence (dhrauvya)." A dravya simultaneously undergoes modal change (its *paryāyas* arise and perish) while its underlying nature persists. This triple-characteristic is also the definition of existence (*sat*) in Jain ontology.

2. **Property/mode definition** (TS 5.38, per Vijay K. Jain 2018): "That which has qualities (guṇa) and modes (paryāya) is a substance (dravya)." Guṇas are permanent attributes inseparable from the substance; paryāyas are transient modifications.

The Jain universe comprises exactly **six dravyas**:

| dravya | Sanskrit | nature |
|---|---|---|
| Soul | jīva | sentient; characterized by consciousness (cetanā) |
| Matter | pudgala | non-sentient; characterized by touch/taste/smell/colour; sole locus of paramāṇus |
| Principle of motion | dharma | medium enabling movement (not the moral/religious dharma) |
| Principle of rest | adharma | medium enabling rest |
| Space | ākāśa | locus providing room for all other dravyas |
| Time | kāla | enables change/sequence; not an astikāya (see below) |

Five of the six (all except kāla) are **astikāyas** — they have multiple space-points (pradeśa) and spatial extension. Time (kāla) is a dravya (TS 5.39: "Time also is a substance") but is not an astikāya: kāla-atoms occupy single, non-combining space-points (Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi, via Vijay K. Jain 2018 commentary on TS 5.39).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain 2018 (Motilal Banarsidass):** TS 5.30 (utpāda/vyaya/dhrauvya definition), TS 5.38 (guṇa/paryāya definition), TS 5.39 (kāla as dravya), Pūjyapāda commentary on kāla's non-astikāya status. Directly fetched. Strong critical-edition provenance.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Dravya" article:** Confirms the six-dravya list and the astikāya/kāla distinction ("Time has only one [space-point]; therefore it is not a conglomerate"). Does not cite specific TS verses. Derivative — used for list confirmation only.

**Signal 3 — Nathmal Tatia 1994:** The same six dravyas and the utpāda/vyaya/dhrauvya formula are standard Jain doctrine; Tatia's edition confirmed (Cambridge Core) but Chapter 5 text not directly fetched. Would raise confidence to high if TS 5.30 and 5.38 wording confirmed from Tatia independently.

**Independence assessment:** Only Signal 1 is independently fetched with verse numbers. Signals converge on the list and definitions. Confidence capped at medium pending Tatia Chapter 5 verification.

**No genuine scholarly contest** was found on the core definition or the six-dravya list. The term "substance" as an English rendering is conventional; some scholars prefer "existent" or "real" to avoid Aristotelian connotations, but this is a terminological preference, not a doctrinal dispute within the tradition.
