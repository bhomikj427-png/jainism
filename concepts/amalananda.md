## Gloss / Divergence map

**Amalānanda** (also **Vyāsāśrama**), **c. 13th century CE**, from the Nāsik–Tryambak region on the Godāvarī, is the author of the ***Vedāntakalpataru*** — the gloss on [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]]'s *Bhāmatī* that is "the best-known" of the six commentaries the *Bhāmatī* attracted, and the work that carried the Bhāmatī school forward as a live scholastic programme rather than a single book. He also wrote a ***Pañcapādikā-darpaṇa*** on the **Vivaraṇa** side, and is credited on that basis with drawing "an early link between the Bhāmatī and the Vivaraṇa schools." A third work, a *Śāstra-darpaṇa* ("a straightforward presentation, in discourse style, of the settled position based on the *adhikaraṇa*s of the Brahma-sūtras"), is named by one source only.

### The finding: the Bhāmatī stack is five storeys, and it has a traditional name

[[prakasatman]] records the Vivaraṇa school as a **four-storey commentarial stack** — sūtra, bhāṣya, *Pañcapādikā*, *Vivaraṇa* — with the school named for the topmost storey rather than for Śaṅkara's own text. The Bhāmatī side has the same shape one storey taller, and the tradition names the whole assembly:

> "**Vedānta Śāstra** means 'the five texts — *brahmasūtra*, the commentary on it by Śrī Śaṅkara, the further commentary on it by Vācaspati Miśra, the commentary on the latter by Amalānanda named *Kalpataru*, and the commentary on that by Appayya Dīkṣita, named *Parimala*'."

| storey | text | author |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | *Brahma-sūtra* | [[brahma-sutra|Bādarāyaṇa]] |
| 2 | *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* | [[shankara|Śaṅkara]] |
| 3 | *Bhāmatī* | [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]] |
| 4 | ***Vedāntakalpataru*** | **Amalānanda** |
| 5 | *Kalpataru-parimala* | Appayya Dīkṣita (16th c.) |

That "Vedānta Śāstra" should denote a *five-member commentarial series* rather than a doctrine or a corpus of śruti is worth registering on its own terms: in this usage the discipline **is** the stack. Note also what storey 5 exists *for* — the *Kalpataru* is "renowned for its depth and complexity, often considered too abstruse for most students," and the *Parimala* was written to make it approachable. **Amalānanda's continuation of the Bhāmatī needed its own continuation to be readable**, which is a fair description of what happened to Advaita scholasticism generally between the 13th and 16th centuries.

The *Kalpataru*'s own work, on the reporting source's account, is that of a **consolidator**: "refining arguments, stabilizing definitions, and responding to objections drawn from contemporaneous schools such as Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, and Dvaita." That is a different job from [[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]]'s or [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]]'s — not a new doctrine of avidyā but the defence of an existing one against named opponents who did not exist when the *Bhāmatī* was written. **Dvaita** in that list is the datable element: it puts the *Kalpataru* after Madhva, and makes the Bhāmatī school's 13th-century form partly a response to him.

### The double authorship, and how much it licenses

Four independent reference sources give Amalānanda **both** the *Kalpataru* (Bhāmatī) and the *Pañcapādikā-darpaṇa* (Vivaraṇa), which is good attestation for the bare fact. What it licenses is narrower than "the schools had merged." The corpus records the fact and declines the inference: **glossing a text of the other school is not adopting its position**, and none of the sources fetched states what the *Pañcapādikā-darpaṇa* argues, whether it defends Padmapāda's line or answers it from the Bhāmatī side. "An early link between the schools" is the sources' phrase and is left as theirs. Until the *Darpaṇa* itself is read, the safe statement is that the two "schools" were **close enough to share a commentator** — which is already a corrective to reading the Bhāmatī/Vivaraṇa split as a sectarian partition, and is less than a synthesis.

**A reference-work error worth naming**, because the corpus can check it: the Centre for Brahmavidya page describes the *Pañcapādikā-darpaṇa* as a commentary on "**Vācaspati Miśra's** Pañcapādikā." The *Pañcapādikā* is [[padmapada|Padmapāda]]'s — this is exactly the datum recorded in [[padmapada]] and [[prakasatman]]. The likely mechanism is contamination from the surrounding sentence: Amalānanda's *other* commentary **is** on Vācaspati, and the attribution slid across. It is the same class of error already logged for Hindupedia in [[vacaspati-mishra]].

### Three formulations of the lineage — and why they are compatible

Unlike [[prakasatman]] and [[sarvajnatman]], whose genealogies do not survive arithmetic, Amalānanda's do:

| source | claim |
|---|---|
| Hindupedia; wisdomlib | "a grand-disciple of **Citsukha**" |
| advaita-vedanta.org | "a disciple of **Sukhaprakāśa**" |
| Centre for Brahmavidya | initiatory guru **Anubhavānanda** (himself a disciple of Ānandātman); *studied under* **Sukhaprakāśa**, who was a disciple of **Citsukha** (author of the *Tattvapradīpikā*) |

The third reading dissolves the first two into one picture: Sukhaprakāśa ← Citsukha makes "grand-disciple of Citsukha" true **through study**, while the *initiatory* guru is a different man. Two apparently competing claims turn out to name **two different relations** — *dīkṣā*-guru and *vidyā*-guru — that a one-line reference entry has no room to distinguish. And it is chronologically coherent: Citsukha is an early-13th-century figure, so a grand-pupil writing in the 13th–14th century needs no special pleading. The corpus keeps this node as its **control case** for lineage claims — the shape a genealogy has when it is *not* doing institutional work.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Hindupedia, "Later Advaitins"** — c. 13th c.; both works with their schools named; "grand-disciple of Citsukha."
2. **The Advaita Vedānta Home Page, "Post-Śaṅkaran Authors"** (last updated 1999; an early scholarly-hobbyist reference in ITRANS transliteration, and **prior to** the Wikipedia-era sources, so not derivable from them) — "amalAnanda, a disciple of sukhaprakASa, wrote the vedAntakalpataru on the bhAmatI and also a pancapAdikA-darpaNa"; "an early link between the bhAmatI and vivaraNa schools." This page also supplied a datum used to refine [[sarvajnatman]].
3. **Centre for Brahmavidya, "Sri Amalananda"** — the alias **Vyāsāśrama**; 13th–14th c.; the Nāsik–Tryambak origin; the full three-name lineage; the third work *Śāstra-darpaṇa*; **and the Pañcapādikā misattribution** recorded above.
4. **Wisdomlib, "Amalānanda"** — catalogue entry: c. 13th c., both works, grand-disciple of Citsukha; the name from *a-mala*, "unblemished."
5. **DharmaRenaissance Blog, "Kalpataru's Wish-Fulfilling Wisdom"** and an **Advaita-L list posting (2006)** — respectively the *Kalpataru*'s consolidating function against Nyāya/Mīmāṃsā/Dvaita, and the "five texts" definition of *Vedānta Śāstra* plus the *Parimala*'s purpose. **Weakest-provenance items in the set** (a blog and a mailing-list archive): used for the traditional *formula* and the *characterisation*, not for any dating or attribution, and neither carries a claim the other four do not already support.
6. **Corpus cross-check** — [[vacaspati-mishra]], [[padmapada]], [[prakasatman]]: the *Bhāmatī*'s content and the four-storey Vivaraṇa stack were recorded in Batches 41–42, which is what makes the five-storey parallel visible and the *Pañcapādikā* misattribution checkable.

**CONVERGED.** Date (13th c.), both principal works and their school-affiliations, and the "early link" characterisation are stated identically by four independent reference sources; the apparent lineage divergence resolves rather than splits. Confidence **medium**, not high: **no work of Amalānanda's was read** — no edition of the *Vedāntakalpataru* or the *Pañcapādikā-darpaṇa* was located, so every doctrinal statement here is *about* his works rather than *from* them, and the entire "early link" claim rests on the bare fact of dual authorship. The *Śāstra-darpaṇa* is single-sourced and flagged as such.
