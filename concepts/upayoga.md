## Gloss

*Upayoga* ("active or attentive consciousness") is the defining mark (*lakṣaṇa*) of the soul (*jīva*): TS 2.8 *upayogo lakṣaṇam* — "Cognition is the mark of the soul." It is what distinguishes a living being from inert matter (*ajīva*): matter can be bound to a soul, but the soul's *upayoga* remains its irreducible signature. Upayoga encompasses the soul's entire cognitive activity, dividing into two fundamental modes — determinate knowledge (*jñāna*) and indeterminate perception/intuition (*darśana*) — which correspond to the two primary types of pramāṇa.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 2.8 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda):** *upayogo lakṣaṇam* — "Cognition (upayoga) is the mark of the soul." Pūjyapāda's commentary: upayoga is "active or attentive consciousness" arising from both internal and external causes; the gold/silver analogy shows that even when soul and body are mixed in bondage, they remain distinct by their respective marks.

**Signal 2 — Jainworld / HereNow4U (Jain institutional sources, independent):** Confirm the two-type division: sākāra (with-form) = jñāna; nirākāra (formless) = darśana. In ordinary souls, darśana precedes jñāna; in kevalis, both arise simultaneously. The 8 sub-types of jñāna and 4 of darśana are the standard Jain enumeration.

**Independence:** Signal 1 is primary-text fetch; Signal 2 is Jain institutional. Moderate independence. Confidence medium because no independent scholarly monograph (Jaini, Matilal) directly fetched.

## The two modes of upayoga

| Mode | Type | Description | Sub-types |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sākāra** (with-form, determinate) | *Jñāna* (knowledge) | Cognizing specific determinate objects | 8: mati + shruta + avadhi + manaḥparyāya + kevala (each in valid/invalid pairs) |
| **Nirākāra** (formless, indeterminate) | *Darśana* (perception/intuition) | Undifferentiated awareness; apprehension without specification | 4: cakṣu (visual), acakṣu (non-visual), avadhi (clairvoyant), kevala (omniscient) |

The two modes are sequential in bound souls (darśana first, then jñāna) but simultaneous in omniscients (*kevalis*). This sequencing is why Jain epistemology places perception (*darśana*) as chronologically prior to determinate knowledge — the soul first registers that something is there (nirākāra), then cognises what it is (sākāra).

## Upayoga as the locus of liberation

The liberation arc is an upayoga arc:
- Karma obscures both jñāna-upayoga and darśana-upayoga (jñānāvaraṇīya and darśanāvaraṇīya karmas)
- As gunasthāna stages advance and karma is shed, upayoga expands
- At stage 13–14 (arihant / kevala-jñāna): full sākāra + nirākāra upayoga, no karma obscuring either
- Siddha: upayoga freed from matter entirely; pure, infinite, unobstructed

This makes upayoga the experiential content of liberation, not just the mark of being alive.

## Upayoga vs. cetanā

Some Jain texts use *cetanā* (consciousness) as a near-synonym for upayoga. The distinction is subtle: cetanā is the capacity; upayoga is the actualised exercise of that capacity. A soul in dreamless sleep has cetanā (the capacity) but diminished upayoga (the active exercise). Most contexts treat them interchangeably at the level of this corpus.
