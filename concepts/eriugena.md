## Gloss / Divergence map

**Johannes Scottus Eriugena** (c. 800 – c. 877) is the one figure in the Latin West between Augustine and the twelfth century who built a **complete Neoplatonic system** — and he did it, on the standard account, **without reading any pagan Neoplatonist**.

### The transmission fact that defines him

> **Eriugena "did not have direct knowledge" of pagan Neoplatonists like [[plotinus|Plotinus]] or [[proclus|Proclus]]. His Neoplatonism derives entirely from Christian Eastern sources** — Pseudo-Dionysius and Maximus Confessor.

He is, in other words, a **third-generation Neoplatonist by inheritance rather than by reading**: he acquired the One, the procession and the return through a Christian intermediary who had absorbed them, and rebuilt the system in Latin from that. His translations are the mechanism — around **860** he produced a complete Latin *Corpus Dionysii*, plus Maximus's *Ambigua ad Ioannem* and *Quaestiones ad Thalassium*, Gregory of Nyssa's *De hominis opificio*, and possibly Epiphanius's *Anchoratus*.

This makes Eriugena the corpus's sharpest case of a phenomenon it keeps meeting: **a system reconstituted downstream of its sources, by someone who never saw them.** Compare [[psellos]] — a Christian who *did* read the pagan texts and preserved them without adopting them; Eriugena is the mirror image, adopting the structure without access to the texts. Between the two, almost the whole problem of how late-antique philosophy reached the Middle Ages is visible.

### The *Periphyseon* and its fourfold division

The *Periphyseon* (c. 862–867) takes ***natura*** as "**the general term for all things that are and all things that are not**" — a category wider than being itself — and divides it four ways by two binary predicates, *creating* and *created*:

| # | species | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **creates and is not created** | God as source |
| 2 | **creates and is created** | the Primary Causes (the divine ideas) |
| 3 | **is created and does not create** | temporal effects — the world of things |
| 4 | **neither creates nor is created** | God as end — non-being, the term of the return |

The elegance is that **1 and 4 are the same God**, taken as origin and as terminus. The scheme is therefore not a hierarchy of four things but a **circuit**: procession from God through the causes into effects, and return through the same stages to God. That the fourth division is *non-being* is the whole apophatic point — the end of the return is not an object.

### God as *nihil*

Following Pseudo-Dionysius, Eriugena holds that "**denials concerning God… are 'more true'**" than affirmations: God transcends every category, including being, and is unknowable in principle — **not even to himself as an object**.

From which the doctrine that got him condemned: God is ***nihil per excellentiam***, "**nothingness through excellence**," and creation is *ex nihilo* in the sense that it comes **out of God's own superabundant nothingness**, with all creation "remaining within divine nature."

⚠️ **This is the point where a careless comparison would ruin the node.** *Nihil per excellentiam* is **not** [[sunyata|śūnyatā]], and not any Indian emptiness. Eriugena's "nothing" is a **superlative predicated of a fully positive source**: God is called nothing because *no* category of being applies to something that exceeds them all, and the world genuinely proceeds from him. Madhyamaka's emptiness is the **absence of svabhāva** in everything including itself, asserted precisely to block any such source. The two use a negation for opposite structural jobs — one to protect a plenum, one to refuse one. The corpus draws the edge and labels it a conflation to avoid.

### The pantheism charge — a real dispute, not a smear

| reading | evidence | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **His system collapses the God–creation distinction** | critics accused him of teaching "**God is the form of all things**" (*forma omnium*); creation is said to remain *within* divine nature; the *Periphyseon* was condemned in **1210** and again by **Honorius III in 1225**, alongside David of Dinant and Amaury of Bène — Amaury having been condemned for saying God is the formal principle of all things; the book stayed on the *Index Librorum Prohibitorum* **until the 1960s** | the fourfold division is monism with Christian vocabulary, and the condemnations read it correctly |
| **He balances transcendence and immanence** | "Eriugena explicitly rejects identifying God as the '**genus**' of which creatures are '**parts**'" — the precise move a pantheist would need to make; and his apophaticism keeps God beyond every category the identification would require | the charge mistakes *theophany* (the world as God's self-manifestation) for *identity*, and the condemnations are guilt by 13th-century association |

The corpus records both and does not adjudicate. Two things are worth noting about the shape of the dispute. First, **the condemnation is 350 years posthumous** and arrives bundled with two contemporaries of the condemners — so it is evidence about 13th-century Paris at least as much as about the 9th-century text. Second, the "genus/parts" refusal is a **technical** disclaimer, and technical disclaimers are exactly what a system under this kind of pressure produces; whether it does real work in his metaphysics is a question about the *Periphyseon*, which was not read here.

### The comparison worth drawing carefully

Eriugena's system and [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]] look alike at a structural level, and the corpus draws the parallel with the disanalogies attached:

| | Eriugena | Advaita |
|---|---|---|
| ultimate | God, *nihil per excellentiam*, beyond being | [[brahman]], *nirguṇa*, beyond predication |
| method | apophatic — denials are "more true" | *neti neti* — not this, not this |
| world | **theophany**: real self-manifestation of God, genuinely created | [[vivartavada|vivarta]]: apparent transformation, not a real one |
| the goal | ***reditus*** — return of all things to God, with an *apokatastasis* tendency | [[moksha-advaita|mokṣa]] — recognition of an identity that was never lost |
| what makes it heterodox at home | pantheism charge; condemned twice | none — it is the orthodox reading of the [[brahma-sutra]] |

The **third row is the load-bearing difference**. Eriugena's world is *made*, and its reality is not in question; Advaita's world is *apparent*, and its reality is precisely what is in question. A structural parallel that ignores that row turns two different systems into one, which is the failure this project exists to prevent. Note also the last row: **the same structural move is heresy in one tradition and orthodoxy in the other** — a fact about institutions, not about the arguments.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "John Scottus Eriugena"** (fetched): dates c. 800 – c. 877 and the *Periphyseon* at c. 862–867; the full translation list with the *Corpus Dionysii* around 860; *natura* as "the general term for all things that are and all things that are not" and the four species; "denials concerning God… are 'more true'"; ***nihil per excellentiam*** and creation from God's "superabundant nothingness"; the pantheism debate **with both sides stated**, including the *forma omnium* accusation and his explicit rejection of the genus/parts model; the **1210 and 1225** condemnations with David of Dinant and Amaury of Bène and the *Index* listing until the 1960s; and the decisive statement that he **had no direct knowledge of Plotinus or Proclus**. Peer-reviewed and by far the strongest signal here.
2. **Search aggregation** (Wikipedia's "De divisione naturae"; the Dumbarton Oaks / Harvard *Periphyseon* edition pages; general reference entries): the fourfold division as "a procession from and return to the One"; **Honorius III** named for the 1225 condemnation; the link to **Amaury of Bène**, "condemned for saying that God is the formal principle of all things," which "**recalled Eriugena's statement that God is the '*forma omnium*'**"; and his dependence on Pseudo-Dionysius together with the **apokatastasis** tendency as factors in "his strained orthodoxy." This supplies the *mechanism* of the guilt-by-association reading, which signal 1 reports without explaining.
3. **Corpus cross-check** ([[neoplatonism]], [[plotinus-one]], [[proclus]], [[henosis]], [[advaita-vedanta]], [[brahman]], [[sunyata]]): the procession/return structure and the apophatic ultimate are already recorded in the corpus from the pagan side, which is what makes the "arrived without reading them" fact assessable rather than merely surprising; and [[sunyata]] supplies the contrast that keeps *nihil per excellentiam* from being mis-glossed.

**CONTESTED**, on the pantheism question — which is the substantive philosophical dispute about him and remains open in the literature. Converged on the dates, the translations, the fourfold division, the apophatic method, and the condemnations. Confidence **medium**: signal 1 is excellent and would ordinarily support more, but it is **one source**, signal 2 is derivative, and the ***Periphyseon* itself was not read in any edition** — which matters more than usual here, because the pantheism question turns on how much work the genus/parts disclaimer actually does in the text.
