## Gloss / Divergence map

**Akṣapāda Gautama** (also **Gotama**; epithet **Dīrghatapas**) is credited as the principal author of the **[[nyaya-sutra|Nyāyasūtra]]**, the founding text of the **Nyāya** ("logic / method") darśana. He is the author-anchor behind the project's Nyāya epistemology files ([[pramana-nyaya]], [[anumana-nyaya]], [[hetvabhasa]], [[tarka]]). His dates are radically uncertain — estimates run from the **6th c. BCE** (a contemporary of the Buddha and Mahāvīra) to the **2nd c. CE** — and whether one person wrote the whole text or it grew by accretion is itself debated. Karl Potter's summary is honest: "we have not the vaguest idea who wrote the Nyāyasūtras or when he lived." He is thus, like [[kapila]], an *anchor by attribution* more than a documented individual.

**The name "Akṣapāda."** The epithet means "one with **eyes on his feet**" — a traditional image of his analytic, ground-watching acuity (later legend literalises it).

**The Gautama name-collisions (NOT-equivalents, flagged in prose).** "Gautama" is one of the most over-loaded names in Indian thought, and the tradition itself warns against three conflations:
- **≠ Gautama Buddha** (Siddhārtha), for whom *Gautama* is a **clan name**, not a philosophical signature — the logician-Gautama defends a substantial **ātman** that Buddhism's anattā denies, so the two are doctrinal opponents who merely share a name.
- **≠ Gautama the Dharmasūtra author** (the *Gautama Dharmasūtra*), a separate legal-ritual writer.

(No nodes exist for these other Gautamas, so the cautions live in prose; a typed `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` edge needs a target node.)

**What he founds.** The Nyāyasūtra opens with the sixteen padārthas (NS 1.1.1) and grounds liberation (*apavarga / niḥśreyasa*) in correct knowledge; its machinery — the **four pramāṇas** (perception, inference, comparison, testimony), the **five-membered inference** (pañcāvayava), and the catalogue of fallacies and debate-faults — is worked out in the doctrine files. **Vātsyāyana's Nyāyabhāṣya** (~5th c.) is the earliest surviving complete commentary and the lens through which Gautama is read.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Akshapada Gautama"** (fetched): principal author of the Nyāyasūtra, foundational text of the Nyāya school; dates 6th c. BCE–2nd c. CE (Potter's "no idea who/when"); composite-authorship question; "Akṣapāda" = "eyes on his feet"; explicit warning not to conflate with Gautama Buddha (clan name); Vātsyāyana's bhāṣya as earliest complete commentary.
2. **Corpus ([[nyaya-sutra]], from Wikipedia "Nyāya Sūtras" + IEP "Nyāya")**: independently records Gautama/Akṣapāda as author (~200 CE), Vātsyāyana ~450, the sixteen padārthas, four pramāṇas, five-membered inference, and niḥśreyasa soteriology.

Two independent reference streams agree on the authorship, the wide dating, the commentary, and the founding content. Confidence **medium**: the date is a centuries-wide range, single-vs-composite authorship is unsettled, and the Nyāyasūtra Sanskrit was not fetched verbatim (only NS 1.2.4 is verified, in [[hetvabhasa]]).
