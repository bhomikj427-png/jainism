## Gloss

*Lakṣmī* (also *Śrī*) is the Hindu goddess of prosperity, wealth, fortune, and auspiciousness — and Viṣṇu's *śakti* (divine energy/consort). Two terms are historically distinct but converge on the same deity: *Śrī* in Vedic literature denotes general auspiciousness (~130 RV occurrences); *Lakṣmī* in Purāṇic texts emphasises material and spiritual wealth. The Vajasaneyī Saṃhitā 31.22 once names them as two separate consorts of Viṣṇu: *"Śrīś ca te Lakṣmīś ca patnyau"* — their later equation is a theological synthesis, not an original identity.

The Viṣṇu Purāṇa states: **"Viṣṇu is the meaning, Śrī is the speech."** This sentence frames Lakṣmī as the *expressive* dimension of the divine — the universe as the utterance of Viṣṇu's will, with Lakṣmī giving that will audible/visible form. In Rāmānuja's *Śrī Vaiṣṇavism* (Viśiṣṭādvaita), Lakṣmī is not a subordinate consort but the supreme goddess and the *necessary mediator* between devotee and Viṣṇu — devotion to her is the gateway to Viṣṇu.

**Tridevi:** Lakṣmī (prosperity / Rajas guṇa) + Sarasvatī (knowledge / Sattva guṇa) + Pārvatī/Śakti (power / Tamas guṇa) form the feminine counterpart to the Trimurti. Each goddess embodies one of the three guṇas and pairs with one of the three gods.

**Key texts and myth:**

| source | content |
|---|---|
| Śrī Sūkta (RV appendix, 5th maṇḍala, 15 vv.) | earliest dedicated hymn; golden-hued, lotus-seated, elephants |
| Vajasaneyī Saṃhitā 31.22 | Śrī and Lakṣmī as distinct (later unified) |
| Rāmāyaṇa (Samudramanthana) | Lakṣmī arises from the churning of the cosmic ocean holding a lotus — epithet *Padmā* |
| Viṣṇu Purāṇa | "Viṣṇu is the meaning, Śrī is the speech"; mother of the world; Viśiṣṭādvaita root |

**Iconography's four-hand schema** encodes the four *puruṣārthas*: *dharma* (righteousness) / *artha* (prosperity) / *kāma* (desire) / *mokṣa* (liberation) — Lakṣmī is not merely material prosperity but the complete human good made visible.
