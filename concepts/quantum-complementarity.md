## Gloss

**Quantum complementarity** (Niels Bohr, 1927) is the empirical principle that quantum systems have pairs of properties — wave-like and particle-like behaviour; position and momentum; energy and time — that are *mutually exclusive in any given experimental arrangement*. In any single experiment, only one of a complementary pair can be fully manifest; the other is correspondingly undefined or blurred. This mutual exclusivity is not a limitation of measurement technique but a fundamental feature of quantum mechanics, quantified by the Heisenberg uncertainty relations (Robertson, 1929: *ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2*). It has nothing to do with subjective perspective, standpoint epistemology, or many-valued logic.

## Why quantum complementarity ≠ anekāntavāda

This is the file's primary research contribution. The surface similarity — both systems resist capture by a single classical description — has driven widespread conflation in popular writing. The differences are decisive:

| Feature | Quantum complementarity | Jain anekāntavāda |
|---|---|---|
| **Domain** | Physical measurement of quantum systems | Metaphysical attributes of any substance |
| **Exclusivity** | Hard: position AND momentum *cannot* simultaneously have sharp values | Soft: attributes coexist in the object; they are perspectivally brought out by *naya* (standpoint), not measurement |
| **Ontology** | Wave function is not "both wave and particle" simultaneously — it is a quantum state that *responds differently* to different measurement setups | All valid predications (asti, nāsti, avaktavya…) are simultaneously true of the object |
| **Mechanism** | Heisenberg commutation relations; measurement disturbance; collapse of the wave function | Multiple-aspect realism; *syāt* hedging marks epistemic limit, not ontological exclusivity |
| **Quantitative?** | Yes — ΔxΔp ≥ ℏ/2 | No — not a mathematical inequality |
| **Experimental prediction** | Makes specific probabilistic predictions about measurement outcomes | Makes no physical predictions |
| **Role of observer** | Measurement apparatus determines which property manifests | *Naya* (standpoint) selects an aspect for *linguistic* description; the aspects still all exist simultaneously in the substance |

**The crux:** Quantum complementarity says "you *cannot* have sharp values of both observables *at once*." Anekāntavāda says "the object *does have* both attributes — permanence and change, unity and plurality — simultaneously." These are structurally opposite ontological claims, not convergent ones. The wave-particle duality resolves into the quantum state; the sat-asat duality of a dravya does not resolve — both are equally real at all times.

## The analogy that misleads

The surface that drives the conflation: both traditions recognise that a single, fixed, classical description is inadequate for describing their respective objects (quantum systems / substances). Both call for perspectival or contextual descriptions. This is a genuine structural-level parallel — and it is the only valid bridge. It earns `structurally-parallel-to`, not `equivalent`.

D.S. Kothari's 1961 lecture drew this bridge explicitly as *analogy* for the benefit of cross-cultural dialogue. The conflation occurred when later popular writers upgraded the analogy to an identity — "the ancient Jains already knew quantum mechanics." This project exists to prevent exactly that reading.
