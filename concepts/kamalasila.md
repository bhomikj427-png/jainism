## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kamalaśīla** (c. 740–795 CE) was an Indian Buddhist monk-scholar of **Nālandā** and the **student of [[santaraksita]]**, whose **Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka** line he continued and defended. He is best known for two things: a body of foundational works, and his role in the **Samye (Lhasa) Debate**.

**Major works.** (i) the three ***Bhāvanākrama*** ("Stages of Meditation"), enormously influential in Tibet; (ii) the ***Madhyamakāloka*** ("Illumination of the Middle Way"), his Madhyamaka magnum opus; and (iii) the ***Tattvasaṃgrahapañjikā***, his commentary on Śāntarakṣita's *Tattvasaṃgraha* — so his work both inherits and elaborates his teacher's.

**The gradual path vs the sudden path (Samye Debate, c. 792–794 CE).** Hosted by the Tibetan king Trisong Detsen at Samye monastery, the debate set **Kamalaśīla, the gradualist**, against **Moheyan (Heshang Mohoyen), the Chinese Chan "subitist."** Kamalaśīla's thesis is that "practitioners must *gradually cultivate the causes and conditions* which make the arrival of awakening possible" — enlightenment comes through the staged cultivation of **insight (vipaśyanā) united with calm-abiding (śamatha)**, of **wisdom (prajñā) together with method (upāya, i.e. compassion/bodhicitta)**, and of the pāramitās and merit — *not* through a single non-conceptual leap of "no-thought" as Moheyan held. The *Bhāvanākrama* is the textual form of this refutation. **Tibetan sources report Kamalaśīla's victory** (Chinese sources claim Moheyan won), and the event is said to have steered Tibetan Buddhism decisively toward the late-Indian gradualist model.

**Note on the school-label.** "Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka" is the standard later (Tibetan doxographic) classification of the Śāntarakṣita–Kamalaśīla line; the sources here call him simply a Madhyamaka adherent. Like his teacher, he is **Svātantrika** — he uses autonomous inference (svatantra-anumāna) to establish emptiness, the method the Prāsaṅgikas reject.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Kamalaśīla"): dates c. 740–795 CE; Nālandā; student of Śāntarakṣita (725–788); Madhyamaka affiliation; the three works (Bhāvanākrama, Madhyamakāloka, Tattvasaṃgraha commentary); the Samye Debate (793 CE) against Moheyan with the gradualist thesis ("cultivation of insight and method in stages") and the contested-victory transmission. **Signal 2** (Wikipedia, "Bhāvanākrama"): the three Sanskrit texts; the gradualist thesis ("gradually cultivate the causes and conditions which make the arrival of awakening possible") against Chan sudden-enlightenment; content — śamatha + vipaśyanā, the bodhisattva path (compassion, bodhicitta), prajñā + upāya, the pāramitās; the Samye context.

The two articles are independent in focus (the person vs the central text) and agree on lineage, works, the debate, and the gradualist doctrine. Medium, not high: the *Bhāvanākrama* / *Madhyamakāloka* were not fetched verbatim; the Samye Debate's outcome is itself a contested historiographical question (the two source-traditions disagree), so the "victory" is reported, not asserted; one source loosely dates the Bhāvanākrama to the 9th century, against the standard late-8th-century dating used here (the Samye debate fixes him to the 790s).
