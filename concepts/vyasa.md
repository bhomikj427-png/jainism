## Gloss

**Vyāsa** ("the arranger / divider"; full name **Kṛṣṇa-Dvaipāyana Vyāsa** — "the dark one, island-born") is the legendary sage to whom Hindu tradition ascribes an enormous share of the Sanskrit canon. The name is a **title for a function**, not merely a personal name: he is called *Vyāsa* because he "divided" the one primordial Veda into four (Ṛg, Sāma, Yajur, Atharva), earning the epithet **Veda-Vyāsa**.

**Traditional attributions:**

| work | role | corpus node |
|---|---|---|
| Mahābhārata | composer (dictated to Gaṇeśa as scribe) | `mahabharata` |
| Division of the Veda into four | the act that names him | — |
| The eighteen *Purāṇas* | arranger/abridger | — |
| Brahma-sūtra (Vedānta-sūtra) | *identified with* Bādarāyaṇa, its author | `brahma-sutra` |

**A character in his own epic.** Within the Mahābhārata, Vyāsa is no mere narrator: through the *niyoga* practice he is the biological grandfather of both warring lineages — father of Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Pāṇḍu, and Vidura — and intervenes as a spiritual counsellor. The author writes himself into the story he tells.

**Divine and immortal status.** Tradition makes Vyāsa a *chiranjīvī* (one of the seven immortals said to survive into the present Kali Yuga) and a **partial incarnation (*aṃśāvatāra*) of Viṣṇu**: the Viṣṇu Purāṇa says that "in every Dvāpara age Viṣṇu, in the person of Vyāsa, divides the Veda." He is honoured as the archetypal *guru* on **Guru Pūrṇimā** (also called Vyāsa Pūrṇimā).

**Historicity caution.** Modern scholarship treats "Vyāsa" as very likely a **role-title** for the Veda-compiler discharged across ages and editors, rather than a single historical author. The traditional attributions are claims of *authority and lineage*, not verifiable biography — and the identification of Vyāsa with **Bādarāyaṇa** (the Brahma-sūtra author) is a tradition-internal conflation of probably-distinct figures, not an established fact.
