## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kaustubha** ("crest-jewel" — an epithet-style formation; the deeper root is thin in the sources consulted and not asserted beyond this gloss) is one of the treasures (*ratna*) produced by the [[samudra-manthana|churning of the cosmic ocean]] — the divine gem that comes to rest permanently on [[vishnu|Viṣṇu]]'s chest, giving him the epithet **Kaustubhadhāri** ("bearer of the Kaustubha"). This file covers the gem itself, not the churning mechanics, which belong to `samudra-manthana.md`.

Unlike several other churning-products in this repo's cluster (the horse Uccaiḥśravas, the poison Halahala — see [[ucchaihshravas]], [[halahala]] — whose ownership or exact identity is contested across texts), **Kaustubha's role is uncontested**: every source checked places it on Viṣṇu's chest with no rival claimant or divergent account. The wider "14 *ratnas*" list it belongs to does vary in count and membership across sources (already logged in `samudra-manthana.md`) — Kaustubha itself is one of the list's *stable* members, not a contested one.

**Continuity into avatāric iconography:** the gem is treated as the same object carried forward into [[krishna|Kṛṣṇa]]'s iconography (Mahābhārata Sabhā Parva ch. 5 describes Kṛṣṇa's Kaustubha "glittering with great luster") — i.e. one continuous attribute across Viṣṇu-proper and his avatāra, not a separately gifted duplicate.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva §XVIII** (Ganguli translation) — the gem emerges from the churned ocean already described as gracing "the breast of Nārāyaṇa," with no separate claiming/gifting scene — the association is immediate in the earliest telling.
2. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.8.6** (Sanskrit + translation) — *"kaustubhākhyam abhūd ratnaṁ... tasmin maṇau spṛhāṁ cakre vakṣo-'laṅkaraṇe hariḥ"*, "the gem named Kaustubha... Hari desired that jewel to decorate his chest" — a genuinely independent textual witness (different text, different era) agreeing with the Mahābhārata on the gem-to-Viṣṇu's-chest association, here made explicit as Viṣṇu's own desire rather than simple description.
3. Wikipedia's "Kaustubha" entry and a Skanda Purāṇa detail (ch. 11, vv. 51–53, illuminating the three worlds) were checked but are single-source/derivative-secondary for anything beyond the core fact — not weighted as independent corroboration, only as corroborating color.

Two genuinely independent primary/primary-adjacent texts (Mahābhārata, Bhāgavata Purāṇa) converge on the core fact → **converged**. Confidence held at **medium** rather than high because the etymology and the Skanda Purāṇa radiance-detail rest on thin, single-source, or unverified-against-critical-edition material.
