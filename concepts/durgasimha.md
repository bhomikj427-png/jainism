## Gloss / Divergence map

**Durgasiṃha** is "the famous commentator of the [[katantra|Kātantra]] Sūtras" (Abhyankar) — author of "the **earliest commentary** on the Kātantra Sūtras," the *Kātantravṛtti*, which is how the Kātantra was read, taught and eventually printed. ⭐ **He is also the densest collision cluster the corpus has met: four distinct identity questions attach to one name, and Abhyankar raises three of them himself without resolving any.**

| point | reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **date** | "lived in the **ninth or the tenth century**" | **Abhyankar** | — |
| | the *Vṛtti* written in the **eighth century** | *Glimpses of the History of Sanskrit Literature* | ⚠ **a live split, and the corpus does not average it** |
| **is he Durgācārya, Yāska's commentator?** | ⚠ "**It is doubtful whether this commentator Durgasiṃha is the same as Durgācārya**, the famous commentator of Yāska's [[nirukta\|Nirukta]]" | **Abhyankar**, raising the doubt himself | **collision #14** — grammar / etymology, two *vedāṅgas* |
| **is "Durgasiṃha" even a name?** | ⚠⚠ "Some scholars believe that the term… was **only a title given to Durgasiṃha for his profound scholarship**, and it was Durgasiṃha who was the author of the well-known work ***Amarakośa***" | **Abhyankar**, reporting "some scholars" | ⭐⭐ **collision #15, and it inverts the usual form**: not two men sharing a name but **one man behind two names, one of them a title** |

| **who he was** | ⚠ "There is a **legend** that Durgasiṃha was the **brother of Vikramāditya**, the founder of the Vikrama Era" | **Abhyankar**, flagging it as legend | nothing — but ⭐ see the pattern below |

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐⭐⭐ Attribution caught in the act, in the manuscript apparatus.** [[prakrta-prakasha]] records Ollett's account of how ancient texts acquire authors — "the will to remember otherwise — to reach back into the past and overwrite it, to reassign identities, to constantly **reauthorize** the text." That is a historian's generalisation. **Abhyankar, without any interest in the point, supplies the mechanism as a bare fact about two shelves**: the Poona manuscript of the Kātantra *Paribhāṣāvṛtti* carries **no author's name**; the India Office Library copy **names Durgasiṃha**. ⭐⭐ **One text, two manuscripts, and an author appears in one of them.** No inference is needed and none is offered. **This is the single cleanest piece of evidence in the corpus for how the attributions it keeps distrusting actually get made.**

⚠⚠ **And it forces a flag on [[paribhasha]].** That node's census — Batch 49's T4, confirmed "in its strongest form" — tabulates fifteen *paribhāṣā* collections across nine systems, including **"gloss by Durgasiṃha | 65 | Kātantra."** ⭐ **Abhyankar, the source of the census, doubts that attribution on the next page.** The *count* is unaffected (the collection exists and has 65 maxims, which is what T4 turned on); **the attribution is not.** Flagged there, not silently corrected — T4's finding was about the **distribution of a technique**, and it survives an anonymous author perfectly well.

**⭐⭐⭐ Four collisions on one name, and they are of three different kinds.** The corpus's taxonomy (Batch 49: six generators; [[prakrta-prakasha]] adds a seventh) now meets a case where several run at once:

| # | the question | kind |
|---|---|---|
| **14** | Durgasiṃha = **Durgācārya**, Yāska's commentator? | ⭐ a **short-form / near-form** collision *across vedāṅgas* — the [[haradatta]] pattern (grammar / another śāstra), a **sixth** instance |
| **15** | "Durgasiṃha" a **title**, the man being **Amarasiṃha** of the *Amarakośa*? | ⭐⭐ **the inverse of a name collision** — one man, two designations, one of which is not a name at all |
| — | the vṛtti-author and the sub-commentary's author, one man or two? | a **self-commentary** ambiguity, which the corpus has not met before |
| — | **brother of Vikramāditya** | ⭐ **royal attachment**, exactly [[sarvavarman]]'s shape |

⭐⭐ **The royal attachment is the most instructive, because the corpus has the control case.** [[sarvavarman]] is attached to the **Sātavāhana** court and rated `low` confidence because "the sole witness to his date is a text whose own existence that same episode explains." Durgasiṃha is attached to **Vikramāditya** — and [[prakrta-prakasha]] records that Cowell's Vararuci is one of the "**nine gems**" of *Vikramāditya's* court, while [[vararuci]] and Cowell both note the *Kathāsaritsāgara*'s Vararuci at the **Nanda** court. ⭐⭐⭐ **Three grammarians of three rival systems, each fastened to a founding king.** ⚠ **The corpus asserts nothing about any of them being true.** What it records is that **being the author of a rival grammar appears to attract a royal patron the way it attracts a divine one** ([[katantra]]'s Kārttikeya legend, [[haradatta]]'s Maheśvara revelation) — ⭐ **and that both are ways of answering the same question: by what authority does this book depart from Pāṇini?** That is [[prakrta-prakasha]]'s seventh generator — **the name as warrant** — showing up as biography rather than as attribution.

**⚠ The date split falls where Batch 47's rule predicts, and [[katantra]] already said so.** Abhyankar: ninth or tenth century. *Glimpses*: eighth. ⭐ **Batch 47's standing caution — a specialist reference work is strong on what its discipline knows internally and weak on chronology — is confirmed a fifth time**, and the corpus does not adjudicate. ⚠ Note that the two sources are not equally weighted on everything: on the *contents* of the Kātantra literature Abhyankar is a specialist with manuscripts in view (he cites the Poona and India Office copies by shelf), and *Glimpses* is a survey.

**⭐ What is not in doubt, and it is the reason the node matters.** Whoever he was and whenever he lived, **the Kātantra was received through him.** Abhyankar: the *Vṛtti* "was once **very popular** as is shown by a number of explanatory commentaries written upon it" — and names three, spanning two centuries: **Guṇakīrti** (14th c.), **Mokṣeśvara** (15th c.), and **Rāmakiśora Cakravartin**'s *Aṣṭamaṅgala*. ⚠ **A commentary with its own commentarial literature is not a gloss; it is the text as used.** ⭐ And when Europe finally printed the Kātantra, it printed **his**: **Julius Eggeling** "edited the Kātantra Vyākaraṇa **with the commentary of Durgasiṃha** and many appendices in **1876**." ⭐⭐ **This is [[katantra]]'s finding — that the Kātantra "won classrooms while Pāṇini won prestige" — with a name attached to the winning: what circulated in the classroom was Durgasiṃha's Kātantra**, and what reached modern scholarship was the same book.

**⭐ And the *Manoramā* collision, from the other end.** [[bhamaha]] establishes that ***Manoramā*** is a genre label naming at least four unrelated works. **Two of them are commentaries on Durgasiṃha's own *Kātantradhātuvṛtti***: Abhyankar ascribes one to **Rāmanātha** and, in a separate entry, one to an unnamed "grammarian of the Kātantra school who lived in the **fifteenth century**." ⚠ **He does not cross-reference them, and the corpus does not assume they are the same notice.** ⭐ So the collision runs in both directions here: **a many-times-used title, attached to a many-times-questioned name.**

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** ⚠ **Weak, and the confidence reflects it.** **Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand, is the source of *everything* substantive above — the date, all four identity questions, the manuscript discrepancy, the sub-commentators, the Eggeling notice. **wisdomlib's *Glimpses***, carried from [[katantra]], supplies only the rival eighth-century date. ⚠ **That is one specialist source and one survey, on a figure about whom the specialist himself raises three unresolved doubts.** ⭐ **CONTESTED**, confidence **low** — ⚠ **the corpus's third `low` node, after [[karma-vargana]] and [[sarvavarman]], and for a third distinct reason**: not thin sources (karma-vargaṇā), not one source that is a fairy tale (Sarvavarman), but **one good source that says four times over that it does not know.** ⭐ **Recording a specialist's own hedges faithfully is what produces this rating, and the rating is the finding.**
