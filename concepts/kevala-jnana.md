## Gloss / Divergence map

Kevala-jñāna is **omniscience** — the highest and fifth type of knowledge in Jain epistemology, arising when the four destructive (*ghātīya*) karmas are completely annihilated. It is complete, unmediated, unobstructed cognition of all substances in all their modes across all time (past, present, future) and all space simultaneously.

**Epistemological ladder** (five knowledge types, ascending):

| # | Sanskrit | Pali/common | mode | scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | mati-jñāna | sensory | indirect | objects via five senses + mind |
| 2 | śruta-jñāna | scriptural | indirect | objects via testimony/language |
| 3 | avadhi-jñāna | clairvoyance | direct | material objects at a distance |
| 4 | manaḥparyāya-jñāna | telepathy | direct | others' mental states |
| 5 | kevala-jñāna | omniscience | direct | all substances, all modes, all time |

(Source: search-result aggregation from wisdomlib, jainpedia, Wikipedia "Kevala jnana.")

**Condition for arising:** Destruction of all four ghātīya karmas — jñānāvaraṇīya (knowledge-obscuring), darśanāvaraṇīya (perception-obscuring), mohanīya (deluding), and antarāya (obstructive). (Source: search-result aggregation.)

**TS verse references** (reported in search results; not directly fetched this run): TS 1.29 (range of kevala-jñāna); TS 10.1 (attainment of omniscience and liberation).

**Relationship to naya and pramāṇa:** From naya.md: "pramāṇa is the complete valid knowledge of all aspects; naya is valid knowledge of one aspect." Kevala-jñāna is pramāṇa in its perfect form — the direct epistemic standard that syādvāda approximates for finite knowers. Samantabhadra's *Āptamīmāṃsā* v. 105: syādvāda illuminates substances *indirectly*, kevala-jñāna *directly* — both are "illuminators of reality."

**Contested sub-point — the kevalin's physical condition:**

| tradition | claim | what it commits to |
|---|---|---|
| Digambara | kevalin is motionless, experiences no hunger or thirst, remains in lotus posture, emits *divyadhvani* (sacred speech not shaped by lips) | the omniscient body is transformed; normal embodiment is incompatible with omniscience |
| Śvetāmbara | kevalin has normal human needs, travels, preaches, interacts with disciples actively | omniscience is a cognitive state, not a physical transformation |

(Source: Wikipedia "Kevala jnana.")

The kevalin remains embodied after achieving kevala-jñāna until the four remaining aghātīya karmas (vedanīya, āyuh, nāma, gotra) exhaust — at which point physical death is also liberation (*mokṣa*/*parinirvāṇa*).
