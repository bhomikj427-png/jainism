## Gloss / Divergence map

The *dravyārthika nayas* are the **substance-standpoint nayas** — the three of the seven nayas (TS 1.33) that attend to the permanent, general, substance-side of reality. Where *paryāyārthika nayas* attend to momentary specific modes, *dravyārthika nayas* attend to what persists across modes — the dravya, its guṇas, and its class-membership.

**Pūjyapāda's characterisation** (via Vijay K. Jain 2018, TS 1.33 commentary): Dravyārthika naya addresses "general attributes" (*utsarga* = general rule, conformity) — it is the naya of the universal and the persistent.

**The three dravyārthika nayas** (standard Jain classification; TS 1.33 with Deepa Baruah 2017 definitions):

| naya | Sanskrit | key move | classic example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Figurative / intentional | naigama | takes the whole substance-with-its-purpose as its object; sees as done what is only in progress | "I am cooking food" — though only preparing, the end-state is the object of intention |
| Generic / class | saṃgraha | treats all members of a class as unified under one essence ("all is being/*sat*") | pointing to a pot and saying "this is clay" — attending to the material, not the shape |
| Practical / particular | vyavahāra | differentiates within the class; distinguishes one member from another | "this is a pot, that is a cup" — practical distinctions within the class of clay-things |

**The structure of the three:** Each is progressively more discriminating:
- Saṃgraha = maximal abstraction (everything is one class)
- Vyavahāra = useful practical differentiation within that class
- Naigama = teleological — takes the goal/purpose as the object, regardless of completion

**Connection to sat (TS 5.30) and dhrauvya:** The dravyārthika nayas are the nayas that attend to the *dhrauvya* (permanence) aspect of existence — they see what persists through the origination and destruction of modes. They are the nayas suited to the guṇa-level of analysis (permanent attributes of substance).

**Classification note — ṛjusūtra:** Pūjyapāda's commentary as extracted from WisdomLib TS 1.33 listed ṛjusūtra in the dravyārthika group (giving 4 dravyārthika + 3 paryāyārthika). The standard classification in Jain scholarship (Deepa Baruah 2017, *A Study of the Philosophy of Jainism*; general Jain tradition) places ṛjusūtra in the paryāyārthika group (3 dravyārthika + 4 paryāyārthika). Since ṛjusūtra explicitly attends to the *present moment only* (discarding past and future), the paryāyārthika classification is philosophically consistent. The possible extraction error in the WisdomLib fetch is noted; the standard 3+4 classification is followed here.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Vijay K. Jain 2018 (TS 1.33 commentary):** Dravyārthika = "general attributes/utsarga"; paryāyārthika = "particular/viśeṣa/apavāda." Distinction confirmed. Naigama/saṃgraha/vyavahāra confirmed as first-group nayas. (ṛjusūtra attribution queried — see note above.)

**Signal 2 — Deepa Baruah 2017 (WisdomLib essay, fetched for naya.md):** Standard 3+4 classification: dravyārthika = naigama + saṃgraha + vyavahāra; paryāyārthika = ṛjusūtra + śabda + samabhirūḍha + evaṃbhūta. Definitions of each. Medium provenance.

**Independence:** Signals 1 and 2 are independent sources, both from fetched material. They agree on the core substance/mode distinction and on naigama/saṃgraha/vyavahāra as dravyārthika. Confidence medium.
