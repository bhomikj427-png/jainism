## Gloss / Divergence map

Pratītyasamutpāda is **dependent origination** — the Buddhist doctrine that all conditioned phenomena arise in dependence on conditions and cease when conditions cease. Nothing possesses independent, inherent existence. This is the structural basis for both the Buddhist account of suffering and the account of liberation.

**The Pali formula (*idappaccayatā*)** — the most compact canonical expression (Samyutta Nikāya, widely cited): *"This existing, that exists; this arising, that arises; this not existing, that does not exist; this ceasing, that ceases."*

**The twelve nidānas (links)** — the classical unfolding of dependent origination across three lifetimes:

| # | Pali/Sanskrit | English | lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | avijjā / avidyā | ignorance (of the four noble truths, of anattā) | past |
| 2 | saṅkhāra / saṃskāra | volitional/karmic formations | past |
| 3 | viññāṇa / vijñāna | consciousness | present |
| 4 | nāmarūpa | name-and-form (psycho-physical organism) | present |
| 5 | saḷāyatana / ṣaḍāyatana | six sense bases (5 senses + mind) | present |
| 6 | phassa / sparśa | contact (sense + object + consciousness) | present |
| 7 | vedanā | feeling-tone (pleasant/painful/neutral) | present |
| 8 | taṇhā / tṛṣṇā | craving | present |
| 9 | upādāna | grasping/clinging | present |
| 10 | bhava | becoming (process of existence) | present |
| 11 | jāti | birth | future |
| 12 | jarāmaraṇa | aging-and-death + grief/sorrow/dukkha | future |

**Root cause:** Ignorance (*avidyā*) — specifically, ignorance of the nature of existence (impermanence, suffering, no-self). Removing ignorance through insight reverses the chain: *avidyā* ceases → *saṃskāra* ceases → ... → *jarāmaraṇa* ceases = nirvāṇa.

## Contrast with Jain karma-theory

| dimension | Buddhist pratītyasamutpāda | Jain karma/āsrava/bandha |
|---|---|---|
| Root cause of suffering | ignorance (*avidyā*) — cognitive, not material | activity (*yoga*) + passion (*kaṣāya*) — behavioural/physical |
| Medium of bondage | process of dependent arising; no material substance | fine physical matter (karma particles) |
| Is there a substrate self? | no — anattā; the chain arises with no enduring substance | yes — jīva is an enduring individual substance |
| Liberation mechanism | insight reverses the chain (*avidyā* ceases) | physical removal of karma matter (nirjarā/tapas) |
| Karma in the chain | saṃskāra (link 2) = volitional formations arising from ignorance | karma = physical matter flowing into jīva via yoga |

The Jain and Buddhist accounts of bondage are structurally parallel in describing *why* beings suffer and *how* liberation is achieved — but they are deeply non-equivalent: one requires physical karma-removal from a real individual soul; the other requires cognitive cessation of ignorance in a process with no enduring self.
