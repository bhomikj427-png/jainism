## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Liber de Causis** ("Book of Causes") is the great **transmission text** by which Neoplatonic metaphysics entered Islamic and Latin philosophy. It is a **9th-century Arabic compilation** (Baghdad milieu), originally titled *Kitāb al-Īḍāḥ fī l-khayr al-maḥḍ*, "Book of the Pure Good," **drawn largely from [[proclus|Proclus]]'s *Elements of Theology*** (with Plotinian material). Its core doctrine — the **First Cause as pure being / pure good**, from which all things flow — is the Neoplatonic One recast as a cascade of causes. Gerard of Cremona rendered it into Latin (1167–87) as the *Liber de Causis*, and it became a **standard scholastic textbook**.

**The headline misattribution (a false equivalence corrected).** Medievally it was read as **Aristotle's** — a capstone of his metaphysics of the First Cause. **Thomas Aquinas (1272), using William of Moerbeke's 1268 Latin Proclus, proved it derives from Proclus, not Aristotle.** So the text sits at a genuine *often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent* joint: a Neoplatonic (Proclan) work long worn as an Aristotelian mask. Albertus Magnus separately ascribed it to "David the Jew."
