## Gloss

In Āyurveda, ***agni*** is **the transformative principle of the body** — "the entity that is responsible for all digestive and metabolic processes in the human beings." It is the engine of the whole physiological system: [[dhatu|dhātus]] are built by it, [[mala|malas]] are thrown off by it, and *ojas* is what survives it.

⚠️ **Tradition split (§8).** This is a *different node* from the Vedic deity [[agni|Agni]], the fire-god of Ṛgveda 1.1 who carries the offering to the gods. The word is one; the concepts are not, and the corpus refuses to let the deity's prestige silently reinforce the physiology or vice versa. The **continuity is real** — Āyurveda's agni is a fire that *cooks an offering into something usable*, and the tradition is aware of the resonance — but a body's digestive capacity is not a god, and the edge between them is typed accordingly.

### Three categories, thirteen agnis

The two sources consulted describe the same scheme at different resolutions — three *kinds*, thirteen *tokens*. This is a difference of granularity, not a disagreement:

| category | count | what it acts on |
|---|---|---|
| ***jāṭharāgni*** | **1** | food in the digestive tract, converting it to absorbable material; located "between the stomach and large intestine in the *pittadharā kalā*" |
| ***bhūtāgni*** | **5** | *pārthiva, āpya, taijasa, vāyavya, nābhasa* — one per [[pancha-mahabhuta|mahābhūta]], processing the elemental constituents of what has been absorbed |
| ***dhātv-agni*** | **7** | one resident in each tissue, transforming the incoming material into that tissue and passing the rest on |

*Jāṭharāgni* is the master: the tradition holds that the other twelve depend on it, which is why almost all Āyurvedic dietetics is addressed to the first of the thirteen.

### The four states of *jāṭharāgni* — the diagnostic payload

This is where agni stops being a definition and becomes a clinical instrument. Digestive fire is not simply strong or weak but has **four qualitatively different states, each keyed to a [[tridosha|doṣa]]**:

| state | character | doṣa |
|---|---|---|
| ***samāgni*** | balanced, regular — "represents the normal state of metabolic fire and maintains the health of an individual" | equilibrium |
| ***viṣamāgni*** | **erratic** — irregular and sporadic, digesting well one day and not the next | **vāta** |
| ***tīkṣṇāgni*** | **hyperactive** — digests too fast, with burning | **pitta** |
| ***mandāgni*** | **sluggish** — slow, weak digestion, with accumulation of ***āma*** (undigested residue) | **kapha** |

Two things make this more than a four-box table.

**First, it is a genuinely non-linear model.** A naïve "more fire is better" scheme has one axis with a good end. This one has **one good state and three distinct pathologies, two of which are excesses and one of which is instability** — irregularity is its own disorder, not a midpoint between too much and too little. That is a real structural insight about a regulated process, and it is why the scheme survives as the backbone of Āyurvedic diagnosis.

**Second, the states are constitutional, not just episodic.** "The *prakṛti* in every individual has a specific state of agni that gets fixed at the time of birth itself" — so agni-state is part of a person's baseline typology alongside their doṣa constitution, and treatment aims at *their* norm rather than a universal one.

### *Āma* and the causal claim

***Āma*** — the unprocessed residue left when *mandāgni* fails to cook what it is given — is the tradition's general mechanism of disease-formation. The seat of agni is the ***grahaṇī***, and "*grahaṇī-vyādhi* is disease due to abnormal agni." The Caraka Saṃhitā at **Cikitsā Sthāna 15/51** makes *mandāgni* "a predominant factor behind various ailments."

That is a strong and specific etiological claim: **a large class of disease is a failure of transformation, and the residue of that failure is itself the pathogen.** It is worth stating in exactly those terms because the popular rendering — "toxins" — imports a modern picture that the classical concept does not carry. *Āma* is defined by its **relation to a process** (it is what agni did not finish), not by its chemistry. Compare, and only compare, the general shape of accumulation-and-clearance models elsewhere; the corpus does not identify *āma* with any modern entity, and notes that the sources most eager to do so are the same ones that gloss *jāṭharāgni* as "amylolytic, proteolytic and lipolytic enzymes" (see the warning recorded in [[dhatu]]).

### What agni is the condition of

> "Sustenance of life, complexion, strength, health, nourishment, luster, **ojas**, teja and prāṇa depend on the status of agni" — Ca. Sa. Cikitsā Sthāna 15/3

and, for the tissue fires specifically:

> "The small portions of agni are present in each dhātu. The increase or decrease of these agni leads to **depletion or overproduction** of dhātu respectively" — A. Hṛ. Sūtra Sthāna 11/24

The second is the one that repays attention: **the relation is inverse**. Raise a tissue's fire and you get *less* of that tissue, because the fire consumes what it processes. Āyurveda is not treating agni as a good to be maximised; it is treating it as a **rate** in a system where either direction of deviation is a disorder — which is the same logic that governs [[tridosha|doṣa]] and [[mala]], and is the strongest single reason to read classical Āyurveda as an equilibrium theory rather than a vitalist one.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Caraka Saṃhitā Online, "Agni"** (fetched; verse-referenced scholarly digitisation): the thirteen agnis in their three groups with all five bhūtāgnis and all seven dhātv-agnis named; **Ca. Sa. Ci. 15/3**; **A. Hṛ. Sū. 11/24** with the inverse relation. ⚠️ Also the enzyme identification, **not adopted** — see [[dhatu]].
2. **Wikipedia, "Agni (Ayurveda)"** (fetched, independent): the definition quoted at the head of this node; the three-category classification with *jāṭharāgni* located "between the stomach and large intestine in the *Pittadhara kala*"; and **all four states** with their doṣa keying — *samyagni*/*samāgni*, *viṣamāgni* (vāta), *tīkṣṇāgni* (pitta), *mandāgni* (kapha). Explicitly checked and found to carry **no** *āma* material and **no** verse citations, which is why signal 3 is needed.
3. **Search aggregation across the Āyurvedic academic press** (*International Journal of Applied Ayurved Research*; *World Journal of Pharmaceutical Research*; *Ayurlog*, "Agnimāndya is a precursor in Grahaṇī disease"; *International Journal of AYUSH* 2025; wisdomlib's *jāṭharāgni* entry): the four states attributed to **Ācārya Caraka**; *mandāgni* as kapha-dominated and *āma*-producing; the **grahaṇī** as the seat of agni and *grahaṇī-vyādhi* as agni-disorder; the constitutional claim that agni-state is fixed at birth with *prakṛti*; and **Ca. Ci. 15/51** on *mandāgni* as a predominant factor behind many ailments.
4. **Lexicon/morphology + corpus cross-check** (§4 signals 3 and 4): *agni* is simply "fire," the same word as the Vedic deity's name — which is precisely why this node exists separately (§8 tradition-split). [[agni]] carries the Ṛgvedic material; [[dhatu]] and [[mala]] were written in this batch from partly the same page-set and index onto the thirteen-agni scheme without friction.

**CONVERGED.** Two independently-authored sources give the same classification (at different granularity — three categories, thirteen tokens, not a disagreement), and the four-state scheme with its doṣa keying appears in both signal 2 and signal 3 with identical content. Confidence **medium**: signal 1 was read through a summary rather than in an edition; the verse references **Ca. Ci. 15/3**, **Ca. Ci. 15/51** and **A. Hṛ. Sū. 11/24** are reported by the sources and were **not verified against a printed edition**; and the *āma* doctrine rests on signal 3, the weakest provenance here.
