## Gloss / Divergence map

In Jain physics, skandha is any **aggregate of two or more paramāṇus** — the compound form of pudgala (matter). Matter exists in exactly two forms: the partless paramāṇu and the aggregate skandha. All macroscopic material things (bodies, karmic matter, karma particles, sensory objects) are skandhas at some level of analysis.

**Aggregation mechanics (snigdha/rūkṣa binding rule):** Paramāṇus do not combine arbitrarily. Each paramāṇu has *intensity points* of the qualities snigdha (oily/viscous/adhesive) or rūkṣa (dry/repellent). Two paramāṇus bind into a skandha when their respective intensity-point values **differ by at least two degrees** — e.g., a paramāṇu of 2-degree snigdha can combine with one of 4-degree rūkṣa. Equal-degree paramāṇus of the same polarity do not bind. (Source: Wikipedia "Skandha (Jainism)" citing Jain commentarial tradition; specific TS verse number for this rule not confirmed in this run.)

**Two processes: formation and dissolution**
- *Saṃghāta* (fusion/combination): paramāṇus or smaller skandhas combine to form larger aggregates.
- *Bheda* (fission/division): aggregates split back into smaller skandhas or paramāṇus.

**Six types of skandha** (Niyamsāra classification, confirmed via Wikipedia):

| type | Sanskrit | example | perceptibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross-gross | sthūla-sthūla | solid (stone, wood) | tangible; cannot reunite once broken |
| Gross | sthūla | liquid (water, milk) | tangible; reunites naturally |
| Gross-fine | sthūla-sūkṣma | light, shadow | visible but not tangible |
| Fine-gross | sūkṣma-sthūla | smell, taste, sound objects | perceptible by non-visual senses |
| Fine | sūkṣma | karma particles | imperceptible to physical senses |
| Fine-fine | sūkṣma-sūkṣma | subtler than karma | imperceptible even to supra-normal senses |

Karma particles (the "fine" skandha) are pivotal in Jain soteriology: they bind to the jīva, conditioning its rebirth, and liberation (*mokṣa*) requires their total removal.

**Critical distinction — Buddhist skandha (khandha) ≠ Jain skandha:**  
In Buddhism, the five *khandhas* (form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness) are the aggregates constituting personal experience and include *mental and material* phenomena. In Jainism, skandha is strictly a *material* aggregate — mental and conscious phenomena belong to jīva alone. The same Sanskrit term covers structurally different concepts across the two traditions.
