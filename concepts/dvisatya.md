## Gloss

The **two-truths doctrine** (*satyadvaya* / *dvisatya*): every Buddhist teaching rests on two levels. MMK 24.8: "the Dharma taught by the buddhas is precisely based on the two truths: a truth of mundane conventions and a truth of the ultimate (*samupāśritya*)."

**Saṃvṛti-satya** (conventional truth): how things appear within ordinary conceptual and linguistic frameworks — causally effective, dependently arisen composites; tables, persons, and fire-from-smoke all function here. **Paramārtha-satya** (ultimate truth): the deepest nature of those same things — in Madhyamaka, this is śūnyatā (absence of svabhāva). The two truths describe the **same phenomena** at different levels, not two separate realms or two grades of entity.

**Epistemological and ontological simultaneously**: conventional nature grounds epistemic practice in conceptual/linguistic frameworks; ultimate nature grounds analytical practice in insight into emptiness. Both are needed: without conventional truth one cannot communicate the Dharma; without ultimate truth one cannot attain liberation (MMK 24.10).

**Candrakīrti** (~600–650 CE, *Madhyamakāvatāra*): conventional truths are dependently arisen and causally effective — "illusion-like" but not nothing. Ordinary beings take conventional reality as foundational; āryas (realised practitioners) perceive both levels simultaneously without conflating them.

**Intra-Buddhist variation**: Vaibhāṣika treats atomic dharmas as ultimately real; Sautrāntika similarly; Yogācāra treats the vijñapti-stream (paratantra) as ultimately real and the parikalpita as conventional fiction; Madhyamaka applies śūnyatā to ALL dharmas including consciousness — the two truths are differently cashed out in each school.

**Cross-tradition**: Advaita Vedānta has a structural parallel (vyāvahārika / pāramārthika / prātibhāsika — three levels, not two; and the ultimate IS Brahman, a positive absolute). Jain naya theory has levels of standpoint but no two-truths hierarchy — nayas are jointly valid partial perspectives, not different levels of the same reality.
