## Gloss / Divergence map

**Brahmā** (Sanskrit root *bṛh*, "to grow, expand, be great"): the Hindu creator deity — one member of the Trimurti (Brahmā creator / Viṣṇu preserver / Śiva dissolver). The earliest Upaniṣadic formulation: *Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* 1.1.1 — "Brahmā arose as first among the gods, the maker of the universe, the protector of the world" (*brahma devanam prathamah sambabhuva*).

**The critical terminological trap:** Brahmā (masculine; the personal creator god) and Brahman (neuter; the impersonal metaphysical absolute) share the Sanskrit root *bṛh* but are fundamentally distinct entities. This is one of the most frequent confusions in introductory treatments of Hindu thought.

**Brahmā's cosmological function:** He creates the universe and its beings, including the ten Prajāpatis (progenitors of humanity: Marīci, Atri, Aṅgirasa, Pulastya, Pulaha, Kratu, Vasiṣṭha, Dakṣa, Bhṛgu, Nārada) and the Saptarṣis (seven great sages). The Purāṇas describe two origin accounts:
1. *Hiraṇyagarbha* (golden womb): at the beginning, Brahmā emerged from a golden cosmic egg — the remaining material expanded to form the universe.
2. *Nābhija* (navel-born): Brahmā emerges from a lotus growing out of Viṣṇu's navel — making Viṣṇu cosmologically prior to the creator.

**Historical development:** In the Vedas, *Prajāpati* ("Lord of Creatures") held the primary creator role. In the Upaniṣadic period, Brahmā gradually supplanted Prajāpati, assuming most of his characteristics. The Purāṇas then elaborated Brahmā's mythology but simultaneously subordinated him further to Viṣṇu or Śiva depending on the sectarian perspective.

**Cosmological time:** One "day of Brahmā" (*kalpa*) equals approximately two billion human years, at the end of which Brahmā inhales and the universe dissolves. This cycle repeats for Brahmā's full lifespan (100 divine years = 311 trillion human years), after which a new Brahmā arises.

**Why Brahmā is rarely worshipped:** Despite being creator, Brahmā has only two primary temples in India (Puṣkar, Rājasthān; Kumbakonam, Tamil Nadu). Two Purāṇic myths explain this:
1. Sage Bhṛgu visited Brahmā and found him absorbed in Sarasvatī's music, ignoring the sage — Bhṛgu cursed that no human would ever worship Brahmā.
2. Brahmā falsely claimed to have found the top of Śiva's cosmic pillar of light (*jyotirliṅga*) — Śiva decreed that Brahmā would receive no proper worship.
