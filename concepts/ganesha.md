## Gloss / Divergence map

**Gaṇeśa** (Sanskrit *gaṇa* "troop, multitude" + *īśa* "lord" → "lord of the gaṇas," i.e. of Śiva's retinue of semi-divine attendants; also **Gaṇapati**, **Vināyaka**, Tamil **Piḷḷaiyār**) is the elephant-headed Hindu deity, the **remover of obstacles (*Vighneśvara*, *Vighnaharta*)** and lord of beginnings, and the patron of intellect, writing, learning, and commerce. He is the **Pratham-pūjya** — the deity invoked *first*, before any other god and at the outset of any undertaking (a journey, a text, a ritual, a business).

**Iconography:** elephant head with a single intact tusk, pot-belly, usually four arms holding axe (*paraśu*), noose (*pāśa*), rosary, and a bowl of sweets (*modaka*); his *vāhana* is a **mouse/rat** (mastery over what gnaws and obstructs).

**Origin myth (the standard cycle):** **Pārvatī** fashions him from clay/earth as her doorkeeper while she bathes; he bars **Śiva**'s entry, Śiva beheads him in the ensuing fight, then — to console Pārvatī — replaces the head with that of the first animal found, an **elephant**. He is thus son of Śiva and Pārvatī and brother of **Kārttikeya/Skanda**.

**Status spectrum.** In the **Gāṇapatya** sect Gaṇeśa is not a subordinate son-deity but the **Supreme Being / Brahman** itself (the *Gaṇapati Atharvaśīrṣa* identifies him with the absolute) — the same supreme-deity move made for Viṣṇu, Śiva, and the Goddess. In pan-Hindu practice he is the universally-invoked obstacle-remover regardless of one's chosen iṣṭa-devatā.

**Scribe of the Mahābhārata.** A widely-attested motif: **Vyāsa** dictates the *Mahābhārata* and **Gaṇeśa serves as the amanuensis** who writes it down (on condition that Vyāsa never pause, and that Gaṇeśa understand each verse before writing) — making the wisdom-god the literal recorder of the great epic.

**Historical note.** Terracotta images appear from ~1st c. CE, but Gaṇeśa's **clearly-recognisable classical form crystallises in the 4th–5th c. CE**, and an independent cult is well established by ~10th c. — i.e. he is a comparatively *late* major deity whose rise to near-universal "worshipped-first" status is post-Vedic and Purāṇic.
