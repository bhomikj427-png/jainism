## Gloss

*Avatāra* (Sanskrit: *ava-* "down" + *tṝ* "to cross"; literally "descent") is the Vaiṣṇava theological concept of Viṣṇu/Brahman descending into the phenomenal world in a specific embodied form to restore dharma when it is threatened. The locus classicus is Bhagavad Gītā 4.7–4.8: "Whenever sacred duty decays and chaos prevails, then I create myself." The avatāra is initiated from *above* by the divine will; it is theophany — God becoming human (or animal). This is structurally opposite to the Jain tīrthaṅkara, which is apotheosis — a human who achieves liberation by their own effort, not a god descending.

## The ten principal avatāras (dashavatāra)

Matsya (fish) → Kūrma (tortoise) → Varāha (boar) → Narasiṃha (man-lion) → Vāmana (dwarf) → Paraśurāma → Rāma → Kṛṣṇa → Buddha/Balarāma → Kalkī (future).

The Bhāgavata Purāṇa states that the number of avatāras is actually innumerable; the ten are the principal ones.

## Avatāra vs. tīrthaṅkara: the structural inversion

| Feature | Avatāra | Tīrthaṅkara |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Descent — divine to human | Ascent — human achieves liberation |
| **Initiative** | Divine will (God descends when needed) | Individual's own right faith/knowledge/conduct |
| **Nature** | God in embodied form | Human being in whom all karma is destroyed |
| **Purpose** | Restore dharma; protect devotees | Establish the teaching ford; liberate oneself |
| **After death** | Returns to transcendence | Becomes a siddha at universe-summit |
| **Interventional?** | Yes — avatāra acts in history | No — tīrthaṅkara post-liberation cannot intervene |
| **Many in one age?** | Multiple avatāras across cosmic time | 24 tīrthaṅkaras per half-cosmic-cycle |
| **Body real or illusory?** | Advaita: real at vyāvahārika; Viśiṣṭādvaita: genuinely real | Always fully embodied human body |

The most important contrast: an avatāra descends because the divine chooses to act in history to protect the world. A tīrthaṅkara is a human being who, by their own effort, destroys all karma and attains omniscience — they do not descend from outside; they ascend from within samsāra. After liberation, a siddha/tīrthaṅkara has no awareness of or agency in the world; they cannot "come back" or intervene.

## The Buddhist inclusion problem

Some Hindu Purāṇic lists include the Buddha as the 9th avatāra of Viṣṇu. This inclusion serves a Hindu theological purpose (assimilation of the Buddhist community); Buddhism does not reciprocate. The Jain tradition similarly does not accept the avatāra framework — tīrthaṅkaras are not avatāras. Listing them as such would misrepresent both traditions.

## Vedāntic sub-debates

- **Advaita:** The avatāra body is real at the conventional (*vyāvahārika*) level but ultimately māyā. Śaṅkara acknowledges genuine divine descent as meaningful within the two-truth framework.
- **Viśiṣṭādvaita (Rāmānuja):** The avatāra body is genuinely real — Viṣṇu truly becomes embodied because his "body" in the cosmic sense is constituted by all souls and matter. The avatāra is the highest form of the Lord's self-expression.
- **Dvaita (Madhva):** God and the avatāra are truly and essentially distinct from souls; the avatāra is a real, unique divine act.
