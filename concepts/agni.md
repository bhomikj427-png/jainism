## Gloss / Divergence map

**Agni** (Sanskrit *agni* from Proto-Indo-European *\*h₁n̥gʷnis*; cognates: Latin *ignis*, Lithuanian *ugnis*, Old Slavonic *ognĭ*): the Vedic fire deity and the divine mediator of *yajña* (sacrifice). The Ṛgveda opens with an invocation to Agni (RV 1.1): *Agnim īḷe purohitaṃ yajñasya devam ṛtvijam* — "I praise Agni, the household priest, the god of sacrifice, the ṛtvik [officiant]." Agni is addressed in over 200 of the Ṛgveda's 1,028 hymns — the highest frequency of any deity — establishing him as the operative centre of Vedic ritual.

**Three ritual functions:**

| function | Sanskrit designation | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Mediator / divine mouth | *Havyavāhana* | "he who carries oblations"; conveys offerings to the gods |
| Sacrificial priest (among gods) | *Hotṛ* | the invoking priest in the divine assembly; Agni performs the priestly role on behalf of humans |
| Guardian of cosmic order | *Ṛtasya gopā* | "guardian of ṛta"; fire as the principle that maintains cosmic and moral law |

**Three cosmic forms (triadic manifestation):** Agni simultaneously manifests as (1) terrestrial fire (*pārthiva*), (2) atmospheric lightning (*vaidyuta*), and (3) celestial sun (*saura*). This triadic structure places Agni at all three cosmic levels simultaneously, making him the connective tissue between earth, mid-space, and heaven — and thus the natural mediator for human-divine communication.

**The ekam-sat formula (RV 1.164.46):** *Indraṃ mitraṃ varuṇam agnim āhuḥ* — "They call it Indra, Mitra, Varuṇa, Agni... To what is One, sages give many a title." This verse, one of the Ṛgveda's most quoted, positions Agni not as one deity among many but as one name for the single underlying reality — the move later Upaniṣads develop into the Brahman doctrine.

**Connection to ṛta:** Agni is explicitly called *ṛtasya gopā* ("guardian of ṛta") in the Ṛgveda. He is the mechanism by which yajña maintains ṛta — the proper exchange between humans and cosmos. Fire transforms the material offering (ghee, grain, soma) into smoke that rises to the divine realm; the devas reciprocate with rain, fertility, and order.

**Upaniṣadic internalization:** The Bṛhadāraṇyaka and Chāndogya Upaniṣads identify Agni with the inner fire of consciousness (*prāṇa*, lifebreath) and ultimately with Brahman. This moves Agni from external ritual mediator to inner principle — the same transition that reinterprets *yajña* from external sacrifice to internal discipline.

**Structural note on the dharma-dravya analogy:** The Batch 26 suggested Agni and Jain *dharma-dravya* are structurally parallel as "enablers." The parallel is limited: Agni is a personal deity with intentional agency (he *carries* offerings, he *receives* praise) who actively mediates; dharma-dravya is an impersonal medium of motion with no will or consciousness, which enables motion without causing it (like water enabling fish to swim). Both are "enabling media" in their respective frameworks — but Agni enables through divine personhood, dharma-dravya enables through ontological medium-hood. The analogy holds only at the most abstract "enabler" level and is labelled accordingly.
