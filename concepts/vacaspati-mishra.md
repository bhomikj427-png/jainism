## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vācaspati Miśra** is the most unusual figure in the classical Indian corpus: **not a partisan of one school but a competent commentator on nearly all of them**, honoured with the epithet ***sarva-tantra-sva-tantra*** — "**for whom all systems are his own**." A Maithil Brahmin born at Andhra Tharhi (Madhubani, Bihar), he produced authoritative sub-commentaries in **five of the six darśanas plus grammar**, and in Advaita his *Bhāmatī* founded a school that carries its name.

### The one-man survey of classical philosophy

| darśana | work | what it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Advaita Vedānta** | ***Bhāmatī*** | gloss on [[shankara|Śaṅkara]]'s *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya*; **names the Bhāmatī-prasthāna**; generated six sub-commentaries of its own |
| Advaita (polemic) | *Brahmatattva-samīkṣā* | on [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]]'s *Brahmasiddhi* — reported **unpublished** |
| **Sāṃkhya** | ***Tattvakaumudī*** | on the [[samkhya-karika|Sāṃkhyakārikā]]; **over 90 medieval manuscripts survive** — a hard transmission datum, and the reason it is the standard Sāṃkhya entry-text |
| **Yoga** | ***Tattvavaiśāradī*** | on the *Yogasūtra* — in practice on [[vyasa-yogabhasya|Vyāsa's bhāṣya]], i.e. a *ṭīkā* on a *bhāṣya* |
| **Nyāya** | *Nyāyavārttika-tātparyaṭīkā*; *Nyāyasūcīnibandha* | sub-commentary in the Nyāya chain running down to the [[nyaya-sutra|Nyāyasūtra]] via [[vatsyayana|Vātsyāyana]]; the *Sūcīnibandha* is an index/supplement |
| **Mīmāṃsā** | *Nyāyakaṇikā*; ***Tattvabindu*** | *Nyāyakaṇikā* on Maṇḍana's *Vidhiviveka*; *Tattvabindu* ("Drop of Truth") an **independent** treatise on Bhāṭṭa [[mimamsa-sutra|Mīmāṃsā]] |
| Grammar / semantics | (within *Tattvabindu*) | a treatment of linguistic meaning surveying **five** competing theories |

That the same man could write the standard gloss on the *Sāṃkhyakārikā* — a **dualist, ātman-plural, prakṛti-real** system — and *also* the founding text of an Advaita sub-school is the datum. It cuts against reading the darśanas as sealed sects, and it is why **doxographic reliability and doctrinal allegiance must be assessed separately** in this literature.

### *Tattvabindu*: five theories of how a sentence means

His Mīmāṃsā treatise sets out the classical Indian semantics debate and takes a side:

| theory | claim | holder |
|---|---|---|
| *sphoṭavāda* | meaning grasped as an indivisible whole "burst" | [[mandana-mishra|Maṇḍana Miśra]] |
| Nyāya view | meaning from concatenating memory traces | [[nyaya-sutra|Naiyāyikas]] |
| Mīmāṃsā view | traces created by the words themselves | Mīmāṃsakas |
| *anvitābhidhāna-vāda* | words denote only as already-connected | [[prabhakara|Prābhākaras]] |
| *abhihitānvaya-vāda* | words denote separately, then connect | [[kumarila-bhatta|Bhāṭṭas]] |

**He endorses the Bhāṭṭa view**, using *lakṣaṇā* (implication) to carry the connection — an Advaitin siding with Kumārila's Mīmāṃsā on semantics while rejecting its metaphysics. See [[sabda-pramana]].

### The Bhāmatī doctrine — and the sharp contrast with Vivaraṇa

| | **Bhāmatī** (Vācaspati) | **Vivaraṇa** ([[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]]) |
|---|---|---|
| **locus (āśraya) of avidyā** | the **jīva**. "Avidyā cannot abide in Brahman. It abides in the jīvas" | "Avidyā rests on **Brahman** but acts on the jīvas" |
| **how many avidyās?** | **plural** — "plural since the jīvas are plural" | **one** — a single universal *mūlāvidyā* |
| Brahman's role | material cause of the world; **object** (*viṣaya*) of avidyā, not its locus; māyā only an **accessory** cause | avidyā itself is the material cause — a *jaḍātmikā śakti* |
| jīva–Brahman | ***avacchedavāda*** — avidyā **limits** Brahman and makes it appear as a jīva, "as a pot limits sky." Brahman bound by māyā is the jīva; Brahman beyond māyā is Īśvara | ***pratibimbavāda*** — jīvas are **reflections** of Brahman in the *antaḥkaraṇa* |
| what liberates | ***prasaṅkhyāna-vāda*** — **meditation** is the main factor; "the *mahāvākyas* do not produce *anubhava*… it is the mind seasoned by meditation that gives such experience"; karma contributes nothing and must be renounced first | **textual study** is the main factor; reflection and meditation only auxiliary aids; "karma is responsible for the rise of knowledge of the Self" |
| later line | Amalānanda (13th c., *Kalpataru*); Appayya Dīkṣita (16th c., *Parimalā*) | — |

The *avidyā*-count is the underrated half of the split. **Bhāmatī pluralises ignorance**, which keeps Brahman clean but concedes real multiplicity at the level that matters for bondage; **Vivaraṇa keeps one ignorance**, which preserves the monism but must then explain how pure consciousness hosts its own occlusion. Both also run the two-tier *mūlāvidyā* (causal) / *tulāvidyā* (effect, responsible for particular error) scheme — so the difference is not that one has root-ignorance and the other doesn't, but **where the root sits and how many roots there are.**

### Three disputes recorded, not smoothed

1. **His date.** The one usable anchor is a colophon date of **898** in the *Nyāyasūcīnibandha* — and the era is unstated. Read as **Vikrama** it yields **c. 840 CE**; read as **Śaka**, **c. 976 CE**. Wikipedia gives both and settles on "9th or 10th century," "at least one generation younger than Ādi Śaṅkara"; Hindupedia's post-Śaṅkara survey states flatly **A.D. 840**; other reference literature gives **900–980**. The corpus keeps the ambiguity, because **the era-reading, not the number, is what is unknown** — a textbook case of a hard datum that underdetermines the fact.
2. **A visible reference-work error.** Hindupedia's own article on him carries a note that "He lived in A. D. 788–820" — **Śaṅkara's traditional dates**, evidently copied from the adjacent entry. Recorded because it is a live demonstration of how derivative reference sources fail (§4 signal 1: copies are not confirmation), and because that same platform elsewhere dates him to 840.
3. **Who holds *ābhāsavāda*.** Wikipedia's *Pratibimbavāda* article assigns it to [[vidyaranya|Vidyāraṇya]]; Hindupedia's post-Śaṅkara survey assigns it to **[[sureshvara|Sureśvara]]'s Vārttika-prasthāna** ("jīvas are *ābhāsas* or appearances of Brahman in the individual minds"). These are compatible if Vidyāraṇya inherits the Vārttika line's position — which is likely but **not stated by either source**, so it is recorded as an open reconciliation. The same survey, notably, puts **Sureśvara with the Vivaraṇa side on the locus** ("the locus of avidyā is Brahman and not the jīvas"), independently confirming the alignment recorded in [[padmapada]] and [[sureshvara]] from IEP.

### The wife legend

Tradition explains the title *Bhāmatī* as his wife's name — the story being that he was so absorbed in the commentary that he only noticed her after it was finished, and named it for her. **The Wikipedia article as fetched does not carry this legend, and no fetched source states it**; it is recorded here as *reported tradition of unverified provenance*, flagged rather than asserted, and should be either sourced or struck on any upgrade pass.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Vāchaspati Misra"** (fetched): 9th/10th c. and the **840 vs 976 Vikrama/Śaka** crux; Andhra Tharhi birthplace and Maithil Brahmin family; ***sarva-tantra-sva-tantra***; the full works list across six domains including the **90+ manuscripts** of the *Tattvakaumudī*; the Bhāmatī school and its jīva-locus / Brahman-as-object doctrine; ***avacchedavāda*** in the "Brahman bound by māyā is the living being" formula; ***prasaṅkhyānavāda*** and meditation-primacy; the **five-theory** *Tattvabindu* semantics survey with the Bhāṭṭa endorsement via *lakṣaṇā*; modern commemoration.
2. **Hindupedia, "Vācaspati Miśra"** (fetched, separate platform): "lived in 9th century A.D."; the *Bhāmatī* as "his gloss on the bhāṣya of Śaṅkara on the Brahmasutras" with **six sub-commentaries**; a works list that **independently matches** signal 1 item-for-item (*Nyāyakaṇikā*, *Brahmatattvasamīkṣā* — **unpublished**, *Tattvabindu*, *Nyāyavārttikatātparyaṭīkā*, *Nyāyasūcīnibandha*, *Sāṅkhyatattvakaumudī*, *Tattvavaiśāradī*); *avacchedavāda*. **No** *sarvatantrasvatantra*, **no** wife legend, **no** era discussion — and the erroneous 788–820 note (see dispute 2).
3. **Hindupedia, "Post-Śaṅkara Advaita"** (fetched; a *different article* on the same platform, so treated as a weakly-independent third signal): the full three-prasthāna map — Vārttika / Bhāmatī / Vivaraṇa — with dates, the **plural-vs-single avidyā** contrast, the *mūlāvidyā*/*tulāvidyā* two-tier scheme, the pot-limits-sky simile for *avacchedavāda*, the *mahāvākya*-vs-meditation line, and the later commentators **Amalānanda** (*Kalpataru*), **Appayya Dīkṣita** (*Parimalā*), **Prakāśānanda** (*dṛṣṭisṛṣṭivāda*, *ekajīvavāda*), plus **Śrīharṣa**'s *Khaṇḍana-khaṇḍa-khādya* and **Madhusūdana Sarasvatī**'s *Advaitasiddhi*.
4. **Search aggregation** (wisdomlib's two studies — *Contribution of Vācaspati Miśra to the Sāṃkhya System* and a *Tattvabindu* study; hindu-blog on the Bhāmatī-prasthāna): the *avacchedavāda* formula in the same words, the plural-avidyā doctrine, *Tattvabindu* as an independent Bhāṭṭa work, and a **900–980** dating — a third value for the date.
5. **Corpus cross-check** ([[padmapada]], [[sureshvara]], [[mandana-mishra]], [[prakasatman]]): the Bhāmatī side of the locus split was recorded in Batches 40–41 from **IEP**, from the Vivaraṇa vantage only. Everything IEP said about Bhāmatī — jīva as locus *and* object, Brahman as *Īśvara* the controller, *tulāvidyā*'s veiling-and-projecting — is reproduced here from a different source-set, and the corpus now has the split **stated from both sides by both camps' partisan literature**.

**CONTESTED** on the date and on the *ābhāsavāda* attribution; **converged** on the works, the epithet, and the Bhāmatī doctrine, where four source-sets agree. Confidence **medium**, not high: none of his own texts was read in any edition; signals 2 and 3 share a platform; the works list, though matched item-for-item, comes from encyclopedia entries rather than a manuscript catalogue; and the wife legend is explicitly unsourced.
