## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Sāṃkhyakārikā** ("Verses on Sāṃkhya") of **Īśvarakṛṣṇa** (fl. ~350 CE, Gupta period) is the **oldest surviving systematic text of the Sāṃkhya school** — "the unquestioned classic of the tradition" and "the earliest extant complete text." Its *terminus ad quem* is fixed by **Paramārtha's Chinese translation, completed by 569 CE**. It is a short, dense work of **72 verses** in the Āryā metre (some manuscripts 73; verse 72 itself says the original had 70). It supplies the primary-text anchor behind the doctrine files [[prakriti-samkhya]], [[purusha-samkhya]], and [[guna-samkhya]]; later commentaries (Gauḍapāda, the *Yuktidīpikā*, Vācaspati Miśra) elaborate rather than revise it.

**The motivating problem (verse 1): the threefold suffering.** The inquiry begins from *duḥkha* of three kinds — *ādhyātmika* (self-caused, bodily/mental), *ādhibhautika* (caused by other beings/external things), and *ādhidaivika* (caused by nature/the supernatural). Ordinary remedies (physicians, ritual, scripture) give only temporary relief; the text offers discriminative knowledge as the route to *absolute* cessation.

**Epistemology — three pramāṇas.** Sāṃkhya admits exactly three means of valid knowledge, in a fixed hierarchy: **perception** (*dṛṣṭa*), **inference** (*anumāna*), and **reliable testimony** (*āptavacana*) — testimony used only where the first two fall silent. (Contrast Nyāya's four and Mīmāṃsā's five/six.)

**Metaphysics — dualism, satkāryavāda, 25 tattvas.** Reality reduces to two ultimate kinds: **puruṣa** (plural, inactive, pure consciousness) and **prakṛti** (one, active, unconscious primordial nature constituted by the three **guṇas** — *sattva*, *rajas*, *tamas*). From the disturbance of prakṛti's equilibrium evolve the **25 tattvas** (unmanifest prakṛti → *mahat*/buddhi → ahaṃkāra → manas, the ten senses, five subtle and five gross elements; plus puruṣa). The causal doctrine is **satkāryavāda**: "the effect already exists in its cause prior to its production" — nothing absolutely new arises, ruling out creation *ex nihilo*. **Classical Sāṃkhya is atheistic** (*nirīśvara*): no creator God is required; evolution is driven by prakṛti's own guṇa-dynamics, drawn out by the mere presence of puruṣa. Liberation is **kaivalya** ("isolation") — the puruṣa's recognition of its absolute difference from prakṛti, ending rebirth and suffering.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Sāṃkhyakārikā"): Īśvarakṛṣṇa; Gupta-period composition; Paramārtha 569 CE terminus; 72/73 verses, Āryā metre, verse 72's "70 originally" remark; verse-1 threefold duḥkha (adhyātmika/adhibhautika/adhidaivika); three pramāṇas (dṛṣṭa/anumāna/āptavacana); satkāryavāda; 25 tattvas with the creative/created classification; three guṇas; silence on Īśvara; commentaries (Gauḍapāda-bhāṣya, Yuktidīpikā, Tattvakaumudī). **Signal 2** (IEP, "Sāṃkhya"): independently confirms the Sāṃkhyakārikā (~350 CE) as "the earliest extant complete text," the three pramāṇas in hierarchical order, satkāryavāda ("the effect already exists in its cause"), explicit atheism, the puruṣa(plural, inactive)/prakṛti(one, active, three-guṇa) dualism, the 25 tattvas, and kaivalya as the goal.

The two are independent (an encyclopedia article and a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia) and agree on dating, structure, epistemology, causation, atheism, and soteriology. Medium, not high: the kārikā verses themselves were **not fetched verbatim** this session (critical editions exist — e.g. Welden/Gauḍapāda on archive.org; Larson's edition — but were not opened); the exact verse-count variant (72 vs 73) and the precise wording of verse 1 are taken from the encyclopedia, not the edition. Britannica was unreachable (HTTP 403) and is not counted as a signal.
