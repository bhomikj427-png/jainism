## Gloss / Divergence map

**Patañjali** is the **compiler of the [[yoga-darshana|Yoga Sūtra]]** and the figure to whom the classical **Yoga darśana** is anchored. He is dated broadly **between the 2nd c. BCE and the 5th c. CE**, most scholars narrowing to **~1st–4th c. CE** (Philipp Maas argues ~400 CE on commentarial grounds); "the only certainty is that it predates the 5th century CE." Almost nothing biographical is known about him.

**Systematizer, not inventor.** The Yoga Sūtra's opening — **YS 1.1 *atha yogānuśāsanam*** ("now, the *continuation* of the teaching of yoga") — signals that Patañjali was "continuing the teachings of Yoga" (the prefix *anu-* marks continuation of prior instruction), not founding it from nothing. His achievement was to **systematize pre-existing, heterogeneous yogic practice** into 196 sūtras so authoritatively that the text became "the canon for the mechanics of generic Yoga" — and "the most translated ancient Indian text in the medieval era." He is the *anchor by authorship* of the school treated in [[yoga-darshana]] and its defining sūtra in [[citta-vritti]].

**Sāṃkhya + Īśvara.** Patañjali, "a notable scholar of the Sāṃkhya school," builds Yoga on Sāṃkhya's metaphysics — plural inactive **puruṣa**, one active three-guṇa **prakṛti**, the goal of **kaivalya** — adding what classical Sāṃkhya lacks: **Īśvara** (a special never-bound puruṣa) as an optional-but-favoured object of devotion ("by the grace of God"), and the eightfold meditative praxis (aṣṭāṅga). Hence Yoga is "seśvara Sāṃkhya," and Patañjali stands to [[kapila]] as the founder who *re-admits a god* to the godless system.

**The grammarian conflation (now a typed edge, not just prose).** A persistent tradition identifies the Yoga-sūtra Patañjali with the grammarian **Patañjali** who wrote the *Mahābhāṣya* (the great commentary on Pāṇini). The identification is almost certainly false: Louis Renou showed "significant differences in language, grammar and vocabulary," and the equation is first made only in **Bhojadeva's *Rājamārtaṇḍa*** — a late conflation of two unrelated authors who share a name.

The motor is theological rather than clerical: he "is regarded as an **avatar of Ādi Śeṣa**," and an incarnate divinity mastering three sciences is a *consequence of the premise* rather than a confusion. See [[patanjali-grammarian]] for the full divergence map and for a dating far firmer than this figure's — mid-2nd c. BCE, triply attested.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Patanjali"** (fetched): dates 2nd c. BCE–5th c. CE (most 2nd–4th c. CE; Maas ~400 CE); compiled the Yoga Sūtra (196 sūtras), a foundation of classical Yoga; scholar of Sāṃkhya; grammarian-conflation rejected (Renou; first equated by Bhojadeva ~10th c.).
2. **IEP, "Yoga"** (fetched, independent): Patañjali ~1st–2nd c. CE, predates 5th c.; *not* yoga's inventor but its systematizer (YS 1.1 *atha yogānuśāsanam*, "continuing" prior teaching); Sāṃkhya supplies the metaphysics, Yoga the meditative method; Īśvara an optional favoured path.
3. **Corpus ([[yoga-darshana]], [[citta-vritti]], [[samkhya-karika]])**: the school's eight limbs, YS 1.2 *citta-vṛtti-nirodha*, and the "seśvara Sāṃkhya" relationship recorded earlier.

Two independent reference works agree on the compiler-role, the broad dating, the Sāṃkhya-plus-Īśvara structure, and the grammarian-conflation verdict. Confidence **medium**: the Yoga Sūtra was not fetched verbatim beyond YS 1.1/1.2; the dating is a wide range; "compiler vs author" is itself a scholarly reading.
