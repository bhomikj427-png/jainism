## Gloss / Divergence map

**Somānanda** is "**the founding author of the Śaiva philosophical tradition known as the Pratyabhijñā**," and his ***Śivadṛṣṭi*** ("The Vision of Śiva") is "**the first philosophical treatise on monistic Śaivism**" — seven chapters arguing that everything is Śiva, and arguing it *against named opponents* rather than asserting it.

He is also the figure who makes [[utpaladeva|Utpaladeva]]'s achievement legible, by contrast. The corpus records in `utpaladeva.md` the datum that decides how to read the pair:

> ***pratyabhijñā* — the term the school is named for — does not occur in the *Śivadṛṣṭi* at all.**

**Somānanda founded a tradition that was later named after a concept he did not use.** That is a specific and unusual relation between a founder and a school, and it is what makes the corpus keep both nodes: the visionary who states the position, and the student who supplies the argument and the name.

### The *Śivadṛṣṭi* is mostly polemic

The most useful thing to know about the text is what it spends its length on. It "examines Śiva's eternal nature and cosmic creation, presenting a theory of non-differentiation where **unity of subject and object, everything being of the nature of consciousness** is central" — and then **substantial portions critique competing schools**:

| target | what is attacked |
|---|---|
| **the grammarians** (Bhartṛhari) | *vivarta* theory and *śabda-brahman*; specifically, in **chapter two**, "Bhartṛhari's view that ***paśyantī* speech was identical to Brahman**" |
| **Śākta** approaches | |
| **Vijñānavādin** monism | Buddhist idealism ([[vijnaptimatrata]]) |
| **[[advaita-vedanta|Advaita Vedānta]]** | its **doctrine of ignorance** |
| others | |

The text then "concludes with Kashmiri Śaivism's historical development and his family lineage."

That shape is worth naming. **A founding text that is largely refutation tells you the position was formulated in a crowded field**, against live rivals, and not in isolation — which is what one would expect in 9th–10th-century Kashmir, where Buddhist *pramāṇa* philosophy ([[dharmakirti]], [[dharmottara]]) was institutionally strong. Compare [[utpaladeva]], whose engagement with Dharmakīrti is more sophisticated still: he **borrows** from the opponent where Somānanda mainly refutes.

### The doctrine — and the point where it separates from Advaita

Somānanda's Śiva "possesses absolute free will (***svātantrya***), manifesting creation through consciousness-bliss (*cit-ānanda*) via knowledge and action powers." And then the decisive clause:

> **"The universe represents Śiva's appearance through free will — not *māyā*'s illusion."** "Ignorance appears" through *svātantrya* as an **epistemological phenomenon rather than an ontological reality.**

⚠️ This is the sentence that keeps Kashmiri Śaivism from being read as Advaita with different names. Both are non-dualisms with an apparent world and an apophatic-sounding absolute. But:

| | Advaita | Somānanda's Śaivism |
|---|---|---|
| the world's status | *vivarta* — apparent transformation; the world's reality is exactly what is in question | Śiva's **real self-expression** through free will; the world is not less real for being Śiva's |
| what produces plurality | *avidyā*/*māyā* — and the post-Śaṅkara tradition then spends centuries deciding **where it resides** ([[prakasatman]], [[vacaspati-mishra]]) | ***svātantrya*** — Śiva's freedom, exercised. Ignorance is something Śiva **does**, not a substrate he suffers |
| the absolute's activity | *nirguṇa* Brahman is actionless | Śiva is *cit-ānanda* with knowledge- and action-powers |

The *Śivadṛṣṭi* attacks Advaita's ignorance-doctrine **by name**, which puts the two systems in a documented adversarial relation rather than a merely typological one. See [[pratyabhijna]], which already records the contrast, and [[maya-advaita]].

### The genealogy, read as a genealogy

Somānanda "claimed descent from the sage **Durvāsas**, who received Āgamic Śaivism's spiritual mission from Śiva himself." Durvāsas "created his son **Tryambaka** directly from his own mind," and the line continued **fifteen generations** until his ancestor Saṅgamāditya's father took a wife.

The corpus records this as **a claim about authority, not about ancestry** — and notes that it does the same double work Ch 29 §5.2 identifies in the Advaita hagiographies: it grounds the teaching in Śiva directly (so the doctrine is revealed, not invented) *while* running through a named human line (so the teacher has standing to transmit it). The mind-born generations are the mechanism by which those two requirements are made compatible. Compare [[govinda-bhagavatpada]] and [[atri]], both mind-born or lineage-constituted figures.

Note also that **[[abhinavagupta|Abhinavagupta]]'s teacher Lakṣmaṇagupta is described as being "in the lineage of Trayambaka"** — so this genealogy is still doing work a century later, and is not a private family claim but the school's charter.

### Two disputes

| dispute | reading A | reading B |
|---|---|---|
| **his dates** | **875–925 CE**, a contemporary of Bhaṭṭa Kallaṭa (Wikipedia) | **c. 900–950 CE** (the academic literature) |
| **his teacher** | **Vasugupta**, author of the *Śiva Sūtras* (Wikipedia) | not stated in the academic summaries consulted |

The dating split is **the same consistent ~25-year shift** already recorded in [[utpaladeva]] across all three generations — Wikipedia's scheme against the academic one. That it reappears here, on a different person, from different pages, confirms it is **two dating schemes and not a dispute about individuals**, which is exactly how [[utpaladeva]] records it. The corpus treats that as corroboration of the diagnosis rather than as new uncertainty.

3. **Corpus cross-check** ([[utpaladeva]], [[pratyabhijna]], [[abhinavagupta]]): [[utpaladeva]] was written in Batch 42 from Ratié's *Journal of Indian Philosophy* articles and independently records the c. 900–950 / c. 925–975 dating pair, the "deviates in style and substance" assessment, and the fact that **the term *pratyabhijñā* is absent from the *Śivadṛṣṭi*** — the single most important claim in this node, and one this node does **not** independently confirm.
4. **Provenance weighting** (§4 signal 5): **Nemec's OUP volume — a full critical study with translation of exactly this text — was identified and not read.** For a figure with one work and one modern monograph devoted to it, that is the entirety of the confidence cap; the material to make this node `high` exists and was located.
