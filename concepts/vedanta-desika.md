## Gloss / Divergence map

**Veṅkaṭanātha** (c. **1269 – c. 1370**), universally called **Vedānta Deśika**, "teacher of Vedānta," is the systematiser of Viśiṣṭādvaita after [[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]] and the man who made it a *school*. More than **a hundred works** are attributed to him, most of them genuinely his, in **three languages** — Sanskrit, Maṇipravāḷa (Sanskritised literary Tamil) and Tamil — and across philosophy, exegesis, doxography and devotional poetry. Elisa Freschi's assessment is the right one to record: "The number of works he produced … and their depth make each assessment of his contribution **preliminary**."

⚠ **Key and alias.** The filename is `vedanta-desika`; `term_iast` is the personal name **Veṅkaṭanātha**, under which the specialist literature and `nathamuni.md` already cite him. **One node, two names — do not mint a `venkatanatha` key.**

### Why the corpus needed him: he is a library of lost books

`nathamuni.md` (Batch 45) records that [[nathamuni|Nāthamuni]]'s ***Nyāyatattva*** is **lost** and is being reconstructed from quotation. Veṅkaṭanātha is the principal quarry: Freschi reports the *Nyāyatattva* is "**quoted largely in the *Nyāyasiddhāñjana***" (citing Vīrarāghavācārya 1976, p. 519).

And not only that book. Through his quotations, fragments survive of **at least five lost works**:

| lost work | author |
|---|---|
| ***Nyāyatattva*** | Nāthamuni |
| ***Tattvaratnākara*** | Parāśara Bhaṭṭa |
| ***Ṣaḍarthasaṅkṣepa*** | Rāmamiśra |
| works of the **Vṛttikāra** | identity debated |
| texts of **Yādavaprakāśa** | Rāmānuja's own former teacher |

**A single 14th-century polymath is the reason five earlier Śrīvaiṣṇava and Vedāntic works are not wholly lost.** This is the same shape `haricandra.md` and `indu.md` recorded for Āyurveda in Batch 45 — a tradition surviving in the quotations of the people who came after it — and it is now attested in a second literature.

### ⭐ The divergence I — the dates are a convention, and now the corpus has a scholar saying so

`nathamuni.md` closed Batch 45 with what it called its sharpest finding: **three consecutive Śrīvaiṣṇava ācāryas with three impossible lifespans (128 / 120 / 120 years)** — "a convention, not three observations." That was the corpus's own inference from arithmetic.

**It is now sourced.** Freschi, in a peer-reviewed encyclopedia, states the general principle and cites a scholar for it:

> "the **lifespans of the earliest teachers of Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta have been prolonged in order to connect them with each other**" — citing **Neevel 1977, pp. 14–16**

⚠ **And the pattern does not stop with the early ācāryas — it includes this node's own subject.** Freschi gives Rāmānuja as **1017–1137** (**120 years**) and Veṅkaṭanātha as c. 1269 – c. 1370 (**about 101**). She adds the general caution that the abundant biographical material "comes largely from **later hagiographies** and therefore may not yield reliable historical data," and that these dates "should be treated as **traditional rather than definitively established**."

**Consequence for this file, stated plainly: the dates in its own front matter are traditional, not established.** They are recorded because the whole literature uses them, and flagged because they belong to a lineage whose chronology is known to have been stretched to make the succession continuous.

### The divergence II — a warning about school-formation that the corpus should adopt

Freschi's most useful sentence is not about Veṅkaṭanātha's doctrines but about how schools come to look like schools:

> "what seems *a posteriori* to be a Vedāntic school would probably **not have appeared such to the school's contemporaries**"

Her point is that Veṅkaṭanātha's systematisation is what **made** Viśiṣṭādvaita retrospectively legible as a unified position, and that "Veṅkaṭanātha's contributions **reconfigured** his predecessor's contributions by giving them a new meaning" — including **fixing Rāmānuja as the definitive reference point**.

⚠ **This bears directly on `vishishtadvaita.md`, `ramanuja.md` and `yamunacarya.md`, and on the corpus generally.** It is the same hazard as [[navya-nyaya|`navya-nyaya.md`]]'s three rival starting points and [[abhava|`abhava.md`]]'s retrofitted seventh category: **the later systematiser's tidy account of the school is evidence about the systematiser, not straightforwardly about the school.** The corpus's *school* nodes are, to an unknown degree, reading their founders through the man who organised them.

### What he actually held

- **On Nyāya — subordination, stated as a rule.** From the *Nyāyapariśuddhi* ("Purification of Nyāya") 1.1.2: *"we will follow the Veda supported by the logical rules according to reality, and the **Nyāya when it agrees with the Veda**. By contrast, we will **not** follow the pure Nyāya."* ⚠ Compare `nathamuni.md`'s observation that the title *Nyāyatattva* "**appropriates the Naiyāyika logical apparatus and rejects only its conclusion**." **Two ācāryas, four centuries apart, take the same stance — and both announce it in the title.** *Nyāya-tattva*, "the truth of Nyāya"; *Nyāya-pariśuddhi*, "the purification of Nyāya." Neither is a refutation; both are annexations.
- **On Mīmāṃsā — an annexation of a different order.** The *Seśvaramīmāṃsā*, "Theistic Mīmāṃsā," is a **new commentary on the *Mīmāṃsāsūtra*** arguing that Pūrva and Uttara Mīmāṃsā are ***ekaśāstra***, **a single teaching**. Freschi: "only with Veṅkaṭanātha that this acceptance becomes an **inclusion of Mīmāṃsā on the same level as** Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta." A ritual-exegetical school with no God is absorbed into a theistic Vedānta as its first half.
- **On Advaita.** The ***Śatadūṣaṇī***, "the hundred refutations," against the impersonal *brahman*.
- **On intellectual intuition — an unusual restriction, and its motive is epistemological.** He "**rigidly negates** the possibility for human beings to attain intellectual intuition" (*yogipratyakṣa*), allowing it **only to God**. ⚠ The reason is not modesty about human powers: **if a yogi could see supersensible truth directly, the Veda would not be the unique access to it.** He restricts the human mind to protect the authority of a text. Compare Gaṅgeśa's *anuvyavasāya* exception in `gangesha.md` — both schools carve out exactly one incorrigible channel, and where they put it is a decision about where authority lives.
- **On ontology.** The *Nyāyasiddhāñjana* defends **six substances** — *prakṛti*, time, God, individual souls, God's knowledge, and God's manifestations — against Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika's nine.

### ⚠ He criticised Yāmuna, and the corpus should record it

Freschi (citing **Clooney 2000, pp. 108–109**): [[yamunacarya|Yāmuna]] "accepted an **anti-Vedāntic proof for the existence of God, namely the inference**," which Veṅkaṭanātha **criticised** in the *Nyāyasiddhāñjana*.

This is not a small internal quarrel. A *Vedāntin* who proves God by inference has conceded the Naiyāyika's method; Veṅkaṭanātha's whole programme is that scripture is the access and logic is subordinate. **So the lineage the tradition presents as a smooth succession — Nāthamuni → Yāmuna → Rāmānuja → Veṅkaṭanātha — contains a methodological reversal at its third joint**, and it is the later man who calls it out.

### Life and afterlife

Born at **Tūppul**, near Kāñcipuram, Tamil Nadu; settled at **Śrīraṅgam**; his maternal uncle **Ātreya Rāmānuja** was a formative influence. He is styled "lion among the poets and philosophers." He was the **first to compose a *stotra* to Hayagrīva**, Viṣṇu as the horse-headed god of knowledge, whom he made a symbol of the union of Vedic Mīmāṃsā with devotion.

⚠ **The Vaṭakalai school claimed him after the fact.** Freschi: "the Vaṭakalai current **retrospectively identified Veṅkaṭanātha as its founder** and adopted *en bloc* his theology and philosophy" — although he had himself "attempted synthesis" on the grace-versus-free-will tension that later divided Vaṭakalai from Teṉkalai. The concrete traces are precise and worth keeping: Vaṭakalai authors began opening their works with praise of **Hayagrīva rather than Gaṇeśa**, on his precedent, and in **1676** dedicated a temple to Hayagrīva. **A man who tried to hold a synthesis together was posthumously enlisted as the founder of one of the halves** — the same retrospective-founder pattern this file warns about above, now applied to him.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Elisa Freschi (Austrian Academy of Sciences), "Veṅkaṭanātha (Vedānta Deśika)," *Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy***, https://iep.utm.edu/venkatan/. A **peer-reviewed encyclopedia entry by a named specialist**, and the load-bearing source for everything here: the dates with their explicit unreliability warning and the Neevel citation, the works in three languages, the *Nyāyapariśuddhi* quotation, the *ekaśāstra* thesis, the *yogipratyakṣa* restriction, the six substances, the five lost works preserved in quotation with the Vīrarāghavācārya reference, the Yāmuna criticism with the Clooney citation, and the Vaṭakalai appropriation with the Hayagrīva evidence.

**Signal 2 — search-level aggregation.** Independently: 1268–1369; "more than one hundred works … covering several branches of learning like Nyāya, Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta and Sahitya"; the list *Nyāyapariśuddhi*, *Nyāyasiddhāñjana*, *Tattvamuktākalāpa* with *Sarvārthasiddhi*, *Adhikaraṇasārāvalī*, *Seśvaramīmāṃsā*, *Mīmāṃsāpādukā*, *Śatadūṣaṇī*; and the statement of his purpose — "to fortify the Vedanta system as explained by Sri Ramanuja against the attacks of the rival schools." ⚠ **It also carries the prolonged-lifespans sentence**, which means that sentence has reached this file twice; those are **not** independent, since the aggregation is drawing on the same IEP entry.

**Signal 3 — corpus-internal, and this is where the file earns its keep.** `nathamuni.md`, written in Batch 45 from an entirely different source (Prof. P. T. G. Y. Sampathkumaracharya in *Hitaya*), independently recorded (a) that the *Nyāyatattva* is lost and reconstructed from citations **including Veṅkaṭanātha's**, (b) that three consecutive ācāryas are given impossible lifespans, and (c) that the title *Nyāyatattva* annexes Nyāya rather than refuting it. **Freschi confirms all three from outside** — the last of them by supplying an exactly parallel title and rule from Veṅkaṭanātha's own pen. Also `ramanuja.md`, `yamunacarya.md`, `vishishtadvaita.md`, `gangesha.md`.

**Independence.** Signal 1 is a single strong source and this file leans on it heavily — a weakness stated, not hidden. Signal 2 is **not** genuinely independent of it. **The real independence is signal 3**: `nathamuni.md` was written before this node existed, from an Indian Sanskrit-university scholar with no connection to Freschi, and its three findings are the three Freschi confirms. That is the check that matters, and it is the reason this file's confidence is not lower.

⚠ **A second-hand chain flagged.** The load-bearing chronological claim — that the lifespans were prolonged — reaches this corpus as **Freschi citing Neevel 1977**. Neevel was **not read**. What the corpus has is a specialist's report of another specialist's argument, corroborated by the corpus's own arithmetic in `nathamuni.md`. **That is good enough to assert the pattern and not good enough to assert any particular corrected date**, and no corrected date is asserted here.

**Why contested.** The dates are explicitly traditional rather than established, and belong to a lineage whose chronology is known to have been stretched; the corpus's picture of Viśiṣṭādvaita as a school is, on this entry's own argument, substantially **his** retrospective construction; and he is claimed as founder by a party he did not found. **Why medium.** One authoritative specialist entry read in full, confirming three independent Batch 45 findings. **Not high**: **not one of his hundred-plus works was read**, in Sanskrit or translation — the *Nyāyapariśuddhi* line is quoted from Freschi's English; Neevel, Clooney and Vīrarāghavācārya are all reached at second hand; the works-list is taken on authority; and `term_devanagari` is **omitted** rather than guessed (§3).
