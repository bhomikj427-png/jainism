## Gloss / Divergence map

**Sarvajñātman** (also **Sarvajñātma Muni**) is the author of the ***Saṃkṣepaśārīraka*** — "a succinct exposition in verses of the views of Śrī Śaṅkara as stated in his bhāṣya on the Brahma-sūtra," **1,240 stanzas across four adhyāyas** (563 · 248 · 365 · 63, in varying metres). Two further works are attributed: the ***Pañcaprakriyā*** (five sections on word-meaning, the *mahāvākya*s, bondage and release) and the ***Pramāṇalakṣaṇa*** (Mīmāṃsā epistemology). He is the third holder of *pratibimbavāda* named in the table in [[prakasatman]] — and the corpus's reason for writing him is that **he is not simply a third vote for the same position.**

### The finding: two axes that the phrase "the Vivaraṇa view" collapses into one

The post-Śaṅkara literature is usually sorted on a single axis — *where does avidyā live?* — with Brahman on one side ([[padmapada]], [[prakasatman]]) and the jīva on the other ([[vacaspati-mishra]]). Sarvajñātman sits on the Brahman side of **that** question and takes the opposite side of a **second, independent** question that the one-axis picture hides:

| axis | Sarvajñātman | [[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]] | [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati]] |
|---|---|---|---|
| **locus (*āśraya*) of avidyā** | pure consciousness — ajñāna "rests on (*āśraya*) the pure self and operates on it as its object (*viṣaya*)" (Dasgupta); "the pure consciousness is the locus and content of avidyā" (Veezhinathan) | Brahman | the jīva (and **plural**, since the jīvas are plural) |
| **material cause (*upādāna*) of the world** | **Brahman alone.** Ajñāna is only the *instrumentality* — "pure Brahman is the ultimate cause of everything through the instrumentality (***dvāra***) of ajñāna," and he "strongly holds that **Brahman in association and jointly with ajñāna cannot be regarded as the material cause**" | *mūlāvidyā*, *bhāvarūpa* and beginningless, **is** the material cause of the world | māyā resting in the jīva as an **accessory** cause |
| **is avidyā positive (*bhāva-rūpa*)?** | **yes** — "positive in its nature (*bhāva-rūpam*)," yet non-independent | yes — the thesis he is credited with originating | yes, but plural and jīva-borne |

What this commits you to: on Sarvajñātman's account you can hold every Vivaraṇa-side doctrine — positive beginningless ajñāna, Brahman as its locus, reflection-theory for the jīva — **and still refuse to let ignorance be the stuff the world is made of.** *Dvāra* is doing precise work: a doorway is the *means* by which something passes, not a co-ingredient of what passes. The corpus records this because it is the cleanest available counterexample to a reading the corpus itself has drawn elsewhere — that Advaita's *mūlāvidyā* simply occupies [[prakriti-samkhya|prakṛti]]'s job-slot (see [[prakasatman]]). For Sarvajñātman it does not, and blocking exactly that was the point.

### A refinement the two sources do not state identically

They agree the locus is not the jīva. They differ on how finely to put what it *is*. Veezhinathan states it flatly — pure consciousness is locus and content. Dasgupta is narrower: the ajñāna associates with **"the pure light of Brahman, which shines as the basis and ground of individual souls (*pratyaktva*)"** — i.e. Brahman-as-inner-self, not Brahman *simpliciter* and not the individual souls. The corpus does not adjudicate; the divergence is a live reminder that "Brahman is the locus" is a slogan covering more than one technical claim. Dasgupta adds that avidyā persists in Brahman **through deep sleep**, and is known to have done so from the memory-experience on waking — an argument from a gap in experience, not from a text.

### The date, and why the best edition does not settle it

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **c. A.D. 900**, a younger contemporary of [[vacaspati-mishra|Vācaspati Miśra]] | **Dasgupta**, reasoning explicitly from the lineage: Śaṅkara dies c. 820, Sureśvara's pontificate is long, "the supposition that Sarvajñātman lived in a.d. 900 may not be very far wrong" | a **computed**, not attested, date — its premises are Śaṅkara's dates and a discipleship |
| **10th century** | Wikipedia (*History of Advaita Vedanta*, listing him with Prakāśātman and Vimuktātman); wisdomlib | the same century, arrived at without the arithmetic being shown |
| **a younger contemporary of Śaṅkara himself**, "ordained by the Bhagavatpāda himself and nominated to succeed him and Sureśvara" at the **Śrī Kāmakoṭi-pīṭha at Kāñcī** | **N. Veezhinathan** — in his chapter in *Preceptors of Advaita* and again in the blurb of **his own critical edition**; and the Kāñchī tradition, which makes this consistent by dating Śaṅkara to **500 BCE** | roughly **fourteen centuries** of swing, and a Sarvajñātman who is a maṭha-pontiff rather than a commentator placed by his commentaries |

A **fourth witness, found while researching [[amalananda]]**, weakens the discipleship the first two readings rest on: *The Advaita Vedānta Home Page* records that Sarvajñātman **salutes his guru as Deveśvara** in his own works, and that "some confusion exists regarding his direct lineage to Sureśvara." That is the strongest kind of evidence available here — what the author says about himself in his own text — and it does not name Sureśvara. It does not refute the discipleship (Deveśvara may be an ordination name, and the corpus does not assume otherwise), but it does mean **Dasgupta's c. 900 is computed from a premise the text itself does not supply.**

This is a **§4 signal-5 case worth naming as such.** The highest-provenance item in the dossier — Veezhinathan's *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka*, University of Madras 1985, Madras University Philosophical Series No. 18, a **critical edition from collated manuscripts** with introduction, notes and four indexes — is edited by the scholar who also carries the Kāñchī succession claim, and the claim appears in the edition's own front matter. **Provenance quality on the text does not transfer to provenance quality on the biography.** The corpus takes the edition as the best available witness to *what the Saṃkṣepaśārīraka says* and does **not** take its blurb as settling *when its author lived*. The pattern is the one already recorded for [[prakasatman]] (a lineage flattened into a guru-śiṣya chain) and for [[govinda-bhagavatpada]] (a joint attested only by the lineage that needs it).

### A tension this node opens in the corpus

[[prakasatman]] records Prakāśātman as **the first** to propound avidyā as of "positive beginningless nature," and records his date as disputed between c. 10th c. and c. 1200–1300. But Dasgupta has Sarvajñātman holding ajñāna **bhāva-rūpa** at c. 900. On Wikipedia's own chronology (Sarvajñātman 10th c., Prakāśātman c. 1200–1300) the priority claim inverts. Either the "first" belongs to Sarvajñātman, or it means *first to systematise* rather than first to assert, or it is evidence for the earlier dating of Prakāśātman. **No source fetched addresses the question**, and the corpus does not settle it — it is logged as an open follow-up rather than resolved by picking whichever date makes the received story work.

### Other theses

- ***Parasparādhyāsa*** (double imposition): false appearances are imposed on objects while false objects are imposed on appearances — leaving reality itself untouched.
- **Ten essential attributes** of Brahman (eternal, pure, conscious, released, true, subtle, existent, all-pervasive, non-dual, blissful), with the Upaniṣads' **negative** descriptions taken to confirm the positive ones rather than cancel them.
- **Only śruti yields Brahman-knowledge**; inference cannot establish it, and asceticism/renunciation is necessary for it. (This makes his *Pramāṇalakṣaṇa* on Mīmāṃsā epistemology a natural companion, not a detour.)
- **The first explicit theory of the *mahāvākya*** (Uskokov): *tat* and *tvam* each carry a directly-denoted (*vācya*) sense — a "spotted" entity, a pure core surrounded by *upādhi*s, the *tat* side including avidyā and so making Brahman the creator — and an indicated (*lakṣya*) sense, the pure core, reachable only by secondary signification. "Without giving up their individual meaning, the two words mingle to obtain a special meaning."

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Dasgupta, *A History of Indian Philosophy* vol. II** (1932), §"Sarvajñātma Muni (A.D. 900)" — the anchor, and the only source that **argues** for its date rather than stating one. Supplies the *dvāra* formula, the explicit denial that Brahman-jointly-with-ajñāna is the material cause, *āśraya*/*viṣaya* both in pure consciousness, *bhāva-rūpam*, *pratyaktva*, *parasparādhyāsa*, the chapter verse-counts, and the deep-sleep argument. Vol. IV independently repeats the causal thesis ("Brahman is the material cause through the instrumentality of māyā") in a *Vallabha* context, i.e. reported by an opponent's doxography rather than by an Advaitin — weak but genuine second sighting.
2. **N. Veezhinathan, "Sarvajñātman," in T. M. P. Mahadevan (ed.), *Preceptors of Advaita*** (1968) — the fullest biography and work-list; states the locus-and-content thesis, the ten attributes, *pratibimbavāda*, the ascetic requirement, and the Kāmakoṭi-pīṭha succession. **Interested on the biography** (see the date table) and used accordingly.
3. **N. Veezhinathan (ed. & tr.), *The Saṃkṣepaśārīraka*, University of Madras, 1985** — bibliographic signal only; a **critical edition with collated manuscripts**, Sanskrit with English translation, 750 pp. **Located, not read** — this is the named upgrade path for every doctrinal claim above.
4. **Aleksandar Uskokov, "Sarvajñātman and the Preliminaries"** (Motilal Banarsidass, 2018) — a fourth and genuinely independent literature (contemporary academic monograph, semantics rather than doxography): the *mahāvākya* theory, *mukhya*/*gauṇa*/*lakṣaṇā* and the *vācya*/*lakṣya* split.
5. **The Advaita Vedānta Home Page, "Post-Śaṅkaran Authors"** (1999) — the Deveśvara salutation and the flagged lineage confusion; independent of and prior to the Wikipedia-era material.
6. **Wikipedia, *Pratibimbavāda*** and ***History of Advaita Vedanta*** — the reflection formula (consciousness reflected in nescience = Īśvara, in the inner sense = jīva) and the bare 10th-c. placement alongside Prakāśātman and Vimuktātman.
7. **Corpus cross-check** — [[prakasatman]], [[padmapada]], [[vacaspati-mishra]], [[sureshvara]], [[maya-advaita]]: the *pratibimba*/*avaccheda*/*ābhāsa* table already recorded there names Sarvajñātman, and the *āśraya* dispute was recorded in Batches 41–42 from IEP. This node is the first to test that framing against a thinker who **splits** the two axes.

**CONTESTED** — decisively on the date and lineage (three readings spanning ~1,400 years), and finely on how the locus is stated. **Converged** on the doctrine: every source gives the same reflection formula and the same locus-side, and the *dvāra* thesis is unopposed in what was fetched. Confidence **medium**, not high: four independent literatures agree, but the *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka* itself was **not read** — Veezhinathan's edition was located only — and signals 2 and 3 are the same scholar, so the biography rests on 1 + 5 + 6 against 2/3, not on four independent votes — and signal 5 puts the discipleship itself, not merely the date, in question.
