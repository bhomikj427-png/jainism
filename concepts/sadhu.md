## Gloss

*Sādhu* ("good one," "ascetic," "monk"; feminine *sādhvī*, nun) is the **fifth** and broadest of the five supreme beings (*pañca-parameṣṭhī*): *Ṇamo Loe Savva-sāhūṇaṃ*, "I bow to *all* the sādhus in the world." The sādhu is the **baseline of the parameṣṭhī ladder** — any monk or nun who has fully renounced household life and taken the five great vows (*mahāvratas*). The salutation's word *savva* ("all") and *loe* ("in the world") is deliberate: it bows to *every* genuine ascetic anywhere, of any order or sect, named or unnamed. The ācārya (order-head) and upādhyāya (teacher) are themselves sādhus who have taken on additional roles; strip those roles away and what remains — the vow-keeping renunciate — is the sādhu. Like the other three living parameṣṭhī, the sādhu is **non-liberated**, still working toward mokṣa.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Namokar Mantra":** Fifth salutation, *Ṇamo Loe Savva-sāhūṇaṃ* — "I bow to all the sages/ascetics"; sādhus = "monks practicing right conduct."

**Signal 2 — Jainsite "Pancha Parmeshthi" (Jain institutional):** Sādhu = renunciate observing the five mahāvratas; assigned **27 qualities** (5 mahāvratas + control of the 5 senses + freedom from the 4 kaṣāyas + 3 guptis + others); explicitly "non-liberated practitioners observing right conduct."

**Signal 3 — Digambara monastic reckoning (Mūlācāra tradition, via JainGPT/search):** The **28 mūla-guṇas** (root-qualities) of a Digambara ascetic: 5 mahāvratas, 5 samitis (carefulnesses), control of the 5 senses, 6 āvaśyakas (obligatory duties), and 7 further rules (plucking the hair, nudity, no bathing, sleeping on the ground, eating once a day standing, etc.).

**Independence:** Three source types agree on the defining feature (mahāvratas) and non-liberated status; the exact quality-count (27 vs. 28) is a sect/reckoning difference, not a disagreement on substance. Confidence medium: the mūla-guṇa lists come from secondary aggregation, not a fetched critical edition.

## Position in the pañca-parameṣṭhī

| being | status | what marks it |
|---|---|---|
| arihant / siddha | **liberated** | beyond all worldly agency — cannot be petitioned |
| ācārya | non-liberated monk | leads + disciplines the order (36 qualities) |
| upādhyāya | non-liberated monk | teaches the āgamas (25 qualities) |
| **sādhu / sādhvī** | **non-liberated monk/nun** | **keeps the five mahāvratas (27 or 28 qualities)** |

The sādhu is the *category that contains the other two living ranks* — every ācārya and upādhyāya is first a sādhu. This is why the mantra reaches it last and widest: having saluted the two specialised roles, line 5 sweeps up **all the rest** of the renunciate community.

## The five mahāvratas as the threshold

What turns a layperson into a sādhu is the upgrade from the partial lay vows (*aṇuvrata*, see anuvrata.md) to the **total** great vows (*mahāvrata*): absolute ahiṃsā, satya, asteya, brahmacarya, aparigraha (Ch 08). This is the same vow-set the ācārya and upādhyāya keep; the sādhu keeps it *without* the added institutional duties. The vows are not symbolic — in Jain physics they are the operative mechanism of saṃvara (stopping karmic influx) and nirjarā (shedding karma), the engine of progress up the gunasthāna ladder (Ch 03, Ch 08).

## Not a god

As with all five, veneration is aspirational. Bowing to "all sādhus" is bowing to the *lived state of full renunciation* as such — the quality one aims to embody — not a petition to any holy man. (See namokara.md.)
