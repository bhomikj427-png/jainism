## Gloss / Divergence map

**Jinendrabuddhi** (c. **710–770 CE**) is the **Buddhist** author of the ***Nyāsa***, properly the ***Kāśikāvivaraṇapañjikā*** — the great commentary on the [[kasika|Kāśikāvṛtti]], and therefore a commentary on a commentary on the [[astadhyayi|Aṣṭādhyāyī]]. Abhyankar: "written in the **eighth century** by the Buddhist grammarian Jinendrabuddhi, who belonged to the **eastern school of Pāṇini's Grammar**."

He is **also** the name on the ***Viśālāmalavatī***, the ṭīkā on [[pramana-samuccaya|Dignāga's *Pramāṇasamuccaya*]] — and ⭐ **this corpus has carried both men, in separate files, without noticing they share a name.**

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **two different men** | **Kobayashi (1977)** | a coincidence of an uncommon name in one century |
| **one man** | **Hayes (1983)** | ⭐ a single figure standing in **both** the Pāṇinian and the Buddhist-epistemological lineages |
| **one man** — "summary and further support of identity"; and notes that "Jinendrabuddhi in turn **seems to use the *Tattvasaṅgraha***" | **Funayama (1999)**, p. 92 | the same, plus a *terminus post quem* after [[santaraksita|Śāntarakṣita]] |
| **one man**, on "**identical author-name in the colophons of the two works**" | **Steinkellner (2005)**, pp. xl–xlii | ⚠ see below — the corpus flags this argument's form |

**The state of the question**, per the EAST database: "there are several **remarkable similarities in style and tone**" between the grammarian's and the Buddhist commentator's works, and "there are **no pieces of evidence that weigh strongly against** the hypothesis." **Three to one, and the majority is later.**

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐⭐ T2 confirmed — and the finding is about this corpus, not about the sources.** The batch queued T2 as a genuine fork: either outcome would be a result. It came back **affirmative in the scholarly mainstream** — and the sharp part is what that means here. The corpus has held, since well before this batch:

- in [[kasika]] and [[panini]] — **Jinendrabuddhi, "a Buddhist grammarian of the eighth century,"** author of the *Nyāsa*, and the man who read Pāṇini's own biography out of A 4.3.94;
- in [[pramana-samuccaya]] — that Dignāga's Sanskrit "is **largely lost**; it is reconstructed from two Tibetan translations and from **Jinendrabuddhi's commentary (Ṭīkā)**, an ongoing scholarly project."

⭐⭐ **Both statements were written from sources that each knew only one half, and the corpus reproduced the split without seeing it.** On the majority reading these are one man, and the consequence is worth stating plainly: **the principal Sanskrit witness to the foundational text of Buddhist logic is a commentary by the man who wrote the standard commentary on the standard commentary on Pāṇini.** ⭐ Note that this is not a doctrinal bridge between grammar and [[pramana-nyaya|pramāṇa]] — nobody is claiming the *Nyāsa* argues epistemology — it is a **bibliographical** one, and it is stronger for that: it is a fact about who was in the room, not an analogy.

⚠ **And it is the fourth transmission finding in four nodes.** [[sarvavarman]]: the sole witness to a date explains its own existence. [[candra-vyakarana]]: two chapters lost, and the loss read as a design. [[candragomin]]: a man split in the specialist literature and merged everywhere else. Here: a text surviving only inside its opponent-tradition's commentator. ⭐ **This batch opened as a batch about rival grammars and has become a batch about how texts reach us.**

**⚠⚠ The corpus's own rule points the other way, and honesty requires saying so.** Steinkellner's ground, as the database summarises it, is "**identical author-name in the colophons of the two works**." ⭐ **That is, in form, precisely the inference this corpus has spent two batches distrusting** — [[vararuci]]'s four men, [[patanjali]]'s three, [[candragomin]]'s three identifications, all generated by "same name, therefore same man." **Applied naively, the corpus's standing suspicion would land it on Kobayashi's side.**

⚠ **But the corpus is reading a one-line database précis of an argument that runs three pages (pp. xl–xlii), and must not reduce a specialist's case to its summary.** ⭐ **The honest resolution is that the identity does not rest on the name at all**: the load is carried by **Hayes's and Funayama's stylistic and doctrinal evidence** — "remarkable similarities in style and tone," and Jinendrabuddhi's apparent use of the *Tattvasaṅgraha*, which is a **content** argument that also constrains his date. **The name is the occasion for the question; the style and the citations are the evidence.** ⭐ That distinction is exactly the one [[vararuci]] needed and did not get: there, no one ever produced *content* evidence for the identification, and Abhyankar's refutation was that Patañjali "does not associate Vararuci with Kātyāyana at all." **The corpus's rule is not "distrust identifications"; it is "distrust identifications that rest on the name alone."** ⭐ **Recorded as a sharpening of the rule, produced by a case that looked like a counterexample.**

**⭐⭐ Collision #11, and a fifth generator: *Nyāsa* is a genre, not a title.** Abhyankar states it outright and the corpus would otherwise have walked into it — **at least five different works across five different grammatical systems are called *Nyāsa***:

| whose | on what | system |
|---|---|---|
| **Jinendrabuddhi** | the Kāśikāvṛtti | Pāṇinian |
| **Devanandin** | the Jainendra grammar | Jainendra |
| **Prabhācandra** | the *Amoghavṛtti* on Śākaṭāyana | Śākaṭāyana |
| a *Vṛtti* (and Hemahaṃsagaṇi's on the Paribhāṣāvṛtti) | [[hemacandra|Hemacandra's]] *Śabdānuśāsana* | Haima |
| **Ugrabhūti** (11th c.), called *Śiṣyahita* | the [[katantra|Kātantra]] | Kātantra |

⭐ The corpus's running taxonomy of collision generators was: a **personal name** (Vararuci, Patañjali, Kātyāyana), a **place-name** (Kāśikā), a **doctrine-name** (Sphoṭavāda), a **short form** (Candra). ⭐ **This is a fifth and the most systematic of them — a *genre* label.** Every system's principal sub-commentary is *the Nyāsa* of that system, the way every system has *its* Paribhāṣāpāṭha. ⚠ **This is why the batch gives the work its own tradition-suffixed node, [[nyasa-vyakarana]]**, rather than letting an unqualified `nyasa` stand — and the suffix is doing real work, since **`nyāsa` is *also* a live technical term elsewhere in this corpus**, at [[nikshepa]].

**⭐ The *Nyāsa* as a reading apparatus, and the mechanism operating twice inside it.** Abhyankar records a grammarian "believed to have written a gloss on the Sūtras of Pāṇini **on the strength of a reference to him in the Nyāsa of Jinendrabuddhi**" (citing *Nyāsa* on 1.1.1) — ⭐ **a lost author whose entire existence in the record is one citation in this commentary.** And [[panini]] already carries the other instance: Jinendrabuddhi deriving *Pāṇini's own name* from Śalātura out of A 4.3.94. ⭐⭐ **The same commentator both preserves an author who would otherwise be nothing and manufactures a biography for the one everybody has.** ⚠ Batch 47's corollary, restated because this is its cleanest illustration yet: **the mechanism is not corrupting, it is invisible** — the same apparatus produces the priceless and the spurious, and only checking tells you which you are holding.

**⭐ The commentarial stack over the Nyāsa, and where Kaiyaṭa's ladder would put it.** The *Nyāsa* itself carries commentaries by **Maitreya Rakṣita** (12th c.), **Śivabhaṭṭa**, **Mahāmiśra**, and ⭐ **Mallinātha, "the standard commentator of prominent Sanskrit classics"**; an *Anunyāsa* is quoted in Sīradeva's *Paribhāṣāvṛtti*. So the chain runs: **Aṣṭādhyāyī → Kāśikāvṛtti → Nyāsa → Anunyāsa / Mallinātha.** ⭐⭐ **That is a fourth and fifth tier on a branch [[kaiyata]]'s maxim does not even address** — *yathottaraṃ munitrayasya prāmāṇyam* ranks the *three sages* on the Mahābhāṣya branch and stops at Patañjali. **The Kāśikā branch has as many tiers and no ranking rule at all**, and its base is the text Abhyankar calls a help to beginners. ⚠ **No source consulted comments on this asymmetry**; it is the corpus's observation from two first-hand entries, and is recorded as such.

**⚠ And Haradatta "has drawn considerably from Nyāsa" in the *Padamañjarī*** — which makes [[haradatta]], the next node in this batch, a reader of this one. ⚠⚠ **CORRECTED one node later.** This file first reported, from Abhyankar, that the *Nyāsa* "unfortunately is available only in a fragmentary state at present." **That is wrong** — the scan breaks mid-sentence across two blocks and the clause belongs to **Maitreya Rakṣita's twelfth-century *Tantrapradīpa***, the commentary **on** the Nyāsa, "very largely quoted by subsequent grammarians, but which unfortunately is available only in a fragmentary state." The *Nyāsa* itself is **fully published** and is the base of an active critical-edition project. ⭐ **An OCR gap silently reattached a relative clause to the wrong noun** — the [[nagesha-bhatta|Hindupedia appositive]] defect of Batch 48 by a different road, caught here by an independent source rather than by re-reading. See [[nyasa-vyakarana]].

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** **Abhyankar** at first hand — the eighth-century date, the Buddhist affiliation, the "eastern school of Pāṇini's Grammar," the *Kāśikāvivaraṇapañjikā* title, ⭐ the five-system *Nyāsa* genre note, the four sub-commentators, the fragmentary state (⚠ **mis-attributed on first reading — corrected above**), the Haradatta debt, and the lost author known only from *Nyāsa* on 1.1.1 — and he says **nothing whatever about Dignāga**, which is itself informative about how separately the two literatures are kept. **EAST (Epistemology and Argumentation in South Asia and Tibet), IKGA / Austrian Academy of Sciences** — a specialist scholarly database, fetched: the 710–770 dating, and the identity controversy with all four positions attributed. ⚠ **These two do not overlap at any point**, which is exactly why the collision survived: **the grammar reference work and the Buddhist-epistemology database each know one man.** In-corpus, `pramana-samuccaya.md` supplies the third leg independently of both. **CONTESTED**, confidence **medium** — the scholarly majority is clear and recent, but ⚠ **none of Kobayashi, Hayes, Funayama or Steinkellner has been read**, all four reach this node through one database entry and one search summary, and **neither the *Nyāsa* nor the *Viśālāmalavatī* has been opened.**
