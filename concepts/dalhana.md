## Gloss / Divergence map

**Ḍalhaṇa** (**Ḍalhaṇācārya**, fl. **12th century**) wrote the ***Nibandhasaṅgraha***, the commentary on the [[sushruta|Suśruta Saṃhitā]] that stands to the surgical corpus as [[cakrapanidatta|Cakrapāṇidatta]]'s *Āyurvedadīpikā* stands to Caraka. Of the several commentaries the *Suśruta Saṃhitā* attracted, "**only Ḍalhaṇa's *Nibandhasaṅgraha* is completely available today**" — the others survive partially or not at all. He belonged to a family of *vaidya*s and was patronised by a king given as **Sahajapāla Deva**.

### The third dating method in three nodes

The title says what the work is: a *nibandha-saṅgraha* is a **collection of compilations**, and the commentary "compiles the views of a large number of authors and commentators… who lived before Ḍalhaṇa." That habit is also what dates him, and by a method neither of the batch's other datable figures required:

| figure | how the date is fixed | what it depends on |
|---|---|---|
| [[cakrapanidatta|Cakrapāṇidatta]] | **regnal anchor** — his family served the Gauḍa king Nayapāla, r. 1040–1070 | an external institution's records (a dynasty) |
| [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]] | **lineage arithmetic** — Śaṅkara d. c. 820, plus a long pontificate, plus a discipleship | the tradition's own account of itself |
| **Ḍalhaṇa** | **citation-bracketing** — he **quotes Cakrapāṇi** (fl. 1060) and is **quoted by [[hemadri|Hemādri]]**, "placing him between the late 11th and the early 13th century" | nothing but the texts, and it yields a bracket rather than a point |

⚠ **The upper wall of that bracket was drawn too tight, and Batch 44 loosened it.** The "early 13th century" figure came from Wikipedia's "Hemādri (fl. 1260)" — but **1260 is the first year of Hemādri's recorded career, not the date of his medical work.** Gode: "Evidently the *Āyurvedarasāyana* must have been composed **between 1271–1309 A. D.**" A *terminus ante quem* is only as late as the citing work, so Ḍalhaṇa's upper bound moves **later** — to **c. 1309**, and no earlier than 1271, *if* the quotation sits in that work. ⚠ **No source consulted names which of Hemādri's works quotes him.** See [[hemadri]]. The lesson generalises: a date-bracket is only as good as the dating of the men who form its walls — and half of this one turned out to be a fact about how carelessly Hemādri had been dated, not about Ḍalhaṇa.

The third method is the weakest in precision — a 150-year window — and the strongest in *independence*: it needs no patron, no maṭha, no hagiography, only that two other datable authors exist and that quotation runs one way in time. It is also the only one of the three that a purely textual tradition can generate about itself. The corpus records the trio because the three nodes together make visible something none of them states: **how well a pre-modern Indian figure can be dated is mostly a fact about what kind of institution they stood next to, not about how much was written about them.**

### What he actually did to the text

Three concrete modifications, each an extension of Suśruta rather than a gloss on him:

- ***Mala* is a *śalya*.** Suśruta defines *śalyatantra* as the branch that removes foreign objects — grass, wood, stone, metal, bone, hair, nails, pus. **Ḍalhaṇa adds *mala***, bodily waste, to that list as something requiring *nirharaṇa*, extraction. This is a genuine category-extension: it makes the surgical branch's object not "things that came in from outside" but "things that must not be where they are," and it puts what [[mala]] treats as *physiology* into the surgeon's domain.
- ***Rakta* as a fourth doṣa, and the reason why.** Suśruta counts blood as a fourth *doṣa* alongside vāta, pitta and kapha. Ḍalhaṇa supplies the justification, and it is **discipline-relative**: *śalyatantra* is the branch concerned with *vraṇa* (wounds), and in the pathogenesis of a wound *rakta* is the main constituent — so the fourth doṣa is a fourth doṣa *for surgery*. That is worth registering carefully, because it means the doṣa-count in classical Āyurveda is **not a single fact about the body but a function of what branch is asking.** A tradition that lets its basic ontology vary by sub-discipline is doing something more interesting than being inconsistent, and the corpus does not flatten it.
- **Two kinds of *saṃśodhana*.** To Suśruta's four means of controlling disease (*saṃśodhana*, *saṃśamana*, *āhāra*, *vihāra*) he adds a division of purificatory treatment into ***bāhyāśraya*** and ***abhyantarāśraya*** — externally and internally seated.

### The answer to the fasting objection

Ḍalhaṇa's most consequential contribution for this corpus lies outside the *Nibandhasaṅgraha*'s surgical core, and was found while writing [[cakrapanidatta]]. Against the *kṣīra-dadhi* mechanism of tissue-nourishment — each dhātu wholly converting into the next, as milk into curd — stands an objection: if conversion were total, a month's fast would leave the body in *śukrakṣaya* with **no intermediate dhātus at all**, every stage having emptied into the next. Ḍalhaṇa's reply divides each dhātu's *pariṇāma* three ways:

| portion | what it becomes |
|---|---|
| ***sūkṣmabhāga*** | the *upadhātu* and the **next** dhātu |
| ***malabhāga*** | that tissue's waste — the *dhātu-mala* of [[mala]] |
| ***sthūlabhāga*** | the **same** dhātu: its own maintenance |

So transformation is **partial** (*krama-pariṇāma*), and the objection dissolves. This is the mechanism [[dhatu]] needs and did not have: it is precisely what lets a "complete transformation" model coexist with a body that still has rasa in it after a month without food. It also shows the commentarial layer doing real work — the objection is not a straw man, and the answer is not a re-assertion of the doctrine but a refinement of it.

### A source used for its philology and refused for its correlations

The paper that supplies the three modifications above states its own method plainly: to read Suśruta and Ḍalhaṇa "**in parallel to western surgical knowledge**," and it does so throughout — *mala*-as-*śalya* is glossed by faecal impaction in obstructive appendicitis, and *rakta*-as-doṣa by haemoglobin, blood sugar and serum bilirubin in wound healing. **The corpus takes the philology and leaves the correlations.** Ḍalhaṇa's reason for admitting *rakta* is that *vraṇa* pathogenesis is blood-centred; that is a statement inside the Āyurvedic system and needs no modern warrant, and supplying one neither strengthens nor weakens it. This is §4's "take the term, leave the editorialising" applied to a source that is otherwise the best available on what Ḍalhaṇa changed — the same handling already recorded for the Caraka-Saṃhitā-Online gloss in [[dhatu]] and Ch 31 §7.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Kanchan M. Borkar & Anantkumar V. Shekokar, "Notable Modifications of Sushrut Samhita by Dalhana," *International Journal of Ayurvedic Medicine* 4(3), 2013, 152–156** — **read in full**: the three modifications (*mala* as *śalya*; *rakta* as fourth doṣa with the *vraṇa* justification; the *saṃśodhana* division), and the statement that Ḍalhaṇa's is the only completely surviving Suśruta commentary. Its western-surgical correlations are recorded as the source's method and not adopted.
2. **Wikipedia, "Dalhana"** — the citation-bracket dating (quotes Cakrapāṇi fl. 1060, quoted by Hemādri fl. 1260) and the *Nibandhasaṅgraha*'s compilatory character. Thin but exact on the one thing that matters most here, and its dating **argument** is reproducible rather than merely asserted — the corpus can check it against its own [[cakrapanidatta]] node.
3. **Sharma et al., *IJRAR* 2024** (read for [[cakrapanidatta]]) — the *sūkṣmabhāga*/*malabhāga*/*sthūlabhāga* division attributed to Ḍalhaṇa as the answer to the fasting objection; cited there to Suśruta Sū. 14:10.
4. **Bibliographic signals** — the *Suśruta Saṃhitā with the Nibandhasaṅgraha of Śrī Ḍalhaṇācārya* in Vaidya Jādavjī Trikamjī Ācārya's standard edition (Chaukhambha), with an Internet Archive scan located; and P. V. Sharma's three-volume *Suśruta-Saṃhitā with Ḍalhaṇa's Commentary along with Critical Notes*. **Neither read.**
5. **Corpus cross-check** — [[sushruta]], [[cakrapanidatta]], [[mala]], [[dhatu]], [[charaka]]: the existing Suśruta node already records the Sū. 15 doṣa/dhātu/mala framing on which this node's *rakta* finding bears directly.

**CONVERGED.** The date-bracket, the commentary's name and character, and its unique completeness are agreed by every source; the three modifications come from a single paper but are of a kind (specific, locatable, citing chapter and verse) that a second source would confirm rather than contest. Confidence **medium** and not high: the *Nibandhasaṅgraha* itself was **not read** in any edition — the largest gap, since this is a node about a commentator's textual decisions; the biography (family, the patron Sahajapāla Deva) rests on search-level report not traced to a primary reference work; and no source consulted names **which** earlier commentators the *Nibandhasaṅgraha* preserves, which is the single most valuable thing about a compilation of lost commentaries and is recorded here as an unfilled hole rather than guessed at.
