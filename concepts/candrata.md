## Gloss / Divergence map

**Candraṭa** is the son of **Tisaṭa**, a sun-worshipper like his father, and the author of a commentary on his father's *Cikitsākalikā* together with the ***Yogaratnasamuccaya*** — a collection of formulae "well known in former ages." Meulenbeld's section "The author" is one sentence long: **"The only fact known is that Candraṭa was the son of Tisaṭa."**

He is the first node in this corpus written primarily from **Meulenbeld's *A History of Indian Medical Literature*** — the standard reference for this literature, which Batch 44 named as its top follow-up after two failed download attempts. It is now local, and it changes the answer.

### ⚠ The claim that queued him did not survive

Candraṭa was queued as "**Vāgbhaṭa's grandson**, the other witness to Jejjaṭa." Meulenbeld disposes of the first half:

> "Another Vāgbhaṭa sometimes confused with the author of the *Saṃgraha*, *Hṛdaya*, or both, need not seriously be considered in the present context. This Vāgbhaṭa appears as the father of Tisaṭa, the author of the *Cikitsākalikā*, in the **untrustworthy colophons** of a number of MSS of that treatise."

So the grandson-of-Vāgbhaṭa lineage rests on **manuscript colophons the standard reference rejects**, and on a Vāgbhaṭa who is in any case *not* the author of the *Aṣṭāṅgasaṅgraha* or *Aṣṭāṅgahṛdaya* — a fourth Vāgbhaṭa, on top of the "at least two men" `vagbhata.md` already records.

**And there is internal evidence against it.** Tisaṭa's own *Cikitsākalikā* opens by praising Sūrya, the Aśvins, Dhanvantari, Suśruta and *his father*, and names Hārīta, Suśruta, Parāśara, Bhoja, Bhela, Bhṛgu, Agniveśa and Caraka as sources. Meulenbeld's comment: **"The name of Vāgbhaṭa is conspicuously absent."** A man does not omit his own father from his list of authorities.

This is the second time in two batches that a concept's queuing rationale has been falsified by the concept itself — compare `nrisimhasrama.md`, Batch 44. **Recorded rather than quietly dropped**, because the failure rate of queue-premises is itself information about where the queue's claims come from.

### What is actually known

**Works.** (1) the commentary on Tisaṭa's *Cikitsākalikā*; (2) the *Yogaratnasamuccaya*, which Meulenbeld identifies with the *Candraṭasaṃgraha* quoted by Niścala and possibly with the separately-listed *Candraṭasāroddhāra*; (3) *Yogamuṣṭi* and (4) *Dravyāvalī*, recorded by P. V. Sharma; (5) a *Vaidyatriniṣattīkā*, "only mentioned by Th. Aufrecht and in the NCC"; and (6) — inferred from Śivadāsasena's quotation of a *Vṛndaṭīkā* by Candraṭa — probably a commentary on Vṛnda's *Siddhayoga*.

**Reach.** The formulary was drawn on by Soḍhala's *Gaḍanigraha*, by **Cakrapāṇidatta**'s *Cikitsāsaṃgraha*, by Dattarāma's *Bṛhadrasarājasundara*, the *Bhāratabhaiṣajyaratnākara* and the *Rasayogasāgara*; it is repeatedly quoted by Niścala, and Meulenbeld judges that **[[hemadri|Hemādri]]'s citations from a "Yogaratna" are in all probability from Candraṭa's work.**

**A stray detail worth keeping.** Anantakumāra quotes a recipe derived from Candraṭa that was **written on a stone pillar (*śilāstambha*) by the Yavanas** — a formulary tradition recording, at third hand, a prescription said to come off an inscription attributed to foreigners.

### The date, and a method worth copying

Meulenbeld's reasoning is the most instructive thing here, because of what he **refuses** to use:

> "The terminus post quem of Tisaṭa and Candraṭa has been deduced from a remark at the beginning of the latter's commentary on the *Cikitsākalikā*, proving that he was acquainted with **Haricandra's and Jejjaṭa's** commentaries. **As [[jejjata|Jejjaṭa]]'s date is disputed, it is preferable to depart from another fact** in establishing the upper chronological limit."

He instead anchors on **Candraṭa's quotation of the *Siddhasāra* and its author Ravigupta, c. A.D. 650**, and closes the bracket with Niścalakara's statement that Cakrapāṇidatta (c. 1050) used Tisaṭa's work — giving **650–1050**, narrowed to **900–1050** if the *Siddhayoga* commentary is his.

⚠ **This independently vindicates Batch 44.** `jejjata.md` was marked **contested** because three dates for Jejjaṭa turned out to be three dates for Vāgbhaṭa. Meulenbeld — writing in 1999–2002, and the standard reference — reaches the same verdict ("Jejjaṭa's date is disputed") and draws the practical consequence this corpus should adopt as a rule: **a disputed date cannot serve as an anchor for another date.** A bracket is only as firm as its weakest end.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — G. Jan Meulenbeld, *A History of Indian Medical Literature*, vol. IIA, "Candraṭa" and "Tisaṭa," read directly.** The standard reference for this literature: a critical, exhaustively footnoted survey of manuscripts, editions and secondary literature. It supplies everything above — the works and their identifications, the citation-reach, the colophon verdict, the *Cikitsākalikā*'s authority-list, the dating argument and its explicit refusal of the Jejjaṭa route. **Signal 2 — Meulenbeld vol. IA**, separately, on the Vāgbhaṭa confusions — a different volume and a different discussion, reaching the untrustworthy-colophons verdict from the Vāgbhaṭa side rather than the Tisaṭa side. **Signal 3 — the authorities Meulenbeld reports and weighs**, which are the real evidence and are not his: P. V. Sharma, Th. Aufrecht and the *New Catalogus Catalogorum*, D. Ch. Bhattacharyya, and the medieval witnesses themselves (Niścala/Niścalakara, Anantakumāra, Śivadāsasena, Śrīdāsapaṇḍita, Śrīkaṇṭhadatta, Ṭoḍara). **Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `jejjata.md`, `vagbhata.md`, `hemadri.md`, `cakrapanidatta.md` and `dalhana.md`, all written in Batch 44 from web-level sources, now testable against the reference.

⚠ **Independence is thin here, and that is stated rather than disguised.** Signals 1 and 2 are one author in one work. What makes the node usable is not source-plurality but **source-kind**: Meulenbeld reports and evaluates the primary witnesses individually, with page-and-verse citations, so his conclusions can be traced to the evidence he names rather than taken on authority. That is the difference between a reference work and a summary — but it is **not** the same as independent confirmation, and no claim here has been confirmed against a second scholar.

**Why converged.** Nothing substantive is in dispute: the filiation to Tisaṭa, the works, the reach and the 650–1050 bracket are all stated by the reference on named evidence, and the one claim that *was* in dispute — the Vāgbhaṭa descent — is resolved against it. **Why medium, not high.** Single-scholar dependence, as above; **no work of Candraṭa's was read**; the *Yogaratnasamuccaya* / *Candraṭasaṃgraha* / *Candraṭasāroddhāra* identifications are Meulenbeld's judgements, reported as such; and the 900–1050 narrowing rests on an inference from one quotation in Śivadāsasena.
