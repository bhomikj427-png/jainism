## Gloss / Divergence map

**Madhusūdana Sarasvatī** wrote the ***Advaitasiddhi***, the line-by-line answer to [[vyasatirtha|Vyāsatīrtha]]'s *Nyāyāmṛta* and the definitive Advaita reply in the three-century Dvaita–Advaita exchange. He was trained in **Navya-Nyāya at Nabadwīp** — under Harirāma Tarkavāgīśa and Mathurānātha Tarkavāgīśa — before taking sannyāsa from Viśveśvara Sarasvatī and moving to Vārāṇasī. He is, on any account, one of the two or three most formidable dialecticians Advaita produced.

He also wrote the ***Bhaktirasāyana***, "the elixir of devotion" — **"the only independent treatise on bhakti written by a major Advaita preceptor"** (Nelson) — and opened a work with a verse asking **"How can I know of any reality apart from Kṛṣṇa?"**

The node is **contested twice**: on **when he lived**, where five positions are on record, and on **whether his central philosophical move works**, where the specialist who has studied it most closely says the question has barely been asked.

### Divergence 1 — five datings

| reading | who holds it | note |
|---|---|---|
| **mid-14th century** | Burnouf and Lassen | reported by Rajagopalan as superseded |
| **late 15th / early 16th century** | K. T. Telang | |
| **early 16th century** | Winternitz; **V. Rajagopalan** ("beginning of the 16th century") | the position with an argument attached |
| **c. 1490–1580** | Wikipedia, from "the third son, Kamalanayana, born in about AD 1490" | a birth-name and a birth-year |
| **c. 1540–1647** | a secondary summary | **a century later than Burnouf, and a 107-year lifespan** |

**Only one of these comes with reasoning, and it is worth recording because the method is sound.** Rajagopalan fixes him by four synchronisms: he **quotes Vidyāraṇya**; he **met Vallabhācārya**, whose son was born in 1516; **Gadādhara the logician** was his contemporary; and **Śeṣagovinda — his student's student — studied with [[appayya-dikshita|Appayya Dīkṣita]]**.

⚠ That last one closes on a node the corpus already holds. `appayya-dikshita.md` anchors Appayya at **c. 1520–1593** on a **dated 1580 inscription** — external, epigraphic evidence, the strongest kind in this corpus. Working back two teaching generations from a man active in the mid-16th century puts Madhusūdana in the **first half of the 16th century**, which supports Telang/Winternitz/Rajagopalan and **tells against the c. 1540–1647 reading**, whose subject would be Appayya's junior rather than his senior by two generations. **This file therefore leans to the early-to-mid 16th century** and says why.

### Divergence 2 — bhakti after liberation

The philosophical problem is stated cleanly by Lance Nelson, and it is not a soft one:

- **Devotion requires** duality between lover and beloved, personhood, and dependence on a divine other.
- **Advaita teaches** that ultimate reality is impersonal Brahman, that the self is identical with it, and that "personhood and relationship are false constructs."

Devotion "seems to require" relationship; Advaita "seems to entail the loss of all the elements required for devotion." An Advaitin may of course allow bhakti as a *preparation* — a practice at the *vyāvahārika* level that purifies the mind for knowledge. **That is not what Madhusūdana claims.** His unconventional teaching is that **devotion is possible *even after* the realisation of identity with Brahman**, duality being "assumed for the sake of devotion" post-liberation.

That is a strong and strange position, and its strangeness is precise: it makes duality something the liberated may **elect**, not merely something the unliberated cannot escape. And it modifies the corpus's own account — `moksha-advaita.md` records Advaita liberation as the epistemic recognition of identity, which on Śaṅkara's account leaves nothing for a devotee to do.

⚠ **Whether the move succeeds is not settled, and this file will not settle it.** Nelson's own assessment is that Madhusūdana's thought "has actually received little careful study" and that existing scholarship is "**largely uncritical**" — he writes explicitly against accepting the claim that these teachings are "the grand climax" of bhakti's evolution. **A position widely admired and rarely examined is exactly what §4 says to distrust**, and the admiration is not evidence.

Note also **what he identifies Kṛṣṇa with**: not a *saguṇa* deity subordinate to a higher impersonal absolute, but **nirguṇa Brahman itself**. That is the hinge — it is what allows the devotion to be directed at the ultimate rather than at a provisional form, and it is what distinguishes his position from every Vaiṣṇava Vedānta in this corpus, which reach the same devotional intensity by making Brahman **personal** instead.

### The works, and two that touch existing nodes

Rajagopalan lists twelve. Beyond the *Advaitasiddhi*, *Advaitaratnarakṣaṇa*, *Bhaktirasāyana*, *Gūḍhārthadīpikā* (on the Gītā), *Siddhāntabindu* (on Śaṅkara's *Daśaślokī*) and *Vedāntakalpalatikā*, note:

- a ***Saṃkṣepaśārīraka-vyākhyā*** — a commentary on [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]]'s 1,240-stanza verse summary of Śaṅkara's Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya, which this corpus already holds;
- an ***Īśvarapratipattiprakāśa***, a ***Mahimnastotra-vyākhyā***, a ***Harilīlā-vyākhyā*** and a ***Bhāgavata-prathamaśloka-ṭīkā*** — a devotional half to the bibliography that is not incidental to it.

### The Akbar story, recorded as a story

A Daśanāmī oral tradition has him petitioning **Akbar** about attacks on ascetics, with **Bīrbal** advising the recruitment and arming of non-brahmins — an origin-legend for the armed *nāgā* ascetic orders. Wikipedia reports that historical verification "remains unconfirmed," while **J. N. Farquhar** found a possible historical basis. **Logged as tradition with one scholar's qualified interest, not as event** — the same treatment `shankara.md` and `udayana.md` give their debate-legends.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — V. Rajagopalan, "Madhusūdana Sarasvatī," in *Preceptors of Advaita*** (the T. M. P. Mahadevan collection), via Wisdomlib. Named author, and the **only source consulted that argues for a date rather than stating one**: the Vidyāraṇya quotation, the Vallabhācārya meeting, Gadādhara, and the Śeṣagovinda–Appayya link; the twelve works; the *Advaitasiddhi*'s purpose; and the Kṛṣṇa verse. **Signal 2 — Lance Edward Nelson, study and English translation of the *Bhaktirasāyana* (2021)**, via Wisdomlib. A specialist monograph on precisely the philosophical question, and the source of the problem-statement, the post-liberation-devotion thesis, "the only independent treatise on bhakti written by a major Advaita preceptor," and — importantly — the **methodological warning** about the state of the scholarship. **Signal 3 — Wikipedia**: the Nabadwīp Navya-Nyāya training under Harirāma and Mathurānātha Tarkavāgīśa; Viśveśvara Sarasvatī and the Daśanāmī initiation; c. 1490–1580 with the Kamalanayana birth-name; the Akbar/Bīrbal tradition and Farquhar. **Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `vyasatirtha.md` (written hours earlier: Sharma's "fifty years after the appearance of the *Nyāyāmṛta* the reaction came from far-off Bengal," and the full reply-chain); `appayya-dikshita.md` (the inscription-anchored 1520–1593 that constrains the dating); `sarvajnatman.md`.

**Independence.** Signals 1 and 2 are strongly independent — an Indian Advaita-tradition scholarly collection and a Western academic study forty-odd years apart, with different purposes — and they do not overlap in content, so neither confirms the other; each is load-bearing in its own area. Signal 4 is the genuinely useful check: `vyasatirtha.md`'s Bengal-fifty-years-later detail, from Sharma 1994, corroborates signal 3's Bengal and independently constrains the date from the *other* side of the debate.

**Why contested.** Five datings spanning three centuries, only one argued; and the central philosophical claim is, on the leading specialist's own account, under-examined rather than settled. **Why medium, not high.** **No work of Madhusūdana's was read** — not the *Advaitasiddhi*, not the *Bhaktirasāyana*, even though Nelson's translation of its first chapter is available and was not opened; the Kṛṣṇa verse is quoted from Rajagopalan's English and **the work it opens is not identified by that source**; the dating inference through Appayya is **this file's own**, not any source's, and is labelled accordingly. **Not low**: two named scholars carry everything substantive.
