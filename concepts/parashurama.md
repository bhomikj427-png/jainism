## Gloss / Divergence map

**Paraśurāma** ("Rāma-of-the-axe," *paraśu* "axe" + *rāma*) is the **sixth of the [[avatara-vedanta|daśāvatāra]]**, the first fully human incarnation of Viṣṇu and, distinctively, one who **overlaps in time with the seventh avatāra**, [[rama|Rāma]] — the two meet directly in the Rāmāyaṇa. Born to the sage **Jamadagni** and **Reṇukā** in the Bhṛgu lineage, he is given an axe by **Śiva** after ascetic practice. When his father orders him to behead his mother for a lapse of chastity and he complies, Jamadagni grants him a boon: he restores Reṇukā to life. The pivotal cycle: the kṣatriya king **Kārtavīryārjuna** steals the family's wish-granting cow and kills Jamadagni; Paraśurāma kills Kārtavīryārjuna in retaliation and then, in a **wider purge**, exterminates the kṣatriya class **twenty-one times over**, filling five lakes with blood at **Samanta-pañcaka** (near Kurukṣetra) before offering the accumulated merit to his ancestors and (per some Purāṇic tellings) gifting the earth to Brahmins. He is reckoned a **cirañjīvī** ("ever-living one") who retires to Mount Mahendra rather than dying, and in the Mahābhārata serves as martial guru to Bhīṣma, Droṇa, and Karṇa. In the Rāmāyaṇa's Bāla Kāṇḍa, he intercepts Rāma after Rāma breaks Śiva's bow at Sītā's svayaṃvara, challenges him to string Viṣṇu's own bow, and on Rāma's success yields his residual Viṣṇu-tejas — read by the tradition as the moment the avatāric "charge" passes from the sixth to the seventh descent.

**A caution the sources themselves force**: the daśāvatāra list is not a uniform, fixed ancient canon. Paraśurāma's slot at position six is comparatively stable across the Purāṇas that give the standard ten (Agni, Padma, Garuḍa, Liṅga, Nārada, Skanda, Varāha), but the list as a whole varies — some traditions (e.g., Jayadeva's *Gīta Govinda*) swap or double up Balarāma and Buddha at positions eight/nine. Paraśurāma's inclusion is well attested; the *closed set of ten* is a Purāṇic-era standardization, not a claim this file extends to the whole list.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata** — the Kārtavīryārjuna vendetta, the twenty-one-fold kṣatriya extermination, and the Samanta-pañcaka blood-lakes are narrated in the Saṃgraha/Anukramaṇikā Parva material; Paraśurāma also appears as martial teacher to Bhīṣma (5.178), Droṇa (1.121), and Karṇa (3.286) — independent narrative threads within the same epic, internally consistent.
2. **Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa, Bāla Kāṇḍa, sargas 74–76** — the encounter with Rāma: the bow-challenge and the transfer of Viṣṇu's power, textually independent of the Mahābhārata material and of separate authorship/transmission.
3. **Viṣṇu Purāṇa** (ch. 4) and **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** (9.15.12–13, parentage; 2.3.47, retirement to Mahendra as a daśāvatāra figure) — Puranic-cycle attestation, structurally consistent with both epics.
4. **Nīlamata Purāṇa** (vv. 1167–1226) and **Padma Purāṇa** (ch. 241, sparing the Ikṣvāku line) — regionally and sectarian-distinct texts, corroborating the extermination narrative and adding the detail that the Ikṣvāku (Rāma's own lineage) was spared, which coheres with Paraśurāma later yielding to Rāma rather than opposing his line.
5. **Wikipedia, "Dashavatara"** — documents the list-instability point directly (Balarāma/Buddha variance at positions 8–9) while confirming Paraśurāma's own position is not among the contested slots.

Five-plus independently transmitted texts (two epics, three-plus distinct Purāṇas) converge on the same narrative skeleton — parentage, matricide-and-restoration, the axe, the kṣatriya purge, cirañjīvī status, and the Rāma encounter — which supports **converged**. Confidence is held at **medium** rather than high because the sourcing pass here relied substantially on tertiary encyclopedic summaries and popular retellings (e.g., for the Śiva-axe-penance specifics and the "earth gifted to Kaśyapa" detail) rather than direct engagement with a critical edition or an academic translation (van Buitenen, Debroy) of the primary Mahābhārata passages — a gap noted for a future upgrade pass rather than smoothed over.
