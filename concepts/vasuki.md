## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vāsuki** is the **king of the nāgas** (serpents), a son of the sage Kaśyapa and **[[kadru|Kadrū]]** the serpent-mother, and brother of **[[shesha|Śeṣa]]** (the eldest) and of Takṣaka and Manasā. His two best-known roles: (1) in the **[[samudra-manthana|Samudra-Manthana]]** he is the **churning-cord**, wound about Mount Mandara, the devas pulling his tail and the asuras his hood; (2) in iconography he is the serpent coiled about **[[shiva|Śiva]]'s neck**. In the Mahābhārata's snake-sacrifice cycle he is also central: cursed along with the nāgas by Kadrū, he arranges his sister's marriage to produce Āstīka, who halts King Janamejaya's sarpa-satra. He bears the gem *Nāgamaṇi*. (No major divergence across sources; the family enumeration of nāga siblings varies slightly by text.)

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva (Ganguli tr.)** — names "Vasuki the cord" in the churning, with the devas at the tail and asuras at the hood; the Āstīka / snake-sacrifice narrative gives his parentage and kingship of the nāgas.
2. **Wikipedia, "Vasuki"** — independently: son of Kaśyapa and Kadrū, king of the nāgas, brother of Śeṣa/Manasā, churning-rope, Śiva's neck-serpent; notes the Buddhist tradition of the Eight Great Dragon Kings.

Two sources agree on parentage, kingship, and both signature roles → **converged**, confidence **medium** (sibling-list and minor narrative details are text-dependent; Purāṇic versions are weakly independent retellings).
