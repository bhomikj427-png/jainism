## Gloss

Adharmāstikāya (adharma-substance, "medium of rest") is one of the six Jain dravyas. It is a single, formless, motionless, eternal, space-pervading substance that serves as the passive condition enabling *rest* for souls (*jīva*) and matter (*pudgala*). Just as dharmāstikāya does not cause motion but merely renders it possible, adharmāstikāya does not force anything to stop — it makes stillness possible for whatever is already tending toward rest. The prefix *a-* marks privation of motion-facilitation; the substance is not "unethical" — *adharma* here has no moral valence.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 5.17 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi):** *gatisthityupagrahau dharmādharmayorupakāraḥ* — "The functions of the medium of motion (dharma) and the medium of rest (adharma) are to assist motion and rest, respectively." Paired with dharmāstikāya in the same sūtra; same passive-facilitation structure.

**Signal 2 — TS 5.6 (directly fetched):** *ā ākāśādekadravyāṇi* — adharmāstikāya is singular (one substance), like dharma and ākāśa, unlike the plurality of jīva and pudgala.

**Signal 3 — Multiple Jain institutional sources (Navkar Sadhana, Jainsite, Jainworld):** Unanimous on function and the earth/tree-shade analogies. No independent scholarly source fetched; all repeat standard Jain teaching. Confidence stays medium.

## Key properties

| Property | Adharmāstikāya |
|---|---|
| Number | One (singular substance) |
| Form | Formless, non-material |
| Location | Pervades entire universe (*loka*) but not *aloka* |
| Function | Passive condition for rest (*sthiti*) |
| Agency | Does not stop anything; does not prevent motion |
| Parts | Spatially divisible into innumerable units (*pradeśa*) |

**Analogy (Pūjyapāda):** As earth supports a horse (or a resting traveler) without causing it to stop, adharmāstikāya is the passive medium that makes rest possible for whatever is already tending toward stillness.

**Alternative analogy (popular tradition):** A weary traveler rests under a mango tree's shade; the shade does not compel rest, it merely provides the condition for rest. This is adharmāstikāya's role.

## Dharma / adharma as a pair

Dharmāstikāya and adharmāstikāya are the Jain solution to a cosmological problem: why do some things in the universe move and others rest? The answer is not physical force but ontological condition. The two substances pervade the same space (the *loka*) without interfering with each other, providing the background conditions for the dynamism of jīva and pudgala. Without both, neither motion nor rest would be possible.

This pairing has no clear parallel in Vaiśeṣika (where motion is a quality of substance, not a separate substance) or Buddhist thought (where dharmas are momentary events, not background mediums).
