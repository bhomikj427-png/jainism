## Gloss / Divergence map

**Syrianus** (late 4th–early 5th c. CE) was **head of the Athenian Neoplatonic school** (succeeding Plutarch of Athens, c. 432 CE) and the **teacher of [[proclus|Proclus]]**, who built his vast system explicitly on Syrianus's lectures. His chief surviving work is a **commentary on Aristotle's *Metaphysics*** — one of very few extant ancient Greek commentaries on it — in which he defends **Platonic Forms as true causes** against Aristotle's objections, and reinterprets the second hypothesis of Plato's *Parmenides* as disclosing the divine hierarchy. Drawing substantially on **[[iamblichus|Iamblichus]]**, he set the late-Neoplatonic frame: the One beyond being, the One-Being holding the Forms, descending through Soul to sensibles.

**Resolution of a contested origin.** The doctrine of the **henads** — participated unities mediating the One and the many — was, on the SEP's reading, "**probably introduced by Syrianus himself** and later elaborated by Proclus." This makes Syrianus the stronger of the two candidate origins for the henads (the other being Iamblichus's "law of mean terms"); see [[proclus-henad|proclus-henad]], where both candidates are recorded.
