## Gloss

*Arihant* (Prakrit: *arahant*; from *ari* = enemy + *hant* = destroyer; also read as *arhant* = worthy one) is a soul that has destroyed all four *ghāti* (soul-obscuring) karmas while still embodied, attaining omniscience (*kevala-jñāna*). An arihant is a living jina — still in a human body, still subject to the four *aghāti* (body-bound, non-obscuring) karmas, but with complete, unobstructed upayoga. When the remaining aghāti karmas are exhausted at the moment of death, the arihant becomes a *siddha* (liberated, bodiless soul). The arihant is first among the *pañca-parameṣṭhī* (five supreme beings) revered in the Namaskar Mantra.

## The karma structure at arihant-state

**Four ghāti karmas (destroyed at omniscience):**

| Karma | Obscures | Result of destruction |
|---|---|---|
| Jñānāvaraṇīya | Knowledge | Ananta-jñāna (infinite knowledge) |
| Darśanāvaraṇīya | Perception | Ananta-darśana (infinite perception) |
| Mohanīya | Right conduct (via kaśāyas) | Ananta-sukha (infinite bliss) |
| Antarāya | Power/energy | Ananta-vīrya (infinite energy) |

**Four aghāti karmas (shed only at physical death):**

| Karma | Effect |
|---|---|
| Āyu | Lifespan — determines how long the body continues |
| Nāma | Body-structure — physical form of the arihant |
| Gotra | Social status — caste/standing at birth |
| Vedanīya | Feeling — residual pleasure/pain through the senses |

The arihant experiences no new karma (no kaśāyas to create bondage), but must exhaust the aghāti karma already accumulated — hence the body persists until life-span expires.

## Arihant in the liberation sequence

Gunasthāna 13 (*sayoga-kevalin*): omniscient, still with bodily yoga (speech, movement) — the teaching phase. Most tīrthaṅkaras spend significant time here.
Gunasthāna 14 (*ayoga-kevalin*): omniscient, without yoga — body in the final shutdown; extremely brief (a few moments).
**→ Siddha**: all eight karmas shed; bodiless, at universe-summit.

A *tīrthaṅkara* is a specific arihant (stage 13) who founded the fourfold community (*catur-vidha saṃgha*) and re-established the teaching. An ordinary arihant (*sāmānya-kevalī*) attains omniscience without this teaching mission.

## Not a god; not a creator

Like all jinas, the arihant does not respond to prayer, grant wishes, or intervene in the world. Jain *pūjā* of arihant images is aspirational — modelling one's own consciousness on the arihant's — not petition to a deity. This is a structural philosophical commitment, not a peripheral theological point.
