## Gloss / Divergence map

***Amṛta*** is the **nectar of immortality** — literally "non-death," from the privative *a-* + the root *mṛ* "to die." It is the prize of the **[[samudra-manthana|Samudra-Manthana]]**: the goal for which devas and asuras churn the ocean, brought up at last by **[[dhanvantari|Dhanvantari]]** in his pot, and secured for the devas alone by **[[vishnu|Viṣṇu]]** in the form of the enchantress Mohinī. In the Ṛgveda the word is already an epithet/synonym of **soma**, the drink of the gods. The term is later **reused in Buddhism** (Pali *amata*) where it names not a literal nectar but the "deathless" — an epithet of *[[nibbana-theravada|nibbāna]]* and, in Vajrayāna, the "sweet rain of Dharma."

**Indo-European cognate (structural comparison, not identity).** *Amṛta* is a cognate of Greek **ambrosia** (*a-* "not" + *brotós* "mortal"), both reflexes of Proto-Indo-European *\*n̥-mr̥-tós* "un-dying" — evidence of a shared Indo-European "immortality-drink" complex (cf. the churning's Dumézil "ambrosia cycle"). This is a **linguistic/structural** parallel; it does not make the Purāṇic churning and the Olympian ambrosia the same myth.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Amrita"** — etymology (*a-* + *mṛ*); churning-origin; Dhanvantari + Mohinī; Ṛgvedic identity with soma; PIE *\*n̥-mr̥-tós* cognate with Greek *ambrosia*; Buddhist *amata*/deathless usage.
2. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva (Ganguli tr.)** — independently attests amṛta as the object of the churning and Dhanvantari rising "with the white vessel of nectar."

The two agree on the churning-role and the term's core sense; the etymology is a hard lexical constraint (privative + *mṛ*), corroborated by the Greek cognate → **converged**, confidence **medium** (the soma-identity and Buddhist reuse are tradition-specific senses, recorded as such).
