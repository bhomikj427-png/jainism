## Gloss

Giving/charity — a virtue present across Jain, Buddhist, and Hindu traditions, always understood as a form of spiritual practice rather than mere philanthropy. The Jain definition (TS 7.38) is precise: *dāna is the giving of one's own objects to another for mutual benefit (anugraha)* — benefiting both giver (who gains merit, karma-reduction) and recipient (who gains support for spiritual practice). All three traditions agree that the donor's intention matters and that giving without expectation of return is higher than transactional giving. They disagree on the metaphysical mechanism that makes giving meritorious.

## Jain definition (TS 7.38 — directly fetched)

**Sanskrit:** *anugrahārthaṃ svasyātisargo dānam*
**Translation:** "Charity is the giving of one's objects to another for mutual benefit — anugraha."
**Pūjyapāda (Sarvārthasiddhi):** *anugraha* = beneficial to both the giver and the recipient. The giver accumulates merit (puṇya); the recipient gains advancement in spiritual knowledge.

### Four elements of Jain dāna
| element | content |
|---|---|
| giver (dātā) | one who possesses resources and willingly renounces them |
| recipient (pātrata) | one who receives — monastic community is the ideal recipient |
| substance (dravy) | material objects being transferred |
| method (bhāva) | the intention and manner of giving |

### Four categories of Jain dāna
| category | Sanskrit | content |
|---|---|---|
| food-giving | āhāra-dāna | sustaining monks (who cannot purchase food) |
| medicine-giving | auṣadha-dāna | providing remedies |
| knowledge-giving | śāstra-dāna | sharing sacred texts and teachings |
| fearlessness-giving | abhaya-dāna | protecting beings from harm |

Abhaya-dāna (fearlessness-giving) is the highest because it directly enacts ahiṃsā.

## Cross-tradition comparison

| feature | Jain | Buddhist | Hindu |
|---|---|---|---|
| metaphysical ground | karma-reduction + puṇya accumulation; supports monastic system which enables mokṣa | mental purification; reduces attachment (tṛṣṇā); generates merit for rebirth + nirvāṇa path | dharmic obligation; karma reciprocity; fulfils social and ritual duty |
| highest form of dāna | abhaya-dāna (fearlessness/protection) | dhamma-dāna (sharing the teaching) | sāttvika-dāna (pure, unconditional, at proper time/place/recipient) |
| primary recipient | Jain monastics (who enable the teacher-student merit chain) | spiritually advanced persons; the Saṅgha | worthy recipient assessed for character; dharmic contexts |
| link to other practices | directly supports ahiṃsā (feeding monks avoids harm by proxy) and aparigraha (non-possessiveness motivates giving) | first of ten pāramitās; foundation for subsequent perfections | Bhagavad Gītā 17.20–22: three types by guṇa (sattvic/rajasic/tamasic) |
| mechanism of merit | karma-particles dislodged by generous act | cetanā (intention) generates positive kamma; selfless giving reduces ego-binding | dharma-karma: giving as cosmic reciprocity |
| expectation of return | explicitly absent in anugraha model | giving "without expectation of return" is highest | sattvic giving: "given without expectation of return, at proper time/place, to worthy person" (BG 17.20) |
