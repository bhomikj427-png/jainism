## Gloss / Divergence map

**Śakti** (Sanskrit, "energy; power; ability; strength"; verbal root *śak-*, to be able): the Hindu concept of **divine cosmic power**, conceived as essentially feminine — the dynamic, creative, activating force that underlies all existence. Śakti is not simply one goddess among many but the universal energy that **animates all deities and all manifest reality**. In Śākta theology, Śakti is identified with the Mahādevī (Great Goddess) who is simultaneously transcendent and immanent: "By her all this is pervaded; she is eternal, having the world as her form" (*Devī Māhātmya*).

**The Śiva–Śakti relationship (the core Śaiva-Śākta theological problem):**

| school | Śiva | Śakti | their relationship |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kashmir Śaivism (Trika) | *Prakāśa* — pure luminous consciousness; the static pole of absolute reality | *Vimarśa* — Śiva's own self-reflective power; his capacity to know and act | Non-dual: Śiva without Śakti is an abstraction; Śakti IS Śiva's self-expression; liberation = recognizing this non-duality |
| Śaiva Siddhānta | Personal God (pati); active through his icchā/jñāna/kriyā-śaktis | Real transformative creative power; works pariṇāmavāda (real transformation) | Dualistic: Śiva and Śakti remain distinct; Śakti is Śiva's *real* power, not his nature |
| Śāktism (Śrī Vidyā / Tantra) | A partial aspect of the goddess; masculine principle | The supreme reality (Mahādevī = Brahman); Śiva is embedded in Śakti | Supremacy of Śakti: the goddess is the absolute; Śiva is her consort / inert without her |
| Popular / Purāṇic | Husband-wife (Śiva-Pārvatī); complementary | Female *prakṛti* to Śiva's *puruṣa* | Complementarity: masculine/feminine cosmic duality that generates the world |

**Major Śākta manifestations (the three primary goddess forms):**
- **Kālī**: fierce, dark, destruction-and-liberation; transcends social norms; representing the power over time (kāla) and death
- **Durgā**: divine warrior, cosmic protector against adharma; slayer of Mahiṣāsura; the "Inaccessible One" (*durgā* = difficult to reach)
- **Tripura Sundarī (Lalitā / Śrī Vidyā)**: "Beautiful One of the Three Cities"; the supreme benign form; associated with Śrī Yantra, mantra practice, and tantric liberation; Brahman in female form

**Devī Māhātmya (primary text, ~5th–6th c. CE):** Part of the Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa, this is the foundational Śākta text in Sanskrit. Three episodes of cosmic battle between the goddess and asuras (Madhu-Kaiṭabha, Mahiṣāsura, Śumbha-Niśumbha) frame a theological claim: the goddess is not a deity who defeats demons but cosmic reality asserting itself against forces of disorder. The text explicitly calls her *nitya* (eternal), *jagadrūpiṇī* (world-formed), and the source of both *māyā* (binding ignorance) and *mahāvidyā* (liberating knowledge).

**Critical distinction — Śakti is NOT Sāṃkhya prakṛti:**

| feature | Sāṃkhya prakṛti | Śakti |
|---|---|---|
| Consciousness | unconscious (acetana) — three guṇas without awareness | conscious / sentient in Śākta theology — the goddess knows and liberates |
| Ontological status | dependent on puruṣa for illumination (reflected consciousness) | co-equal or supreme — Śiva is "inert without Śakti" (common Tantric formulation) |
| Soteriological role | source of bondage; liberation = puruṣa's separation from prakṛti | source of both bondage (māyā-śakti) AND liberation (vidyā-śakti); the goddess herself liberates |
| Relation to God | no personal creator — Sāṃkhya is atheistic | Śakti IS the supreme being in Śāktism; relates personally to devotees |

**Critical distinction — Śakti is NOT Advaita māyā:**
Advaita's māyā is the *ignorance-producing superimposition* that makes brahman appear as a world of multiplicity — it is ultimately unreal (*vivartavāda*). Śakti in Śaiva and Śākta traditions is **real, positive, conscious power** — the world's arising from Śakti is a real transformation (*pariṇāmavāda*) or a real self-expression (*ābhāsa*), not an illusion to be dispelled. Both Śaiva Siddhānta and Kashmir Śaivism explicitly reject Advaita's māyāvāda on exactly this ground: Śakti is not the veil over truth but the very expression of it.
