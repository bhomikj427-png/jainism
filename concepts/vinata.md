## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vinatā** is a daughter of Dakṣa, co-wife of Kaśyapa, and mother of [[aruna|Aruṇa]] (the elder) and [[garuda|Garuḍa]] (the younger) — the bird/sun line, counterpart to her sister [[kadru|Kadrū]]'s serpent line. In the Ādi Parva she asks for two sons mightier than Kadrū's thousand, but impatiently breaks one egg prematurely, releasing the half-formed Aruṇa, who curses her to servitude — borne out when she loses the Ucchaiḥśravas wager to Kadrū and is enslaved. Her bondage is the engine of Garuḍa's epic: to free his mother, Garuḍa must fetch the **amṛta** (nectar) from the gods. Vinatā is thus the maternal origin-point of the bird-vs-serpent enmity and the narrative pretext for Garuḍa's heroics; her premature son Aruṇa goes on to become the dawn and charioteer of Sūrya.

**Variant (recorded, not reconciled).** Sources agree on daughter-of-Dakṣa and the two sons (some add a daughter, Sumati); the solar-dynasty extension is detailed mainly in the Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva** — the Vinatā/Garuḍa narrative: the premature egg, Aruṇa's curse, the enslavement, the nectar-quest (the underlying epic source).
2. **Wikipedia, "Vinata"** — Dakṣa parentage; sons Aruṇa, Garuḍa (and Sumati); the curse and bondage.
3. **Mahābhārata retellings (mahabharataonline / themahabharata.org)** — corroborate the enslavement and nectar-quest (derivative, weaker provenance).

Encyclopedic source + epic text agree on a very stable core → **converged**. Confidence **medium**: the corroborating retellings are derivative and the solar-dynasty material is variant-dependent.
