## Gloss / Divergence map

**Viṣṇu** (Sanskrit: *viś* "to pervade / to enter everywhere"; glossed by Yāska, 4th c. BCE, as "one who enters everywhere" — *yasmāt viśvam idaṃ viprasuṣṭaḥ*): the supreme deity of **Vaiṣṇavism**, characterised as sustainer and preserver of the universe within the Hindu Trimurti (Brahmā creator / Viṣṇu preserver / Śiva dissolver).

**Six divine qualities (ṣaḍ-bhaga):** jñāna (knowledge), bala (power), aiśvarya (sovereignty), śrī (prosperity/beauty), vīrya (energy), and tejas (splendour). The name *Bhagavān* ("possessing bhaga") derives from this six-fold quality set; the Bhāgavata Purāṇa uses all three designations interchangeably: *brahman* (impersonal absolute), *Paramātman* (immanent self-in-all), and *Bhagavān* (personal supreme — BhP 1.2.11).

**Avatāra doctrine (BG 4.7–4.8):** Whenever *dharma* declines and chaos prevails, Viṣṇu descends (*avatārate*) into the world in an appropriate embodied form. The Bhāgavata Purāṇa lists 10 principal avatāras (Daśāvatāra); the total is described as innumerable. This doctrine structurally distinguishes Vaiṣṇavism from both Jainism (no divine intervention post-liberation) and Advaita (descent understood as conventional/māyā level only).

**Viṣṇu's relationship to Brahman — three incompatible readings:**

| school | founder | Viṣṇu ↔ Brahman relation |
|---|---|---|
| Advaita Vedānta | Śaṅkara (8th c.) | Viṣṇu is the *saguṇa* (qualified) face of nirguṇa Brahman; the personal God is real at vyāvahārika level, ultimately non-different from Brahman |
| Viśiṣṭādvaita (Śrī Vaiṣṇavism) | Rāmānuja (11th–12th c.) | Viṣṇu *is* Brahman with genuine qualities; selves and matter are real modes *within* Viṣṇu (body–soul analogy); māyā ≠ illusion |
| Dvaita | Madhvācārya (13th c.) | Viṣṇu and individual selves are ontologically distinct always; pañcabheda (five-fold eternal difference); liberation = eternal proximity, not identity |

**The critical NOT-equivalent:** Viṣṇu (personal God, *sa-guṇa*, historical agent) ≠ Brahman (impersonal absolute, *nir-guṇa*, beyond all relation). The Bhāgavata Purāṇa equation (BhP 1.2.11) is explicitly from the Vaiṣṇava standpoint — Advaita interprets the same verse as subordinating Bhagavān to the ultimately nirguṇa Brahman. The two readings are mutually incompatible.
