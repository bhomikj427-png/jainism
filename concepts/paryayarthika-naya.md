## Gloss / Divergence map

The *paryāyārthika nayas* are the **mode-standpoint nayas** — the four of the seven nayas (TS 1.33) that attend to specific, momentary, particular modes (*paryāyas*) of a substance, rather than to what persists across modes. They are progressively more fine-grained than the dravyārthika nayas — reaching down to the specific act of a specific word at a specific moment.

**Pūjyapāda's characterisation** (via Vijay K. Jain 2018): Paryāyārthika naya addresses "constantly changing conditions or modes" (*viśeṣa* = particular/specific; *apavāda* = exception, exclusion from the general rule).

**The four paryāyārthika nayas** (standard 3+4 classification; from Deepa Baruah 2017 and naya.md):

| naya | Sanskrit | key move | classic example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Straight/momentary | ṛjusūtra | attends ONLY to the object's present mode, discarding all past and future aspects | the jar *right now*, not "the clay that was a lump yesterday" |
| Verbal / synonymous | śabda | treats synonymous words as sharing identical meanings, regardless of etymological origin | *kumbha*, *kalaśa*, *ghaṭa* (three words for "jar") all mean the same thing |
| Etymological | samabhirūḍha | argues synonymous words express different meanings via their respective etymological roots | *kumbha* (from the root for "to swell") connotes something different from *ghaṭa* (from "to unite/close") — same jar, different aspect |
| Functional / specialised | evaṃbhūta | a word applies ONLY when the object is actively performing the function the term denotes | "cow" (*go*) applies strictly to an animal while it is going/moving — the term and the act must coincide |

**The progressive narrowing:** The four nayas form a descending series of specificity:
- Ṛjusūtra = most general of the four (present-moment focus, but still about the object as a whole in this moment)
- Śabda = language-level (synonymy = identity)
- Samabhirūḍha = etymology-level (same word-form, different connotation)
- Evaṃbhūta = function-level (word applies only in the moment of function) — the most specific of all seven nayas

**Ṛjusūtra and Buddhist momentariness:** Ṛjusūtra is often compared structurally to the Buddhist *momentariness* doctrine (Abhidharma dharmas exist for only one moment). Jain commentators use ṛjusūtra as the naya that *comes closest to* the Buddhist view — while noting that taking ṛjusūtra as the *only* truth (making it a nayābhāsa) leads to Buddhist-style denial of the persisting substance. Anekāntavāda says ṛjusūtra is valid but must be paired with dravyārthika nayas.

**Connection to paryāya (paryaya.md):** The four paryāyārthika nayas are the epistemological tools that correspond to the paryāya-level of ontology — they pick out the *utpāda/vyaya* (origination/destruction) face of existence (*sat*, TS 5.30).
