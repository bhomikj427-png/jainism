## Gloss

**The three jewels** (*ratna-traya*, also *tri-ratna*) — the integrated triad that *jointly* constitutes the Jain path to liberation: **right faith** (*samyak-darśana*), **right knowledge** (*samyak-jñāna*), and **right conduct** (*samyak-cāritra*). The locus classicus is the opening sūtra of the entire Tattvārtha Sūtra, *TS* 1.1: *samyagdarśanajñānacāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* — "Right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct, **together**, constitute the path to liberation (*mokṣa-mārga*)."

The decisive grammatical point, stressed by Pūjyapāda, is that *mārga* ("path") is **singular**, not plural: the three are one path, not three alternative paths. The commentary "refutes the view that each of these singly constitutes the path to liberation." Nor are they a temporal sequence one climbs rung by rung — right faith and right knowledge "are attained by the soul simultaneously," like the heat and light of the sun appearing together once the clouds disperse. The dependency is logical, not chronological: right faith is what makes knowledge and conduct *samyak* ("right") at all; knowledge without right faith is mere information, conduct without it mere behaviour. The triad is therefore an interdependent whole — remove any one jewel and the path collapses.

The **content** of each jewel is fixed elsewhere in the TS: the first jewel is conviction in the seven *tattvas* as they actually are (*TS* 1.2, see [samyak-darshana.md](samyak-darshana.md)); the second is the five kinds of *jñāna* — *mati, śruta, avadhi, manaḥparyaya, kevala* (*TS* 1.9), valid cognition organised as *pramāṇa*; the third is the restraint of body, speech, and mind enacted as the *mahāvratas* (ascetics) or *aṇuvratas* (laypeople) (see [charitra.md](charitra.md)).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 1.1 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018, with Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*):** Sanskrit *samyagdarśanajñānacāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* confirmed verbatim; the singular-*mārga* argument and the clouds/sun simultaneity analogy are from the commentary. **Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Ratnatraya":** independently lists the three jewels with the same content-assignments (jewel 1 = seven tattvas; jewel 2 = jīva/ajīva knowledge; jewel 3 = the five vows) and the "work together" interdependence claim. **Signal 3 — Jainpedia ("The Three Gems") and aggregated teaching sources:** confirm "one of the three cannot exist exclusive of the others." Three genuinely independent signals converge on both the verse and the interdependence reading. Confidence **medium** (not high): the verse text rests on a single critical edition (Vijay K. Jain 2018); Nathmal Tatia's independent Chapter-1 rendering remains unfetched — the corpus-wide ceiling.

**False-friend flagged:** the Buddhist *triratna* (Three Jewels = Buddha, Dharma, Saṅgha) shares the "three jewels" label but is a structurally different object — three *refuges* (an enlightened teacher, his teaching, the community), not three components of a single soteriological path. Same name, different role; this is documented in the chapter's conflation alert, not drawn as a graph edge to a non-existent node.
