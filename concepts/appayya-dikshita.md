## Gloss / Divergence map

**Appayya Dīkṣita** (**c. 1520–1593**), born at Adaiyapālam near Tiruvaṇṇāmalai in the Tamil country, is the storey-5 author of the Bhāmatī stack recorded in [[amalananda]] — his ***Kalpataruparimala*** glosses Amalānanda's *Vedāntakalpataru*, and earned him the title ***Advaita-Sthāpanācārya***, "the teacher who established Advaita." He wrote roughly **100 treatises** across Vedānta, Mīmāṃsā, poetics, grammar and devotional poetry, under close royal patronage from **Cinnabomma Nāyaka of Vellore for nearly three decades (1549–1578)**; a 1580 inscription by Śivappa Nāyaka calls him "the sole emperor of **Śaiva** Advaita."

That inscription is the problem this file exists to state. The same man is:

| the work | what it does | which side it puts him on |
|---|---|---|
| ***Kalpataruparimala*** | makes Amalānanda's abstruse *Kalpataru* readable; completes the five-text *Vedānta Śāstra* series ([[amalananda]]) | classical [[advaita-vedanta|Advaita]], **Bhāmatī** line |
| ***Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*** | surveys the *internal divergences* of Advaita — *ekajīva-vāda*, *nānājīva-vāda*, *bimba-pratibimba-vāda*, *sākṣitva-vāda* — recording each earlier Advaitin's reading | Advaita, **as doxographer rather than partisan** |
| ***Nyāyarakṣāmaṇi*** | his own gloss on Śaṅkara's *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* | Advaita, in his own voice |
| ***Śivārkamaṇidīpikā*** | his "monumental treatise": a commentary on **Śrīkaṇṭha's** *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya*, retrieving an obscure text and reconstructing a whole theology from it | **Śivādvaita** — a school with a real God |
| ***Madhvatantramukhamardana***, ***Madhvamatavidhvaṃsana*** | titled demolitions of [[dvaita-vedanta|Madhva's]] school | polemic, against a named target |

### The finding: which of these was his position

Two literatures answer differently, and the disagreement is about *what kind of thing* a Sanskrit scholastic's oeuvre is.

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **Advaitin foremost.** Remembered as "an influential preceptor of the Advaita tradition"; the Śaiva work is the specialised corner of a career whose centre is the *Parimala* and the title it won | the received tradition, and the encyclopedia summaries that follow it | that the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* is an exercise — a scholar demonstrating he can build any system — and that the Vellore inscription is patron's flattery |
| **Śivādvaitin, and the school's actual founder.** He "single-handedly established" Śivādvaita Vedānta; the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* is its "magnum opus"; his was "the Śaiva oeuvre" of a "legendary advocate of Śaiva religion" | **Jonathan Duquette**, *Defending God in Sixteenth-Century India* (OUP 2021), the first full study of that oeuvre, "based to a large extent on hitherto unstudied primary sources" | that Śivādvaita is not a footnote to an Advaitin's career but the project the career was for — and that "Advaita-Sthāpanācārya" records a *reception*, not a self-description |
| **Historically Śivādvaita, remembered as Advaita** — both, with the tension left in | Wikipedia, which holds together "widely remembered as an influential preceptor of the Advaita tradition" and "historically rooted in the Śivādvaita school as its most prominent representative," and adds that his mature works "explicitly synthesized his Śaiva theology within an Advaita framework" | that the question is partly about **memory** rather than about doctrine, and that the answer may differ depending on whether you ask his texts or his tradition |

The corpus does not settle this, and notes that the specialist monograph — the item with by far the best provenance — is the one that most sharply displaces the received picture. Its thesis is **located but not read**; the summary above is from the publisher's description, which is a statement of the argument and not the argument.

### A polemical target that leaves no trace in the titles

The most transferable result here is a caution about reading a scholar's opponents off his tables of contents. Appayya wrote works *named* for their target — *Madhvamata-**vidhvaṃsana***, "the demolition of Madhva's doctrine," and *Madhvatantra-mukha-mardana*, "the crushing of the face of Madhva's system." Against [[vishishtadvaita|Viśiṣṭādvaita]], Wikipedia notes, textual evidence suggests he "composed no work **explicitly** directed against Rāmānuja's philosophy," and the legendary confrontations with the Viśiṣṭādvaitin **Tātācārya** are hagiography. Yet on Duquette's reading the Śaiva oeuvre "was mainly directed against Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta, the dominant Vaiṣṇava school of philosophical theology in his time and place."

These are compatible, and their compatibility is the point: **the opponent you title a book after and the opponent you write a whole theology against need not be the same opponent.** Against Madhva — a school he could name and refute in the ordinary polemical register — he wrote refutations. Against the Vaiṣṇava school that actually held the ground he wanted, he built a rival system instead, which is the more serious form of attack and the one invisible to a bibliography. The corpus records this as a **method warning**, not a claim about Appayya's intentions.

### The *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*, and the one line where it parts from this corpus

The *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha* — "a compendium of ideas and perspectives within Advaita" — is a **16th-century doxography of a single school's internal disagreements**: it collects the divergent positions of earlier Advaitins on the number of jīvas, the reflection theory, the status of the witness-consciousness, and sets the contrary views out properly rather than only the author's own. Structurally that is very close to what this repository does with a `contested` node, and closer still to what [[prakasatman]], [[sarvajnatman]] and [[vacaspati-mishra]] are for.

It parts company at the last step. The reporting sources agree that Appayya holds the divergent sub-schools to **converge** in the end — "they all ultimately converge on the same fundamental insight." This corpus's §4 does not have that move available: convergence is an *output* that the signals either produce or fail to produce, and a survey that guarantees agreement in advance has decided the question before collecting the evidence. The comparison is worth drawing precisely because the two projects look alike and differ on exactly one commitment. It is also why the *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha* is the **named upgrade path** for [[prakasatman]], [[sarvajnatman]] and [[vacaspati-mishra]] alike: whatever its framing, it is a pre-modern witness to what those men were taken to have held, written by someone with access to texts that have since been lost.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Appayya Dikshita"** — dates 1520–1593; the Cinnabomma Nāyaka patronage 1549–1578 and the 1580 Śivappa Nāyaka inscription; ~100 works; the works-list; the Śrīkaṇṭha retrieval; the Advaita-reputation / Śivādvaita-rootedness tension **stated as a tension**; the Tātācārya legends and the absence of an explicit anti-Rāmānuja work; the rival Vijayendra Bhikṣu's 104 works.
2. **Jonathan Duquette, *Defending God in Sixteenth-Century India: The Śaiva Oeuvre of Appaya Dīkṣita*, Oxford Oriental Monographs, OUP 2021** — the specialist monograph and the highest-provenance item; **located, not read**, its thesis taken from the publisher's description: Śivādvaita "single-handedly established," the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* as its magnum opus, the oeuvre "mainly directed against Viśiṣṭādvaita," Śrīkaṇṭha's *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya* as the source of exegesis.
3. **The *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha* literature** (Chinmaya International Foundation course description; Centre for Brahmavidya; Advaita-L archive) — the text's doxographic scope and the four named sub-doctrines; the 1973 edition of S. R. Krishnamurthy Sastri; the convergence claim. Three separate platforms, all **downstream of the traditional account** and so not four independent votes.
4. **Advaita-L archive (2006)** — the five-text *Vedānta Śāstra* formula (see [[amalananda]]); the *Parimala* composed at the request of Nṛsiṃhāśrama; the *Advaita-Sthāpanācārya* title.
5. **Hindupedia, "Later Advaitins"** — 16th c.; the *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*, *Parimalā*, the anti-Madhva works, and works "reconciling Śivādvaita with Advaita-Vedānta" — the reconciliation framing stated as such.
6. **Corpus cross-check** — [[amalananda]], [[vacaspati-mishra]], [[shaivism]], [[dvaita-vedanta]], [[vishishtadvaita]]: the five-storey stack recorded one node ago is what makes his position in it exact, and the corpus's existing Śaiva and Vaiṣṇava nodes are what make the polemical geography legible.

**CONTESTED** — on which tradition he belongs to, which is not a question of dates or attributions (those are secure and agreed) but of how to weigh a bibliography against a reputation. Confidence **medium**: dates, patronage, works and the inscription are uncontested across independent sources, but the decisive item is a 2021 monograph **read only through its publisher's description**, and **no work of Appayya's was read** — neither the *Siddhāntaleśasaṃgraha*, whose contents are reported here at second hand, nor the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā*. Two attempted fetches of scholarly sources (Duquette's OUP page and Bronner's "A Renaissance Man in Memory," which treats the *memory* side of the question directly) returned 403; both are named upgrade paths.
