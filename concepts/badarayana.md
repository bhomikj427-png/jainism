## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bādarāyaṇa** is the traditional author of the *Brahma Sūtra* (= *Vedānta Sūtra* / *Śārīraka Sūtra*), the third member of the *prasthāna-traya* and the systematizing root text of Vedānta — ~555 terse aphorisms reconciling Upaniṣadic teaching on Brahman. He matters as the shared base text that every later Vedānta ācārya (Śaṅkara, Rāmānuja, Madhva) glosses, each reading his cryptic sūtras into a different system.

The **authorship of the Brahma Sūtra is converged**; what is **contested — and is the finding** — is his identity:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Bādarāyaṇa is **distinct** from Veda-Vyāsa | early Vedāntins (Upavarṣa, Śaṅkara per the Triveni analysis); modern historical scholarship | a separate historical sūtra-author; the Vyāsa equation is a later overlay |
| Bādarāyaṇa **=** Veda-Vyāsa (the Mahābhārata compiler) | later Advaitins, 8th–9th c. onward | a single legendary super-author; flagged by critics as "patently an anachronism" |
| text in surviving form c. 400–450 CE; core material 200 BCE – 200 CE | Wikipedia / wisdomlib | the figure anchors a long-redacted text, not a datable individual |

Note also the alias cluster *Brahma Sūtra = Vedānta Sūtra = Śārīraka Sūtra* — one text under three names (aliases, not a conflation).
