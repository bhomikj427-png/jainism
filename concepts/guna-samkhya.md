## Gloss / Divergence map

In Sāṃkhya, guṇas are the **three fundamental strands or constituents of all of prakṛti (primal matter/nature)**: sattva, rajas, and tamas. They are not qualities possessed by individual substances but the three *components* that together constitute ALL material existence — including both the physical world and the mind.

**The three guṇas:**

| guṇa | Sanskrit | character | role in manifest world |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sattva | sattva | clarity, harmony, lightness, illumination | intellect, cognition, virtue |
| Rajas | rajas | activity, passion, mobility, energy | movement, desire, dynamism |
| Tamas | tamas | heaviness, inertia, obscurity, resistance | solidity, resistance, ignorance |

All three are always present in every phenomenon — it is their *proportion* and *dominance* that characterises each thing. A stone is tamas-dominant; a philosopher's intellect is sattva-dominant; a warrior's anger is rajas-dominant.

**Scholarly debate on what guṇas *are*:** Three positions within Sāṃkhya scholarship:
- **Substances**: guṇas are the ultimate material constituents of prakṛti
- **Qualities**: guṇas are properties of prakṛti, not separate entities
- **Capacities/tendencies**: guṇas are functional dispositions or forces

(Search-result aggregation cites this as an active scholarly discussion; specific scholars not named in the sources.)

**The equilibrium/disturbance cosmology:** When the three guṇas are in perfect balance → *avyakta* (unmanifest prakṛti) = pure potential. Proximity of puruṣa disturbs the balance → creation proceeds from *mahat* through the 25 tattvas (see prakriti-samkhya.md). The manifest world is the result of guṇa-imbalance.

## Guṇa in Sāṃkhya vs. guṇa in Jain philosophy

These are related by shared vocabulary but structurally different:

| dimension | Sāṃkhya guṇa | Jain guṇa (in dravya system) |
|---|---|---|
| What is it? | universal strands constituting ALL of prakṛti | a permanent, inseparable quality specific to a particular dravya |
| Scope | every material phenomenon (incl. mind) | each dravya has its own guṇas (e.g., pudgala has rūpa/sparśa/rasa/gandha/varṇa; jīva has upayoga) |
| Number | always three (sattva/rajas/tamas) | varies by dravya; not a universal three |
| Do all substances share them? | yes — all of prakṛti has all three guṇas | no — each dravya has its own defining guṇas; jīva's guṇas ≠ pudgala's guṇas |
| Role of guṇa | constitutive (prakṛti IS the three guṇas) | descriptive (guṇa is an attribute of dravya, not identical with it) |
