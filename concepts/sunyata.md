## Gloss / Divergence map

Śūnyatā is **emptiness of *svabhāva* (inherent existence/own-nature)** — the Madhyamaka Buddhist thesis that no phenomenon possesses independent, self-subsisting existence. It is emphatically not nihilism (the claim that nothing exists) but anti-essentialism: things exist conventionally; what they lack is *inherent* existence.

**Svabhāva** = the property of being self-subsistent — existing independently, not relying on anything else for what one is. Nāgārjuna's argument (Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, ~2nd c. CE): nothing can have svabhāva because everything is dependently arisen (*pratītyasamutpāda*). What is dependently arisen depends on conditions; what depends on conditions cannot have intrinsic/independent nature.

**The key equation (MMK 24.18):**
*"Whatever is dependently arisen — that is explained as emptiness. That [emptiness] is dependent designation; that is the middle path."*
Śūnyatā = pratītyasamutpāda: the same doctrine about dependent arising, seen from the perspective of what is absent (inherent nature). (Source: search-result aggregation, Nāgārjuna's blog, encyclopediaofbuddhism.)

**"Emptiness of emptiness":** Śūnyatā itself is empty — it is not a positive absolute or ultimate ground. If emptiness *were* itself inherently real, it would be just another *svabhāva*-bearing thing; but emptiness is itself dependently designated. This prevents Madhyamaka from becoming a new absolutism. (Contrast: Advaita Brahman IS a positive absolute — Sat-Cit-Ānanda.)

**What śūnyatā is NOT:**
- Not nihilism — phenomena exist conventionally (tables, persons, karma, the Buddhist path — all conventionally real and causally efficacious)
- Not the same as Advaita Brahman — Brahman is a positive, eternal, conscious ultimate; śūnyatā is the *absence* of a falsely imagined type of existence

**Two-truths framework:** Conventional truth (*saṃvṛti-satya*) — phenomena exist and function; ultimate truth (*paramārtha-satya*) — no phenomenon has inherent existence. Śūnyatā is the ultimate truth; it doesn't abolish the conventional.

## Śūnyatā vs. Jain dravya realism

This is one of the sharpest contrasts in the corpus:

| dimension | Buddhist śūnyatā (Madhyamaka) | Jain dravya |
|---|---|---|
| Do substances have inherent natures? | NO — all dharmas/substances are empty of svabhāva | YES — jīva's nature is upayoga; pudgala's nature is rūpa; these are genuine svabhāvas |
| Is the world ultimately real? | conventionally real; ultimately empty | both conventionally AND ultimately real (the 6 dravyas are equally real) |
| Is there a substrate? | no inherent substrate; only dependently arising processes | yes — eternal dravyas with genuine qualities (guṇas) |
| Purpose of the claim | soteriological: insight into śūnyatā liberates from grasping | ontological: accurate mapping of what exists |
| Role of language | language designates conceptually; no word picks out an inherent nature | language can describe real properties of real substances |

Jain anekāntavāda posits that dravyas have BOTH permanent substance-natures AND changing modes — this is Jain realism. Madhyamaka denies that any nature is inherent/intrinsic to anything — anti-essentialism. These are directly opposed metaphysical positions.
