## Gloss

*Śrutajñāna* (scriptural/testimonial knowledge) is the second of the five Jain knowledge-types. It arises from comprehension of verbal or written content — hearing words, reading texts, receiving teaching — as opposed to direct sensory contact with an object. Like matijñāna, it is *parokṣa* (indirect), because it operates through the medium of language and symbol. It is causally dependent on matijñāna (TS 1.20: scriptural knowledge is *preceded by* sensory knowledge), but extends further: properly understood, śruta can convey knowledge about substances and realities that the senses cannot directly access. The canonical Jain *āgamas* are the primary vehicle of śruta-jñāna, but the Samayasāra's key clarification stands: the text itself is not the knowledge — understanding generates the knowledge in the learner.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 1.9 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda):** *matiśrutāvadhimanaḥparyayakevalāni jñānam* — "Knowledge is of five kinds: sensory-knowledge, scriptural-knowledge, clairvoyance, telepathy, and omniscience." Śruta is the second, immediately following mati.

**Signal 2 — TS 1.26 (already fetched in mati-jnana.md):** *matiśrutayornibandho dravyeṣvasarvaparyāyeṣu* — "The range of sensory and scriptural knowledge extends to all six substances but not to all their modes." Both share the same scope ceiling: all dravyas, but partial access to paryāyas.

**Signal 3 — Pūjyapāda commentary on TS 1.9 (via WisdomLib fetch):** Śruta = "that through which ascertained objects are heard"; "scriptural knowledge is preceded by sensory knowledge" (TS 1.20). The causal dependence is explicit.

**Signal 4 — Kundakunda, *Samayasāra* (cited via Wikipedia "Jain Epistemology"):** "Scripture itself isn't knowledge — rather, understanding scriptural content generates knowledge in the learner." Separates the vehicle from the cognitive act.

**Independence:** Signals 1+2 are primary-text fetches; Signal 3 is commentary from the same edition; Signal 4 is a separate canonical text. Confidence medium: TS 1.20 (the direct dependency verse) confirmed from Pūjyapāda but not independently fetched.

## Relationship to matijñāna

| Feature | Matijñāna | Śrutajñāna |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Sensory/mental contact with object | Hearing/reading verbal or written content |
| **Medium** | Sense organs + manas | Language, symbol, scripture |
| **Scope (TS 1.26)** | All 6 substances, partial modes | All 6 substances, partial modes (same) |
| **Causal dependence** | No prior Jain knowledge-type required | Preceded by matijñāna (TS 1.20) |
| **Parokṣa?** | Yes | Yes |
| **Non-sensory reach** | Limited to what the senses touch | Can extend further via properly-understood text |

Śruta *extends* mati by providing access to the knowledge of others and to traditional wisdom about trans-sensory realities — but it cannot surpass mati in epistemic directness; it is still mediated by language.

## Types of śruta-jñāna

Two structural categories in the Jain scholastic tradition:
- **Aṅgapraviṣṭa**: Knowledge contained within the *aṅgas* (the twelve canonical Āgama texts)
- **Aṅgabāhya**: Knowledge in texts outside the twelve aṅgas (the wider Āgama literature)

This division tracks the Jain canonical structure. Each category has sub-divisions (twelve kinds each) in detailed classifications, but the philosophical content of śruta-jñāna does not depend on the specific textual category.

## The authority question

The Samayasāra's distinction is philosophically important: the āgamas are vehicles, not guarantors. Mere reading of a text does not produce śruta-jñāna — active understanding (*parijñāna*) is required. This blocks a naive "authority of scripture" view and aligns with the Jain epistemological principle that all knowledge (including scriptural) must ultimately be integrated into the knower's own cognitive activity (*upayoga*).
