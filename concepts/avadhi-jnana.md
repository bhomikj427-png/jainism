## Gloss

Clairvoyant knowledge of material (pudgala) objects — the third of five knowledge types, classified as direct (pratyakṣa) because it arises from the soul's own capacity without sensory mediation. "Avadhi" means bounded: the knowledge is limited by substance, mode, time, and place, and apprehends only physical matter, not non-material dravyas. Gods and infernal beings possess it innately by birth (bhava-pratyaya); humans and animals can acquire it through karma-purification (guṇa-pratyaya).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 1.21 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018):** *bhavapratyayo'vadhirdevanārakāṇām* — "Clairvoyance based on birth is possessed by the celestial and the infernal beings." Exact Sanskrit confirmed.

**Signal 2 — Pūjyapāda, Sarvārthasiddhi on TS 1.21:** Clairvoyance arises in gods and hell-beings "not because of any special merit such as observance of vows or restraint, but by reason of their state of existence." Only right-believing beings possess genuine avadhi; wrong-believers have vitiated (erroneous) clairvoyance.

**Signal 3 — TS 1.12 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018):** *pratyakṣamanyat* — "The remaining [three knowledge types] are direct." Pūjyapāda: direct knowledge "manifests only in the soul on the destruction-cum-subsidence or destruction of karmas, without the help of the senses." Avadhi, manah-paryaya, and kevala are the three pratyakṣa types.

**Signal 4 — Hemacandra, Triṣaṣṭiśalākāpuruṣacaritra (11th c.):** avadhi-jñāna is innate for gods and hell-inhabitants; for others it arises through six modes characterised by destruction and suppression of karma-matter.

**Signal 5 — Nandī Sūtra (Jain canonical text):** Lists avadhi as one of five knowledge types, consistent with TS 1.9 classification.

All signals converge. Confidence medium: TS 1.21 and 1.12 fetched directly; Tatia 1994 chapter text not independently confirmed.

## Two primary types

| type | cause | who possesses it |
|---|---|---|
| **bhava-pratyaya** (birth-based) | mode of existence as soul | gods (deva) and hell-beings (nāraki) — innate, not earned |
| **guṇa-pratyaya** (merit-based) | destruction/suppression of āvaraṇīya karma | humans and animals — acquired through ascetic practice |

## Six varieties of guṇa-pratyaya avadhi (TS 1.23)

| variety | characteristic |
|---|---|
| anānugāmika | extinguishes when the knower changes location |
| ānugāmika | persists despite change of location |
| hīyamānaka | sphere of knowledge diminishes or disappears |
| vardhamānaka | sphere expands progressively |
| anavasthita | fluctuating / intermittent |
| avasthita | constant and unwavering |

## Three scope levels (extent-based)

- **deśāvadhi** — limited spatial range (partial clairvoyance)
- **paramāvadhi** — unrestricted temporal scope (supreme clairvoyance)
- **sarvāvadhi** — total clairvoyance; available only to Tīrthaṅkaras

## Scope constraint

Avadhi apprehends only pudgala (physical matter). Non-material dravyas — jīva, dharma, adharma, ākāśa, kāla — are outside its reach. This is what distinguishes it from kevala-jñāna, which knows all substances and all modes.
