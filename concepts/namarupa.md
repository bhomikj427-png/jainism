## Gloss / Divergence map

Nāmarūpa ("name-and-form") is **link 4 in the 12-nidāna chain of pratītyasamutpāda** — the psycho-physical organism viewed as a conditioned process. It arises in dependence on vijñāna (consciousness, link 3) and itself conditions the ṣaḍāyatana (six sense bases, link 5).

**Position in the chain:**
... → vijñāna (link 3) → **nāmarūpa (link 4)** → ṣaḍāyatana (link 5) → ...

**Components:**

| component | Sanskrit/Pali | content |
|---|---|---|
| Nāma ("name"/mental) | nāma | feeling (*vedanā*), perception (*saṃjñā*), volition (*cetanā*), contact (*sparśa*), attention (*manasikāra*) — the four formless khandhas minus vijñāna |
| Rūpa ("form"/physical) | rūpa | the four material elements (earth/water/fire/wind) and derived material forms |

Together nāma + rūpa constitute the **psycho-physical organism** — the entire "person" seen as a conditioned, interdependent process. This is what vijñāna "descends into" at conception to produce a new life-stream.

**The loop — Mahānidānasutta (DN 15):** In this sutta, the Buddha describes a mutual conditionality: vijñāna conditions nāmarūpa, AND nāmarūpa conditions vijñāna. They are not one causing the other unidirectionally but mutually co-arising. This "loop" within the 12-link chain is philosophically significant: consciousness and the psycho-physical organism are interdependent, like "two sheaves of reeds leaning against each other." Remove one and the other falls. (Source: Wikipedia "Namarūpa"; encyclopediaofbuddhism.org.)

**Connection to the five khandhas:** Nāmarūpa is a causal-process analysis of what the five-khandha analysis describes structurally. Rūpa (physical) = the first khandha; nāma (mental) = vedanā + saṃjñā + saṃskāra + vijñāna (khandhas 2–5, minus vijñāna which is link 3). The two analyses describe the same apparent "person" from different angles.

**Nāmarūpa and anattā:** The analysis dissolves the apparent unity of "person" into conditioned sub-components. There is no sixth element (a self) behind the nāma and rūpa — the "person" IS the nāmarūpa process, conditioned by vijñāna and conditions in turn.

## Nāmarūpa vs. Jain jīva + pudgala

Jainism analyses the same apparent "person" as a jīva (soul) + a pudgala body:
- Jain jīva: a real, enduring individual substance that changes modes (*paryāya*) but persists as dravya
- Buddhist nāmarūpa: a conditioned process arising from vijñāna and prior conditions; no substrate; no enduring self

The Buddhist nāmarūpa analysis is anti-essentialist (consistent with anattā/śūnyatā). The Jain analysis posits a genuine substance (jīva) as the substrate of the mental life. Nāma ≠ jīva: nāma is the mental component of a conditioned process; jīva is an eternal individual substance.
