## Gloss / Divergence map

Saṃvara is **stoppage of karmic influx** — the deliberate blocking of āsrava, achieved through ethical and meditative practice. It is the sixth tattva and the soteriological turning point: the moment the soul stops accumulating new karma while nirjarā erodes what has already bonded.

**TS definition** (reported from search-result aggregation; verse text not fetched from critical edition this run): *āsravanirodhah saṃvaraḥ* — "The obstruction of influx (āsrava) is stoppage (saṃvara)."

Saṃvara is achieved through a comprehensive set of practices that TS Chapter 9 enumerates. These target both *yoga* (the activity-channel that opens the soul to influx) and *kaṣāya* (the passions that make karma stick):

| practice category | Sanskrit | count | targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restraints | gupti | 3 | control of mind, speech, body — directly counters yoga |
| Carefulness | samiti | 5 | mindful walking, speech, eating, handling objects, waste disposal |
| Virtues | dharma | 10 | forbearance, modesty, purity, truthfulness, restraint, austerity, renunciation, non-attachment, humility, continence |
| Contemplations | bhāvanā | 12 | reflective meditations on impermanence, helplessness, etc. |
| Endurance of hardships | parīṣahajaya | 22 | equanimity toward hunger, thirst, cold, heat, mosquitoes, etc. |
| Conduct | cāritra | several | the full ethical code (samyak-cāritra of tri-ratna) |

(Source: search-result aggregation from jainworld.com and dharmapedia; verse text not independently verified from critical edition.)

**Structural logic:** The three guptis (restraints of mind/speech/body) are the direct inverse of the three yogas that define āsrava (TS 6.2). Restraining yoga closes the gate that āsrava had opened.

**Position in the soteriology:**
āsrava → bandha → **saṃvara** (no new karma) → nirjarā (shed old karma) → mokṣa

Saṃvara does not undo existing bondage (that is nirjarā's task) — it only stops accumulation. Both must operate together for liberation.
