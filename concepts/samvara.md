## Gloss / Divergence map

Saṃvara is **stoppage of karmic influx** — the blocking of āsrava, the soteriological turning point at which the soul ceases accumulating new karma. It is the fifth of the seven tattvas (TS 1.4).

**TS 9.1 Sanskrit:** *āstravanirodhaḥ saṃvaraḥ* — "The obstruction of influx is stoppage." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly from WisdomLib.)

**Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (via Vijay K. Jain 2018) identifies **two forms of saṃvara**:
- **Bhāva-saṃvara** (psychic/mental stoppage): the cessation of the internal activities and dispositions that generate transmigration — i.e., stopping the kaṣāya and yoga at the level of intention and awareness
- **Dravya-saṃvara** (material stoppage): the actual interruption of the inflow of karma-matter into the soul — the physical-level consequence of bhāva-saṃvara

The commentary further specifies which karmas cease binding at each of the **fourteen guṇasthānas** (stages of spiritual development) as saṃvara progressively deepens. Full karmic stoppage is only achieved at guṇasthāna 13 (immediately before liberation).

**The practices of saṃvara** — TS 9.2–9.5 and subsequent verses enumerate a comprehensive set:

| practice category | Sanskrit | count | what it counters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Restraints | gupti | 3 | mind/speech/body activity (*yoga*) — the direct inverse of āsrava's three-yoga channel |
| Carefulness | samiti | 5 | mindful walking, speech, eating, handling objects, waste disposal |
| Virtues | dharma | 10 | forbearance, modesty, purity, truthfulness, restraint, austerity, renunciation, non-attachment, humility, continence |
| Contemplations | bhāvanā | 12 | reflective meditations (on impermanence, helplessness, saṃsāra, etc.) |
| Endurance of hardships | parīṣahajaya | 22 | equanimity toward hunger, thirst, cold, heat, insects, etc. |
| Conduct | cāritra | — | the full ethical code (*samyak-cāritra*) |

(Source: TS 9.2–9.5 via JainSquare Chapter 9 translation; verse-for-verse correspondence confirmed against search-result aggregation.)

**Structural logic:** The three *guptis* (restraints of mind/speech/body) are the direct inverse of the three *yogas* that constitute āsrava (TS 6.2: *yoga* = the activity-channel). Restraining yoga closes the gate āsrava opened. The kaṣāya-targeting practices (bhāvanā, parīṣahajaya) address the adhesive that makes karma stick — removing that adhesive at root.

**Position in the soteriology:**
āsrava → bandha → **saṃvara** (no new karma) → nirjarā (shed old karma) → mokṣa

Saṃvara does not undo existing bondage (nirjarā does that) — it only stops accumulation. Both must operate together.
