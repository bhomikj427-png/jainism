## Gloss / Divergence map

**Svarbhānu** ("sun-splendor" — *svar* "heaven/sun-light" + *bhānu* "radiance," per Monier-Williams-derived etymology, with the commentarial gloss reading it as the being who eclipses that splendor) names the asura behind both [[rahu|Rāhu]] and [[ketu|Ketu]] — but the sources reveal **two distinct textual layers**, not one continuous story, structurally the same kind of stratification already logged for [[indra|Indra]] and [[parashurama|Paraśurāma]]/[[kalki|Kalki]]:

| layer | content | source |
|---|---|---|
| **Ṛgvedic (older)** | Svarbhānu the asura pierces **Sūrya** "through and through with darkness" by *māyā*, plunging creatures into confusion; **Indra** shatters his magic; the sage **Atri**, through tapas and a sacred formula, locates and restores the hidden sun. **No churning, no amṛta theft, no beheading, no Rāhu/Ketu split appear in this hymn at all** | Ṛgveda 5.40.5–9 (Maṇḍala V, a Family Book — among the oldest strata of the text) |
| **epic/Puranic (later)** | Svarbhānu disguises himself among the devas during the [[samudra-manthana|churning]], drinks stolen amṛta, is exposed by Sūrya and Candra, and is beheaded by Viṣṇu's discus — head becomes Rāhu, body becomes Ketu (fully narrated in `rahu.md`/`ketu.md`, not repeated here) | Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva §XVIII |

The two layers are linked by **name-continuity, not plot-continuity** — a self-contained Vedic eclipse-etiology myth and a wholly separate Puranic amṛta-theft-and-beheading myth share only the name Svarbhānu. This is a genuinely converged finding (independent sources agree the layers are distinct), but it sits inside an overall `contested` file because of the identity question below.

**CONTESTED — is Svarbhānu simply Rāhu under an older name, or a distinct prior whole that Rāhu/Ketu are parts of?** The sources do not agree:

| reading | what it commits you to | source |
|---|---|---|
| **outright identity** | Svarbhānu = Rāhu, the same being under two names across two eras — the Vedic demon *is* the Puranic Rāhu, full stop | Sāyaṇa's commentary on RV 5.40.5; several encyclopedic/devotional sources that gloss Rāhu as "also known as Svarbhānu" |
| **whole-then-split** | Svarbhānu is the **pre-beheading whole being**; Rāhu and Ketu are its two post-beheading *parts* (head and body), so Svarbhānu is not identical to either — "Svarbhānu became both entities" | Wikipedia, "Svarbhānu" |

Because casual sources flatten Svarbhānu and Rāhu into one name while careful ones insist on distinguishing whole-being from head-only-part, this file draws the sanctioned two-type edge pair (§5) toward Rāhu: real shared vocabulary/identity claim, but not asserted as flat equivalence.

**Genealogy — also unsettled, not smoothed over.** Sāyaṇa's own commentary offers two alternative parentages in the same breath: son of **Kaśyapa and Dānu**, or son of **Vipracitti and Siṃhikā** (the more common Puranic default, also given in secondary compilations as "eldest of fourteen sons of Vipracitti and Siṃhikā"). A separate Matsya/Saura Purāṇa lineage instead makes him son of Bāṇāsura, son of [[bali|Bali]] — an unrelated third line. One consistent thread across compiled Puranic genealogies: Svarbhānu is father of **Prabhā/Suprabhā**, who marries Āyus and becomes mother of Nahuṣa, tying the asura line into the lunar (Candra) dynasty — recorded as the one stable genealogical fact, distinct from the contested parentage-of-Svarbhānu-himself question. (Note: Purāṇic dictionaries also list unrelated *namesakes* — a Svarbhānu son of Kṛṣṇa and Satyabhāmā, another linked to Soma/Budha — these are distinct figures sharing a name, not variants of this one, and are not conflated here.)

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Ṛgveda 5.40.5–9** (Griffith translation, Wikisource) — primary-text anchor for the independent Vedic layer: Svarbhānu, Sūrya, Indra, Atri, no churning content whatsoever.
2. **Sāyaṇa's commentary on RV 5.40.5** (via Wilson translation, wisdomlib.org) — the commentarial anchor (§4 signal 2) that both supplies the genealogy alternatives and asserts the outright-identity reading with Rāhu — a real commentarial position, not a modern gloss.
3. **Mahābhārata, Ādi Parva §XVIII** — already the anchor text for `rahu.md`/`ketu.md`; independently confirms the Puranic beheading layer is a separate narrative from the Vedic one (no cross-reference to RV 5.40 appears in the epic account).
4. **Wikipedia, "Svarbhānu"** — the whole-then-split, non-identity framing, and the antiquity claim (Svarbhānu appears "twice in the Family Books of the Rigveda," among the oldest textual strata) — this dating claim rests on the encyclopedic source rather than a footnoted critical edition, held as a slightly weaker sub-claim within an otherwise medium-confidence file.

Two independently attested, genuinely distinct textual layers (Vedic eclipse-myth vs. Puranic churning-beheading myth) sharing one name → the layering itself is **converged**. But the identity-vs-part-relationship between Svarbhānu and Rāhu is a live commentarial/scholarly split → **contested** overall, confidence **medium**: the core two-layer finding is well-attested, but genealogy and the identity question are genuinely unresolved rather than thinly sourced.
