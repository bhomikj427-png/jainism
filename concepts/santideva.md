## Gloss

**Śāntideva** (~685–763 CE) was an Indian Buddhist monk and Mādhyamika philosopher active at Nālandā monastery in North India. He is the author of the *Bodhicaryāvatāra* ("Way of the Bodhisattva"), the most widely read Mahāyāna ethical-philosophical text in the Tibetan tradition, and the *Śikṣāsamuccaya* ("Compendium of Training"). A third work, the *Sūtrasamuccaya*, is lost. He was a follower of Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka.

**The *Bodhicaryāvatāra*** (BCA): Ten chapters, approximately 1,000 verses in Sanskrit. Presents the progressive development of **bodhicitta** — the awakening-mind dedicated to liberating all sentient beings — through the six pāramitās (perfections): generosity, ethics, patience, effort, meditation, wisdom. Chapter 6 (patience, *kṣānti*) and Chapter 9 (wisdom, *prajñā*) are philosophically the most influential. Chapter 9 presents a Madhyamaka argument for śūnyatā that became the standard exposition in Tibetan scholasticism. Chapter 6's analysis of anger (no self = no one to be angry at) grounds Śāntideva's ethics in metaphysics.

**Biography**: The traditional life-narrative — son of a king who renounced the throne after visions of Mañjuśrī, outwardly appearing indolent at Nālandā ("eating, sleeping, using the toilet"), then reciting the BCA extemporaneously to embarrass his critics — is classified by scholars as pious legend (Encyclopedia of Buddhism). The historical core: Nālandā scholar, Madhyamaka tradition, 8th century.

**108 commentaries** were reportedly written on the BCA in India alone (Encyclopaedia of Buddhism); the text remains a living commentary object in Tibetan Buddhism (Paṭrul Rinpoche's *Words of My Perfect Teacher*; the Dalai Lama's repeated public teachings on it).
