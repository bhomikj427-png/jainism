## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Mīmāṃsāsūtra** (Pūrva Mīmāṃsā Sūtra) of **Jaimini** (~300–200 BCE; terminus debated) is the founding text of the **Pūrva Mīmāṃsā** darśana, the school of Vedic exegesis (*mīmāṃsā* = "reflection/investigation"). It is by far the largest of the sūtra-texts — ~2700 sūtras in **twelve adhyāyas** subdivided into sixty pādas — and is the primary-text anchor behind [[mimamsa-pramana]]. The principal and only complete commentary is **Śabara's Śābarabhāṣya**, on which the two great sub-schools of **Kumārila Bhaṭṭa** and **Prabhākara Miśra** built.

**Dharma defined by injunction.** The work opens (MS 1.1.1) with *athāto dharma-jijñāsā* — "now, therefore, the inquiry into dharma." Its master-definition is **MS 1.1.2: *codanā-lakṣaṇo 'rtho dharmaḥ*** — "dharma is that purpose/object marked out by Vedic injunction (*codanā*)." Dharma is thus *ritual duty*, the karma-kāṇḍa of action commanded by the Veda; crucially it **cannot be known by perception** — only the Vedic injunction reveals it, so Vedic authority is supreme on what one ought to do.

**Apauruṣeyatva — the authorless, eternal Veda.** Jaimini holds the Veda to be **apauruṣeya**: uncreated, eternal, and authorless — "there is no divine revealer; the Veda itself is authoritative." This pairs with the doctrine of the **eternality of sound/word (śabda-nityatva)**: words and their meaning-relation are permanent, not conventional. (The intrinsic self-validity of cognition, *svataḥ-prāmāṇya*, and the resulting pramāṇa-theory are elaborated in [[mimamsa-pramana]].) The system also develops **apūrva** — the unseen potency by which a sacrifice performed now produces its result later. Early/Jaimini Mīmāṃsā is overwhelmingly ritual-and-dharma-focused rather than liberation-focused (whether mokṣa is even attainable is treated as a later question).

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Purva Mimamsa Sutras"): Jaimini; ~300–200 BCE; foundational text of Pūrva Mīmāṃsā; ~2700 sūtras in twelve adhyāyas / sixty pādas; MS 1.1.2 *"Codanā-lakṣaṇo 'rtho dharmaḥ"* quoted; dharma not knowable by perception; apauruṣeya (Veda eternal/uncreated); eternality of sound; Śābara-bhāṣya as principal commentary; Kumārila + Prabhākara sub-schools; the ritual/karma orientation. **Signal 2** (search aggregation — Dharmawiki, indiaphilosophy.wordpress, indianphilosophy.50webs.com): independently gives the twelve adhyāyas / sixty pādas; the same MS 1.1.2 definition of dharma via codanā; the apūrva doctrine and the catalogue of sacrifices; apauruṣeyatva ("the Veda was practically the only God for Jaimini… no divine revealer"); Śābara-bhāṣya as the only extant complete commentary; Kumārila and Prabhākara.

The two are independent (an encyclopedia and a cluster of darśana-reference sites) and agree on author, date, structure, the MS 1.1.2 dharma-definition (verbatim in both), apauruṣeyatva, the commentary, and the sub-schools. Medium, not high: the Mīmāṃsāsūtra Sanskrit was **not fetched from a critical edition** — only MS 1.1.2 is given verbatim by the sources, while MS 1.1.1 is paraphrased ("opens with the inquiry into dharma"); Britannica was unreachable (HTTP 403). The doctrinal content here overlaps with the already-written [[mimamsa-pramana]].
