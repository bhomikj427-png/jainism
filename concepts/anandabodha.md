## Gloss / Divergence map

**Ānandabodha** (also **Ānandabodha Yati**, **Ānandabodha Bhaṭṭāraka / Bhaṭṭārakācārya**), 11th–12th c., is the **Advaita dialectician whose arguments the later school ran on**. Dasgupta's verdict is the whole of his importance in one sentence: **"Most of Ānandabodha's arguments were borrowed by the later writers of the Vedānta school."**

He closes the second of the two "(unwritten)" rows `citsukha.md` has carried since Batch 44 — Citsukha commented on his *Nyāyamakaranda*, as did Śrīharṣa's own commentator Anubhūtisvarūpa Ācārya.

This node is **converged** — a change of register in a batch otherwise made of disputes. What he thought, what he wrote and what he was for are agreed; the divergences are bibliographic, and none of them changes the picture.

### The two arguments that were borrowed

**1. Against difference.** Dasgupta gives it compactly:

> "Difference cannot be perceived by sense-perception, since difference cannot be perceived without perceiving both the object and all else from which it differs."

The structure is worth marking because [[sriharsa|Śrīharṣa]]'s version — that no awareness can present one object as distinct from another *without also presenting them as non-distinct* — is visibly the **same move, tightened**. Ānandabodha argues that perceiving difference would require perceiving the whole field it is difference *from*; Śrīharṣa argues that the very act of relating two things concedes their relation. `sriharsa.md` records that the destructive method "visibly points somewhere" exactly here — and this file supplies the generation it points back to.

**2. Avidyā as the third thing.**

> "The cause of world-appearance cannot be either real or unreal … it must have for its cause something which is neither real nor unreal … the neither-real-nor-unreal entity is *avidyā*."

This is the **anirvacanīya** ("indefinable") characterisation of avidyā — and it is precisely the doctrine [[ramanuja|Rāmānuja]]'s *saptavidhā anupapatti* was built to destroy, one of whose members (per `ramanuja.md`'s unverified list) is that *defining it as indefinable is self-contradictory*. **The corpus now holds both ends of that quarrel**: the Advaitin's positive statement of the doctrine and the Viśiṣṭādvaitin's catalogue of its untenabilities, in nodes written a day apart.

### The divergences, all bibliographic

| question | reading A | reading B |
|---|---|---|
| **how many works?** | **three** — *Nyāyamakaranda*, *Nyāyadīpāvalī*, *Pramāṇamālā* (Dasgupta, who "does not mention a fourth") | **four**, adding the *Nyāyadīpikā* (Hindupedia and the aggregators) |
| **when?** | "probably in the eleventh or the twelfth century" (Dasgupta) | **A.D. 1200** (Mr. Tripathi, in Dasgupta's own footnote); "12th century" (Wisdomlib) |
| **whose pupil?** | disciple of **Vimuktātman** (10th c.) — the aggregators | **Dasgupta does not say so.** He names only Anubhūtisvarūpa Ācārya (late 13th c.), teacher of Ānandajñāna, as a *commentator* on Ānandabodha's works |

⚠ **The Vimuktātman discipleship is single-sourced and is not asserted here.** It comes from web aggregation; the one scholarly history consulted, which discusses his sources and his critics in detail, does not mention it. Recorded as a lead.

⚠ **One phrase in the aggregators is loose, and this file will not adopt it.** They describe his works as "dialectical works on Advaita-Vedanta written in the **Navya-nyāya style**." This file may not say Ānandabodha wrote in Navya-Nyāya: the aggregators give no argument, and the phrase is most likely reaching for "in a technical dialectical idiom." Compare `sriharsa.md`, where SEP places the Naiyāyika answers to Śrīharṣa **after** Gaṅgeśa.

⚠⚠ **AMENDED IN BATCH 46 — the original ground for rejecting that phrase does not survive.** This file previously called the phrase *anachronistic*, on the ground that "Navya-Nyāya begins with Gaṅgeśa in the 14th century — two to three centuries after Ānandabodha." Writing [[navya-nyaya|`navya-nyaya.md`]] showed that **the school has three rival starting points**, not one: with Gaṅgeśa's text (c. 1325); with **Udayana, c. 1025–1100** — "Prācīnanyāya ended with him. At the same time, Navyanyāya started with him" (Deb, *The Navya-Nyāya Theory of Pakṣatā*); or with the **Nyāya–Vaiśeṣika merger "at some point in the 11th or 12th century"** (Ganeri). On the latter two — which converge on the eleventh century from independent criteria — an 11th–12th-c. Advaitin writing technical dialectic is a **contemporary of Navya-Nyāya's formation**, not two centuries early. **The charge of anachronism was itself resting on an unexamined periodisation.** The phrase is still declined, but now for the right reason — it is unargued, not impossible.

### The witness-value: two texts he proves existed

Ānandabodha turns out to be, like several figures this batch has produced, **useful less for what he says than for what he shows was in circulation**:

- He **refers to Vācaspati's *Tattvasamīkṣā***. `vacaspati-mishra.md` records Vācaspati's commentary on the *Brahmasiddhi* as the **unpublished** *Brahmatattva-samīkṣā*. An 11th–12th-c. Advaitin citing it is a witness that the work existed and was read. ⚠ That the two titles name one work is the **natural reading and not a sourced identification**; recorded as such.
- He **criticises [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]]'s view of the self as pure bliss — without naming him.** An unnamed target identified by a modern historian is a *reconstruction*, and this file reports it as Dasgupta's, not as a fact about the text. Its chronological consequence, if right, is that Sarvajñātman precedes him.

And in the other direction: **[[vyasatirtha|Vyāsatīrtha]] "collected standard Vedānta arguments from Ānandabodha and [[prakasatman|Prakāśātman]] for refutation."** That is the *Nyāyāmṛta*'s target list, named — and it means the great Dvaita–Advaita debate of the 15th–16th centuries was conducted against **this** man's arguments, four centuries after he made them. It is the single best measure of Dasgupta's "most of his arguments were borrowed."

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — S. N. Dasgupta, *A History of Indian Philosophy*, vol. 2, "Ānandabodha Yati,"** via Wisdomlib. The load-bearing source and the only scholarly one: the three works, the 11th–12th c. dating with Tripathi's 1200 in a footnote, the difference-argument and the avidyā-argument in Dasgupta's own words, the *Tattvasamīkṣā* reference, the unnamed criticism of Sarvajñātman, Citsukha and Anubhūtisvarūpa as commentators, the borrowing verdict, and the Vyāsatīrtha target-list. **Signal 2 — Wisdomlib's own definition page** (aggregating Hindupedia and citing T. M. P. Mahadevan's *Preceptors of Advaita*, Dasgupta, and P. K. Gode): four works, "12th century," the Devanagari आनन्दबोध. **Signal 3 — search-level aggregation** (Hindupedia, advaita-vedanta.org): the Vimuktātman discipleship, the "Navya-nyāya style" phrase, and "the first and foremost task of the Advaitin is to refute difference and establish identity, which Ānandabodha does with consummate skill." **Signal 4 — corpus-internal**: `citsukha.md`'s standing unwritten row and its Hindupedia five-work list, which independently names a commentary on the *Nyāyamakaranda*; and `sriharsa.md`, whose difference-argument this file's is the ancestor of.

**Independence.** Signal 1 is a 1932 scholarly history; signals 2 and 3 descend from a common encyclopedia layer and agree with each other for that reason, not from confirmation. Where they conflict with signal 1 — the fourth work, the discipleship, the "Navya-nyāya" description — **signal 1 wins or the claim is dropped.** Signal 4 is a genuine internal check written two batches earlier.

**Why converged.** Every substantive question — school, period, method, the two arguments, the transmission to Citsukha and Śrīharṣa and the reception by Vyāsatīrtha — is agreed, and the three divergences are bibliographic and explicitly bounded. **Why medium, not high.** **No work of Ānandabodha's was read**: both arguments are quoted from Dasgupta's paraphrase, not from the *Nyāyamakaranda*, an edition of which is available (Internet Archive) and was not opened; the school-internal claims rest on one historian; and the two most interesting items — the *Tattvasamīkṣā* identification and the unnamed Sarvajñātman target — are inferences that this file marks as inferences.
