## Gloss / Divergence map

**Utpaladeva** is the Kashmiri Śaiva philosopher who turned his teacher's visionary monism into an **argued philosophical system**, and gave it the name it is known by. His *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* (IPK) is "regarded as the definitive formulation of the school's philosophy," and the concept it is named for — ***pratyabhijñā***, recognition — **is not found in Somānanda's *Śivadṛṣṭi*** at all. The doctrine is treated in [[pratyabhijna]]; this node is the **author, the corpus, and the transmission**.

### The lineage, and a 25-year disagreement about it

Three generations, universally agreed in order and disputed in date:

| | Wikipedia | the academic literature (Torella, Ratié, and the studies indexed with them) |
|---|---|---|
| **Somānanda** (*Śivadṛṣṭi*) | 875–925 CE | c. 900–950 CE |
| **Utpaladeva** (IPK) | **c. 900–950 CE** | **c. 925–975 CE** |
| Lakṣmaṇagupta → **Abhinavagupta** (IPK *Vimarśinī*, *Vivṛtivimarśinī*, *Tantrāloka*) | (later exegete) | c. 975–1025 CE |

The corpus already carries **c. 925–975** in [[pratyabhijna]] from a different source-set, so the shift is recorded rather than resolved. The *sequence* — Somānanda taught Utpaladeva; Lakṣmaṇagupta taught Abhinavagupta; **Abhinavagupta is Utpaladeva's grand-disciple** — is not in dispute anywhere.

The doctrinally significant fact is that **Utpaladeva "deviates in style and substance from his teacher's"** articulation. Somānanda's *Śivadṛṣṭi* is a Śaiva vision argued against Bhartṛhari's grammarian absolutism and against [[dharmakirti|Dharmakīrti]]; Utpaladeva supplies the *epistemological* argument — and coins its central term. This is the reverse of the usual Indian pattern, where the commentator elaborates the founder: here **the second man supplies the thesis.**

### His works

| work | what it is |
|---|---|
| ***Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā*** (IPK) | the root verses — the school's definitive statement |
| ***Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā-vṛtti*** | his own **short** auto-commentary; survives |
| ***Īśvarapratyabhijñā-vivṛti*** (*Ṭīkā*) | his own **long** auto-commentary — see below; **almost entirely lost** |
| *Siddhitrayī* — *Īśvarasiddhi*, *Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi*, *Sambandhasiddhi* | three short demonstrations: of the Lord, of the **non-inert knower**, and of **relation** |
| *Śivadṛṣṭi-vṛtti* | commentary on his own teacher's work |
| ***Śivastotrāvalī*** | Śaiva devotional hymns — the same author in the **bhakti** register, which the corpus notes because arguing for identity with Śiva and singing to Śiva are here one career, not two |

### The lost *Vivṛti* — a transmission story worth the space

The IPK's most important commentary is **Utpaladeva's own** long one, and it is **the thing that did not survive**. What is known is specific enough to be a case study in how a philosophical text degrades:

- Only "a small part of it has been preserved in a **single, very incomplete manuscript**," edited and translated by **Raffaele Torella**.
- Additional fragments have since been recovered, "most notably… **the lengthiest *Vivṛti* fragment known to date in the margins of a manuscript containing Abhinavagupta's *Vivṛtivimarśinī***" — i.e. the root text survives as marginalia inside its own commentary's commentary.
- The decay is **datable**: "it is certain that the *Vivṛti* was already fragmentary when **Śaṅkarakaṇṭha** made marginal annotations on the *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī* in the **seventeenth century**."

Two consequences the corpus should hold onto. First, **Abhinavagupta's dominance over Utpaladeva is partly an artefact of survival**, not only of talent — the sources note his works "eventually overshadowed" Utpaladeva's own, and the overshadowing is literal: what we read of Utpaladeva's self-interpretation, we read *through* Abhinavagupta's manuscripts. Second, this is the same hazard already recorded for [[vindhyavasin]], [[carvaka]] and the Chaldean Oracles — **a doctrine reaching us only through the channel of a more successful successor or an opponent** — but in its mildest and best-documented form, since here the channel is a devoted follower and the recovery work is ongoing and published.

### Dharmakīrti: the adversary he is made of

The single most important thing about Utpaladeva's method is that **his Buddhist opponent is also his toolkit**. Dharmakīrti's philosophy "pervasively influenced Utpaladeva and, through him, his grand-disciple Abhinavagupta," and Torella's assessment is that the engagement "resulted in… absorption of their doctrines and terminology" — Utpaladeva borrows [[apoha]] and *anupalabdhi* outright.

The manoeuvre is precise, and worth stating exactly because it is easy to garble:

> The Pratyabhijñā philosophers **used Dharmakīrti's own arguments for [[vijnaptimatrata|Vijñānavāda]] against the Sautrāntikas' inference** — and then **used that same discussion to argue that their idealism is superior to the Vijñānavādins' own.**

So the Buddhist idealist is first enlisted against the Buddhist realist, and then defeated by the position he was enlisted to defend. What the Śaiva has that the Buddhist idealist lacks is a **persisting, self-aware, free subject**: consciousness that is *ajaḍa* (non-inert) and possessed of ***svātantrya*** (absolute freedom). Momentary consciousness-events cannot **recognise**, because recognition just is the identification of a present cognition with a past one by one subject. That is the IPK's core argument, and it is aimed at exactly the doctrine of momentariness Dharmakīrti defends.

The school's most original extension of *svātantrya* is its **account of our awareness of other minds**: on the Pratyabhijñā analysis, awareness of another person consists in **sensing that other's freedom** — a route to intersubjectivity through agency rather than through inference from behaviour.

### *Ābhāsavāda* and the Sāṃkhya borrowing

Utpaladeva's positive ontology is ***ābhāsa*** — the objects of the world are *manifestations* of Śiva's consciousness, not products of a separate matter. The corpus notes two things about this. (1) The **word** is the same one Advaita uses for its third theory of the jīva (see [[prakasatman]], [[vidyaranya]]) and the doctrines are **not the same**: for the Śaiva the *ābhāsa* is Śiva's **real self-expression**, for the Advaitin an *ābhāsa* is a **partial appearance that lacks the properties of the real**. Same term, opposite verdict on the world's status. (2) Pratyabhijñā gives a **Śaiva reading of [[satkaryavada|satkāryavāda]]**, taking over the Sāṃkhya notion of *abhivyakti* (manifestation) and transforming it — the effect pre-exists in the cause, but the "cause" is free consciousness rather than [[prakriti-samkhya|prakṛti]], so manifestation becomes an act of will rather than a mechanical unfolding.

He also draws on **Bhartṛhari** for the linguistic constitution of reality, and reads scripture universalistically — the sources note a range "from Vaiṣṇava to Buddhist" brought under one interpretation.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Utpaladeva"** (fetched): c. 900–950; student of **Somānanda (875–925)**; the works list including the *Siddhitrayī* trilogy by name, the two auto-commentaries, and the *Śivastotrāvalī*; recognition as "merely the triggering… of an act of identification, which does not reveal anything new"; **Torella's** assessment of the Dharmakīrti absorption and the borrowing of *anupalabdhi* and *apoha*; *ābhāsa*; Bhartṛhari; the universalistic scriptural range; and that Abhinavagupta's works "eventually overshadowed his own."
2. **Peer-reviewed academic literature, indexed via search** — Isabelle Ratié, "Utpaladeva's Lost *Vivṛti* on the *Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā*," *Journal of Indian Philosophy* (Springer), and "In Search of Utpaladeva's Lost *Vivṛti*…: A Report on the Latest Discoveries," same journal: the *Vivṛti*'s near-total loss; **Torella's** edition of the single incomplete manuscript; the longest fragment recovered **in the margins of an Abhinavagupta *Vivṛtivimarśinī* manuscript**; **Śaṅkarakaṇṭha's 17th-century** annotations proving the text was already fragmentary then. This is the only **journal-grade** signal in this node and it carries the transmission section outright.
3. **Search aggregation** (Ratié, "The Pratyabhijñā Śaiva Critique of Dharmakīrti on the Body"; "Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta on the Freedom of Consciousness"; "A Śaiva interpretation of the *satkāryavāda*: the Sāṃkhya notion of *abhivyakti* and its transformation in the Pratyabhijñā treatise"; "Somānanda's *Śivadṛṣṭi* as an Argument against Dharmakīrti"; "Otherness in the Pratyabhijñā Philosophy"): the **c. 925–975 / 900–950 / 975–1025** three-generation dating; that *pratyabhijñā* **does not appear in the *Śivadṛṣṭi***; that Utpaladeva "deviates in style and substance" from Somānanda; the Vijñānavāda-against-Sautrāntika-then-against-Vijñānavāda manoeuvre in its own words; *svātantrya* as the essence of consciousness and as the medium of our awareness of others; and the *satkāryavāda*/*abhivyakti* transformation.
4. **Lexicon/morphology** (§4 signal 3, applied to the doctrine's key word rather than the name): *prati-* + *abhi-* + √*jñā*, "to know again" — already recorded in [[pratyabhijna]] and consistent with the "does not reveal anything new" gloss.
5. **Corpus cross-check** ([[pratyabhijna]], [[spanda]], [[dharmakirti]], [[apoha]], [[vijnaptimatrata]]): [[pratyabhijna]] independently carries **c. 925–975**, the anti-*kṣaṇavāda* argument, *vimarśa*, and the Advaita contrast — written from a different source-set and agreeing on every doctrinal point. The disagreement is confined to dates.

**CONTESTED** on dating only (a consistent ~25-year shift across the whole lineage, i.e. one dating scheme against another rather than a dispute about a person); **converged** on the lineage order, the works, the Dharmakīrti relation, and the *Vivṛti*'s loss. Confidence **medium**: signal 2 is genuinely strong — peer-reviewed, and about manuscripts rather than doctrine — but it was reached **through search summaries, not by reading the articles**, and none of Utpaladeva's texts was consulted in Torella's edition. Confidence would go to high on a reading of Torella's *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā of Utpaladeva with the Author's Vṛtti* (Motilal Banarsidass), which was identified and not read.
