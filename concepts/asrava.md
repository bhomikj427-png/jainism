## Gloss / Divergence map

Āsrava is **karmic influx** — the process by which fine karma particles (the sūkṣma pudgala of karma.md) flow toward and into the soul. It is the third of the seven tattvas (fundamental categories) and the beginning of the bondage problem.

**TS 6.2 Sanskrit:** *sa āsravaḥ* — "The activity of the body, the speech, and the mind — the *yoga* — is the influx (āsrava)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass.)

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* (via Vijay K. Jain 2018): "Just as the gate from which water flows into a lake is called *āsrava*, similarly the activity or *yoga*, which is the conduit for the flow of karmic matter into the soul, is called the influx."

**Yoga here means** the threefold activity of mind (*manas*), speech (*vāc*), and body (*kāya*) — any vibration or exertion of the soul's capacities. This is *not* the Pātañjala yoga of meditation postures; in Jain usage, *yoga* = "vibration/activity" that opens the soul-space to incoming karma.

**Two kinds of āsrava** (from commentarial tradition; confirmed by search-result aggregation):

| kind | Sanskrit | condition | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gross/binding | sāmparāyika | yoga accompanied by kaṣāya (passions — anger/pride/deceit/greed) | genuine bandha (bondage); karma sticks and extends saṃsāra |
| Subtle/non-binding | īryāpatha | yoga without kaṣāya (only in highly restrained practitioners) | karma touches the soul momentarily but does not stick; no lasting bondage |

The key mechanism: **yoga opens the channel; kaṣāya acts as the adhesive.** Without passion, karma particles flow through but do not bind — like water on a lotus leaf. With passion, they adhere. This is why ethical restraint (*saṃvara*) targets both yoga (reducing activity) and kaṣāya (uprooting passions).

**Position in the soteriology:**
*āsrava* (influx) → *bandha* (bondage from āsrava + kaṣāya) → *saṃvara* (blocking āsrava) → *nirjarā* (shedding bonded karma) → *mokṣa*

Āsrava is what saṃvara reverses; nirjarā then removes what already accumulated from prior āsravas.
