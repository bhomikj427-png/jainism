## Divergence map

The *niścaya-naya* / *vyavāhara-naya* pair is the Jain "two standpoints" device, systematized by Kundakunda: **niścaya** is the *absolute / transcendental* standpoint (a thing as it is in its own intrinsic nature) and **vyavāhara** is the *conventional / empirical / practical* standpoint (a thing as it is in relation to attributes, other substances, space, and time). Applied to the soul: from *niścaya*, the jīva is *always* pure consciousness, never the doer of material karma; from *vyavāhara*, the jīva binds karma, suffers, and climbs the gunasthāna ladder (Ch 03). What is *contested* — both within Jainism and in cross-tradition comparison — is the **relative authority of the two standpoints**:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **co-equal realism** (both standpoints capture genuine, simultaneously-true aspects of one real thing) | mainstream naya theory; the anekāntavāda tradition (naya.md) | both views are *true and partial*; neither is "less real"; this is the orthodox Jain self-understanding |
| **niścaya-privileging** (vyavāhara is provisional/instructional; the absolute view is the *real* truth, the conventional only a teaching-ladder) | strong readings of Kundakunda's Samayasāra; later Digambara mysticism | the soul's real nature is pure; talk of bondage is a concession to the unawakened — a reading that *looks* Advaita-like and is debated as such |

This internal tension is the reason the file is tagged **contested**: the device is agreed, its hierarchy is not.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Wikipedia "Kundakunda" / "Samayasara":** Defines niścaya (pure liberated soul) vs vyavāhara (realm of karma and delusion); the Samayasāra presses the niścaya view of the always-pure soul.

**Signal 2 — Jain doctrinal sources (Nayavāda material, Jaingpt):** Place niścaya/vyavāhara *inside* naya theory — they are nayas (partial valid standpoints), so under anekāntavāda *both* are genuinely true; niścaya treats substances "in a timeless, space-less manner," vyavāhara "in relation to attributes, space, time."

**Signal 3 — Stanford Encyclopedia "Theory of Two Truths in India" + comparison sources:** Establish that the Jain device, *despite structural similarity*, differs from the Buddhist two truths in interpretation — Jainism "maintains both perspectives as ultimately true simultaneously" (realism), whereas Madhyamaka accords the conventional only *provisional* existence (empty of inherent essence).

**Independence:** Encyclopedic, Jain-doctrinal, and a philosophy-reference source converge on the *structure* and on the *cross-tradition contrast*; they diverge (genuinely, in the tradition itself) on the internal hierarchy. Confidence medium.

## NOT the Buddhist or Advaita two-truths — keep the realism

This is the concept's prime-directive moment. The "two truths / two standpoints" shape recurs across Indian thought, and the temptation is to flatten them into one idea. The structures are parallel; the *verdicts on the conventional world* are not:

| system | the two levels | status of the *conventional* level |
|---|---|---|
| **Jain** niścaya / vyavāhara | absolute nature / relational description | **both genuinely true** — the world is real; vyavāhara is not a falsehood (co-equal-realism reading) |
| **Buddhist** (Madhyamaka) paramārtha / saṃvṛti | ultimate / conventional | conventional is **empty of inherent essence** — "neither real nor unreal," provisional (sunyata.md, catuskoti.md) |
| **Advaita** pāramārthika / vyāvahārika | Brahman-only / world-appearance | conventional is ultimately **false/sublated** — māyā, cancelled at liberation (maya-advaita.md) |

The Jain orthodox position is the *odd one out*: it is a **pluralist realist** two-standpoints, not a two-*truths* in which one level dissolves the other. Even the niścaya-privileging Kundakunda reading does *not* say the world is illusory (the six dravyas remain real — Ch 09); it says the soul's *real essence* is purer than its entangled appearance. Calling niścaya/vyavāhara "the Jain version of the two truths" imports either Buddhist emptiness or Advaita illusionism into a system that rejects both — exactly the kind of quiet term-mapping the charter forbids. The honest edge to each is `structurally-parallel-to` **and** `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`, never identity.
