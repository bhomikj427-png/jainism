## Gloss

**Nikṣepa** (also *nyāsa*, "placement / installation") is the Jain fourfold method for fixing the **intended sense** of a term before reasoning about its referent. Any word, the tradition holds, can denote in four distinct registers, and confusion arises when an arguer slides between them; nikṣepa makes the registers explicit. *TS* 1.5 (directly fetched): *nāmasthāpanādravyabhāvatastannyāsaḥ* — "These [the tattvas just named] are installed (denoted) in four ways: by name (*nāma*), by representation (*sthāpanā*), by substance-in-potential (*dravya*), and by actual present state (*bhāva*)."

Per Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*:

| register | Sanskrit | what it picks out | worked example (*jīva*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name | nāma | the bare conventional label, assigned irrespective of qualities, for social identity | calling *anything* "soul" (e.g. naming a person "Jīva") |
| Representation | sthāpanā | a symbol or likeness — wood, clay, painting, an idol, a token — taken as "this is that" | a statue or image standing for a soul / a Tīrthaṅkara |
| Substance(-in-potential) | dravya | that which *will* attain the relevant qualities, or once had them — the potential bearer | a soul not yet manifesting, or the future-enlightened soul of a Tīrthaṅkara |
| Actual state | bhāva | the substance as characterised by its **present mode** — the thing actually in the condition named | a soul actually conscious / one actually being a Tīrthaṅkara now |

The placement of this sūtra is deliberate: it comes immediately after *TS* 1.4 (the seven tattvas) and *TS* 1.3 (how right faith arises), supplying the **interpretive discipline** for the whole text — before investigating "soul," "influx," "liberation," one must specify in which of the four registers the term is being used. Nikṣepa is thus a hermeneutic gateway (*nyāsa* = "what the inquiry is set upon"), the disambiguation layer beneath the [naya](naya.md) standpoint-analysis and [anekantavada](anekantavada.md) many-sidedness.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 1.5 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018, with Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*):** Sanskrit *nāmasthāpanādravyabhāvatastannyāsaḥ* confirmed verbatim; the four definitions and the *jīva* example are Pūjyapāda's. **Signal 2 — WisdomLib "Nikṣepa: 25 definitions" (aggregated lexical/scholarly entries):** independently gives the same four registers ("gateways of investigation"/"entry points for investigation of truth") and the Tīrthaṅkara worked example (nāma = name, sthāpanā = image, dravya = future-enlightened soul, bhāva = actual Tīrthaṅkara). The two converge on both the count and the content. Confidence **medium**: TS 1.5 rests on the single Vijay K. Jain 2018 critical edition; Signal 2 is an aggregation page, not an independent critical edition, and Tatia's Chapter-1 rendering is unfetched (corpus ceiling).

**Distinction held (not conflated):** nikṣepa is *not* the same device as [naya](naya.md). Naya analyses a *real object* into valid partial standpoints (substance-view vs mode-view); nikṣepa analyses a *term* into the four ways it can denote (label / image / potential / actual). The dravya/bhāva nikṣepa pair *echoes* the dravyārthika/paryāyārthika naya pair in vocabulary, but they answer different questions — "what does this word point at?" vs "from which valid angle is the object being asserted?".
