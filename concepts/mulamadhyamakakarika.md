## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Mūlamadhyamakakārikā** ("Root Verses on the Middle Way," MMK) of **Nāgārjuna** (c. 150–250 CE — "the most important Buddhist philosopher after the historical Buddha himself") is the **founding text of the Madhyamaka school** of Mahāyāna Buddhism. It is the primary-text anchor behind [[madhyamaka]], [[sunyata]], [[dvisatya]], [[catuskoti]], and [[prasanga-nagarjuna]]. It consists of **~450 verses (kārikās) in 27 chapters**, each examining a Buddhist category — causation (pratyaya), motion, the senses, the aggregates, time, the self, action, the Buddha, the four noble truths, **nirvāṇa** — and showing that none has **svabhāva** (inherent existence / own-being).

**Method.** Nāgārjuna advances no positive thesis of his own; he uses **reductio (prasaṅga)** and the **tetralemma (catuṣkoṭi)** to show that any position positing svabhāva collapses into absurdity. The opening **dedication negates eight extremes** (no cessation, no arising; no annihilation, no permanence; no coming, no going; no difference, no identity) — the *aṣṭa-niṣedha*, a non-affirming negation.

**The pivotal verse (MMK 24.18).** "Whatever is dependently co-arisen, that is explained to be emptiness; that, being a dependent designation, is itself the middle way." This single verse **identifies emptiness (śūnyatā), dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda), dependent designation (upādāya prajñapti), and the middle way (madhyamā pratipad)** — the doctrinal heart of the school.

**Two truths (MMK 24.8–10).** The teaching rests on the distinction between **conventional truth (saṃvṛti-satya)** — how things appear within our conceptual frameworks — and **ultimate truth (paramārtha-satya)** — how they are under analysis. And **emptiness is itself empty**: it is not a new absolute, only the absence of svabhāva. The MMK's reception runs through **Buddhapālita, Bhāvaviveka, and Candrakīrti's Prasannapadā** — the lineage along which the later Prāsaṅgika/Svātantrika split forms.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Wikipedia, "Mūlamadhyamakakārikā"): Nāgārjuna ~150 CE; foundational Madhyamaka text; ~450 verses / 27 chapters; refutation of svabhāva across Buddhist categories via reductio; the eight-fold negation in the dedication ("non-affirming negation"); MMK 24.18 quoted equating emptiness / dependent origination / dependent designation / middle way; the two-truths framework; commentaries by Candrakīrti, Buddhapālita, Bhāvaviveka. **Signal 2** (SEP, "Nāgārjuna"): dates c. 150–250 CE; founder of Madhyamaka; the MMK as his major (~450-stanza) work; the central thesis that all phenomena lack svabhāva because dependently originated; emptiness identified with dependent origination and the middle way; the two truths; emptiness itself empty / no thesis of his own; the reductio method and tetralemma.

The two are independent (a reference article and a peer-reviewed philosophy encyclopedia) and agree on dating, structure, method, the central 24.18 identity, the two truths, the self-emptying of emptiness, and the commentarial lineage. Medium, not high: the MMK kārikās were **not fetched verbatim** beyond the two summaries' quotation of 24.18 (the doctrinal content is, however, cross-checked against the already-written [[sunyata]], [[madhyamaka]], and [[dvisatya]], which cite MMK 24.18 / 24.8); Nāgārjuna's exact dates and the authenticity of some attributed works remain scholarly questions.
