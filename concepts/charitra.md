## Gloss

Right conduct — the third of the tri-ratna (three jewels), alongside samyak-darśana (right faith) and samyak-jñāna (right knowledge). Cāritra denotes disciplined restraint of all censurable movements of speech, body, and mind — the practical elimination of the passions (kaṣāyas) and control of yoga (bodily/mental/verbal activity) that causes karma-influx. It is the operative mechanism through which the soteriological arc (āsrava → bandha → saṃvara → nirjarā → mokṣa) is completed: without right conduct, karma-matter cannot be stopped or shed.

## TS 1.1 (directly fetched) — the defining verse

**Sanskrit:** *samyagdarśanajñānacāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* ॥ १.१ ॥
**Translation:** "Right faith (samyag-darśana), right knowledge (samyag-jñāna), and right conduct (samyak-cāritra), together, constitute the path to liberation (mokṣa-mārga)."

**Pūjyapāda, Sarvārthasiddhi:** The singular "path" (mokṣa-mārga, not "paths") is deliberate — all three jointly constitute liberation's direct cause. This "refutes the view that each of these singly constitutes the path." They are simultaneous, not sequential: "heat and light of the sun are manifested simultaneously when clouds disperse." Right conduct depends on knowledge; knowledge depends on right faith; right faith enables knowledge. No element works alone.

## Two levels of charitra

| level | Sanskrit | practitioner | vows observed | scope |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| perfect/complete conduct | sakala-cāritra | monks and nuns (ascetics) | five mahāvratas (great vows) in their absolute form | total renunciation; no worldly activities permitted |
| imperfect/qualified conduct | vikala-cāritra | laypeople (śrāvakas/śrāvikās) | five anuvratas (minor vows) adapted to household life | partial restraint; worldly responsibilities accommodated |

The vikala-cāritra layperson also undertakes supplementary practices: fasting (pauṣadha), limiting possessions (dik-parimāṇa), and regular meditation.

## The five mahāvratas as the content of sakala-charitra

| vow | Sanskrit | content |
|---|---|---|
| non-violence | ahiṃsā | complete non-harm to all jīvas — the foundational mahāvrata |
| truthfulness | satya | no harmful, careless, or deceptive speech |
| non-stealing | asteya | not taking what is not given |
| celibacy | brahmacarya | complete abstention from sexual activity |
| non-attachment | aparigraha | no possessiveness towards any object |

These five were each given dedicated files in Batch 5–7 (ahimsa.md, satya.md, asteya.md, brahmacarya.md, aparigraha.md).

## Conduct and karma cessation

Cāritra has two karma-management effects:
- **Saṃvara function:** restraining yoga (activity of body/speech/mind) and passions stops new karma influx
- **Nirjarā function:** active tapas (internal austerities: dhyāna, vyutsarga etc.) sheds accumulated karma

Cāritra is therefore not merely ethical behaviour — it is the soul's karmic management system. The gunasthāna (spiritual stages) ladder maps the progressive refinement of cāritra from stage 4 (first true right faith) up to stage 14 (total conduct: shedding of the final traces of activity).

## Relationship to the other two ratnas

Right conduct **presupposes** right knowledge (samyak-jñāna), which **presupposes** right faith (samyak-darśana). However, per Pūjyapāda, the three are also simultaneously manifest — a practitioner who has right faith without right conduct has not yet achieved the complete ratnatraya. The common error is treating them as a temporal sequence rather than an integrated whole.
