## Gloss / Divergence map

Jīva is **soul** in Jain ontology: one of the six dravyas (substances), the only conscious one. Its defining mark is given in the *Tattvārthasūtra* by a single compact sūtra:

**TS 2.8: *upayogo lakṣaṇam*** — "Consciousness (upayoga) is the distinctive characteristic of the soul."

*Upayoga* is conscious activity or cognitive engagement, not merely passive awareness. It is of two types (TS 2.9: *sa dvividho*): **darśana** (perception/undifferentiated apprehension) and **jñāna** (knowledge/differentiated cognition). These further subdivide into 12 sub-types across embodied and liberated states (including the supreme omniscience, *kevala-jñāna*).

All souls divide into two classes (TS 2.10: *saṃsāriṇo muktāś ca*): **saṃsārin** (transmigrating/worldly — bound by karma and cycling through embodiments) and **mukta** (liberated — karma-free, at rest at the summit of the universe, in permanent omniscient upayoga).

Additional key characteristics reported across Jain sources (verse attribution not directly verified in this run for all items):
- **Amūrta** (immaterial) — jīva has no physical form, in direct contrast to pudgala (which is mūrta, formed matter).
- **Svadeha-parimāṇa** — the soul exactly fills the body it currently inhabits (it contracts/expands to fit each body form).
- **Kartā** (agent) — the soul is the doer of its karmic actions and the experiencer (*bhoktā*) of their results.

Importantly: jīva is **not** identified with a universal Ātman. Jainism is pluralistic — there are infinitely many jīvas, each ultimately distinct; no jīva is absorbed into or identical with another or with any absolute Being. This is a structural difference from Advaita Vedānta.
