## Gloss / Divergence map

Aparigraha is **non-possessiveness / non-attachment** — the fifth of the five great vows (*mahāvratas*), completing the ethical framework that underpins Jain liberation. It operates at two levels: external (limiting or eliminating material possessions) and, more fundamentally, internal (eliminating the mental attitudes of attachment and aversion).

**TS 7.8 Sanskrit:** *manoḍjñāmanoḍjñendriyaviṣayarāgadveṣavarjanāni pañca* — "Giving up attachment (rāga) and aversion (dveṣa) for agreeable and disagreeable objects of the five senses constitutes five observances for the vow of non-attachment." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; fetched directly.)

**Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*** (ibid.): "The observances involve renouncing attachment and aversion in the presence of agreeable and disagreeable objects of the senses — touch, taste, smell, sight, sound." The five observances correspond to the five senses.

**Two levels of aparigraha:**

| level | Sanskrit | description | who practices |
|---|---|---|---|
| External | parigraha-tyāga | limiting or completely eliminating material possessions | monks: total renunciation; laypeople: set a limit (*parimāṇa*) |
| Internal | ākiṃcanya | abandoning rāga (attachment) and dveṣa (aversion) toward all sense objects — the root mental attitude | required for all practitioners; the internal is more fundamental |

**The internal definition is primary:** TS 7.8 defines aparigraha through its five observances as renunciation of *rāga-dveṣa* — not merely through not owning things. A monk without possessions who still has mental attachment is not truly aparigraha; a layperson with possessions held without rāga is more genuinely practising it than a monk with suppressed but unremoved attachment.

**Connection to the karma mechanism:**
- *Rāga* (attachment) and *dveṣa* (aversion) are forms of **kaṣāya** (passion) — the adhesive that makes karma stick (bandha.md TS 8.1)
- Aparigraha directly targets kaṣāya at the affective root
- Reducing rāga-dveṣa contributes to both **saṃvara** (stopping new karma) and **nirjarā** (loosening old karma)
- Complete aparigraha = total kaṣāya removal → prerequisites for mokṣa

**The Digambara interpretation:** The most rigorous application of aparigraha for monks is *digambara* practice — total non-possession, including clothing. The sky (*dik*, *ambara*) is the only covering. This is the logical extreme of external aparigraha: if all possessiveness must be renounced, clothing as a possession must also go. The Śvetāmbara tradition permits white robes for monks, holding that the five vows do not require nakedness.

**The five mahāvratas (complete list):**
1. Ahiṃsā (non-violence)
2. Satya (truthfulness)
3. Asteya (non-stealing)
4. Brahmacarya (celibacy)
5. **Aparigraha** (non-possessiveness)
