## Gloss / Divergence map

In Jainism, *tattva* ("reality/truth/that-which-is") refers to the **seven fundamental categories of existence** that together constitute the complete framework of Jain ontology and soteriology. The Tattvārthasūtra is literally named after them (*tattva* + *artha* + *sūtra* = "aphorisms on the meaning of reality").

**TS 1.4 Sanskrit:** *jīvājīvāsravabandhasaṃvaranirjarāmokṣāstattvaṃ* — "Soul, non-soul, influx, bondage, stoppage, gradual dissociation, and liberation constitute the reality (tattva)." (Vijay K. Jain 2018, Motilal Banarsidass; TS 1.4 fetched directly.)

**The seven tattvas and their location in the TS:**

| # | tattva | Sanskrit | TS chapters | content |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Soul | jīva | 1–2 | the conscious substance; defined by upayoga |
| 2 | Non-soul | ajīva | 5 | the five non-conscious substances |
| 3 | Influx | āsrava | 6 | karma flowing toward soul via yoga |
| 4 | Bondage | bandha | 8 | karma adhering to soul via kaṣāya |
| 5 | Stoppage | saṃvara | 9 | blocking new karma via gupti/samiti |
| 6 | Dissociation | nirjarā | 8–9 | shedding existing karma via tapas |
| 7 | Liberation | mokṣa | 10 | complete annihilation of all karma |

**Pūjyapāda's ordering logic** (Sarvārthasiddhi, via Vijay K. Jain 2018): "The soul is mentioned first as all fruits are experienced by it. The non-soul is mentioned next as it serves the soul. Influx follows [because it concerns both soul and non-soul]. Bondage follows influx causally. Stoppage opposes bondage and is mentioned next. Dissociation occurs after stoppage, and liberation represents the final outcome."

**The narrative arc:** The seven tattvas tell a complete story — 1–2 establish *what exists*; 3–4 describe *how the problem arises* (karma enters and binds); 5–6 describe *how the problem is solved*; 7 is *the solution*. The TS is structured as this arc.

**Nine-tattva variant:** Some commentators add *puṇya* (merit) and *pāpa* (demerit) to give nine tattvas. TS itself (via Pūjyapāda) says these are "implied in influx and bondage" and separate enumeration is unnecessary. The nine-tattva count is common in popular Jain teaching; the seven-tattva count is TS-canonical.

**TS 1.1** (the opening verse): *samyag-darśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* — "Right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct together are the path to liberation." This is the tri-ratna (three jewels) — the ethical-soteriological summary paired with the ontological seven tattvas as the TS's two foundational verses.
