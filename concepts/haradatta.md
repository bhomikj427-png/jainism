## Gloss / Divergence map

**Haradatta** — "a very learned grammarian of the **Southern School**," per Abhyankar — wrote the ***Padamañjarī***, one of the two great commentaries on the [[kasika|Kāśikāvṛtti]], alongside [[jinendrabuddhi|Jinendrabuddhi's]] [[nyasa-vyakarana|*Nyāsa*]], from which Abhyankar says he "has **drawn considerably**." Nine files in this corpus invoked him before he had a node.

## Reasoning across signals

**⭐⭐ The structural finding, and the corpus did not have it: Pāṇinian grammar had *regions*, and they read different sub-commentaries.** Abhyankar, in two separate entries read at first hand:

- **Jinendrabuddhi** "belonged to the **eastern school** of Pāṇini's Grammar";
- **Haradatta** was "of the **Southern School**" — and ⭐ "**the Benares School of Grammarians follow [the *Padamañjarī*] more than the equally learned another commentary *Kāśikāvivaraṇapañjikā* or *Nyāsa*.**"

⭐⭐ **Three named regional schools — eastern, southern, Benares — and a recorded fact about which text one of them preferred.** And the corpus already knows who the Benares school *is*: [[bhattoji-dikshita]], [[kaundabhatta]] and [[nagesha-bhatta]], the Varanasi household Batch 48 reconstructed. **So the tradition this corpus spent Batch 48 on had a choice between two commentaries on the Kāśikā and took the southern one.**

⚠⚠ **And here is the inference the corpus refuses.** Jinendrabuddhi was a **Buddhist**; Haradatta was a **Śaiva**; the Benares school was Brahminical. The available explanation writes itself — and **Abhyankar gives no reason at all**, no source consulted gives one, and there are at least three others on the page: the *Padamañjarī* is later and could simply have superseded; Abhyankar says Haradatta "**has drawn considerably from Nyāsa**," so a reader of the *Padamañjarī* gets much of the *Nyāsa* anyway; and Abhyankar separately reports that "in the Padamañjarī Haradatta is said to have given **everything of importance from the Mahābhāṣya**" — which for a Benares school built on the *Mahābhāṣya* is a sufficient reason with no theology in it. ⭐ **§0: this is exactly the shape of reading that only works if you supply the motive. Recorded as an unexplained fact, with four candidate explanations and no verdict.**

**⭐ T3 — answered in the honest negative, with the location now known.** The batch asked whether Haradatta's complementary reading of the three sages "survives first-hand checking." **It has not been checked at first hand, and this node does not claim otherwise.** What is true:
- **Deshpande's account of it *was* read at first hand** in Batch 48 (the Gonda Lecture PDF), so the corpus is one remove from Haradatta, not two.
- The corpus holds **one Haradatta datum verified independently of Deshpande**: Abhyankar preserves his reasoning that Sphoṭāyana "was the first advocate of the Sphoṭa theory **and hence he was nicknamed** Sphoṭāyana" ([[sphota]], *Padamañjarī* on A 6.1.123) — ⭐ **which is Haradatta reading a doctrine out of a proper name, i.e. the same reading-out-of-names habit [[jinendrabuddhi]] shows with Śalātura.** Two commentators, two centuries, one method.
- ⭐ **And the text is free and locatable**: archive.org carries the Kāśikāvṛtti *with both* the *Nyāsa* and the *Padamañjarī*. **T3 is not blocked; it was not done.** Carried as a sharp, cheap follow-up rather than left vague.

**⭐⭐ A correction this node forces on [[katantra]], and it is a chronological one.** That node set the two grammars' origin legends side by side — Pāṇini's Śivasūtras from Śiva, the Kātantra from Śiva's son at Śiva's direction — and observed that "the deities stand in exactly the relation the two grammars stand in." It flagged that no design was being claimed. ⚠ **But it left standing an unexamined assumption: that the Pāṇinian legend is the senior one and the Kātantra's the derivative.** Deshpande's finding removes that. If **Haradatta gives the first attestation *in the Pāṇinian tradition*** of Pāṇini receiving the Śiva-sūtras from Maheśvara, then that legend surfaces in the record in the **11th–13th century** — which is **contemporary with or later than Somadeva's *Kathāsaritsāgara***, the vehicle of the Kātantra's Kārttikeya story. ⭐ **In the surviving record, neither divine warrant is demonstrably older than the other.** ⚠ Surfacing in the record is not origination, and the corpus asserts nothing about which legend is actually older — only that **the seniority it was tacitly assuming is not evidenced.** [[katantra]] corrected in place.

**⚠ Collision candidate #12, and the corpus is deliberately not resolving it.** Wikipedia's stub states that the *Padamañjarī*'s Haradatta "is also the author of a commentary on the *Āpastamba Dharmasūtra*, the *Gautama Dharmasūtra* and other texts," with **no doubt raised and no argument given**, citing **Patrick Olivelle, *Language, Texts, and Society* (2005), pp. 301 ff.** ⭐ **Note the shape**: an identification of a grammarian with a same-named author **in a different śāstra**, asserted in the accessible source without visible argument. That is [[vararuci]] (grammar/Prākṛt), [[patanjali]] (grammar/yoga/medicine), [[candragomin]] (grammar/poetry) and [[jinendrabuddhi]] (grammar/pramāṇa) a fifth time — ⭐ **the corpus has now met the same pattern once in every śāstra it has touched.**

⚠ **But the rule this batch sharpened at [[jinendrabuddhi]] applies, and it cuts both ways:** *distrust identifications that rest on the name alone* — and **the corpus does not know whether Olivelle argues this identification or inherits it**, because Olivelle has not been read. ⭐ **Jinendrabuddhi is the cautionary case in the other direction**: there the merge looked like the name-fallacy and turned out to rest on style and citation. **So this is flagged as a candidate, not diagnosed as a defect**, and the follow-up is to read Olivelle. ⚠ Note also that a Dharmaśāstra commentator being a **Śaiva of the Chola country** who also wrote on grammar is entirely unremarkable for a South Indian *paṇḍita*, so the prior here is much friendlier to identity than in the Vararuci case.

**⚠ The date is genuinely open, and the disagreement runs the *opposite* way to Batch 47's rule.** Batch 47's caution says a specialist reference work is strong on internal contents and **weak on chronology**. Here the two-century spread is between **Wikipedia and "most sources" at the 11th century** and ⭐ **working philologists collating his text at the 13th** — Ajotikar, Ajotikar & Scharf, who need his date because they are using him as a **dated witness** to the Kāśikā's text. ⭐ **The party with the strongest professional reason to get the date right is the outlier.** ⚠ **The corpus does not adjudicate** and specifically does **not** average: a 13th-century Haradatta would put roughly two centuries between him and Kaiyaṭa, which would weaken — though not break — Deshpande's "near-contemporary" framing of the two men's contrasting theologies. **Recorded as a tension between two claims the corpus holds, both from sources it rates highly.**

**⚠ An independence trap, met for the second time in one batch.** The search summary returned "Haradatta was a very learned grammarian of the Southern School, and the Benares School of Grammarians follow the Padamañjari more than the equally learned commentary…" as though from a separate authority. **It is Abhyankar, verbatim** — wisdomlib's "Vyakarana glossary" again, the same reprint [[katantra]] caught and flagged. ⭐ **The standing rule set two nodes into this batch has now paid for itself twice.**

**⭐ Small first-hand data the corpus can hold on its own.** Abhyankar cites the *Padamañjarī* by chapter and verse in several entries, which is worth more than a biography: a technical term for verbal appendages "appears to be used **for the first time** for such forms by Haradatta" (*Padamañjarī* on VII.2.31); and "**Haradatta has tried to explain how the term *āmreḍita* means the first member**" (*Padamañj.* on VIII.1.2). ⭐ **He is cited as an authority who innovates terminology, not merely as a glossator** — which is the strongest evidence here for Abhyankar's placing him beside Kaiyaṭa, Bhaṭṭoji and Nāgeśa in the preface's list of the grammarians who "developed by their substantial contributions the work of Pāṇini as a science."

**Independence (§4 signal 1).** **Abhyankar** at first hand — the Southern School, the Benares School's preference, the Nyāsa debt, the "everything of importance from the Mahābhāṣya" report (citing *Mahābhāṣya* D.E.S. ed. Vol. VII pp. 390–391), the two *Padamañjarī* citations, and the preface's list. **Deshpande, 2018 Gonda Lecture** — read at first hand in Batch 48: the Chola/Śaivite placement, the *Padamañjarī*'s salutation to Śiva, the first Pāṇinian attestation of the Maheśvara revelation, the absence of the Śeṣa motif, the complementary three-sages view, the contrast with Kaiyaṭa. **Ajotikar, Ajotikar & Scharf** — read at first hand this batch: the 13th-century date, and the *Padamañjarī*'s role as a dated witness to the Kāśikā's text. **Wikipedia** — the 11th-century date and the Dharmasūtra attribution with Olivelle. ⚠ **These are four genuinely independent sources and they conflict on the one thing that matters chronologically.** **CONTESTED**, confidence **medium** — ⚠ **the *Padamañjarī* has not been read**, and T3 is explicitly **not** discharged.
