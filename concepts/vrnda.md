## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vṛnda** (probably **A.D. 800–950**, Eastern Bengal) wrote the ***Siddhayoga***, a therapeutic compendium of **3,728 verses** and some prose in **eighty-two chapters** — one of the two or three works that define the post-classical Āyurvedic formulary tradition, authoritative by [[cakrapanidatta|Cakrapāṇidatta]]'s time and still being quoted extensively by [[hemadri|Hemādri]] three centuries later.

⚠ **He is also the third leg of this batch's Āyurveda trio, and the three interlock.** `tisata.md`'s date narrows to 900–1050 **only on a conditional about a commentary on Vṛnda's work**; `niscalakara.md` is the witness who tells us where Vṛnda came from. **Reading any one of these three required the other two.**

### The book

The first verse praises **Śiva and Caṇḍī, Dhanvantari, Suśruta and other sages, and Ātreya**. The second does something more useful to a historian: **Vṛnda announces that the order of diseases in his work will follow the scheme of Mādhava's *Gadaviniścaya*** (the *Mādhavanidāna*), and that he will set out therapeutic procedures **from various sources** with remarks as the occasion requires.

**An author stating his organising principle is borrowed, and his content compiled, in his second verse.** That single declaration does two things: it makes the *Siddhayoga* a **therapy** to Mādhava's **diagnosis** — the two halves of a working medical curriculum — and it fixes Vṛnda after Mādhava for all subsequent dating.

Structure: 21 verses of general pathology and therapy; ch. 1 on fevers, then sixty-seven more "arranged for the greater part, in fact, along the lines of the *Mādhavanidāna*"; ch. 69 *rasāyana* and 70 *vājīkaraṇa*; then nine chapters on procedures (*sneha*, *sveda*, *vamana*, *virecana*, *bastividhi*, *nirūha*, *dhūmavidhi*, *nasya*, *kavala*); and three on *ariṣṭa*, *svastha* and miscellany.

### What is new in it

- ⭐ **The earliest descriptions of two diseases.** ***Snāyukaroga*** (*visphoṭa* 55.15–19) — which Meulenbeld identifies as **dracontiasis, Guinea-worm disease**, an infestation by the nematode *Dracunculus medinensis* — and ***vardhma*** (*vṛddhi* 40.20–23), a disorder with "distinctive swellings in the groins," which had been "incidentally referred to earlier" and "acquired an independent status after Vṛnda characterized it as such."
  ⚠⚠ **Note the contrast, because it is the guard-rail this corpus needs.** For *snāyukaroga* Meulenbeld supplies a confident modern identification down to the Linnaean binomial; for *vardhma*, four lines later, he writes that **"the identity of this disease … has still to be determined."** **The same scholar, the same page, one identification made and one refused.** That is what responsible retrospective diagnosis looks like — and it is the reason this file reports the Guinea-worm identification as **Meulenbeld's**, not as something the *Siddhayoga* says. Vṛnda described a syndrome; a 20th-century scholar matched it to a parasite. **Those are two different acts and the corpus must not merge them (§0).**
- **A disease-name and a disease restored.** He uses *niścaraka* for a type of diarrhoea; and he describes *kubjatva* (hump-backedness, 22.34), a *vāta* disease **absent from the *Mādhavanidāna*** though present in Caraka and Vāgbhaṭa — so his declared dependence on Mādhava's scheme is not slavish.
- **Firsts in treatment**: probably the earliest recorded prescription of ***pārasīyayavānī*** against intestinal worms (7.1); an ointment of *dhattūra* juice and **mercury (*rasendra*)** against lice (7.13); a decoction of **aśoka bark** against uterine bleeding (63.5); and the ***nāgārjunavartī***, an eye-medicine "the formula of which is said to have been **written by [[nagarjuna|Nāgārjuna]] on a pillar in Pāṭaliputra**" (61.148–152), which "may be found for the first time in the *Siddhayoga*." ⚠ **The pillar is the text's own story about where the recipe came from, not a fact about Nāgārjuna** — recorded as an attribution-legend, and note it is the same *kind* of claim as the Kālī boon in `gangesha.md`: a tradition explaining an unusual piece of knowledge by an extraordinary origin.
- **Two absences that function as dates.** **"Opium and Cannabis are absent from Vṛnda's *materia medica* and iatrochemistry is still of small importance."** In this literature the arrival of opium, cannabis and mercurial chemistry are chronological markers; their absence places him early. ⚠ Compare `rasesvara.md` — the alchemical tradition has not yet arrived.
- **Religion inside the therapy.** Religious elements "are not uncommon"; a *yantra* called *ubhayatriṃśaka* is described; *prāṇāyāma* is recommended for *śūla*. And: **"A single reference to *[[sunyata|śūnyatā]]* may point to Vṛnda's acquaintance with Buddhist doctrines."** ⚠ **One word, and Meulenbeld says "may point to."** Recorded at exactly that strength — a single lexical trace is a hint about a physician's reading, not evidence of a Buddhist affiliation.

### The divergence I — the date, and an external anchor that evaporates

| proposal | who | basis | outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| **at least a century before Cakrapāṇidatta** | **J. Jolly** | it "had become an authoritative work in the latter's time" | Meulenbeld: "**rightly remarked**" |
| **A.D. 975–1000** | **D. Ch. Bhattacharyya** | the **absence** of borrowings from Mahuka's *Haramekhalā* (which he dates 965) and from [[candrata|Candraṭa]] (950), both quoted by Cakrapāṇi — so Vṛnda must be their contemporary | Meulenbeld: "his arguments **do not preclude an earlier date**" |
| **9th century** | **P. V. Sharma** | the mention of [[jejjata|Jejjaṭa]] in the *Siddhayoga* | Meulenbeld: "**Jejjaṭa's date, however, is earlier in my opinion**" |
| **before the later 9th c.** | **M. Z. Siddiqi** | the *Siddhayoga* is referred to by the Arab author **Ibn Wāḍiḥ al-Yaʿqūbī** | **dismissed** |
| **probably 800–950** | **Meulenbeld** | later than Mādhava, earlier than Cakrapāṇidatta | the conclusion adopted here |

⚠⚠ **The al-Yaʿqūbī claim deserves its own line, because of what it would have been.** An **external, non-Indian, independently dated** witness is the strongest evidence available anywhere in this literature — Ch 35's finding is precisely that "every absolute year entered from *outside* the medical literature." Here such a witness is offered, and it fails on identification: **"the work mentioned by this Arab author proves to be Ravigupta's *Siddhasāra*."** **The one chance to anchor Vṛnda from outside the tradition turns out to be about a different book.** The corpus should hold this alongside `jejjata.md` and `tisata.md`: even the outside anchors have to be identified first, and identification is the hard part.

⚠ **And note the shape of Bhattacharyya's argument — dating by what an author did *not* read.** It is a real argument (Cakrapāṇi quotes Mahuka and Candraṭa; Vṛnda does not; so Vṛnda may predate them) and Meulenbeld's objection is exactly right: **absence of borrowing gives a soft bound in one direction only.** A man may fail to quote a book for many reasons besides its not existing.

### The divergence II — how many Vṛndas?

**P. V. Sharma** holds there were **two** medical authors called Vṛnda: the author of the *Siddhayoga*, and a later **Vṛndakuṇḍa**, "who probably descended from Kārttikakuṇḍa" — the latter being, on his account, the author of a *Vṛndaṭīkā* / *-ṭippaṇa* on the *Carakasaṃhitā* quoted by Niścalakara, Śivadāsasena and Adhamalla. **G. Halder** adds that Vṛndakuṇḍa, also called Vṛndāvana, wrote a *Vṛndasindhu* and a *Padaviniścaya*.

**Meulenbeld declines it**: "The evidence available is **not strongly in favour of this hypothesis**. Niścalakara and Śivadāsasena seem to refer **indiscriminately** to Vṛnda and Vṛndakuṇḍa, and Adhamalla mentions an author of glosses, who may well be the author of the *Siddhayoga* himself."

⚠⚠ **This matters to Batch 46 as a whole, and it is recorded as a corrective.** This batch has logged **four** name-collisions — Vācaspati Miśra I/II, two Harirāma Tarkavāgīśas, two Vyāsatīrthas, the open Jñānottama question — and drawn from them the rule that *"same name" is weak evidence of "same person."* **Here is the rule's mirror image: a proposed split, examined and not sustained.** "Different work under the same name" is likewise weak evidence of *different persons*. **The pattern this batch found is a reason to check, not a licence to split**, and Vṛnda is the case that keeps it honest.

⚠ **A third confusion, and its cause is instructive.** **A. F. R. Hoernle** held that the *Siddhayoga* was written by **Mādhavakara**, and that the *Rogaviniścaya* and the *Siddhayoga* were "clearly the two parts of a single work by one author." Meulenbeld's explanation is one clause long: **"Hoernle was unaware of the existence of the *Mādhavacikitsā*."** And the confusion had a handle: the *Siddhayoga* **is also known as the *Vṛndamādhava***, and as the *Vṛndasaṃgraha* in Niścalakara's *Ratnaprabhā*. **A title containing another author's name, plus one text the scholar had not seen, produced a wrong attribution that had to be undone.**

### The man

> "**No particulars about Vṛnda are known**, except that he may have lived in Eastern Bengal, i.e., the country now called Bangla Desh."

The one positive datum comes from where it always does in this cluster: **[[niscalakara|Niścalakara]] mentions that Vṛnda belonged to *Varendra***, a region the *Siddhayoga* itself refers to. **Three nodes in this batch, and Niścalakara supplies evidence in all three.**

### The commentaries — and the conditional that dates Tīsaṭa

**Only one commentary survives**: the ***Vyākhyākusumāvalī*** (Śrīkaṇṭhadatta's). But the record shows others existed:
- the *Kusumāvalī* itself "contains two references to an **earlier commentary**";
- **Śivadāsasena's commentary on the *Cakradatta* quotes [[candrata|Candraṭa]]'s *Vṛndaṭīkā***;
- **Adhamalla** cites the author of a *ṭippaṇa* on Vṛnda "whose opinions disagree with those found in the *Kusumāvalī*";
- Hemādri's quotations carry "short remarks which impress as being from an **unknown commentary**."

⚠⚠ **The second bullet is the hinge of another node.** `tisata.md` records that Meulenbeld narrows Tīsaṭa and Candraṭa to **900–1050** on the condition "**if** Candraṭa wrote a commentary on Vṛnda's *Siddhayoga*, as seems to follow from a remark of Śivadāsasena." **That remark is this one.** So: **a 16th-century commentator's passing quotation of a lost commentary is what tightens a 10th-century author's date by 250 years** — and the corpus can now see both ends of the inference in one place.

⚠ Two further commentary-attributions **rejected** by Meulenbeld: **P. K. Gode**'s that **Brahmadeva** wrote on the *Siddhayoga* — "careful study of these quotations revealed that the majority refer to verses taken from or inspired by Suśruta," i.e. Brahmadeva's commentary is on the *Suśrutasaṃhitā*; and **P. V. Sharma**'s that **Nāradatta**, Cakrapāṇidatta's teacher, also commented on it — "**cannot be substantiated**."

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — G. Jan Meulenbeld, *A History of Indian Medical Literature*, vol. IIA, §"Vṛnda," pp. ~79–82 — read at first hand**, archive.org `Meulenbeld-HIML`, retrieved this batch by the recorded method. ⚠ **Single-source dependence, as in `tisata.md` and `niscalakara.md`.** The mitigation is the same and is unusually strong here: **within this one section Meulenbeld endorses one scholar (Jolly) and rejects or qualifies five others** (Bhattacharyya, P. V. Sharma three times, Siddiqi, Hoernle, Gode). **The reader is not being handed a consensus; the disagreement is on the page with each party named.**

**Signal 2 — corpus-internal, and it is genuinely load-bearing here.** `tisata.md` (the Śivadāsasena conditional, whose other end is in this file), `candrata.md` (the *Vṛndaṭīkā*; and Bhattacharyya's 950 dating of Candraṭa used as a bound on Vṛnda), `niscalakara.md` (the Varendra testimony and the *Vṛndasaṃgraha* title), `cakrapanidatta.md` (the terminus ad quem), `jejjata.md` (whose disputed date defeats Sharma's 9th-c. proposal — **the third time this batch that Jejjaṭa's unsettledness has blocked an argument**), `hemadri.md`, `haricandra.md`, `rasesvara.md`, `nagarjuna.md`, `sunyata.md`.

**Why contested.** Four scholarly datings spanning 800–1000, one of them resting on an external witness that turns out to concern a different book; a live dispute about whether "Vṛnda" is one author or two; and a 19th-century misattribution of the whole work to another man. **Why medium.** The reference work read at first hand, with every disagreement attributed and Meulenbeld's own reasoning visible. **Not high**: **the *Siddhayoga* was not read** — every verse-reference here is Meulenbeld's citation, not a text consulted; Jolly, Bhattacharyya, P. V. Sharma, Siddiqi, Hoernle, Halder and Gode are all reached only through him; the corpus has **no node for Mādhava or the *Mādhavanidāna***, though this file shows the *Siddhayoga* is organised on it; and `term_devanagari` is **omitted** rather than guessed (§3).
