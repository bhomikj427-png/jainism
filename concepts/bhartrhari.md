## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bhartṛhari** is the philosopher of the grammatical tradition — author of the [[vakyapadiya|Vākyapadīya]] and of the *Mahābhāṣyadīpikā* (or *Tripādī*), a commentary on the [[mahabhashya|Mahābhāṣya]]. He is the figure who turned [[vyakarana|Vyākaraṇa]] from a technical discipline into a metaphysics, and who **argued that grammar should count as a *darśana*** — an argument that, as [[vyakarana]] records, had to be *made*, which is evidence it was not standardly granted.

Abhyankar's judgement, from inside the tradition: Sanskrit grammar as a science "was **carried to perfection** by the stalwart grammarian Bhartṛhari."

### The divergence: a 300-year spread, and one bad witness

⚠ **Four sources, four datings.** This is the widest unresolved spread in the batch.

| date | who holds it (source) | basis |
|---|---|---|
| **c. 400 CE** | **SEP**, "Language and Testimony…" | Unstated; the earliest proposal met |
| **c. 450–510 CE** | **IEP**, "Bhartrihari" — also citing T. R. V. Murti's chronology (Vasurāta → Bhartṛhari 450–510 → Dignāga 480–540) | Placement relative to a teacher and a Buddhist contemporary |
| **c. 485–540 CE** | **Wikipedia** | "florescence c. 5th to 6th century CE" |
| **7th century CE** | **Abhyankar** (read at first hand) | The older consensus, resting on **Yijing** |

**The witness that causes the trouble.** The Chinese pilgrim **Yijing (I-Tsing, 635–713)**, who travelled in India, "mentions Bhartrhari in his travel notes" and reports him as author of the *Vākyapadīya* and of a commentary on the Mahābhāṣya. Taken at face value his testimony places Bhartṛhari in the **7th century**. But Wikipedia records the modern verdict: "researchers have found **some of the details given by Yi-Jing to be erroneous, specifically the time period** that he was alive." IEP adds that Yijing was confused about the man in another way too — **mistaking him for a Buddhist.**

⚠ **A witness demonstrably wrong about a subject's religion is a poor witness for his dates**, and that — rather than any positive evidence — is the ground on which the 7th-century dating was abandoned. The corpus notes what this means: **the current 5th–6th c. consensus rests largely on discrediting the only external witness, not on replacing him.** The spread is unresolved and is recorded as such.

### ⚠ A correction the corpus must make to its own method

**Abhyankar holds the dating the rest of the field has abandoned.** He is the batch's best source — a specialist lexicon, read at first hand, and repeatedly decisive on technical questions where the encyclopedias are vague or wrong ([[astadhyayi]], [[katyayana]], [[sphota]]). Here he is simply behind.

**The lesson is about signal-weighting, and it qualifies how this batch has been reading.** "Read at first hand" and "specialist" have been treated as strong marks of reliability throughout Batch 47. They are — **for what a discipline knows internally**: technical vocabulary, what a term means, what a manuscript tradition contains, which attributions the tradition itself doubts. They are **weak** for anything depending on scholarship *outside* the discipline that has since moved — chronology above all. **Abhyankar (1961) is authoritative on *pratyāhāra* and out of date on Bhartṛhari's century, and both facts are true of the same book.** Recorded as a standing caution on the batch's own principal source.

### The philosophy: language as the ground of everything

***Śabdādvaita*** / ***[[sabdabrahman|śabdabrahman]]*** — "word monism." IEP: cognition and language "at an ultimate level are ontologically identical concepts that refer to one supreme reality, *Brahman*." The *Vākyapadīya*'s opening verse states it: "The *Brahman* is without beginning and end, whose essence is the Word, who is the cause of the manifested phonemes."

SEP puts the metaphysical commitment precisely: "language constitutes the ultimate principle of reality (*śabdabrahman*). Both the signifier words and the signified entities in the world are perceived to be a **transformation (*pariṇāma*)** of the ultimate unified principle of language." ⚠ **Note the word *pariṇāma*** — real transformation, not [[vivartavada|*vivarta*]] appearance. **Bhartṛhari's monism is a transformation-monism**, which puts him structurally on [[parinamavada]]'s side rather than [[advaita-vedanta]]'s, whatever the surface resemblance of "one reality" language.

**Sentence-holism.** The sentence, not the word, is the real linguistic unit. SEP: "the linguistically given entity is a sentence… elements abstracted through this procedure have **no reality of any kind**. They are ***kalpita*** ('imagined')." Words and phonemes are analytic conveniences. The sentence "conveys its entire unitary meaning **in a flash**."

***Pratibhā*** — the "instantaneous flash," "a moment of recognition" by which a hearer grasps the meaning. IEP quotes the striking formulation: **"Sentence meaning is produced by word meanings but is not constituted by them."** Meaning arises from "the particular concatenation of [analyzable] units, **not because those units are meaningful in themselves**."

⚠ **He is therefore against his own tradition's earlier position.** Kātyāyana and Patañjali hold ***abhihitānvaya*** — word-meanings grasped separately, combined afterwards ([[mahabhashya]]). Bhartṛhari rejects exactly this. **The *munitraya* and its greatest philosopher are opposed on the central semantic question**, which is a useful corrective to treating "the grammarians" as one voice.

### T2: he did not invent sphoṭa

The batch queued this and it **fails** for Bhartṛhari's authorship. IEP is explicit: "it was **Patañjali** who explicitly discusses *sphoṭa* in his *Mahābhāṣya*," and SEP that Patañjali "introduced the term *sphoṭa* for the first time." What Bhartṛhari did is **extend** it: Wikipedia notes he applied it "to **each** element of the utterance, *varṇa* (the letter or syllable), *pada* (the word), and *vākya* (the sentence)," **unlike Patañjali's narrower application.** Full divergence map in [[sphota]].

### What is not settled, and is not pretended to be

- **Is the grammarian the poet of the *Śatakatraya*** (the *Nīti-*, *Śṛṅgāra-* and *Vairāgya-śataka*)? Wikipedia "treats Bhartrhari as a single figure authoring both… without documenting scholarly dispute"; IEP does not raise it. ⚠ Given that this batch has found the same name silently merged in **four** other cases ([[katyayana]], [[patanjali-grammarian]], and twice in [[vyakarana]]), **a reference work's silence is not evidence of agreement.** The corpus does **not** assert the identification and does not deny it; it records that its sources do not argue the point.
- **Is the *Vṛtti* on kāṇḍas I–II by Bhartṛhari himself?** Neither IEP nor Wikipedia records a dispute. **The corpus did not test this and says so.** Carried as a follow-up.
- **Influence.** IEP says only that his work "was recognized and addressed by schools of… Mīmāṃsā, Vedānta… and Buddhism," "without elaborating specific doctrinal influences." SEP does not mention Dignāga at all. ⚠ **The frequently-repeated claim that Bhartṛhari influenced Dignāga's [[apoha]] is not supported by any source consulted here**, and is therefore **not asserted**. Follow-up.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **IEP, "Bhartrihari"** (fetched): 450–510 CE; Murti's chronology; three kāṇḍas, ~635 verses; Helārāja as "his first major commentator"; the *darśana* argument; *śabdatattva* as "word principle"; *pratibhā*; **and the decisive T2 statement that Patañjali discussed sphoṭa first.**
2. **Wikipedia, "Bhartṛhari"** (fetched, independent): 485–540; the Yijing testimony and its errors; the three kāṇḍa names (*Brahma-*, *Vākya-*, *Pada-kāṇḍa*); the *Mahābhāṣyadīpikā*/*Tripādī*; sphoṭa extended to varṇa/pada/vākya against Patañjali's narrower use.
3. **SEP, "Language and Testimony in Classical Indian Philosophy"** (fetched, independent, scholarly): c. 400 CE; *kalpita*; the "in a flash" holism; *śabdabrahman* as ***pariṇāma***; the *abhihitānvaya*/*anvitābhidhāna* map and where the grammarians sit; that later grammarian-philosophers **Kauṇḍabhaṭṭa and Nāgeśabhaṭṭa rejected Bhartṛhari's unitary model** — the tradition did not simply absorb him.
4. **Abhyankar, *A Dictionary of Sanskrit Grammar*** (**read at first hand**): "carried to perfection by the stalwart grammarian Bhartṛhari of the **seventh century A.D.**" — the superseded dating, valuable precisely as a fixed point of the older consensus.
5. **Independence check**: IEP and Wikipedia agree on a 5th–6th c. band but with **non-overlapping** ranges (450–510 vs 485–540); they are not copies of one another, and neither is a copy of SEP's 400. Four genuinely distinct positions.

**CONTESTED**, on the dating, and the divergence has a locatable cause (one discredited external witness). Confidence **medium**: the doctrine is corroborated across three independent scholarly sources with consistent detail, but no verse of the *Vākyapadīya* was read in a critical edition or translation, and three separate questions are left explicitly open.
