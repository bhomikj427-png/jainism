## Gloss

**Right faith / right insight** — the first of the three jewels (*ratnatraya*) that together constitute the path to liberation. *TS* 1.1: *samyagdarśanajñānacāritrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ* — "right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct are the path to liberation." Samyagdarśana is foundational: it is what makes the other two *samyak* ("right"). Knowledge without right faith is mere information; conduct without it is mere behaviour. It is the gateway, conventionally the **4th guṇasthāna** (*samyagdṛṣṭi*), the first stage at which spiritual progress genuinely begins.

**Definition — *TS* 1.2:** *तत्त्वार्थश्रद्धानं सम्यग्दर्शनम्* (*tattvārthaśraddhānaṃ samyagdarśanam*) — "belief in the substances/realities ascertained as they are is right faith." Pūjyapāda glosses *tattva* as the intrinsic nature of a substance and *artha* as its ascertainment: right faith is conviction in the **seven tattvas** (jīva, ajīva, āsrava, bandha, saṃvara, nirjarā, mokṣa) as they actually are — including that souls and non-souls exist, the cosmos is beginningless and uncreated, and substances transform by their own nature. It is *conviction*, not blind belief: faith in the correct ontology, not faith despite evidence.

**Two modes of arising — *TS* 1.3:** *तन्निसर्गादधिगमाद्वा* (*tan nisargād adhigamād vā*) — right faith arises either by *nisarga* (spontaneously, from the internal subsidence/destruction of the faith-deluding *darśana-mohanīya* karma, without instruction) or by *adhigama* (acquisition, through teaching about the substances). Both require the internal karmic cause; they differ only in whether external instruction triggers it. Pūjyapāda also distinguishes *sarāga* right faith (with-attachment — marked by *praśama* tranquillity, *saṃvega* fear of saṃsāra, *anukampā* compassion, *āstikya* conviction) from *vītarāga* right faith (without-attachment — pure soul-consciousness).
