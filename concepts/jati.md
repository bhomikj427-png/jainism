## Gloss

The Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika theory of **universals** (*jāti* in Nyāya usage; *sāmānya* in Vaiśeṣika). A universal is defined as "eternal, one, and exists in many" (Annambhaṭṭa, *Tarkasamgraha*): the same cowhood (*gotva*) inheres in every individual cow, providing the metaphysical basis for the common designation. Unlike tropes or mental concepts, sāmānya is a **real, distinct ontological category** — one of the six Vaiśeṣika padārthas (substance, quality, motion, universal, particular, inherence).

**Function in inference**: causal regularities are relations between universals, not bare particulars. When inferring fire from smoke, the invariable connection (*vyāpti*) holds because "smokiness" and "fieriness" are universals that are always co-instantiated. Without real universals, vyāpti has no ground. Udayana's argument: causality is uniform and necessary; this uniformity cannot reside in unrepeatable particulars; therefore universals must be real.

**Three defining criteria**: (1) eternality (*nitya*) — not created when an instance is; (2) unity (*eka*) — one universal, not one per instance; (3) inherence (*samavāya*) — the universal inheres in its instances through a distinct real relation. These criteria exclude pseudo-universals (*upādhi*): a co-extensional property is not a separate universal; individuators (*viśeṣa*) do not form a higher universal; inherence itself has no universal.
