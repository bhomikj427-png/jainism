## Gloss

The Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika theory of **universals** (*jāti* in Nyāya usage; *sāmānya* in Vaiśeṣika). A universal is defined as "eternal, one, and exists in many" (Annambhaṭṭa, *Tarkasamgraha*): the same cowhood (*gotva*) inheres in every individual cow, providing the metaphysical basis for the common designation. Unlike tropes or mental concepts, sāmānya is a **real, distinct ontological category** — one of the six Vaiśeṣika padārthas (substance, quality, motion, universal, particular, inherence).

**Function in inference**: causal regularities are relations between universals, not bare particulars. When inferring fire from smoke, the invariable connection (*vyāpti*) holds because "smokiness" and "fieriness" are universals that are always co-instantiated. Without real universals, vyāpti has no ground. Udayana's argument: causality is uniform and necessary; this uniformity cannot reside in unrepeatable particulars; therefore universals must be real.

**Three defining criteria**: (1) eternality (*nitya*) — not created when an instance is; (2) unity (*eka*) — one universal, not one per instance; (3) inherence (*samavāya*) — the universal inheres in its instances through a distinct real relation. These criteria exclude pseudo-universals (*upādhi*): a co-extensional property is not a separate universal; individuators (*viśeṣa*) do not form a higher universal; inherence itself has no universal.

### ⚠ Contested from inside the school (added Batch 46)

This file's account of universals is **Udayana's** — the *jāti-bādhaka* exclusion criteria that say when no genuine universal can exist. Batch 46 found that the school's most formidable later logician **attacked it**.

Ganeri: "Some Naiyāyikas, especially **Bhāsarvajña and [[raghunatha-siromani|Raghunātha]]**, reject the **'cross-connection'** [*saṅkara*] impediment, pointing out the problems encountered trying to apply it to non-biological natural kinds (e.g. **redness and greenness overlap, as do pothood and goldhood**). **Raghunātha suggests that consistent application of this principle would eliminate virtually all universals from the ontology.**"

That is an internal *reductio*: apply Udayana's rule honestly and Nyāya realism about universals empties out. **So what this file records is not "the Nyāya theory of universals" but one dominant statement of it, contested by name within the tradition.**

⚠ Ganeri adds an assessment of his own, which is **not** Raghunātha's: Udayana's criterion "is thus not a necessary one, and neither is it sufficient" — none of the impediments explains why *blue/green* is more natural than Goodman's *grue/bleen*. Recorded as the modern commentator's verdict, kept separate from the historical dispute.

⚠ Ganeri also reports **Matilal (1971: 76)** *speculating* that "one might discover in the Nyāya doctrine of generic property a remote influence of the socio-religious ideas of the Brahmins" — the non-overlapping principle read as opposing cross-breeding. **Ganeri marks this as speculation and so does this file.** It is recorded because it is in the literature, not because it is established; no weight is placed on it.
