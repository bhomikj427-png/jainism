## Gloss

Śāntarakṣita (725–788 CE) is the major synthesizer who fused three previously distinct Buddhist streams: Nāgārjuna's **Madhyamaka** (emptiness), Asaṅga's **Yogācāra/Cittamātra** (mind-only), and the **Dignāga–Dharmakīrti pramāṇa** tradition (logic and epistemology). Later Tibetan doxography names the resulting school **Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Madhyamaka**. His two principal works are the *Madhyamakālaṃkāra* ("Ornament of the Middle Way," with his own *vṛtti*) and the encyclopedic *Tattvasaṃgraha* (~3,600 verses), which analyses and refutes every major Indian school, Buddhist and non-Buddhist.

**The defining move — a two-storey ontology graded by the two truths:** Śāntarakṣita accepts Yogācāra's mind-only (*cittamātra*) and reflexive awareness (*svasaṃvedana*) **only at the level of conventional truth** (*saṃvṛti*), and then applies Madhyamaka emptiness to consciousness *itself* at the ultimate level (*paramārtha*). MA 92 states the program exactly: "By relying on the Mind-Only system, know that external entities do not exist. And by relying on this Middle Way system, know that no self exists at all, even in that [mind]." Mind-only is thus a *better* analysis of the conventional — it dissolves external objects — but it is not the final word, because the mind is itself empty of intrinsic nature.

**The "ladder" / sliding scale of analysis:** the *Madhyamakālaṃkāra* leads the reader up successive standpoints — Sautrāntika external realism → Yogācāra mind-only (which refutes external objects) → Madhyamaka (which refutes the ultimate reality of mind too). Each lower view is provisionally adopted, then transcended: "shifting provisionalities." This is soteriological pedagogy, not eclecticism.

**The core emptiness argument — *neither-one-nor-many* (*ekānekasvabhāva*):** anything with intrinsic nature must be either truly singular or truly plural (the two are jointly exhaustive); Śāntarakṣita argues nothing can be either; therefore nothing has intrinsic nature. MA 1: "These entities … have no inherent nature at all because in reality they have neither a singular nor manifold nature, like a reflection." This is a **Svātantrika** procedure — an independent (*svatantra*) positive inference using Dharmakīrti's logical machinery — which is exactly what Prāsaṅgika (Candrakīrti) rejects.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Śāntarakṣita"): gives MA 1, 61–62, 91, 92 with translations; the three-tier ladder (Sautrāntika 1–15 / Yogācāra 16–44 / Madhyamaka 45+); the apoha defense via three negation-types against Kumārila; Kamalaśīla (740–795) as student. **Signal 2** (Encyclopedia of Buddhism, "Śāntarakṣita"): independently confirms dates, both works, the "Yogācāra-Svātantrika-Mādhyamika" label, the neither-one-nor-many "like a reflected image" argument, and the conventional/ultimate split (Yogācāra as "the superior way of analyzing conventional truth"). The two agree on every substantive point.
