## Gloss

**Pramoda** (Sanskrit, "joy, delight, gladness") is the **second of the four right sentiments (bhāvanās)** of **Tattvārtha Sūtra 7.11**: *maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni ca…* It is **joy at the sight of the virtuous** — delight (and reverence) toward beings who are better qualified or more advanced on the path of liberation than oneself.

Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi* glosses pramoda as **"fervent affection together with veneration in the presence of the virtuous (guṇa-adhika)."** The object is specified in the verse by *guṇādhika* — those superior in virtue/qualities. Pramoda is therefore the deliberate cultivation of gladness at others' spiritual excellence, the antidote to envy and to the pride (*māna*, a kaṣāya) that resents another's superiority.

**Cross-tradition note (parallel, not identity).** Pramoda is structurally the Jain counterpart of Buddhist **muditā** (sympathetic/altruistic joy, the third brahmavihāra) — both are the cultivated gladness at others' good that opposes envy. But pramoda is aimed specifically at the *virtuous/spiritually advanced*, while muditā more broadly meets another's good fortune of any kind; and each sits in a different soteriology. Parallel, not identity.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **TS 7.11 — directly fetched** (Jainworld translation): "joy at the sight of the virtuous" as the second right sentiment; verse text *maitrī-pramoda-kāruṇya-mādhyasthāni…*
2. **Wikipedia, "Maitrī"** (search) + classical-commentary gloss: pramoda = "fervent affection as well as veneration in the presence of the virtuous"; object specified as *guṇādhika* (those superior in virtue).
3. **Jainism Simplified, Ch.13 (Bhavanas)**: independent exposition of pramoda as appreciation/joy toward the more advanced.

Three signals converge on definition, the *guṇādhika* object, and the source-verse. Confidence medium: verse fetched; Sarvārthasiddhi gloss via secondary quotation.
