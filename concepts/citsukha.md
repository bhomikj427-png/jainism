## Gloss / Divergence map

**Citsukha** (*cit* + *sukha*, "joy of consciousness"), **c. 1220 CE**, pupil of **Gauḍeśvarācārya — also called Jñānottama** — and guru of **Sukhaprakāśa Muni**, is the Advaita dialectician whose ***Tattvapradīpikā***, universally known as the ***Citsukhī***, does two jobs at once: it **dismantles the Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika categories** and it **supplies Advaita with formal definitions of its own terms**. Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum* records the work also as *Pratyaktattvadīpikā* / *Tattvadīpikā*.

The four chapters run: (1) Vedāntic concepts — self-revelation (*svaprakāśa*), the self as consciousness, ignorance, falsity (*mithyātva*); (2) the systematic refutation of the Nyāya categories; (3) the possibility of realizing Brahman; (4) the nature of final liberation.

### The finding: Citsukha glossed both sides of a dispute this corpus keeps apart

The corpus already holds most of Citsukha's bibliography as **separate nodes**, and had not noticed that one man wrote on all of them. Set his commentaries against the files that already exist:

| *Abhiprāyaprakāśikā* | Maṇḍana's *Brahmasiddhi* | [[mandana-mishra]] |
| (commentary) | Sureśvara's *Naiṣkarmyasiddhi* | [[sureshvara]] |
| (commentary) | Śrīharṣa's *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya* | [[sriharsa]] — **written Batch 45**; SEP names the commentary the *Khaṇḍanabhāvadīpikā* |
| (commentary) | Ānandabodha's *Nyāyamakaranda* | — (unwritten) |

Rows 2 and 3 are the interesting ones. [[sureshvara]] and [[mandana-mishra]] are joined in this corpus by an `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` edge, drawn precisely because **their stated doctrines oppose**: avidyā located in Brahman for Sureśvara, in the jīva for Maṇḍana; knowledge directly liberating versus meditation required. The corpus keeps them apart on purpose, so that the traditional legend identifying the two men is *taught* rather than assumed.

Citsukha wrote on **both**.

That is a fact worth stating carefully, because it is easy to over-read. It does **not** show the dispute was regarded as settled, nor that Citsukha harmonised the two, nor which side he took — **neither commentary was read in this pass, and no source consulted says what either argues.** What it does show is that by c. 1220 both texts were live enough, and canonical enough, to be worth glossing by the same author; the opposition the corpus records is an opposition *within* a body of literature that one commentator treated as his own. The corpus records the fact and files the question: **whose locus of avidyā does the *Abhiprāyaprakāśikā* defend?** That is a well-posed question this node cannot answer.

### Definition as a philosophical move

Dasgupta's characterisation is the sharpest thing said about him and it is a claim about *method*: Citsukha "had all Śrīharṣa's powers of acute dialectical thought" but went further by **formally defining key Vedānta concepts, not merely refuting Nyāya positions**.

That distinction matters. Śrīharṣa's *Khaṇḍanakhaṇḍakhādya* is destructive: it attacks Nyāya's definitions and does not offer replacements, on the principle that the opponent's failure is the whole argument. Citsukha keeps the destructive apparatus and then **defines anyway** — *svaprakāśa* as "that which is entitled to be called immediate, though it is not an object of any cognition"; *mithyātva* as "the non-existence of a thing in that which is considered to be its cause."

Read the *svaprakāśa* definition slowly and the shape of the problem shows: consciousness must be admitted as **immediate without being an object**, because anything that is an object requires a further consciousness to cognise it, and the regress has to stop. The definition is engineered to that constraint. It is not a description of an experience; it is a term built to a specification, which is what makes Citsukha a definer rather than a describer.

### Nāgārjuna's method, and the line drawn against it

Two independent sources record that Citsukha, like Śrīharṣa, "makes effective use of the dialectical method seen in the works of **Nāgārjuna**" — and that "both acknowledge this fact, but **criticize the madhyamaka school for not affirming the ultimate reality of brahman**."

This is the single most valuable datum on the node, because it is the **tradition drawing the line itself**, in advance of every modern argument about whether Advaita is crypto-Buddhism. The Advaitins are recorded as conceding the *technique* and refusing the *conclusion*. Method-sharing is not doctrine-sharing, and here the identity of the borrowed method is not an inference by a later comparativist — it is acknowledged by the borrowers, alongside the refusal. The edge to [[madhyamaka]] below is therefore drawn as the sanctioned pair: **structurally parallel, explicitly not equivalent.**

### Where the sources wobble

- **Date.** Dasgupta: "about A.D. 1220." advaita-vedanta.org: 13th c. Hindupedia's dedicated page: "early thirteenth century." But **Hindupedia is internally inconsistent** — as quoted in the Wisdomlib aggregation it says "12th century CE." The 13th-century placement carries the weight (three sources, one of them Dasgupta) and the 12th-century stray is noted rather than averaged in.
- **A name-collision in the lexicon.** Monier-Williams's *Citsukha* is "Name of a scholiast on Bhāgavata-purāṇa (pupil of Śaṃkarācārya)." That is **not this man** — a Bhāgavata commentator is a different literary role, and the discipleship claim, on any reading, places him elsewhere. The lexicon entry is recorded here so the corpus does not later mistake one for the other; **no attempt is made to identify who MW's Citsukha was**, which no source consulted addresses.
- **The commentary list is ragged.** advaita-vedanta.org gives three (Khaṇḍana, Brahmasiddhi, Naiṣkarmyasiddhi); Hindupedia gives five; Dasgupta adds independent treatises on inference and epistemology. The lists are compatible — each is a subset of the fullest — but no two agree on the total, and none is presented as exhaustive.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — **Dasgupta**, *History of Indian Philosophy* vol. 2 (a standard scholarly history, 1922, pre-web); **the Advaita Vedānta Home Page** (1999, ITRANS, independent of the Wikipedia era and already used in [[amalananda]] and [[sarvajnatman]]); **Hindupedia**; and — the best of them for this node — **Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum***, quoted through Wisdomlib. These are four genuinely different documentary traditions, and they are not reworkings of one another.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor, and unusually well served: Citsukha *is* a commentator, his own base texts are named, and his *Tattvapradīpikā* in turn drew a commentary from **Pratyagbhagavān c. 1400**, so the stack is visible in both directions.
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — *cit* + *sukha*, "joy of consciousness"; Monier-Williams consulted, and it returned a **different person of the same name** (see above), which is exactly the hard constraint a lexicon is for.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/` against his bibliography. Three of his five named commentary-targets are already nodes, and two of those three ([[mandana-mishra]], [[sureshvara]]) are joined by a deliberate NOT-equivalent edge. The sweep is what produced this node's central finding; it was not visible from any single source.
5. **Provenance** — **Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum*** is a 19th-century critical manuscript catalogue and the strongest provenance available here. It independently confirms the full lineage chain — "pupil of Gauḍeśvarācārya, guru of Sukhaprakāśa Muni" — that [[amalananda]] had to assemble from three reference pages. This is a **corpus-level result**: a genealogy pieced together from institutional web pages in Batch 43 is now corroborated from a manuscript catalogue, and the Amalānanda ← Sukhaprakāśa ← Citsukha chain no longer rests on the weaker layer.

**CONVERGED** on date (c. 1220), teacher, pupil, principal work and its double function. Confidence **medium**, not high, for reasons this node states rather than hides: **no work of Citsukha's was read** — every doctrinal statement here is a report of a report, including the two definitions, which reach this file through Dasgupta's paraphrase and not from the *Citsukhī*; the commentary list differs between sources; and one source contradicts itself on the century.

⚠ **Corroborated Batch 45, from a genuinely independent direction.** Dasgupta (1932) is quoted above saying Citsukha "had all Śrīharṣa's powers of acute dialectical thought" but went further by *defining* rather than only refuting. Nilanjan Das, *SEP* "Śrīharṣa" (2018) — a different author, 86 years later, writing about [[sriharsa|Śrīharṣa]] rather than about Citsukha — independently records that Citsukha "developed arguments from this work further" in the *Tattvapradīpikā*, and names his commentary on the *Khaṇḍana* as the *Khaṇḍanabhāvadīpikā*. **Two independent sources agreeing on both the dependence and its direction** — a 1932 history and a 2018 encyclopedia entry, neither derivable from the other. The Śrīharṣa → Citsukha dependence is now the best-attested claim in this file.
