## Gloss / Divergence map

**Govinda Bhagavatpāda** (Govindapāda) occupies the one link in the Advaita lineage that **must** exist and is **least attested**: he is [[gaudapada|Gauḍapāda]]'s disciple and [[shankara|Śaṅkara]]'s guru, the joint that makes the *paramaguru* relation between the other two possible. The chain is stated identically everywhere: **Gauḍapāda → Govinda Bhagavatpāda → Śaṅkara**, and he "is named after Gauḍapāda in the Guru Paramparā of Śṛṅgeri Śāradā Pīṭham."

And that is close to all there is. The reporting sources state the position plainly:

> "**Little is known of his life and works, except that he is mentioned in all the traditional accounts (Śaṅkara-vijayams) as the teacher of Ādi Śaṅkara.**"

He is a **lineage-position with a name attached** — required by the genealogy, documented by the genealogy, and by nothing outside it. This is the strongest instance in the corpus of the pattern [[vyasa]], [[valmiki]] and [[kapila]] each show in weaker form: *the further back a tradition's authority reaches, the more its early links are constituted by the reaching.*

### The Narmada episode

The *Mādhavīya Śaṅkaravijaya* has them meet at **Omkareshwar** on the Narmada. Govinda asks the young Śaṅkara who he is; Śaṅkara answers with an extempore verse stating Advaita, and is initiated. In the best-known version Śaṅkara **stops a Narmada flood by setting his *kamaṇḍalu* in its path**, saving the guru who lay in samādhi in a cave nearby. The cave is shown at Omkareshwar today (Govindeśvara Gufā).

The episode is the reason the corpus can say something useful about him after all: **its function is to establish that Śaṅkara already possessed the doctrine before he was taught it.** The disciple arrives complete; the guru's role is to recognise, not to transmit. A hagiography that needed to prove doctrinal *descent* would have written the scene the other way round. So the story authenticates **Śaṅkara's originality** while formally preserving the lineage — which is exactly the double duty the Advaita tradition needed done, and a reason to treat the whole episode as **constructed to a purpose** rather than remembered.

### Why the low confidence is the finding

This node is `confidence: low` **by evidence, not by effort**. Five searches/fetches returned: one Wikipedia article, its handwiki mirror (a **derivative copy — not a second signal**), a wisdomlib definition stub, a devotional site, and the alchemical-attribution thread. No critical edition, no inscription, no independent text, no date. Under §8 this would justify `blocked`; it is recorded as `contested` instead because the *disagreements themselves* are well enough attested to be mapped, and because the absence is informative: **the Advaita lineage's own middle link has no evidence independent of the lineage.**

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Wikipedia, "Govindapada"/"Govinda Bhagavatpada"** (fetched): the Gauḍapāda→Govinda→Śaṅkara chain; Gauḍ Sāraswat Brahmin community; the *Śaṅkara-vijayams* as the sole attestation; the *Mādhavīya* Omkareshwar/Narmada meeting with the *kamaṇḍalu* and the cave; **incarnation of Śeṣa**. Explicitly **no** dates, **no** works, **no** discussion of historicity.
2. **HandWiki, "Biography:Govinda Bhagavatpada"** (fetched): a **derivative mirror** of signal 1 — treated as *not* independent, and noted as such. It adds two items signal 1 lacks: the "**little is known of his life and works**" formulation, and the **first verse of the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*** as the one textual naming — the latter valuable precisely because the corpus can already discount it.
3. **Search aggregation** (the Bhāratīya Vidvat Pariṣat thread "Identity of Govindabhagavatpāda, author of Rasahṛdayatantra"; the Internet Archive scan of the MLBD *Rasahṛdaya-tantra* with Chaturbhuja Miśra's *Mugdhabodhinī*; wisdomlib; a devotional Omkareshwar site): the **9th-century alchemical attribution**, the fact that its identity with Śaṅkara's guru is **an open scholarly question rather than a settled one**, and the Patañjali-reincarnation tradition together with the shared Śeṣa motif that generates it.
4. **Wikipedia, "Raseśvara"** (fetched, independently authored): the three root texts (*Rasārṇava*, *Rasahṛdaya*, *Raseśvara-siddhānta*), the Cowell & Gough attribution "composed by Govinda Bhagavat and Sarvajña Rāmeśvara," the school's origin "**about the commencement of the Christian era**," and the doctrine — liberation "only… by using mercury to acquire an imperishable body," *pārada* glossed as "the means of conveyance beyond transmigratory existence." This is the signal that turns the alchemical attribution from a curiosity into a **dating conflict**.
5. **Corpus cross-check** ([[shankara]], [[gaudapada]], [[vidyaranya]]): the *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi*'s rejected attribution and the *Śaṅkara-vijaya* genre's late, constructed character were both established in Batches 40–41 from different source-sets, and both bear directly here.

**CONTESTED**, and `confidence: low`. Only one genuinely independent biographical source exists (signal 2 being a mirror of 1); the two substantive attributions — the alchemical authorship and the Patañjali identity — are respectively **disputed** and **motif-generated**; and the sole textual naming is in a work of disputed authorship. Nothing here should be upgraded without a critical study of the *Rasahṛdaya-tantra*'s authorship question.
