## Gloss / Divergence map

**Damascius** (c. 462 – after 538 CE) was the **last scholarch of the Athenian Neoplatonic school**, heading it from c. 515 until **Justinian's 529 CE edict closed it**; he then left for the Persian court of Khosrow I. His major work, ***Problems and Solutions concerning First Principles***, is an **aporetic** treatise whose signature move is to posit an utterly transcendent, **unknowable "Ineffable" (*arrhēton*) prior to and "uncoordinated with" the One**. His argument is radical: the first principle so exceeds intelligible being that grasping it forces the reasoning soul to **suspend the principle of non-contradiction** — to admit contradictory statements as true of what lies beyond all cognition. Methodologically he **critically extends [[proclus|Proclus]]** (he studied under Marinus, Proclus's successor) by turning the Proclan hierarchy against itself, ever mindful of the limits of dialectic.

**Nuance (recorded).** Damascius is often read as steering the school **away from [[theurgy|theurgy]] toward textual study and aporetic dialectic** — though his treatise still bears on theurgy and epistemology. His De principiis is also noted as conspicuously free of anti-Christian polemic.
