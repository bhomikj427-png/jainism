## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kalki** is the **tenth and future [[avatara-vedanta|daśāvatāra]]**, the not-yet-arrived incarnation of [[vishnu|Viṣṇu]] who will appear at the close of the current **Kali Yuga** to end it and inaugurate a new **Satya/Kṛta Yuga**. The core cosmological role — riding a white horse, wielding a sword, exterminating thieves and the wicked (especially *mlecchas*), and re-establishing dharma — is attested from the **Mahābhārata** forward and is stable across the tradition. Two confidence tiers need to be kept separate rather than blended:

| tier | content | attestation |
|---|---|---|
| **high** | 10th/future avatāra; ends Kali Yuga; white horse, sword; destroys the wicked; restores dharma | Mahābhārata onward — the converged core |
| **medium** | born to **Viṣṇuyaśas** and **Sumatī** in **Śambhala** village; horse named Devadatta; training under Paraśurāma; gifts from Śiva; marriages; wars against Koka/Vikoka | Viṣṇu Purāṇa → Bhāgavata Purāṇa → Kalki Purāṇa — a **genealogical chain of textual elaboration**, not three independently triangulated witnesses (§4's "copies are not confirmation" applies directly here); the Mahābhārata's own prophecy is thinner (deeds only), and this research pass could not independently confirm the village/parentage/horse-name are present in the Mahābhārata itself rather than read back into it from the later Purāṇic paraphrase |

**Contested points, mapped rather than smoothed:**

| point | divergence | source |
|---|---|---|
| etymology | *kalka* ("filth/sin") + destroyer, vs. a root tied to "white horse" — unresolved, disputed even within Sanskrit lexicography | Wikipedia "Kalki," citing the philological disagreement |
| who ends Kali Yuga | most Purāṇas: Kalki. **Vāyu Purāṇa** 2.36–37 instead assigns this to a distinct figure, **Pramīti** | González-Reimann, cited via Wikipedia "Kalki" |
| pan-Puranic systematization | some texts (e.g. **Yuga Purāṇa**) omit Kalki entirely; no single tidy scheme unifies all sources | Dimmitt, cited via Wikipedia "Kalki" |

Kalki's **ordinal position as tenth** is, notably, stable across every list found — even where the 8th/9th slots vary by swapping Balarāma, Kṛṣṇa, and Buddha (as already noted for [[parashurama|Paraśurāma]]'s neighboring slots), the terminal/10th position is untouched.

**Cross-tradition note (typed honestly, not identified):** the Buddhist **Kālacakra Tantra** names a line of righteous Śambhala-based kings "Kalki" (culminating in Rudra Cakrin) — Donald Lopez states the Buddhist usage "likely borrowed [the term] from Hindu Vedic texts," i.e. a vocabulary borrowing, not an independent coinage. Separately, **Maitreya** (the future Buddha) is a *structurally* comparable eschatological-savior figure in comparative-religion scholarship — but Maitreya has no concept file in this repository yet, so no link is drawn this batch; a future `structurally-parallel-to` edge is a candidate once that node exists. Neither comparison licenses treating Kalki and any Buddhist figure as the same figure.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Mahābhārata, Vana Parva 3.188–189 (Mārkaṇḍeya's prophecy)** — the earliest attestation; independent of the Puranic chain below. Content: exterminates robbers/thieves, gifts the earth to Brāhmaṇas at a horse-sacrifice, restores the dharma ordained by Brahmā, retires to a forest. This is the load-bearing convergence anchor.
2. **Viṣṇu Purāṇa, Bk. 4, ch. 24** (H.H. Wilson tr.) — "Kalki will be born in the family of Viṣṇuyaśas, an eminent Brāhman of Sambhala village... will destroy all the Mlechchhas and thieves... and will then reestablish righteousness upon earth." First appearance of the parentage/village detail.
3. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa 12.2.18–20** — corroborates Viṣṇuyaśas and Śambhala, adds the mother's name Sumatī, absent from the Viṣṇu Purāṇa passage — an elaboration, not an independent second witness.
4. **Kalki Purāṇa** — a late, dedicated text (Doniger dates the tradition to 1500–1700 CE, with a Dhaka manuscript fixing a floruit of 1726 CE); massively expands the biography (Paraśurāma as martial teacher, Śiva's gifts including the horse Devadatta, marriages to Padmāvatī and Rāma, wars against Koka/Vikoka). Read as the terminus of the elaboration chain rather than a fourth independent source.
5. **Wikipedia, "Kalki"** — used as an aggregator/pointer to named scholarship (Doniger, Dimmitt, González-Reimann, Lopez, Mitchiner), which supplied the contested points above; treated as a lead to named scholars, not itself a primary source.

Verdict: **converged** on the cosmological core (high confidence), with the biographical layer held at **medium** confidence specifically because the sourcing chain from Viṣṇu Purāṇa through the Kalki Purāṇa is a case of textual inheritance rather than triangulated independent agreement — exactly the trap §4 flags. Overall file confidence set to **medium** to reflect the blend honestly rather than rounding up.
