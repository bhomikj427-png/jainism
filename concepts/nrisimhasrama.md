## Gloss / Divergence map

**Nṛsiṃhāśrama**, 16th century, is the Advaita saṃnyāsin who commented his way across the **entire [[prakasatman|Vivaraṇa]] stack** and left, on his own tradition's assessment, no new doctrine at all. Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum* records him as "**pupil of Gīrvāṇendra Sarasvatī and Jagannāthāśrama, guru of Nārāyaṇāśrama**" — and separately as guru of Mahīdhara and of Saccidānandāśrama, so his teaching line ran wide.

His works, as the sources jointly give them:

| work | what it is | corpus node |
|---|---|---|
| ***Bhāvaprakāśikā*** / *Pañcapādikāvivaraṇaprakāśikā* | on Prakāśātman's *Vivaraṇa* — **completed at Puruṣottamapura in 1547** | [[prakasatman]] |
| ***Vedāntaratnakoṣa*** | on Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā* | [[padmapada]] |
| ***Tattvabodhinī*** | on Sarvajñātman's *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka* | [[sarvajnatman]] |
| ***Bhedadhikkāra*** | "Condemnation of difference" — polemic against the Madhva school | [[dvaita-vedanta]] |
| *Advaitadīpikā*, *Tattvaviveka*, *Advaitabodhadīpikā* | independent *prakaraṇa-grantha*s | — |

### The finding: he glossed the Vivaraṇa side entire, and the corpus now has both halves

[[amalananda]] established that the two Advaita sub-schools are **parallel commentarial stacks** — sūtra, bhāṣya, then a sub-commentary the school is named for. Nṛsiṃhāśrama wrote on **storey 3 and storey 4 of the Vivaraṇa side** (Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā*, Prakāśātman's *Vivaraṇa*) and on [[sarvajnatman|Sarvajñātman]]'s *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka* besides. He is the Vivaraṇa side's systematic commentator.

Set that against [[citsukha]], written earlier in this same batch, and a symmetry appears that neither node could see alone:

| | [[citsukha]] (c. 1220) | **Nṛsiṃhāśrama** (16th c.) |
|---|---|---|
| glossed | Śaṅkara's bhāṣya, Maṇḍana's *Brahmasiddhi*, Sureśvara's *Naiṣkarmyasiddhi*, Śrīharṣa, Ānandabodha | Padmapāda's *Pañcapādikā*, Prakāśātman's *Vivaraṇa*, Sarvajñātman's *Saṃkṣepaśārīraka* |
| covers | the **early** post-Śaṅkara generation, across its internal quarrels | the **Vivaraṇa stack**, top to bottom |
| own contribution | formal definitions (*svaprakāśa*, *mithyātva*) — Dasgupta says he went beyond Śrīharṣa | *"has not put forward any new interpretation of the Vedānta, yet as a commentator he is superb"* |

Both men are hubs. The difference is what a hub can be: one added an apparatus of definitions the school did not have, and the other added nothing and is praised anyway. **"Superb commentator, no new interpretation" is offered by the source as a compliment**, and it is worth pausing on rather than passing over. A tradition that can say that has a settled view of what commentary *is for* — and it is not originality. This corpus's Ch 34 is built on exactly that question; this node is the cleanest datum it has for it.

Doctrinally he sits where his commentaries sit: he takes *ajñāna* as "the beginningless primal cause of the universe and as one which is removable by the intuitive knowledge of Brahman," and he adopts **the Vivaraṇa school's definition of *mithyātva***. Consistent, and consistent with having chosen those base texts.

### Two wobbles the sources leave open

**1. The floruit does not fit the dated work.** *Preceptors of Advaita* has him "flourish[ing] in the **second half** of the sixteenth century," and advaita-vedanta.org calls him "an extremely influential teacher in the 16th century." But Aufrecht records the *Pañcapādikāvivaraṇaprakāśikā* as **completed in 1547** — the first half. The two are reconcilable (a long career, with a major work at its start), but they are not the same claim, and the "second half" placement should not be repeated as though it were unopposed. A manuscript colophon outweighs a floruit estimate.

**2. Tamil country or Puruṣottamapura?** Hindupedia calls him "a prolific author from the **Tamil region**." Aufrecht has him completing a work at **Puruṣottamapura** — Purī, in Odisha, most of a subcontinent away. Nothing here is contradictory: a saṃnyāsin travels, and where a manuscript was finished is not where its author was born. But no source consulted states his origin *and* explains the Purī colophon, so the corpus records both and asserts neither.

### The claim this node was queued to check — and it did not survive

This node entered the queue because **Ch 34 §3 states that Appayya Dīkṣita wrote the *Kalpataruparimala* at Nṛsiṃhāśrama's request.** Checking it is the first thing a node on Nṛsiṃhāśrama should do, and the result is negative:

- ***Preceptors of Advaita*, ch. 37 — a chapter devoted entirely to Nṛsiṃhāśrama — does not mention Appayya Dīkṣita at all.**
- **The Advaita Vedānta Home Page's entry does not mention him either.**
- **The Centre for Brahmavidya's page on Appayya, fetched specifically to check this, does not mention Nṛsiṃhāśrama** — it names the *Parimala* and says nothing about a request.
- The claim traces, as far as this pass can follow it, to **one 2006 mailing-list posting**, which [[appayya-dikshita]] already carries as "low provenance."

So the relationship is **plausible and unconfirmed**: two eminent Advaitins of the same century, one of whom the other might well have asked for a readable *Kalpataru*. But three sources that had every occasion to say so did not, and one that says so is a list post. The edge between the two nodes is drawn as `shares-vocabulary-with` — contemporaries in one school — and **not** as influence. Ch 34's sentence is qualified accordingly.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Three+ sources, tested for independence** — **Aufrecht's *Catalogus Catalogorum*** (19th-c. critical manuscript catalogue, via Wisdomlib); ***Preceptors of Advaita*** (a book-length treatment with a chapter on him); **The Advaita Vedānta Home Page** (1999); **Hindupedia**. Four documentary layers, and the two strongest — the catalogue and the book — are not derivable from the web sources.
2. **Commentarial tradition** — the anchor works twice here: his base texts are all identified and are all already nodes, and his own teaching line is recorded in both directions (two teachers named, three pupils named).
3. **Lexicon + morphology** — Devanagari नृसिंहाश्रम; the name is *Nṛsiṃha* (the man-lion) + *āśrama*. Note the Cologne dictionaries return only "*Nomen proprium* of a man" — no help, correctly. What **is** informative is internal: his guru **Jagannāth-āśrama** and his pupil **Nārāyaṇ-āśrama** carry the same suffix, so *-āśrama* is functioning as an **order-name across three generations**, not as a personal epithet. That is visible from these sources alone and needs no external claim about monastic orders.
4. **Internal consistency** — swept `concepts/` against his bibliography: **three** of his commentary-targets are existing nodes ([[padmapada]], [[prakasatman]], [[sarvajnatman]]), all on the Vivaraṇa side. That is what makes the Citsukha comparison above possible, and it is a result of the sweep, not of any source.
5. **Provenance** — heavily weighted to Aufrecht, which supplies the only **hard datum** on the node: a place and a year (Puruṣottamapura, 1547) for a specific work. Everything dated in this file that is not that colophon is a floruit estimate, and the file says which is which.

**CONVERGED** on identity, monastic line, works, school-alignment and doctrinal position — four independent layers agree and none contradicts another on these. Confidence **medium**: **no work of his was read**; the floruit and the colophon date disagree by a half-century; his region is unresolved; and the single claim that put him in the queue is single-sourced and, on three checks, unsupported.
