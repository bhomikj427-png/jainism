## Gloss

Kaṣāya (literally "that which gains worldly life again": *kaṣa* = worldly existence, *āya* = gain) denotes the four passions — anger (*krodha*), pride (*māna*), deceit (*māyā*), and greed (*lobha*) — that act as an adhesive causing karma-matter to stick to and bind the soul. They are the principal internal cause of *sāmparāyika* (with-passion) karma influx, and their eradication is the central mechanism of Jain soteriology.

## The 16 sub-types matrix

Each of the four passions manifests at four intensity levels, giving 16 types of conduct-deluding karma (*charitra-mohanīya*):

| Intensity | Duration / effect | Gunasthāna impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Anantānubandhi** (infinite-chain) | Lifelong; blocks right faith | Eliminated at stage 4 (right faith arises) |
| **Apratyākhyāna** (non-renounceable) | ~1 year; blocks partial vows | Eliminated at stage 5 (partial vows) |
| **Pratyākhyāna** (renounceable) | Milder; blocks full renunciation | Eliminated at stage 6 (full monastic vows) |
| **Saṃjvalana** (smoldering) | Slight; blocks perfect conduct | Eliminated at stages 10–12 |

The 4 passions × 4 intensities = 16 of the 28 sub-types of *mohanīya* karma. The other 12 sub-types are the *darśana-mohanīya* (right-faith-deluding) karmas.

## Counterpart: no-passion activity

*Akaṣāya* (no-passion) yoga produces *īryāpathika* karma — momentary, effortlessly shed, causing no bondage. A fully liberated being (siddha) has no kaṣāya; a monk at gunasthāna 13–14 is in this state.
