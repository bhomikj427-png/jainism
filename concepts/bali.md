## Gloss / Divergence map

**Bali** (honorific **Mahābali**, "the great Bali") is the daitya king — grandson of **[[narasimha|Narasiṃha]]'s** devotee **Prahlāda**, son of **Virocana**, of the same **Hiraṇyakaśipu**/Kaśyapa lineage — who conquered the three worlds through righteous rule, was outwitted rather than defeated by [[vamana|Vāmana]]'s three-pace trick, and is remembered not as a villain but as the tradition's paradigmatic **righteous asura**: sources uniformly describe his reign, even after dispossessing Indra, as an age of peace and prosperity. This file covers Bali himself — genealogy, moral standing, and cult afterlife — and does not re-derive the Trivikrama narrative already told from Vāmana's side in [[vamana]].

**The moral crux (converged):** once Bali recognizes Vāmana as Viṣṇu mid-gift, his guru **Śukrācārya** curses/warns him to renege on the vow. Bali completes the gift anyway. Bhāgavata Purāṇa 8.21 has him state he would rather lose everything than be regarded as a cheat — vow-keeping and truthfulness outrank self-preservation. This is the load-bearing philosophical point of the Bali material, distinct from the cosmological mechanics of Vāmana's expansion.

**CONTESTED — grace or punishment?** Sources genuinely split on how to frame Bali's dispossession:

| reading | what it commits you to | source |
|---|---|---|
| **grace, not punishment** | Bali is sent to rule **Sutala**, a suffering-free realm built by Viśvakarman and personally guarded by Viṣṇu; promised future Indra-hood in the Sāvarṇi Manvantara; counted among the seven **cirañjīvī** (immortals). Dispossession *is* the liberation — stripping material attachment from an already-virtuous soul | Bhāgavata Purāṇa Canto 8 (mainstream Vaiṣṇava/ISKCON reading) |
| **grace, independently attested** | Mahābhārata/Harivaṃṣa: Bali "was shorn of his sins and became immortal," and grants the boon willingly and "well-pleased" — textually independent of the Bhāgavata Purāṇa, corroborating the grace-frame from outside the Purāṇic corpus | Mahābhārata/Harivaṃśa, via Wikipedia "Vamana" |
| **harsher/older punishment strand** | some tellings (surviving alongside the grace-frame even within the Puranic corpus) have Bali literally dragged to Pātāla by Garuḍa — a non-graceful downfall, not an elevation | Wikipedia "Mahabali," noting textual variance |

**CONTESTED — a second, distinct split over Onam itself:** Bali is believed, in Kerala, to return annually to visit his former subjects on **Thiruvōṇam** (the Onam harvest festival) — but *what the festival means* is itself contested scholarship, not folklore trivia:

| reading | what it commits you to | source |
|---|---|---|
| **devotional/allegorical** | Onam is Viṣṇu's yearly grant of homecoming to a beloved, virtuous former king; some devotional writers read it allegorically as "the self who gained the Divine" | Kerala Tourism (official); Swarajya |
| **Dravidian/Dalit-historiographical** | Deva/Asura is read as an Āryan/Draviḍa proxy pair; Onam folk-songs read as resistance literature against Brahmanical/caste narrative; one scholarly strand reads the myth as an allegory for the suppression of Buddhism in Kerala by Vaiṣṇava Brahmanism. Bali entered mainstream academic attention specifically *through* Dalit historiography, not royal or Brahmanical patronage | The South First, citing K.S. Gracy and Ajay Shekhar |

Both splits are recorded as live disagreements, not resolved into a single gloss — hence overall `status: contested`.

**Etymology:** बलि (*bali*) is independently Ṛgvedic-attested with the base sense "offering, gift, oblation," later extending to tax/tribute (Manusmṛti, Mahābhārata) and propitiatory sacrifice. Whether the king's proper name *derives from* this common noun, or the reverse, is **not confirmed** by any source consulted — treated here as plausible but unverified, not asserted as established etymology.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Bhāgavata Purāṇa, Canto 8, ch. 15–23** (via wisdomlib.org translation) — the fullest genealogy and the grace-framing of Sutala; direct quote on truthfulness (<15 words, Bali on refusing to "be regarded as a cheat").
2. **Mahābhārata / Harivaṃśa** (via Wikipedia "Vamana," citing the epic text) — textually independent attestation outside the Purāṇic chain, corroborating both the genealogy and the grace-not-punishment frame ("shorn of his sins and became immortal").
3. **Wikipedia, "Mahabali"** — documents the surviving harsher/Garuḍa-drags-him-down strand, preventing the file from silently rounding the tradition to only the grace-reading.
4. **Kerala Tourism (official) + Swarajya + The South First** — three genuinely distinct vantage points (state-tourism-devotional, culturally-conservative-allegorical, and Dalit-historiographical-academic) on Onam's meaning — independent enough to render as a contested table rather than a single narrative.
5. **Wiktionary (Monier-Williams-derived)** — etymological base sense of बलि, used as a hard lexical constraint per §4 signal 3; explicitly does not resolve name-directionality.

Genealogy and moral-crux content converge across independent textual lineages (Bhāgavata + Mahābhārata/Harivaṃśa) → high sub-confidence there. But two separate, live divergences (grace-vs-punishment; devotional-vs-Dravidian Onam readings) keep the file's overall status **contested**, confidence **medium** — not low, because the divergences themselves are well-documented and mapped, not a sign of thin sourcing.
