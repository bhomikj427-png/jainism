## Gloss

Dharmāstikāya (dharma-substance, "medium of motion") is one of the six Jain dravyas. It is a single, formless, motionless, eternal, space-pervading substance that serves as the passive condition enabling movement for souls (*jīva*) and matter (*pudgala*). It does not cause or force motion — it merely renders motion possible, the way water renders a fish's swimming possible without itself swimming. The name *dharma* here is a false cognate: it has nothing to do with moral duty or cosmic order; it is a purely ontological term for this functional substance.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — TS 5.17 (directly fetched, Vijay K. Jain 2018 / Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi):** *gatisthityupagrahau dharmādharmayorupakāraḥ* — "The functions of the medium of motion (dharma) and the medium of rest (adharma) are to assist motion and rest, respectively." Passive facilitation (*upakāra*), not active causation.

**Signal 2 — TS 5.6 (directly fetched):** *ā ākāśādekadravyāṇi* — dharma, adharma, and ākāśa are each one (single, non-multiple) substances, unlike jīva and pudgala which are many. Confirms dharmāstikāya = singular substance.

**Signal 3 — Navkar Sadhana / Jainsite (Jain institutional sources):** Independently confirm the fish-in-water analogy. Vijay K. Jain's commentary gives "earth supports the horse" as the adharma analogy; popular Jain education sources prefer "tree shade" for adharma (not inconsistent — both convey passive facilitation of rest). No primary-text citation in popular sources.

**Independence:** Signals 1 and 2 are primary-text fetches from the same critical edition; independent of Signal 3 (popular institutional). Confidence medium because only one critical edition consulted (Vijay K. Jain 2018).

## Key properties

| Property | Dharmāstikāya |
|---|---|
| Number | One (singular substance) |
| Form | Formless, non-material |
| Location | Pervades entire universe (*loka*) but not *aloka* |
| Function | Passive condition for motion (*gati*) |
| Agency | Does not move itself; does not compel others |
| Parts | Spatially divisible into innumerable units (*pradeśa*) |

**Analogy (Pūjyapāda):** As water facilitates the fish's movement without itself moving or driving the fish, dharmāstikāya is the passive medium that makes movement possible for whatever is already tending to move.

## Name-trap: dharma ≠ dharma

The word *dharma* here shares its Sanskrit form with the Hindu/Buddhist concept of moral duty or cosmic order, but the referents are entirely different. Dharmāstikāya is an ontological substance discovered by Jain physicalist analysis; it bears no evaluative meaning. The conflation is a reading error produced by the shared name, not a substantive connection.
