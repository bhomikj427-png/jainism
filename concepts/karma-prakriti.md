## Gloss

*Karma-prakṛti* ("karma-nature," the *type* of karma) is the Jain classification of bound karmic matter into **eight fundamental species (mūla-prakṛti)**. This is the *prakṛti-bandha* ("nature-bondage") — one of the four aspects of every karmic bond (alongside duration, intensity, and quantity; see bandha.md). The Tattvārtha Sūtra fixes the eight in TS 8.4, **directly fetched** (Vijay K. Jain 2018, with Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*):

> **ādyo jñānadarśanāvaraṇavedanīyamohanīyāyurnāma gotrāntarāyāḥ** (TS 8.4)
> "The first [nature-bondage] is of eight kinds: knowledge-obscuring (*jñānāvaraṇa*), perception-obscuring (*darśanāvaraṇa*), feeling-producing (*vedanīya*), deluding (*mohanīya*), life-determining (*āyuḥ*), name/physique-determining (*nāma*), status-determining (*gotra*), and obstructive (*antarāya*)."

The eight split into two families: the **four *ghāti* (destructive)** karmas that directly obscure the soul's intrinsic powers, and the **four *aghāti* (non-destructive)** karmas that merely determine the conditions of embodiment. This is the master-table behind the whole karma machinery of Chapter 03 and behind the arihant/siddha distinction (arihant.md): an arihant has destroyed the four *ghāti* (hence omniscience) while the four *aghāti* keep the body going until death.

## Reasoning across signals

**Signal 1 — Tattvārtha Sūtra 8.4 (Vijay K. Jain 2018, fetched directly from WisdomLib):** Sanskrit + English of the eight-fold list; this is the *prakṛti-bandha*, the first of the four bondage-aspects of TS 8.3.

**Signal 2 — Wikipedia "Karma in Jainism":** Independently gives the same eight, explicitly grouped into four *ghāti* (jñānāvaraṇa, darśanāvaraṇa, mohanīya, antarāya) and four *aghāti* (vedanīya, āyu, nāma, gotra); flags *mohanīya* as the pivotal deluding karma.

**Independence:** A directly-fetched critical-edition verse (Signal 1) plus an independent encyclopedic source (Signal 2) agree on the eight and the ghāti/aghāti grouping. Confidence medium: the *sub-type* enumeration (below) is from secondary aggregation, not the fetched commentary.

## The eight, grouped

| family | karma | what it does | destroyed at... |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ghāti** (destructive — obscures the soul) | jñānāvaraṇa | obscures knowledge | omniscience (gunasthāna 12→13) |
| | darśanāvaraṇa | obscures perception | omniscience |
| | mohanīya | **deludes** — produces the kaṣāyas, distorts faith + conduct | the *first* ghāti to go; its fall triggers the rest |
| | antarāya | obstructs energy/capacity | omniscience |
| **aghāti** (non-destructive — shapes the body/life) | vedanīya | produces pleasure/pain sensation | only at death (→ siddha) |
| | āyuḥ | fixes lifespan | death |
| | nāma | determines body, form, physique | death |
| | gotra | determines status/family of birth | death |

**Mohanīya is the linchpin.** Of the eight, the deluding karma is the most consequential because it generates the *kaṣāyas* (passions; kashaya.md) that drive fresh influx, and it corrupts both right faith (darśana-mohanīya) and right conduct (cāritra-mohanīya). The gunasthāna ladder (Ch 03) is essentially the staged demolition of mohanīya; once it is gone, the other three ghāti karmas collapse almost at once, and omniscience (kevala-jñāna) dawns.

## Sub-natures (uttara-prakṛti) — a note on the count

Each of the eight mūla-prakṛtis subdivides into *uttara-prakṛtis* (sub-natures): traditionally jñānāvaraṇa 5, darśanāvaraṇa 9, vedanīya 2, mohanīya 28, āyu 4, gotra 2, antarāya 5, and **nāma the largest by far** (the physique-karma, with dozens of sub-types). The grand total is commonly given as **148** in the Digambara karma-literature (the *Karma-grantha* / *Gommaṭasāra* tradition), though other reckonings give 158; the figure varies by source and **was not in the fetched TS 8.4 commentary** — so it is reported here as a traditional secondary figure, not a directly-verified one. The point that survives the count-variation: the eight species fan out into a richly articulated taxonomy that the *Karma-grantha* literature works out in detail (this file gives the eight roots; the full sub-tree is beyond the directly-sourced scope).
