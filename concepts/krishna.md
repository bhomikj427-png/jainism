## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kṛṣṇa** (Sanskrit, "black, dark") is among the most widely worshipped Hindu deities: cowherd (*Govinda*/*Gopāla*), divine statesman and charioteer of Arjuna, speaker of the **Bhagavad Gītā**, and object of intense *bhakti*. His narrative spans the Mahābhārata, the Harivaṃśa appendix, and above all the Bhāgavata Purāṇa (childhood līlā in Vraja, the *rāsa-līlā* with the gopīs).

The **contested** point is not who Kṛṣṇa is but **what he ultimately is** — avatāra of Viṣṇu, or the supreme being himself:

| reading | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Kṛṣṇa is the **eighth avatāra of Viṣṇu** | mainstream Vaiṣṇavism; Viṣṇu/Bhāgavata Purāṇa framing | Viṣṇu is the source-deity; Kṛṣṇa is one (supreme) descent among the daśāvatāra |
| Kṛṣṇa is **svayaṃ bhagavān** — God in his own right, *source* of Viṣṇu | Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism (Caitanya); Bhāgavata Purāṇa 1.3.28 *kṛṣṇas tu bhagavān svayam* | the descent-relation is reversed: Viṣṇu emanates from Kṛṣṇa, not vice-versa |
| Kṛṣṇa is the saguṇa expression of nirguṇa Brahman | Advaita (Śaṅkara on the Gītā) | the personal Kṛṣṇa is real at the vyāvahārika level, ultimately non-different from attribute-less Brahman |

These are mutually incompatible about the direction of dependence between Kṛṣṇa and Viṣṇu and about whether the personal God is final.

**The critical NOT-equivalent:** "Kṛṣṇa is an avatāra of Viṣṇu" and "Kṛṣṇa is svayaṃ bhagavān, the source of Viṣṇu" are not two ways of saying the same thing — they disagree on which deity is ontologically prior.
