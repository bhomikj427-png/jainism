## Gloss / Divergence map

**Marinus** of Neapolis (b. c. 440 CE, Flavia Neapolis — modern Nablus — in Palaestina Secunda) was **[[proclus|Proclus]]'s student and direct successor (*diadochos*)** as head of the Platonic Academy in Athens, appointed during Proclus's final infirmity and continuing after his 485 CE death. During the later Christian persecution of pagan philosophers, Marinus was eventually forced to take refuge at Epidaurus, where he died.

**The *Life of Proclus* — biography as philosophical argument.** Marinus's most consequential surviving work is *Proclus, or On Happiness*, a eulogy written the year after Proclus's death (internally dated by a predicted eclipse). It is not neutral biography: it is **explicitly structured to demonstrate that Proclus achieved *eudaimonia*** by ascending a graded scale of virtues (civic → purificatory → contemplative → theurgic), and it is openly hostile to Christianity. It remains the **primary historical source** for Proclus's life precisely because it is also advocacy — a fact this file records rather than smooths over.

**Other work.** Marinus also wrote a surviving introduction/commentary on Euclid's *Data*, plus astronomical texts (including on the Milky Way). He reportedly **destroyed his own commentary on Plato's *Philebus*** at a student's (Isidore's) urging — a self-effacing act preserved in the later biographical tradition, itself illustrating how thin and second-hand the record for this period can be.
