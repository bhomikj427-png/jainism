## Gloss / Divergence map

**Kāla Bhairava** ("Bhairava" from *bhīru*, "fearsome" — "one who destroys fear / is beyond fear"; **Kāla** = "time, death, the colour black") is the **fierce, wrathful manifestation of [[shiva|Śiva]]** — "a powerful manifestation, or avatar, of Shiva." Where Naṭarāja embodies Śiva's cosmic dance and the liṅga his aniconic ground, Bhairava is Śiva in his **terrible, transgressive, time-as-destroyer aspect**: "the unyielding force of time and cosmic order," before whom "all beings are subject to time and destiny."

**Origin — the severing of Brahmā's fifth head.** When Brahmā arrogantly claimed supremacy, Śiva produced Bhairava (from "the region between his brows" / his third eye), who **decapitated Brahmā's fifth head** for the falsehood. This incurred **brahmahatyā** (the sin of brahmin-murder); the skull stuck fast to his hand, and Bhairava was condemned to **wander the three worlds as a skull-bearing beggar (the Kāpālika archetype)** until he reached **Kāśī (Vārāṇasī)**, where the skull finally fell and he was absolved.

**Kotwāl of Kāśī — guardian of time and death.** Bhairava is the **kṣetrapāla** (guardian) of sacred sites — said to guard each of the 52 Śākta pīṭhas — and supremely the **"Kotwāl (police-chief) of Kāśī."** In Vārāṇasī it is Bhairava, not Yama, who governs death: "death itself cannot enter Kashi without his sanction," and he keeps the karmic records of the city's dead. His iconography is fierce: **dog (śvāna) vāhana**, garland of skulls, serpent ornaments, human-bone dress, blazing eyes.

**Tantric apex.** In **Kashmir Śaivism's Trika system** Bhairava is not merely a fierce form but **the Supreme Reality, "synonymous with Para Brahman"** — the absolute consciousness (the *Vijñāna Bhairava Tantra* takes its name from this).
