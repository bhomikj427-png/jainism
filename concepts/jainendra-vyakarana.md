## Gloss / Divergence map

The **Jainendra Vyākaraṇa** (also *Jainendra Śabdānuśāsana*) is **[[devanandin|Devanandin's]]** rival system of Sanskrit grammar — reportedly the **first Sanskrit grammar written by a Jain monk** — built on the [[astadhyayi|Aṣṭādhyāyī]] with the Vedic material dropped and, ⭐⭐ **with almost every one of Pāṇini's technical terms replaced by a different word.**

| what is claimed | who holds it | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| Based on Pāṇini, "the section on **Vedic accent** and the rules of Pāṇini explaining **Vedic forms** being, of course, **neglected**" | **Abhyankar**, s.v. | the same profile as every other post-Pāṇinian system — ⚠ **and [[candra-vyakarana]] has already shown that this class-level claim can describe a transmission loss rather than an author's choice** |
| Available "in **two versions, one consisting of 3000 sūtras and the other of 3700 sūtras**" | **Abhyankar**, s.v. | a text of unsettled extent |
| The two are **northern (~3000)** and **southern (~3700)** recensions | ***Glimpses of the History of Sanskrit Literature*, ch. 24.4** | ⭐ the difference is **regional**, not chronological — 700 sūtras of difference across a geography |
| Principal commentary the ***Mahāvṛtti* of Abhayanandin** (9th c.); a second *Vṛtti*, the ***Laghu-jainendra* of Mahācandra** | **Abhyankar** (the *Mahāvṛtti*, and its 9th-c. date); *Glimpses* (the pair) | a full commentarial apparatus, i.e. a live school and not a curiosity |
| Devanandin wrote his **own *Nyāsa*** on it | **Abhyankar**, s.v. *Nyāsa* | ⚠ and this is where **collision #17** starts — see [[devanandin]] |

## Reasoning across signals

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ The finding, and it is a counted one

**Every mention of this grammar in the standard dictionary of Sanskrit grammar is a mention of a word it replaced.**

```
"in the Jainendra" in Abhyankar's Dictionary of Sanskrit Grammar   →  32 sentences
of which explicitly of the form "a technical/conventional term in
the Jainendra Vyākaraṇa for [Pāṇini's X]"                          →  28
```

The replaced terms, read off Abhyankar's Latin glosses, include: **the accusative case**, **the genitive case affix**, **the locative**, **singular number**, **the second person**, **root** (*dhātu*), **crude base** (*prātipadika*), **suffix/termination** (*pratyaya*), **elision** (*lopa*), **indeclinable** (*avyaya*), **long vowel** and **short vowel**, **the nasal consonants**, **the reduplicative syllable** (*abhyāsa*), **the *niṣṭhā* participles**, **the second member of a compound** (*uttarapada*), **the subordinate member** (*upasarjana*), **the causal agent**, **the *ik* pratyāhāra**, and the **guṇa/vṛddhi** pair.

⭐⭐⭐ **Including the term Pāṇini defines in his first sūtra.** Abhyankar: "a term used in the Jainendra Grammar **instead of the term *vṛddhi* of Pāṇini** which stands for ā, ai and au; cf. **P. I.1.1**." **The Aṣṭādhyāyī's opening rule is *vṛddhir ādaic*. The Jainendra changes the word in it.**

### 2. ⭐⭐⭐ And this qualifies a Batch-50 finding rather than confirming it

Batch 50's **T4** established, at first hand in Cowell's *Prākṛtaprakāśa*, that **the metalanguage crosses system boundaries** — *adhikāra*, *pratyāhāra*, the numbered *paribhāṣā*s, all shared between rival systems and even across the Sanskrit/Prākṛt line. [[paribhasha]] carries the strong form: "a shared *method for generating* them," running "the same inferential machinery over its own text."

⚠⚠ **The Jainendra is the counter-case, and it is a sharp one.** It **keeps the machinery** — it has its own Paribhāṣāpāṭha, and Abhyankar's own gloss covers **108 maxims** drawn from Abhayanandin's *Mahāvṛtti* (item ix of his census); Abhyankar cites *Jain. Pari.* 20, 66 and 85 like any other system's. **And it discards the vocabulary.**

⭐⭐ **So the corrected statement is: what crosses system boundaries is the *technique*, not the *terminology* — and the two can be separated, because one system separated them.** ⚠ **Batch 50's finding is not refuted; it is given its boundary.** *(The corpus's inference from a counted census; no source states it.)*

⚠ **And a further caution the corpus imposes on itself**: it is tempting to read the substitution as sectarian — a Jain grammar refusing Brahminical words. **Nothing consulted supports that**, Abhyankar offers no motive, and there are ordinary alternatives (brevity: several of the Jainendra's replacements are visibly **shorter** than Pāṇini's terms, which is what the *sūtra* form rewards). ⭐ **The corpus records the substitution as a fact and the motive as unknown**, and notes that its own sources are grammarians reporting equivalences, not historians explaining choices.

### 3. ⭐ What it shares, and with whom

Abhyankar treats **Jainendra, Śākaṭāyana, Cāndra and Haima** as a family that can be cited against each other rule for rule — e.g. the term for *karman*/object: "**Cāndra II.1.43; Jainendra I.2.119; Śāk. IV.3.120; Hem. III.3.31**"; the term for a **conflict of two rules**: "Jain. 1.2.39, Śāk. 1.1.46, Hema. VII.4.119"; the singular-number term "found used in the **Jainendra, Śākaṭāyana and Haima** grammars."

⭐⭐ **Note who those are: [[sakatayana-jain|a Yāpanīya Jain]], [[hemacandra|a Śvetāmbara Jain]], [[candragomin|a Buddhist]], and a Digambara Jain.** ⚠ **The corpus does not claim a shared Jain grammatical tradition** — Abhyankar's groupings are a lexicographer's convenience, and the Cāndra sits inside them. What it can say is that the four systems are **mutually citable at the level of the individual rule**, which is what makes the vocabulary substitution *visible* at all: someone had to build the concordance.

### 4. ⚠ Two recensions, and a pattern now on its third instance

**3000 sūtras in the north, 3700 in the south.** ⭐ Batch 49/50 found the *Prākṛtaprakāśa* in an **eight-chapter** and a **twelve-chapter** recension, and [[haradatta]] found that "Pāṇinian grammar turns out to have **regions**." ⭐⭐ **This is a third grammar that exists in more than one size, and the second where the sizes are geographical.** ⚠ **The corpus does not know which recension is Devanandin's**, whether the 700 sūtras were added or lost, or whether either published version corresponds to the *Mahāvṛtti*'s text. **[[prakrta-prakasha]]'s lesson applies directly: an absent block at the end of a text is where "added" and "lost" are hardest to tell apart, and the corpus should not guess.**

### 5. The apparatus, and what it says about the school's life

**Abhayanandin's *Mahāvṛtti*** (9th c., per Abhyankar) is the principal commentary; **Mahācandra's *Laghu-jainendra*** is a second; Devanandin's **own *Nyāsa*** stands over the sūtras; and Abhyankar has an entry for **a 12th-century Jain grammarian of Arjurika near Kolhapur** who wrote a gloss on a Jainendra commentary. ⭐ **Sūtra, autocommentary, vṛtti, sub-gloss — the same four-tier stack the Pāṇinian tradition built, assembled independently around a different root text**, and [[kaiyata]]'s authority-ladder observation transfers whole.

### Independence (§4 signal 1)

**Abhyankar 1961**, read at first hand and overwhelmingly — the structure, the two versions, the *Mahāvṛtti*, the authorship, the Paribhāṣā census, and **the 32-sentence term census, which is this node's headline and which was taken by the corpus rather than reported by him**. ***Glimpses of the History of Sanskrit Literature*, ch. 24.4** — an independent secondary book already used by [[katantra]] and [[candra-vyakarana]] — for the **north/south** recensions and the second *Vṛtti*. ⚠⚠ **No line of the grammar itself has been read**, in any edition or translation; **everything here is about the text rather than in it.**

**CONTESTED**, confidence **medium**. Contested on extent (two recensions, 700 sūtras apart), on which is the author's, and on the meaning of the substitution. Firm and countable: the substitution itself, the commentarial stack, the authorship. ⚠ **Not high, and the reason is exactly `DRIFT.md` D1**: this is a node about a book, built entirely from a dictionary and a survey.
