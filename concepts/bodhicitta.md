## Gloss

*Bodhicitta* (*bodhi* "awakening" + *citta* "mind") is the **awakening mind** — the Mahāyāna Buddhist intention to attain full Buddhahood for the benefit of *all* sentient beings. It is the defining commitment that distinguishes the bodhisattva path from the Theravāda arhat path: a bodhisattva vows to remain in *saṃsāra* until every being is liberated, rather than seeking individual escape. Bodhicitta permeates every aspect of Mahāyāna teaching and practice; without it, no action — however virtuous — rises to the level of a transcendent perfection (*pāramitā*).

**Two aspects** (Śāntideva, *Bodhicaryāvatāra*):

| type | Sanskrit | metaphor | content |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aspirational | *bodhipraṇidhicitta* | deciding to start the journey | the vow/wish to attain Buddhahood for others |
| Engaged | *bodhiprasthānacitta* | actually walking the path | active practice of the six pāramitās motivated by the vow |

Both are necessary: the aspiration without practice is an empty wish; the practice without the altruistic vow is not yet bodhicitta.

**Relative vs. absolute bodhicitta** (the Madhyamaka dimension):
- *Relative bodhicitta*: the compassion-motivation — working for the good of all beings within the conventional world.
- *Absolute bodhicitta*: the wisdom of *śūnyatā* (emptiness) — recognizing the empty nature of both self and all phenomena. Without this, compassion risks sentimentality (cling to the beings one "helps"); without relative compassion, insight risks nihilism.

**The six pāramitās** are bodhicitta in action: *dāna* (generosity), *śīla* (ethics), *kṣānti* (patience), *vīrya* (effort), *dhyāna* (meditation), *prajñā* (wisdom). Each is a "perfection" only when motivated by bodhicitta — the same action done for self-benefit is merit, not liberation.

Śāntideva: *"Those who long to transcend... must never abandon bodhicitta. When bodhicitta has arisen, he is straightway hailed son of the Sugatas, to be revered in the worlds."* (*Bodhicaryāvatāra*)

Atisha's formulation: *"buddhō bhaveyaṁ jagato hitāya"* — "May I become a Buddha for the world's benefit."

**Distinction from Theravāda:** The arhat path aims at individual liberation through the extinction of *āsava* (taints); the bodhisattva path via bodhicitta delays individual parinirvāṇa out of altruistic commitment. This is the deepest structural difference between the two vehicles — not a matter of gradual/sudden method but of *scope of intention*.
