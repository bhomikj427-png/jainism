## Gloss

The "Middle Way" school of Mahāyāna Buddhism, founded by Nāgārjuna (~150–250 CE). Central thesis: every dharma whatsoever is empty (*śūnya*) of intrinsic existence (*svabhāva*) — the set of ultimately existent things is an empty set. MMK 24.19: "there is no dharma whatsoever that is not dependently originated, therefore there is no dharma whatsoever that is not empty." This is *not* nihilism: dependent origination makes phenomena conventionally real (MMK 24.20 — emptiness *enables* the Four Noble Truths rather than undermining them).

**Two truths**: saṃvṛti-satya (conventional truth — phenomena are dependently originated, conceptually designated, and thus conventionally real) and paramārtha-satya (ultimate truth — phenomena are empty of svabhāva). The two truths describe the same phenomena at different levels; neither can be collapsed into the other.

**Prasaṅga method** (Prāsaṅgika reading): Nāgārjuna does not advance positive theses; he shows that any opponent's position generates *absurd consequences* when applied to itself. This is reductio, not syllogism. Svātantrika sub-school (Bhāvaviveka, ~500-570 CE) reformulates arguments as formal independent inferences (*svatantra-anumāna*) using Dignāga's epistemological tools — rejected by Prāsaṅgika (Candrakīrti) as illegitimate.

**Middle way**: between eternalism (*śāśvatavāda* — enduring substances) and nihilism (*ucchedavāda* — no continuity, no karma). Emptiness of svabhāva resolves both: things neither permanently persist nor completely annihilate.

**Yogācāra dispute**: Yogācāra posits the vijñapti-stream as ultimately real. Madhyamaka (especially Candrakīrti's *Madhyamakāvatāra*) rejects all ontological privileging including consciousness-only — vijñaptis are also empty.
