## Gloss / Divergence map

**Karma-mārga** ("path of action"; also *karma-yoga*): liberation through right action performed without attachment to results (*nishkama karma*). One of the three classical Hindu paths to *mokṣa* alongside *jñāna-mārga* and *bhakti-mārga*.

**Key verse — BG 2.47** (Krishna's foundational teaching, directly cited):
> "You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action. Never consider yourself the cause of the results of your activities, and never be attached to not doing your duty."

**BG 3.19**: "Without being attached to the results of activities, one should act as a matter of duty, for by working without attachment one attains the Supreme."

**Nishkama karma** (nish + kama + karma = action without desire):
- The practical core: perform duty (*dharma*) as a matter of obligation, not for personal reward
- Action that is *dharmic* and *detached* purifies the mind (*citta-śuddhi*) and does not generate new karma-bondage
- In contrast to *sakāma karma* (action with desire for fruit), which binds

**The *karma* in karma-mārga ≠ karma in karma theory**: in the path of action, *karma* = action/work (*kriyā*); in karma theory (Jain/Buddhist/Hindu), *karma* = the accumulated force/matter of past actions that binds the soul. Different usage of the same word — important to distinguish.

**Contrast with the other paths:**

| path | means | suited to |
|---|---|---|
| karma-mārga | right action without attachment | those engaged in worldly/social life |
| jñāna-mārga | direct knowledge of ātman=Brahman | philosophically inclined |
| bhakti-mārga | loving devotion to a personal deity | devotionally inclined |

BG recommends karma-yoga specifically for *householders* engaged in worldly life who cannot renounce; monastic renunciation is not required.
