## Gloss / Divergence map

**Vaiṣṇavism** (*Vaiṣṇavasampradāya*): the major Hindu religious tradition that holds **Viṣṇu** (and/or his avatāras — primarily Kṛṣṇa and Rāma) as the supreme being. Its defining mark is **bhakti** (loving devotion) to a personal God. Vaishnavas number approximately one billion and constitute the largest sub-tradition within Hinduism. The tradition divides into several sampradāyas (transmission lineages) distinguished by their Vedānta reading, their supreme avatāra focus, and their regional base.

**Four principal Vedānta sampradāyas:**

| sampradāya | founder | period | Vedānta reading | supreme focus | geographic base |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Śrī Vaiṣṇavism (Viśiṣṭādvaita) | Rāmānuja | 1017–1137 CE | qualified non-dualism — selves and matter are real modes *within* brahman | Viṣṇu / Lakṣmī | Tamil Nadu / South India |
| Brahma-Madhva (Dvaita) | Madhvācārya | 1238–1317 CE | dualism — Viṣṇu and jīvas are eternally distinct; pañcabheda (five-fold difference) | Viṣṇu / Kṛṣṇa | Karnataka / Udupi |
| Kumāra-Nimbārka (Dvaitādvaita) | Nimbārkācārya | ~7th–12th c. CE | simultaneous difference and non-difference | Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa pair | Mathurā / Vrindāvan |
| Rudra-Vallabha (Śuddhādvaita) | Vallabhācārya | 1479–1531 CE | pure non-dualism without māyā; world is Kṛṣṇa's play (*līlā*) | Kṛṣṇa | Gujarat |

**Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇavism** (Caitanya Mahāprabhu, 1486–1534 CE) is a fifth major school, affiliated with the Madhva lineage but distinctive: it centers on **Rādhā-Kṛṣṇa** devotion and the theology of *acintya-bhedābheda* (inconceivable difference-and-non-difference). Its primary texts are the **Bhāgavata Purāṇa** and **Bhagavad Gītā** as read by the Gosvāmins of Vṛndāvan.

**Shared Vaiṣṇava doctrines across sampradāyas:**
- **Avatāra doctrine**: Viṣṇu descends (*avatārate*) into the world to restore *dharma* (BG 4.7–4.8). The Bhāgavata Purāṇa lists **10 principal avatāras** (Daśāvatāra: Matsya, Kūrma, Varāha, Narasiṃha, Vāmana, Paraśurāma, Rāma, Kṛṣṇa, Buddha, Kalkin).
- **Lakṣmī**: Viṣṇu's śakti (divine consort); Śrī Vaiṣṇavism makes Lakṣmī a co-mediator.
- **Bhakti**: devotion as the primary means of liberation — not karma-mārga alone nor jñāna-mārga alone. All sampradāyas accept the Bhagavad Gītā's Chapter 12 (Bhakti Yoga) as normative.
- **Pāñcarātra Āgamas**: liturgical-theological texts accepted as authoritative by all four sampradāyas (alongside the Upaniṣads, Bhagavad Gītā, and Brahmasūtra).

**Contrast with Advaita Vedānta:** Rāmānuja and Madhva both explicitly opposed Śaṃkara's Advaita:
- Rāmānuja: the Upaniṣads teach *brahman with attributes* (sa-guṇa), not attribute-less consciousness; māyā as cosmological illusion undermines bhakti (you cannot devotionally relate to a brahman that is nirguna and without a real devotee)
- Madhva: Viṣṇu and souls are ontologically distinct *always*; identity language in the Upaniṣads is secondary (gauṇa), not literal
- The shared Upaniṣadic text thus anchors three mutually incompatible metaphysical positions
