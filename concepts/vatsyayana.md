## Gloss / Divergence map

**Pakṣilasvāmin Vātsyāyana** is the author of the *Nyāya-Bhāṣya*, the **earliest extant commentary** on Gautama Akṣapāda's *Nyāya Sūtra*. His bhāṣya turns the terse Nyāya aphorisms into a working epistemology of the *pramāṇas* (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) and sets the agenda that the whole later Nyāya tradition (Uddyotakara, Vācaspati Miśra, and onward) then glosses. He is the layer through which the Nyāya Sūtra became a living philosophical system.

**Name-collision (teach in prose; single node, per the [[patanjali]] precedent).** This Vātsyāyana — the Nyāya logician, c. 5th c. CE — must **not** be conflated with **Vātsyāyana Mallanāga**, author of the *Kāmasūtra*. Same surname, different people, different works — a classic confusion. The Kāmasūtra author is out of scope (no node); should one ever be added it must take a disambiguating key, never the bare `vatsyayana`.

**Dating.** Mainstream scholarship places him c. 5th century CE (he necessarily post-dates Gautama's sūtra, c. 150 CE); older summaries occasionally pushed him earlier, but this does not undercut the authorship claim.

## Reasoning across signals

1. **Matthew R. Dasti, *Vātsyāyana's Commentary on the Nyāya-sūtra: A Guide* (Oxford UP, 2023)** — a dedicated critical monograph: authorship of the first extant Nyāya-Bhāṣya, c. 5th c. CE.
2. **Gangopadhyaya (tr.), *Nyāya Philosophy: Gautama's Nyāyasūtra and Vātsyāyana's Bhāṣya* (1967)** — an independent translation/critical edition.
3. **Wikipedia, "Pakṣilasvāmin Vātsyāyana"** — corroborating; the Kāmasūtra name-collision.

Three genuinely independent sources (a 2023 Oxford monograph, a 1967 translation, an encyclopedia) agree that he authored the first extant Nyāya commentary → **converged**, confidence **high**. Only the dating is loose, which does not touch the core attribution.
