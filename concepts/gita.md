## Divergence map

The *Bhagavadgītā* (BG) is a 700-verse dialogue between Kṛṣṇa and Arjuna embedded in the *Bhīṣmaparva* of the Mahābhārata (chapters 23–40). Technically *smṛti* (remembered) rather than *śruti* (revealed), it was elevated by all three major Vedānta founders to the status of a canonical authority — constituting, with the Upaniṣads and Brahma-Sūtras, the **prasthānatrayī** (triple foundation). The structural question that drives the inter-school dispute is: *which of the three paths — karma-yoga, jñāna-yoga, bhakti-yoga — is the text's real aim?*

| reading | who holds it (commentary) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| **jñāna-primacy**: BG ultimately teaches Brahman-realization through knowledge; karma and bhakti are preparatory rungs | Śaṅkara's *Gītābhāṣya* (~8th c.) | ātman = Brahman (Advaita); the personal God Kṛṣṇa is Saguṇa Brahman, a provisional object; final liberation is knowledge dissolving individuality |
| **bhakti-culmination**: BG teaches surrender (*prapatti*) and loving devotion to Viṣṇu-Nārāyaṇa as the highest, with karma and jñāna subordinated | Rāmānuja's *Gītābhāṣya* (~11th c.); Yamunācārya's three-hexad division | souls and matter are modes of Brahman's body (Viśiṣṭādvaita); liberation = dwelling in Brahman's presence, individuality maintained |
| **bhakti as supreme devotion in eternal dualism**: BG teaches total bhakti to Viṣṇu, who is utterly distinct from jīvas; knowledge of five eternal differences is liberating | Madhva's *Gītā-tātparya* (~13th c.) | five eternal distinctions (pañcabheda) are real; soul-liberation is eternal devotional service, individuality fully preserved |

All three readings cite the same verses but interpret them through divergent ontological lenses. The **charama-śloka** (BG 18.66: *sarvadharmān parityajya mām ekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja* — "abandon all dharmas and take refuge in me alone") is the clearest bhakti anchor; Śaṅkara reads even this as upāya (means), not final teaching.
