## Gloss

*Spanda* ("cosmic pulsation," "divine vibration") is the foundational concept of the **Spanda sub-system** of Kashmir Śaivism, one of its three philosophical streams (alongside Āgama Śāstra and Pratyabhijñā Śāstra). Spanda interprets Śakti as the **spontaneous, self-subsisting vibration of universal consciousness** — not physical motion in space, but the perpetual throb of Śiva's awareness as it freely unfolds reality from within itself.

**Key texts:**
- **Śiva Sūtras** — Vasugupta (~9th c. CE); tradition: revealed to Vasugupta in a dream inscribed on a rock at Śāradāpīṭha. Foundational for all Kashmir Śaivism.
- **Spandakārikā** — Bhatta Kallata (Vasugupta's student, ~9th c. CE); the primary doctrinal treatment of spanda as a philosophy.
- **Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam** — Kṣemarāja (Abhinavagupta's student, ~11th c. CE): *"Awareness, free and independent, is the cause of the performance of everything"* — this formulation bridges Spanda and Pratyabhijñā.

**The doctrine:**

Spanda is *not* physical vibration. It is Śiva-consciousness understood as **intrinsically active and dynamic** — not the static, featureless *nirguṇa* Brahman of Advaita but a consciousness that pulses with its own freedom (*svātantrya*) and creative power. This pulsation is why the universe exists: reality is *real* manifestation (*ābhāsa*) of consciousness, not appearance-in-illusion. This makes Spanda's position **non-illusionist** — the world is not māyā in the Advaita sense.

The paradox of **"vibrationless vibration"**: Śiva at the ultimate level is *viśrānta* (at rest, beyond all motion), yet the universe is real dynamic expression. Spanda is the vibration that does not compromise the absolute's inner stillness — like the ocean whose surface pulses without the depths moving.

**Relation to the broader Kashmir Śaiva system:**

| sub-system | content | key figure |
|---|---|---|
| Āgama Śāstra | direct Śiva-revelation (tantric root texts) | tradition |
| Spanda Śāstra | cosmic pulsation — experiential/poetic layer | Vasugupta / Kallata |
| Pratyabhijñā Śāstra | philosophical argument for recognition | Utpaladeva / Abhinavagupta |

Pratyabhijñā *inherits* Spanda's dynamism and converts it into a rigorous epistemological argument (see pratyabhijna.md). Spanda is the phenomenological-experiential layer; Pratyabhijñā is the philosophical-systematic layer.

**Distinction from Advaita:** Advaita's Brahman is *nirguṇa* (without qualities, without power) — śakti belongs to māyā, not to Brahman itself. Spanda's Śiva IS inseparable from Śakti; the world's dynamism is not a fall from but an expression of the absolute. This is the same Advaita-vs-Kashmir-Śaivism split documented in pratyabhijna.md.
