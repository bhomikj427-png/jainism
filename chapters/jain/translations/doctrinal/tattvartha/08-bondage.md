# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 8 (Bandha, Karmic Bondage)

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra, Adhyāya 8. Having covered influx (Adhyāya 6) and the vows that check it (Adhyāya
7, not yet drafted in this reading room — see work queue), the treatise turns to the fourth *tattva* named at
1.4: *bandha*, the actual binding of karmic matter to the soul. This chapter names the five causes of bondage,
the four aspects every bound karma has, works through the eight principal karma-types and their many
sub-types (already the subject of the existing `karma-prakriti.md` node), their duration and intensity, and
closes with a genuinely contested sūtra distinguishing beneficial (*puṇya*) from harmful (*pāpa*) bondage.

**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at equal weight, no spine. This chapter's fork shape is
**cleaner than any prior adhyāya in this reading room**: it opens with one merge and closes with one
split/content-variant, and — unlike Adhyāya 6's unresolved end-of-chapter arithmetic, or Adhyāya 2's untraced
gap — the two forks are exact mirror images that **cancel perfectly**, so both recensions land on "8.26" as
the chapter's final sūtra number despite disagreeing about what's inside several of the sūtras along the way.

- **Opening merge (8.1–8.3 SB/SBT → 8.1–8.2 SS):** Śvetāmbara states the five causes of bondage (8.1), then
  gives the passion-driven attraction of karmic matter (8.2) and "the result is bondage" (8.3) as two separate
  sūtras. Digambara's Sarvārthasiddhi (SS) states the same content in one sūtra (SS 8.2) — a clean content-free
  merge, the same *kind* of fork as Adhyāya 5's 5.2–5.3 merge and Adhyāya 6's 6.3 merge.
- **A quiet running –1 offset holds through the whole middle of the chapter** (SB 8.4 = SS 8.3, ... SB 8.25 =
  SS 8.24) — confirmed sūtra-by-sūtra against Tatia's own "(SS ...)" cross-reference apparatus for every sūtra
  in this reading room's coverage.
- **Closing split *and* genuine content-variant (SB 8.26 → SS 8.25–8.26):** Śvetāmbara's Umāsvāti gives one
  sūtra (marked "not SS" in Tatia's apparatus) naming **eight** things as beneficial karmic bondage: pleasant
  sensation, the near-perfect enlightened world-view, laughter, relish, male disposition, auspicious lifespan,
  auspicious body, and auspicious status. Digambara's SS splits this into two sūtras **and narrows the list to
  four**: SS 8.25 names only pleasant sensation, auspicious lifespan, auspicious body, and auspicious status as
  beneficial (dropping the enlightened-world-view and the three quasi-passion items entirely); SS 8.26 then
  states "other bondages are harmful," matching the second half of the Śvetāmbara sūtra. **This is not mere
  renumbering** — it is a substantive doctrinal disagreement about whether the (near-perfect) enlightened
  world-view and three quasi-passions (laughter, relish, male disposition) count as beneficial karma at all,
  given that all four are, by the chapter's own classification (8.10), varieties of *destructive*/deluding karma
  — see the dedicated section below. **Net effect: the SB's single "extra" sūtra at the end is exactly absorbed
  by the SS's "extra" sūtra at the start (8.1–8.3→8.1–8.2), so SB and SS both end the chapter at sūtra 26** —
  a clean bracketing this reading room has not seen before and did not need to leave as an open item.
- **A genuine intra-Digambara commentarial split, not a Digambara/Śvetāmbara one:** Tatia's translator's note
  records that even within the Digambara tradition, Vīrasena's two commentaries disagree with each other on
  this exact question — his *Dhāvalā* on the *Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama* treats the four destructive karmas as
  *necessarily* harmful (implying the contested four cannot be beneficial bondage, agreeing with SS's narrower
  list), while his *Jayadhavalā* on the *Kaṣāyaprābhṛta* accepts the four factors as causes of beneficial
  bondage (agreeing with SB's fuller list). This is recorded as a genuine open disagreement **within** the
  commentarial anchor itself (CLAUDE.md §4 signal 2) — the split is not simply "Digambara vs. Śvetāmbara" but
  cuts across it.

**Confidence:** high on sūtras 8.1–8.4/SS 8.1–8.3 (read in full, cross-checked against both sources, the

## Sūtras 8.1–8.3 (SB/SBT) / 8.1–8.2 (SS, merged) — the causes of bondage

**Text (IAST), 8.1, both recensions identical:** *mithyādarśanāvirati-pramāda-kaṣāya-yogā bandhahetavaḥ*

**Padaccheda:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| *mithyā-darśana* | compound, "false-view" | *mithyā* "falsely, wrongly, contrary to truth" (adv.); *darśana* "seeing, viewing; view, doctrine" | MW, via wisdomlib.org |
| *a-virati* | negation + noun | *virati* "cessation, desisting, abstinence" (MW); already the textual root of Adhyāya 7's vows, not yet drafted in this reading room | MW |
| *pramāda* | noun | "intoxication; carelessness, negligence, indifference" (MW) — the plain general sense already carries the Jain technical one without narrowing | MW |
| *kaṣāya* | noun | already the `kashaya.md` node's own term (passion) | — |
| *yoga* | noun | already treated in the Adhyāya 6 unit of this reading room — literal "activity" of body/speech/mind, **not** the meditative-discipline sense of `yoga-darshana.md` | — |
| *bandha-hetavaḥ* | compound, plural | *bandha* "binding" (MW) + *hetu* "cause" (MW) | MW |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both Tatia (SB/SBT) and Vijay K. Jain (SS) agree on the same five causes
and their fixed sequence, and both commentarial traditions add the same structural point: each cause
*presupposes* the ones after it in the list but not the reverse — wrong-belief cannot occur without
non-abstinence, negligence, passion, and activity also being present, but activity alone (in a passionless
soul) can occur without any of the other four. Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi-derived commentary adds detail
absent from Tatia's summary treatment: it maps the five causes directly onto the fourteen-stage *guṇasthāna*
ladder (already the `gunasthana.md` node's own subject) — the wrong-believer (stage 1) has all five causes
operating; stages 2–4 lose wrong-belief but keep the other four; stage 5 (partial vow-taker) operates under a
mix of restraint/non-restraint plus negligence, passion, and activity; stages 6–10 lose non-abstinence
entirely (the ascetic has taken full vows) and narrow further to passion and activity alone by stage 7; stages
11–13 retain only activity; and the final stage (*ayogakevalī*) has no cause of bondage operating at all — a
clean textual anchor for the `gunasthana.md` node's own stage-by-stage karmic-cessation account, cited here
rather than re-derived.

**Assembled reading:** Deluded world-view, non-abstinence, negligence, the passions, and activity [of body,
speech, and mind] are the causes of bondage.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "The five causes of bondage are: deluded world-view, non-abstinence, laxity, passions and the
  actions of the body, speech and mind."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, SS): "Wrong belief (*mithyādarśana*), vowlessness (*avirati*), passions (*kaṣāya*) and
  activities (*yoga*) [with negligence, *pramāda*, folded into the compound] are the causes of bondage
  (*bandha*)."
- Both translators converge exactly on the five-fold list and its order; no disagreement.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A — this sūtra is moral-psychological causation, not physics.

## Sūtras 8.2–8.3 (SB/SBT, both "SS 8.2 in part") — the merge fork

**Text (IAST), both recensions identical in wording, split differently in numbering:**
*sakaṣāyatvāj jīvaḥ karmaṇo yogyān pudgalān ādatte* / *sa bandhaḥ*

**Recension status:** DIVERGENT (numbering only — content identical) — see table.

| aspect | Śvetāmbara (Umāsvāti) | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi) |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------------|
| number | SB 8.2 + SB 8.3 (two sūtras) | SS 8.2 (one sūtra, both clauses) |
| text (IAST) | *sakaṣāyatvāj jīvaḥ karmaṇo yogyān pudgalān ādatte* (8.2); *sa bandhaḥ* (8.3) | same two clauses, undivided |
| what it commits you to | the "attraction" and the "naming it bondage" are presented as two logical steps | the same two steps are one continuous statement — no numbering weight given to the seam between them |

**Padaccheda:** *sa-kaṣāya-tvāt* ("because of [its] being possessed of passion," ablative of state, from
*kaṣāya* — the `kashaya.md` node's term); *karmaṇaḥ yogyān* ("fit for [becoming] karma" — MW *yogya*,
"suitable, fit, proper"); *pudgalān ādatte* ("takes in/seizes the material particles" — MW *ā-dā*, "to take,
seize, receive"); *bandhaḥ* ("bondage, binding" — MW *bandha*).

A lexical note worth flagging explicitly, since it runs *against* the recurring pattern of every prior chapter
in this reading room (where MW's general entry gives only an outer bound and the Jain technical sense must be
supplied by commentary): Monier-Williams' own entry for *bandha* already lists, among its many senses,
**"mundane bondage, attachment to this world"** as a recognized philosophical usage opposed to liberation —
the general lexicon here already carries the doctrinal sense nearly intact, rather than needing Sarvārthasiddhi
or the *bhāṣya* to supply it from a narrower or unrelated base meaning. This is a genuine contrast worth
recording rather than silently treating as "business as usual."

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi rendering and Tatia's SB/SBT
commentary give the same mechanism: the soul, though itself immaterial, has been associated with matter
beginninglessly (there is no "first" bondage to explain, since positing one would make the already-liberated
Siddhas' current unbound state impossible to distinguish in kind); the *jīva*-with-karma's own passion-driven
vibration (*yoga*, already covered) is what converts co-located but as-yet-inert matter particles into karmic
matter proper. Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi note explicitly uses this sūtra to refute two rival views: that
karma is an "unseen force" rather than actual matter (refuted by the sūtra's use of *pudgala*, "matter"), and
that bondage is merely the abstract union of a substance with its qualities (refuted by the sūtra's specific
causal wording, *ādatte*, "takes in").

**Assembled reading:** Because of [its] possessing passion, the soul takes in particles of matter fit to
become karma. That is bondage.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Because of its passions, the soul attracts and assimilates the material particles of karmic
  bondage" (8.2) / "The result is bondage" (8.3).
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, SS 8.2, undivided): "The living being — *jīva*, the soul — actuated by passions
  (*kaṣāya*), retains particles of matter (*pudgala*) fit to turn into karmas. This is bondage (*bandha*)."
- Agreement in substance; the only difference is the SB/SS numbering seam described above.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A.

## Sūtra 8.4 (SB) / SS 8.3 — the four aspects of bondage

**Text (IAST), both recensions identical:** *prakṛti-sthity-anubhava-pradeśās tad-vidhayaḥ*

**Padaccheda:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| *prakṛti* | noun | general MW range: "the original or natural form of anything; nature, character, disposition" (26 distinct senses recorded); **the Jain technical sense — "species/nature of bondage," *prakṛti-bandha* — is explicitly documented in the same lexical source as a named doctrinal narrowing**, not left for this reading room to infer | MW, via wisdomlib.org |
| *sthiti* | noun | "standing, staying; duration, continuance" (MW) — the general sense already matches the Jain technical "duration of bondage" directly, no narrowing needed | MW |
| *anubhava* (Digambara wording) / *anubhāga* (variant) | noun | MW range for *anubhava*: "perception, apprehension; experience; cognition; result, consequence" — narrowed by commentary (both SB and SS) to specifically the *intensity or quality* of a karma's eventual fruition, not experience in general; a secondary form *anubhāga* is glossed by the Cologne lexica (Cappeller; Böhtlingk–Roth) as "subdivision" or "a subordinate part," with Böhtlingk–Roth flagging their "feeling, sensible quality" gloss as possibly an error for *anubhāva* — the two forms are treated as referring to the same aspect of bondage by both Jain commentarial traditions regardless of which base form a given manuscript reads | MW; Cappeller and Böhtlingk–Roth via Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries, wisdomlib.org |
| *pradeśa* | noun | already treated in the Adhyāya 5 unit of this reading room as "space-point" (matter's/soul's smallest partless unit of spatial occupation) — here reused in a related but distinct technical sense, "quantum/count of karmic particles assimilated," not spatial location; the reuse of one term for two distinct technical senses (location-unit vs. particle-count) is flagged, not silently merged | cross-reference, `pudgala`/Adhyāya 5 unit |
| *tad-vidhayaḥ* | compound | "these [are] the modes/varieties [of it]" — MW *vidhi*, "mode, manner, kind" | MW |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both recensions and both consulted translations agree exactly: bondage
has four aspects — *prakṛti* (which of the eight karma-types this bound matter will become), *sthiti* (how
long until the karma fully discharges), *anubhāva*/*anubhaga* (how intensely it will be felt on rising), and
*pradeśa* (how much karmic matter, by count, was assimilated). The Sarvārthasiddhi, per both Tatia's and Vijay
K. Jain's reporting, illustrates the four with a shared simile: *prakṛti* is compared to the fixed bitter taste
of the colocynth fruit versus the fixed sweet taste of sugar (a *kind* of taste); *sthiti* to the differing
lengths of time milk from a goat, cow, or buffalo stays sweet before souring (a *duration*); *anubhāva* to
milk's varying strength of taste, mild to strong (an *intensity*); and *pradeśa* is left as the bare quantity
of particles, with no further simile needed.

**Assembled reading:** Nature/type, duration, intensity of fruition, and quantity of particles assimilated —
these are the modes [i.e., the four aspects] of that [bondage].

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "There are four aspects of bondage: type, duration, intensity (quality) of fruition, and mass
  of material particles assimilated."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, SS): "Bondage (*bandha*) is of four kinds: according to the nature or species —
  *prakṛti*, duration — *sthiti*, fruition — *anubhava* (or *anubhāga*), and the quantity of space-points —
  *pradeśa*."
- Both name the same four aspects in the same order; Vijay K. Jain's rendering explicitly notes the
  *anubhava*/*anubhāga* variant-spelling issue flagged above.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A — a four-fold taxonomy of moral-causal mechanics, not a physical
model; no parallel is invoked by either source and none is manufactured here.

## Sūtras 8.5–8.25 (SB) / SS 8.4–8.24 — content survey: the eight karma-types, their sub-types, duration, and intensity

Per the running –1 offset established at the opening merge, this entire stretch holds a clean one-sūtra
Digambara-behind numbering (SB 8.5 = SS 8.4, ... SB 8.25 = SS 8.24) confirmed against Tatia's own apparatus at
every sūtra; **no further numbering or content fork appears anywhere in this stretch**, a genuine point of
contrast with every other multi-sūtra run surveyed elsewhere in this reading room.

The eight principal karma-types already named at 6.11–6.26 (and the subject of the existing `karma-prakriti.md`
node) are given their sub-type counts (8.6/SS 8.5): 5 knowledge-covering, 9 intuition-covering, 2
sensation-producing, 28 deluding, 4 lifespan, 42 body-making, 2 status, 5 obstructive — 97 sub-types in total.
Each is then detailed in turn: the 5 knowledge-coverings mirror the five *jñānas* of Adhyāya 1 (already
covered in this reading room's own unit #4); the 9 intuition-coverings include the four intuition-types plus
five kinds of sleep, with the Sarvārthasiddhi noting a special dormant/hibernating variety capable of driving
cruelty in dreams; the 28 deluding sub-types split into 3 varieties of view-delusion (deluded, near-perfect,
and mixed world-view) and 25 of conduct-delusion (16 passions at four intensities each, plus the 9
quasi-passions already covered in the Adhyāya 6 unit); the 42 body-making sub-types (8.12/SS 8.11) are the
chapter's longest single list, covering everything from realm-of-birth and body-type down to bone-joint
configuration, gait, and — as the list's final and most auspicious item — "the qualities of a Jina" (i.e., the
karma that produces a Tīrthaṅkara's own body, cross-referencing the sixteen-cause list already covered at
6.23); and status-karma's two sub-types (high/low) are illustrated in Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi-sourced
commentary with named examples (birth in Magadha, Aṅga, or the Hari/Ikṣvāku lineages as high-status
illustrations; outcaste and low-occupational groups as low-status ones) — flagged here as period-bound social
material Tatia's own translator's note contextualizes as reflecting "the distribution of power and wealth" of
its historical setting, not treated as an eternal metaphysical ranking by either translator.

Duration (8.15–8.21/SS 8.14–8.20) gives the maximum and minimum binding-periods for each karma-type in
ocean-measured periods (*sāgaropama*, an astronomically large traditional unit) and Indian time-units,
scaling from thirty times ten-to-the-fourteenth *sāgaropamas* for the four longest-binding types down to a
single *antarmuhūrta* (under forty-eight minutes) for the shortest. Intensity (8.22–8.24/SS 8.21–8.23)
explains *anubhāva* mechanically: bound karma "ripens" and can transfer its effect between sub-types of the
*same* principal karma-type (e.g., one flavor of deluding-karma converting to another) but never across
principal types — with one further asymmetry Tatia's translator's note preserves rather than smooths over: the
deluded, near-perfect, and mixed world-view sub-types can transfer only in specific directions (deluded →
near-perfect or mixed; never the reverse from mixed back to deluded), a directional constraint neither
recension disputes.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A throughout — enumerative karma-mechanics, not physical cosmology.

## Sūtra 8.26 (SB, "not SS") / SS 8.25–8.26 — beneficial and harmful bondage: the closing fork

**Recension status:** DIVERGENT — genuine content variant, not mere renumbering (see side-by-side below).

| aspect | Śvetāmbara (Umāsvāti, single sūtra) | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi, split into two) |
|--------|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| number | SB 8.26 | SS 8.25 (beneficial list) + SS 8.26 (harmful) |
| text (IAST), beneficial list | *sad-vedya-samyaktva-hāsya-rati-puruṣaveda-śubhāyur-nāma-gotrāṇi puṇyam* — **eight** items | *sad-vedya-śubhāyur-nāma-gotrāṇi puṇyam* — **four** items only |
| items named | pleasant sensation; near-perfect enlightened world-view; laughter; relish; male disposition; auspicious lifespan; auspicious body; auspicious status | pleasant sensation; auspicious lifespan; auspicious body; auspicious status |
| items *dropped* by SS | near-perfect enlightened world-view (*samyaktva*); laughter, relish, and male disposition (three of the nine quasi-passions named at 8.10) | — |
| what it commits you to | four destructive/deluding-karma items *can* constitute beneficial (*puṇya*) bondage | those same four items **cannot** be classed as beneficial bondage at all — only the four non-destructive-karma items qualify |
| harmful-bondage clause | folded into the same sūtra: "other [bondages] are harmful" | separate sūtra, SS 8.26: *ato 'nyat pāpam*, "other bondages are harmful" — same content, own number |

**Padaccheda:** *sad-vedya* ("pleasant-feeling," from *vedya*, "to be felt/known" — MW; the pleasure-producing
half of sensation-karma, 8.9); *samyaktva* ("the state of being correct/true" — MW, the near-perfect
enlightened world-view of 8.10); *hāsya*, *rati*, *puruṣa-veda* (three of the nine quasi-passions named at
8.10 — laughter, relish, and the male sexual disposition); *śubha-āyus/nāma/gotra* ("auspicious lifespan/body/
status" — the corresponding sub-types named at 8.11–8.13); *puṇyam* / *pāpam* (already the `punya.md` /
`papa.md` nodes' own terms).

**Disambiguation (from commentary) — the load-bearing finding of this chapter:** This is not a case where
commentary cleanly resolves an ambiguity; it is a case where **the commentarial tradition itself is split, and
the split cuts across the Digambara/Śvetāmbara line rather than following it.** Tatia's translator's note
records that the Śvetāmbara *bhāṣya* tradition (SBT) is itself "embarrassed" by its own sūtra's inclusion of
the enlightened-world-view and the three quasi-passions as beneficial bondage, precisely because all four are,
by the chapter's own classification at 8.10, varieties of the four *destructive* (*ghātiyā*) karmas — and
"neither the scripture nor the ancient literature on karma mentions these four types" elsewhere as beneficial.
The Digambara Sarvārthasiddhi (SS 8.25) resolves this by simply not including them. But the disagreement does
not stop at the Digambara/Śvetāmbara boundary: Tatia's note further records that **within the Digambara
tradition itself**, the two commentarial works of Vīrasena disagree — his *Dhāvalā* commentary on the
*Ṣaṭkhaṇḍāgama* (XIII.352) states that the four destructive karmas are *necessarily* harmful (agreeing with
SS's narrower list), while his separate *Jayadhavalā* commentary on the *Kaṣāyaprābhṛta* accepts the same four
factors as valid causes of beneficial bondage (agreeing with the Śvetāmbara sūtra's fuller list). Per CLAUDE.md
§4's own method — the commentarial tradition is the anchor for disambiguation — this sūtra is the rare case
where **the anchor itself does not converge**, and the honest finding is the disagreement, not a resolution of
it. This reading room records it as such rather than picking a side.

**Assembled reading:** *(Śvetāmbara)* Pleasant sensation, the near-perfect enlightened world-view, laughter,
relish, male disposition, auspicious lifespan, auspicious body, and auspicious status are beneficial bondage;
other [bondages] are harmful. *(Digambara/SS)* Pleasant sensation, auspicious lifespan, auspicious body, and
auspicious status are beneficial bondage [SS 8.25]; other [bondages] are harmful [SS 8.26].

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): gives both readings explicitly, side by side, exactly as tabulated above, with the translator's
  note on the SBT's own discomfort and the Vīrasena intra-commentarial split.
- Vijay K. Jain — not independently re-verified for this specific sūtra within this session's time budget;
  flagged as an open item (see below) rather than silently assumed to match Tatia's SS numbering.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A — a moral-theological classification dispute, not physics.
