# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 4: The Celestial Beings (deva)

> **Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī), Adhyāya 4 — the four classes of gods, their internal
> ranks, colourings and pleasures; the heavens tier by tier; the terminal (lokāntika) gods; and the long
> lifespan-arithmetic section closing the cosmology begun in Adhyāya 3.
> **Recensions:** Digambara (SS = Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, sūtras 4.1–4.42) and Śvetāmbara
> (SB = Umāsvāti's *Svopajña-bhāṣya*, SBT = ṭīkā, sūtras 4.1–4.53) — **equal weight, no spine**.
> **Editions used:** Nathmal Tatia (tr.), *That Which Is*, 1994 (archive.org OCR
> `ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`, read locally — its apparatus prints every SS variant sūtra
> in place); Vijay K. Jain (ed./tr.), *Tattvārthasūtra with Sarvārthasiddhi explanation*, 2018 (archive.org
> OCR `Tattvartha18CompleteWeb`, read locally, incl. the Devanāgarī+IAST appendix for Chapter 4).
> **Status:** drafted (first pass, both recensions).
> **Walk-order note:** Śvetāmbara sequence with both numbers printed; SS variant/merged sūtras walked at
> their SB positions, SS-only sūtras at their own. Table-of-contents choice, not favoritism.

---

## Chapter shape (read this first)

Adhyāyas 7 and 3 each turned on **one** big fork. Adhyāya 4 is the opposite: **the fork-densest chapter in
the reading room** — no single block, but divergence running through the whole second half, *in both
directions at once*, and, uniquely so far, divergence that is **doctrinal-cosmological, not just textual**:

1. **The two recensions count the heavens differently.** Śvetāmbara: 12 kalpa heavens + 9 Graiveyakas + 5
   Anuttara heavens = **26**. Digambara: 16 kalpa heavens + 9 Graiveyakas + **9 Anudiśa ("subdirectional")
   heavens with no Śvetāmbara counterpart at all** + 5 Anuttaras = **39**. This is not renumbering — the two
   traditions transmit differently-shaped upper cosmoses, and Tatia's edition prints two separate full-page
   cosmos diagrams, one per tradition, rather than merging them.
2. **Śvetāmbara-only material**: the colouring sūtra pair (4.2, 4.7), the lifespan header (4.29), and —
   mirror of Adhyāya 3's Digambara-only geography — a **four-sūtra Śvetāmbara-only astronomy run (4.49–4.52)**
   on the lifespans of planets, constellations and stars.
3. **Digambara-only material**: the closing sūtra SS 4.42 (the terminal gods' lifespan, height and colour).
4. **Genuine numeric divergences inside shared content**: the empyrean lifespan ladder disagrees at
   Mahāśukra (SB 17 vs SS "somewhat more than 16" ocean-measured periods), and the minimum luminous-god
   lifespan (SB one-quarter vs SS one-eighth of a pit-measured period).

Net counts: Śvet. 53 sūtras, Dig. 42. After Adhyāya 3 (where the Digambara text ran 21 sūtras longer), this
chapter runs the other way — **11 sūtras longer on the Śvetāmbara side** — reinforcing the census
observation that neither recension is "the long one"; each expands where its own transmission elaborated.

## SB 4.1 / SS 4.1

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *devāś caturṇikāyāḥ*

**Assembled reading:** The gods are of four orders (*nikāya*, "class/community" — MW "a group, class,
assemblage"): **mansion-dwelling** (bhavanavāsin), **forest** (vyantara), **luminous** (jyotiṣka) and
**empyrean** (vaimānika) — dwelling respectively in the upper reaches of the first infernal land and parts
of the middle world; in mountains, caves and forests (SS: on innumerable remote islands — a small
commentarial habitat divergence); in the skies of the middle world; and in the heavens above. Their shared
character per both commentaries: playful, extravagant, powerfully embodied by past austerity.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "The gods fall into four classes."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): "The celestial beings (deva) are of four classes (nikāya)."

## SB 4.2 + 4.7 / SS 4.2 — the colouring fork

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — two Śvetāmbara sūtras vs one differently-worded Digambara sūtra, with a
real doctrinal nuance between them.**

| aspect | Śvetāmbara | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi) |
|--------|------------|------------------------------|
| number | 4.2 **and** 4.7 (Tatia marks both "not in SS") | 4.2 |
| text (IAST) | 4.2 *tṛtīyāḥ pītaleśyāḥ* ("the third class has fiery colouring"); 4.7 *pītānta-leśyāḥ* ("[the mansion and forest gods have] colourings ending with fiery," i.e. black/blue/grey/fiery) | *āditas triṣu pītāntaleśyāḥ* ("the first three classes have colourings ending with fiery") |
| doctrine | mansion and forest gods: any of the first four colourings; **luminous gods: only ever fiery-red** | all three lower classes may be **born** with any of the four, "although in maturity they are necessarily red" |
| what it commits you to | the luminous gods' redness is categorical | the redness is developmental — birth-colour varies |

Both agree on the empyrean gods (fiery for the first two kinds, yellow for the next three, white above —
that sūtra, SB 4.23/SS 4.22, is shared). The SBT adds the load-bearing clarification that all of this
concerns **physical (dravya) leśyā only** — a god's *psychic* colouring may be any of the six. This sūtra
group is a textual anchor for the existing `leshya.md` node alongside Adhyāya 3's infernal-colouring
passage: the two chapters give the doctrine's two poles (hell's fixed inauspicious colourings; heaven's
graded auspicious ones).

## SB 4.3–4.6 / SS 4.3–4.6 — types, ranks, chiefs (content survey)

**Recension status:** numbering shared; chief-name variants in commentary (below).

- **4.3**: within the classes — **ten** types of mansion gods, **eight** of forest, **five** of luminous,
  **twelve** of graded empyrean gods (the *kalpopapanna*; the non-graded stand above them, 4.18).
- **4.4** (*indra-sāmānika-trāyastriṃśa-pāriṣadyā-'tmarakṣa-lokapālā-'nīka-prakīrṇakā-'bhiyogya-kilviṣikāś
  caikaśaḥ*): each class replicates a full court — chief (*indra*), co-chiefs, ministers ("the
  thirty-three"), counsellors, bodyguards, custodians, army chiefs, citizens, attendants, menials. → The
  generic use of *indra* as a **rank-title** (every god-class has its indras) rather than a personal name is
  exactly the structural note recorded in the existing `indra.md` node on the Jain reception of the Vedic
  god — this sūtra is its anchor.
- **4.5**: forest and luminous gods lack ministers and custodians.
- **4.6** (*pūrvayor dvīndrāḥ*): the first two classes have **two chiefs per type** (north and south). The
  SB's twenty-chief table survives with name-variants Tatia tabulates against the SS (Bala/Vairocana;
  Harisaha/Harisiṃha-Harikānta; Velamba/Vailamba; Pratirūpa/Apratirūpa…) — transmission-level variants in
  the onomastics, not different pantheons. Among the ten mansion-god types: the *suparṇakumāra* ("vulturine
  youths") carry the **garuḍa** as emblem (cf. the `garuda.md` node's Vedic/Hindu career of that bird — a
  shares-vocabulary case, not the same figure); the SB names the luminous gods' chiefs as **the suns and
  moons themselves** (cf. `surya.md` for the Hindu solar deity this demotes-by-multiplication — see 4.14).

## SB 4.8–4.10 / SS 4.7–4.9 — the gradation of celestial pleasure (content survey)

**Recension status:** parallel at −1 offset (SB 4.7 has no SS counterpart); mūla essentially shared.

A three-sūtra ladder both traditions read identically, and one of the chapter's most philosophically pointed
passages: gods up to the second heaven (Aiśāna) enjoy **copulation** (*kāya-pravīcāra*) "as intense in their
passion as human beings"; then, in successive pairs of heavens, satisfaction comes by **touch → sight →
sound → thought alone**; and above the graded heavens (*pare 'pravīcārāḥ*) there is **no sexual enjoyment at
all** — a state both commentaries insist is not deprivation but "joy far superior to" every grade below.
Each refinement yields *more* pleasure, not less: the text builds a cosmological argument that
gratification's intensity varies inversely with its coarseness — the higher the heaven, the less the god
needs, which is the vow-architecture of Adhyāya 7 written into the map of the cosmos.

## SB 4.11–4.16 / SS 4.10–4.15 — the four classes in detail (content survey)

**Recension status:** parallel at −1 offset; one wording variant at 4.13/SS 4.12 (SB plural *sūryāś
candramasaḥ* vs SS dual *sūryacandramasau* — "suns and moons," differently numbered grammatically).

- **4.11/SS 4.10**: the ten mansion-god types (fiendish, serpentine, lightning, vulturine, fiery, stormy,
  thundering, oceanic, island, cardinal-point youths — *kumāra*, "youths," because they remain eternally
  adolescent in form), with the SB's vivid iconography (crest-jewels, snake-hoods, thunderbolts, crocodile
  and lion emblems).
- **4.12/SS 4.11**: the eight forest-god types (kinnara, kimpuruṣa, mahoraga, gandharva, yakṣa, rākṣasa,
  bhūta, piśāca) — the classical Indian spirit-bestiary absorbed wholesale into Jain cosmology as a *class
  of gods*, some attending on humans.
- **4.13/SS 4.12**: the five luminous-god types: **suns, moons, planets, constellations, scattered stars**
  — all plural: two suns over Jambūdvīpa alone, 132 in the human region (with the tripling-formula for
  successive rings), each moon with an entourage of 28 constellations, 88 planets and 66,975 × 10¹⁴ stars.
  The named planet-groups (Budha/Mercury, Śukra/Venus, Bṛhaspati/Jupiter, Aṅgāraka/Mars, Śanaiścara/Saturn)
  ride at stated heights above Meru's plateau.
- **4.14/SS 4.13**: over the human region the luminous gods **circle Mount Meru perpetually** in their
  vehicles (semi-circular, drawn by 4000 lion-, elephant-, bull- and horse-gods on each side).
- **4.15/SS 4.14** (*tatkṛtaḥ kālavibhāgaḥ*): **"the division of time is made by them"** — conventional
  time (the *āvalikā* up to the *śīrṣaprahelikā*, and beyond into pit- and ocean-measured metaphor) is
  defined by the luminous gods' revolutions. Cross-ref TS 5.22 and the `kala-dravya.md` node: Adhyāya 5's
  *kāla* is the substance time; this sūtra grounds *measured* time — the two levels the Digambara
  commentaries themselves distinguish as niścaya/vyavahāra kāla.
- **4.16/SS 4.15**: beyond the human region the vehicles stand **stationary** — luminaries fixed in place,
  radiating constant "cool and warm rays."

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED — labelled, never feeds disambiguation):** the temptation at 4.13–4.16 is
to read a heliocentric/astronomical system in or out of the text. Neither is honest: this is a flat-ringed,
Meru-centred sky with *plural* suns and moons as **living gods**, and its purpose is the taxonomy of divine
births, not celestial mechanics. `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` with any modern astronomy; the
time-measurement sūtra (4.15) is the one genuinely structural overlap (defining time operationally by
celestial motion), and even there the units being defined are karmic-cosmological, not observational.

## SB 4.17–4.24 / SS 4.16–4.23 — the empyrean gods and THE heavens fork

**Recension status:** frame sūtras parallel at −1 offset; **the heavens-list sūtra (SB 4.20 / SS 4.19) is a
genuine content fork**, the chapter's headline.

Frame: the vaimānika ("vehicle-dwelling") gods are the fourth class (4.17/SS 4.16), divided into **graded**
(*kalpopapanna* — with courts and chiefs) and **non-graded** (*kalpātīta* — "beyond the kalpas," rankless)
(4.18/SS 4.17), their heavens stacked "one above the other" (4.19/SS 4.18).

**The list sūtra, side by side:**

| aspect | Śvetāmbara (SB 4.20) | Digambara (SS 4.19, per VKJ appendix) |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------------------------|
| kalpa heavens | **12**: Saudharma, Aiśāna, Sanatkumāra, Māhendra, Brahmaloka, Lāntaka, Mahāśukra, Sahasrāra, Ānata, Prāṇata, Āraṇa, Acyuta | **16**: Saudharma, Aiśāna, Sanatkumāra, Māhendra, **Brahma + Brahmottara, Lāntava + Kāpiṣṭha, Śukra + Mahāśukra, Śatāra + Sahasrāra**, Ānata, Prāṇata, Āraṇa, Acyuta |
| chiefs | 10 indras over 12 heavens (the last four heavens share two chiefs) | 12 indras over 16 heavens (chiefs 5–8 rule two heavens each) |
| above the kalpas | 9 Graiveyakas + 5 Anuttaras (Vijaya, Vaijayanta, Jayanta, Aparājita, Sarvārthasiddha) | 9 Graiveyakas + **9 Anudiśas (subdirectional heavens — no Śvet. counterpart)** + 5 Anuttaras |
| total | **26 heavens** | **39 heavens** |

Tatia's own apparatus flags "some additional variations in this sutra between the SS and SB/SBT traditions"
and prints the two traditions' cosmos diagrams separately. The middle four Śvetāmbara heavens each *split
in two* on the Digambara side (with name-doublets — Lāntaka appears as Lāntava, Mahāśukra's pair adds a
plain Śukra, Sahasrāra's adds Śatāra), and an entire nine-heaven tier (the Anudiśas) exists only in the
Digambara cosmos. Unlike every numbering fork so far, **this one changes what the cosmos contains.**

The shared qualitative sūtras that follow are among the text's most quoted: each higher heaven exceeds the
one below in lifespan, sovereignty, blessedness, lustre, purity of colouring, sense-range and clairvoyance
(4.21/SS 4.20) — while **desire for movement, height, possessions and egotism successively decrease**
(4.22/SS 4.21): the gods shrink from seven cubits to one, and from 3,200,000 vehicles per god (Saudharma)
to five (the highest heavens), possession thinning as blessedness grows. Colouring ascends fiery → yellow →
white (4.23/SS 4.22); heavens up to the Graiveyakas are the graded ones (4.24/SS 4.23). The
nine Graiveyakas sit at the **neck** (*grīvā*) of the cosmic person — the `loka-jain.md` node's
anthropomorphic cosmography made literal.

## SB 4.25–4.27 / SS 4.24–4.26 — the terminal gods and the black gaps (content survey)

- **4.25/SS 4.24** (*brahmalokālayā lokāntikāḥ*): the **lokāntika** ("world-end") gods dwell at
  Brahmaloka's border — "divine saints," sexless, liberation-bound within a few births. A genuine SB-vs-SBT
  divergence over where exactly: SB — eight varieties in the eight directions around Brahmaloka; SBT — they
  dwell near the **black gaps** (*kṛṣṇarāji*), regions of black matter "with an enormous power of
  attraction," which the gods dare not approach in their vehicles "for fear of being pulled in" (Tatia's
  note, citing the Śvetāmbara Inner Corpus).
- **4.26/SS 4.25**: the nine varieties (Sārasvata, Āditya, Vahni, Aruṇa, Gardatoya, Tuṣita, Avyābādha,
  Marut, Ariṣṭa) mapped to the eight compass points plus centre — with small SB/SS placement divergences
  (SS omits the Maruts from the sūtra but houses them between two others; SS puts the Ariṣṭas north, SBT
  at centre).
- **4.27/SS 4.26** (*vijayādiṣu dvicaramāḥ*): gods of the first four conquest-heavens have **two human
  births remaining** (human → once more god → human → liberation); Sarvārthasiddha's residents exactly
  one. The SBT records *and rejects* the view that the intermediate god-birth must be in Sarvārthasiddha —
  a live intra-Śvetāmbara commentarial disagreement preserved rather than smoothed.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** *kṛṣṇarāji* ≠ black hole, however irresistible the image of black
regions that pull in those who approach. The structural echo (dark, attracting, avoided) is exactly the
kind of surface resemblance §0 warns about: the black gaps are located beside a specific heaven in a
finite anthropomorphic cosmos, made of "black matter" in the scriptural sense, and known by revelation,
not inference from gravitation. `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`; recorded here *because* the
conflation is so tempting, per the rule that false equivalences are flagged, never silently omitted.

## SB 4.28 / SS 4.27 — the definition of subhumans

**Recension status:** Both recensions parallel (−1 offset).

**Text (IAST, Śvet.):** *aupapātika-manuṣyebhyaḥ śeṣās tiryagyonayaḥ*

**Assembled reading:** All beings other than those of spontaneous birth (*aupapātika* — the gods and
infernals, who materialize full-formed; cf. TS 2's birth-taxonomy) and humans are **subhumans**
(*tiryagyoni* — animals, plants, micro-organisms, and the element-bodied beings). Their habitat is the
whole of cosmic space, "not defined"; the taxonomy of divine births closes by naming everything it
excluded. One quiet structural point both commentaries make: the four *gati* (destinies) of the Jain
rebirth map — infernal (Adhyāya 3), subhuman (here), human (3.14–3.18), divine (this chapter) — are now
all textually anchored.

## SB 4.29–4.53 / SS 4.28–4.42 — the lifespan section: a fork cluster (content survey + census)

**Recension status: DIVERGENT throughout — six distinct forks in 25 (Śvet.) / 15 (Dig.) sūtras.** The two
traditions carry the same *kind* of data (maxima and minima, god-class by god-class) but slice it into
sūtras entirely differently, and twice disagree on the numbers themselves.

| # | SB | SS | fork type | detail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L1 | 4.29 | — | Śvet.-only header sūtra | *sthitiḥ* — "now, lifespans" |
| L2 | 4.30–4.32 | 4.28 | **content-variant merge** | SB organizes mansion-god maxima by *chieftainship* (southern chiefs vs northern, with the asura chiefs Camara and Bali as exceptions at one ocean-measured period and a little more); SS organizes by *god-type* (asura 1 o.m.p., nāga 3 p.m.p., suparṇa 2.5, dvīpa 2, the remaining six types 1.5) — same domain, different taxonomy, not inter-derivable line by line |
| L3 | 4.33–4.35 | 4.29 | merge + small numeric nuance | SB: Saudharma 2 o.m.p., Aiśāna "a little more"; SS: both "a little more than two" |
| L4 | 4.36–4.37 | 4.30–4.31 | **numeric divergence inside the ladder** | shared rungs at 7 (Sanatkumāra[+Māhendra]), 10 (Brahma-tier), 14 (Lāntaka-tier), 18 (Sahasrāra-tier), 20 (Ānata/Prāṇata), 22 (Āraṇa/Acyuta) — but **Mahāśukra-tier: SB 17 vs SS "somewhat more than 16"**; and every SS rung is doubled across its paired heavens (the 16-heaven structure propagating into the arithmetic) |
| L5 | 4.38 | 4.32 | structural (Anudiśa insertion) | above 22: +1 o.m.p. per Graiveyaka (23…31); then SB: four Vijaya-heavens 32, Sarvārthasiddha 33; SS: **nine Anudiśas 32**, all five Anuttaras 33 |
| L6 | 4.39–4.42 | 4.33–4.34 | merge + numeric nuance + anomaly-handling | minimum lifespans; SB: Saudharma minimum 1 p.m.p.; SS: "somewhat more than" 1; both then run the elegant recursive rule *paratah paratah pūrvā pūrvā'nantarā* — **each heaven's minimum equals the maximum of the heaven below**; the SB notes the rule breaks at Sarvārthasiddha (min = max = 33, not 32), which the SS's own figures render consistent |
| L7 | 4.43–4.46 | 4.35–4.38 | parallel | infernal minima (each hell's minimum = the maximum of the hell above; first hell 10,000 years); mansion and forest gods' minima also 10,000 years |
| L8 | 4.47–4.48 | 4.39–4.40 | small numeric nuance | forest-god maximum: SB 1 p.m.p., SS "somewhat more"; luminous gods: SB "more than 1 p.m.p." (sun +1000 years, moon +100,000), SS "somewhat more than 1" |
| L9 | **4.49–4.52** | — | **Śvet.-only block** (4 sūtras) | maxima of planets (1 p.m.p.), constellations (½), stars (¼), and minima of stars (⅛) — the astronomical fine-grain exists as sūtra only in the Śvetāmbara text, the mirror image of Adhyāya 3's Digambara-only geography |
| L10 | 4.53 | 4.41 | **numeric divergence** | minimum lifespan of the (non-stellar) luminous gods: **SB ¼ p.m.p. vs SS ⅛ p.m.p.** |
| L11 | — | **4.42** | **Dig.-only closing sūtra** | all lokāntika gods live exactly 8 o.m.p., stand five cubits, white in colouring |

The recursive minimum-equals-the-floor-below rule (L6) deserves the highlight: it makes the entire heaven
stack **gapless in lifespan** — a god cannot fall through a crack between heavens' spans — and both
traditions state it in identical words (*paratah paratah pūrvā pūrvā'nantarā*) while disagreeing about the
numbers it operates on.
