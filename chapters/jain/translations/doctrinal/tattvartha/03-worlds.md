# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 3: The Lower and Middle Worlds

> **Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī), Adhyāya 3 — the seven infernal lands and their
> inhabitants' sufferings and lifespans; the concentric island-ocean geography of the middle world;
> Jambūdvīpa's continents, mountains, lakes and rivers; the time-cycles; the human habitat and its limits.
> **Recensions:** Digambara (SS = Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, sūtras 3.1–3.39) and Śvetāmbara
> (SB = Umāsvāti's *Svopajña-bhāṣya*, SBT = ṭīkā, sūtras 3.1–3.18) — **equal weight, no spine**.
> **Editions used:** Nathmal Tatia (tr.), *That Which Is: Tattvārthasūtra*, 1994 (archive.org OCR
> `ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`, read locally); Vijay K. Jain (ed./tr.), *Tattvārthasūtra with
> Sarvārthasiddhi explanation*, 2018 (archive.org OCR `Tattvartha18CompleteWeb`, read locally — its
> Devanāgarī+IAST appendix supplied the complete SS mūla for all 39 sūtras).
> **Status:** drafted (first pass, both recensions).
> **Walk-order note:** entries follow the Śvetāmbara sequence with both numbers printed (`SB 3.x / SS 3.y`);
> the Digambara-only block SS 3.12–3.32 is walked at its own position. Table-of-contents choice, not favoritism.

## Chapter shape and THE fork (read this first)

Adhyāya 3 settles a question Adhyāya 7 raised. That chapter found one five-sūtra Digambara-only "block
promotion" (bhāvanā lists as SS mūla vs SB commentary) and flagged it as a new fork type. **This chapter has
the same fork type at four times the size — a twenty-one-sūtra Digambara-only block (SS 3.12–3.32)** carrying
Jambūdvīpa's detailed sacred geography (mountain colours, summit lakes, lotus-dwelling goddesses, the
fourteen rivers and their tributaries, continent widths, the time-cycles and the stable continents'
conditions). Tatia's own apparatus introduces it in exactly those terms: "This sutra [SS 3.12] begins the
twenty-one additional sutras in the SS which describe the colours of the mountains and their mineral
wealth, as also the lakes, rivers, gardens…" — and his note at SB 3.11 states that **the SB covers the same
ground in its commentary** ("The SB details the locations and dimensions of the seven continents of Jambu
and the six mountains which divide them. For these details, see SS 3.32"). So block promotion is not an
Adhyāya-7 curiosity; it is a *recurring structural relationship* between the recensions: content the
Śvetāmbara tradition holds at bhāṣya rank, the Digambara tradition holds at sūtra rank.

The same rank-difference occurs in miniature at **3.2**, inside the otherwise-shared opening run: SB 3.2 is
two words (*tasu narakāḥ*, "in them, the hells"), with the number of hell-dwellings per land supplied by
the SB commentary; **SS 3.2 is a long numeric sūtra that puts those very numbers into the mūla itself**
(*tasu triṃśat-pañcaviṃśati-pañcadaśa-daśa-tri-pañconaika-narakaśatasahasrāṇi pañca caiva yathākramam* —
thirty, twenty-five, fifteen, ten, three lakh, one-less-than-a-lakh [99,995], and five hells, respectively).
The figures **agree** across the recensions (SB's commentary table: 3,000,000 / 2,500,000 / 1,500,000 /
1,000,000 / 300,000 / 99,995 / 5) — what differs is which textual rank carries them.

Net arithmetic: numbering runs identical from 3.1 to 3.11; the block SS 3.12–3.32 then opens a constant
**+21** offset (SB 3.12 = SS 3.33 … SB 3.18 = SS 3.39); chapter-end counts (Śvet. 18, Dig. 39) are exactly
explained, with no residual gap — the clean-arithmetic pattern of Adhyāyas 7, 8 and 9 rather than the open
items of 2 and 6.

Where the recensions genuinely *disagree on content* in this chapter, it is — fittingly for a cosmography —
**numeric**: the depths of the infernal lands and the temperature-distribution across the seven hells (both
below, at 3.1 and 3.3). Those are commentarial-tradition divergences over a shared mūla, the same fork-genus
as Adhyāya 5's atomic-bonding tables.

## SB 3.1 / SS 3.1

**Recension status:** mūla near-identical with **one Śvetāmbara-only word** — Tatia's own footnote: "In the
SS tradition, this sutra does not include *pṛthutaraḥ*, translated as 'with each lower land having a wider
base than the one above.'" The commentarial numbers under the shared text then diverge substantially (below).

**Text — Śvetāmbara (IAST, per Tatia):** *ratna-śarkarā-vālukā-paṅka-dhūma-tamo-mahātamaḥprabhā bhūmayo
ghanāmbu-vātākāśa-pratiṣṭhāḥ saptādho **pṛthutarāḥ***
**Text — Digambara (IAST, per VKJ appendix):** *ratnaśarkarāvālukāpaṅkadhūmatamomahātamaḥprabhā bhūmayo
ghanāmbuvātākāśapratiṣṭhāḥ saptadho'dhaḥ*

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| *ratna…prabhā* | seven-member bahuvrīhi naming the lands | *ratna* "jewel, gem"; *śarkarā* "gravel, grit, pebbles, shingle, gravelly mould or soil (mostly plural); ground or candied sugar; potsherd" — the gravel/pebble branch operative here, not the sugar branch; *vālukā* "sand"; *paṅka* "mud"; *dhūma* "smoke"; *tamas* "darkness"; *mahātamas* "great darkness"; *-prabhā* "light, lustre, hue" — each land is named "having the hue of X" | MW, via wisdomlib.org (śarkarā entry fetched; the rest are uncontested common headwords consistent across both editions' renderings) |
| *bhūmayaḥ* | fem. nom. pl. | "earth, ground, land" | MW |
| *ghana-ambu-vāta-ākāśa-pratiṣṭhāḥ* | bahuvrīhi | "supported on (*pratiṣṭhā* 'standing-place, base') dense (*ghana*) water (*ambu*), wind (*vāta*), and space (*ākāśa*)" | MW; both editions concur on the decomposition |
| *saptadhā adhaḥ* | adverbs | "sevenfold, below" | MW |
| *pṛthutarāḥ* (Śvet. only) | comparative adj., fem. pl. | "wider, broader" (*pṛthu* + -tara) | MW; flagged by Tatia's own apparatus as absent from SS |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The support-stack is read by both traditions the same way: each land
floats on a sheath of dense ocean, which floats on dense air, then thin air, then self-supporting space —
the SBT illustrating water-standing-on-air with its six-step balloon experiment (inflate, tie at neck and
middle, empty the top, fill with water, retie, untie the middle: the water now rests on air). The
Śvetāmbara-only *pṛthutarāḥ* makes the widening-downward shape (1, 2.5, 4, 5, 6, 6.5, 7 "ropes" per the SBT)
part of the sūtra itself; the SS reaches the same funnel shape without the word.

**A genuine numeric commentarial divergence, presented as the comparison it is:**

| aspect | Śvetāmbara (SBT) | Digambara (SS) |
|---|---|---|
| depths of the seven lands (yojanas, top→bottom) | 180,000 · 132,000 · 128,000 · 120,000 · 118,000 · 116,000 · 108,000 | 180,000 · 32,000 · 28,000 · 24,000 · 20,000 · 16,000 · 8,000 |
| the interleaving layers | dense ocean 20,000 yojanas; dense-air/thin-air/space layers *increase* with depth, none less than innumerable yojanas | dense ocean, dense air and thin air **each** 20,000 yojanas |

Only the first land's depth agrees. The two traditions transmit different quantitative cosmographies under
an identical sūtra — the largest bare-number divergence this reading room has recorded.

**Assembled reading:** Below [the middle world] are seven lands, one beneath the other, each resting on
dense water, dense air, thin air and space [Śvet. adds: each wider than the one above]: the gem-hued,
pebble-hued, sand-hued, mud-hued, smoke-hued, dark, and pitch-dark.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "The infernal beings live in seven lands which are one below the other (with each lower land having a wider base than the one above)… gem-hued, pebble-hued, sand-hued, mud-hued, smoke-hued, dark and pitch-dark lands."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): renders the same seven names with the *ghanāmbu-vāta-ākāśa* support-stack (SS text, without the width clause).

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A — and deliberately so. Nothing here is geology or astronomy; the
*rajju* ("rope") and *yojana* are scriptural units inside a soteriological map (where you can be reborn, and
what conduct sends you there). Treating the seven prabhās as strata in the modern geophysical sense would be
the exact §0 failure-mode.

**Links to concept nodes:** [[loka-jain]] (the cosmic-person shape, the three-vase image, and the rajju
measurements are that node's subject; this sūtra is its textual anchor for the lower world)

## SB 3.2 / SS 3.2 — the same numbers at two textual ranks

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — mūla-rank fork (miniature of THE block fork).**

| aspect | Śvetāmbara | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi) |
|--------|------------|------------------------------|
| number | SB 3.2 | SS 3.2 |
| text (IAST) | *tasu narakāḥ* — "in those [lands are] the hells" | *tasu triṃśatpañcaviṃśatipañcadaśadaśatripañconaika-narakaśatasahasrāṇi pañca caiva yathākramam* |
| where the counts live | SB commentary: 3,000,000 · 2,500,000 · 1,500,000 · 1,000,000 · 300,000 · 99,995 · 5 dwelling-places, land by land | in the sūtra itself: thirty, twenty-five, fifteen, ten, three hundred-thousands; a lakh-less-five [99,995]; and five |
| what it commits you to | the numbers are exposition — revisable commentary | the numbers are scripture — fixed with the text |

The two traditions agree on every figure; they disagree on whether the figures are *canon*. Strata per land
(SB commentary): 13, 11, 9, 7, 5, 3, 1 — the infernal architecture thins as it descends, ending in the
single vast stratum of the seventh land.

## SB 3.3–3.6 / SS 3.3–3.6 — the infernal condition (content survey)

**Recension status:** numbering shared; SS 3.3 opens with an explicit *narakāḥ* subject-word the SB text
carries over from 3.2 — wording, not content. One genuine commentarial divergence at 3.3 (temperatures, below).

- **SB/SS 3.3** (*[narakā] nityāśubhatara-leśyā-pariṇāma-deha-vedanā-vikriyāḥ*): the infernal beings are
  perpetually subject to increasingly inauspicious **leśyā** (soul-colouring), metabolism, bodies,
  experiences and shape-shiftings. The commentary's leśyā exposition — physical vs psychic colouring, the
  six colours (black/blue/grey inauspicious, red/yellow/white auspicious), and the land-by-land assignment
  (pure grey in the first hell darkening to blackish in the seventh) — is the TS's own textual anchor for
  the existing `leshya.md` node. Their protean bodies change shape to escape suffering and multiply it
  instead.
  **The temperature divergence (commentarial, over shared mūla):** SBT — heat rises to its maximum in the
  third hell; the fourth is hot with one cold pocket; the fifth reversed (cold with a hot pocket); sixth
  cold, seventh colder. SS — heat continues through the **fourth**; the fifth is hot above and cold below;
  sixth and seventh cold only. Same architecture, different thermostat — recorded, not harmonized.
- **SB/SS 3.4** (*parasparodīrita-duḥkhāḥ*): "they inflict pain on one another." Deluded clairvoyance
  (cross-ref the avadhi treatment in Adhyāya 1/`avadhi-jnana.md`) lets them perceive only what frightens;
  they improvise weapons out of the earth itself and fight like crow and owl, snake and mongoose.
- **SB/SS 3.5** (*saṃkliṣṭāsurodīrita-duḥkhāś ca prāk caturthyāḥ*): "and, above the fourth land, pain
  inflicted by the malevolent demonic gods" — fifteen classes of mansion-dwelling sadist deities (cross-ref
  4.1) who force molten iron down throats and roast, grind and boil the infernals for sport. The SB's
  aetiology: these are souls who *squandered* good karma — austerities performed in deceit, greed and
  deluded world-view (cross-ref 8.1) yield karma "apparently beneficial but with a sting in the tail."
  Tatia's translator note generalizes: it is the motivation, not the austerity, that determines its fruit —
  the same disposition-over-event logic as Adhyāya 7's hiṃsā definition.
- **SB/SS 3.6** (*teṣv eka-tri-sapta-daśa-saptadaśa-dvāviṃśati-trayastriṃśat-sāgaropamā sattvānāṃ parā
  sthitiḥ*): maximum lifespans in the seven lands — 1, 3, 7, 10, 17, 22, 33 *sāgaropama* ("ocean-measured
  periods"). The commentary adds the rebirth-mechanics: which species can fall how far (non-rational
  five-sensed animals only to the first hell … male humans and rational aquatics to the seventh); gods and
  infernals cannot be reborn directly in hell, nor infernals in heaven; and the post-infernal prospects
  thin with depth — souls from all seven hells can still attain the enlightened world-view in the next
  life, from the first five can take monastic restraint, from the first four can be liberated, and only
  those from the first three can become a **Jina**.

## SB 3.7–3.11 / SS 3.7–3.11 — the middle world's ring-geography (content survey)

**Recension status:** numbering shared; one minor mūla wording variant at 3.7 (Śvet. *jambūdvīpa-lavaṇādayaḥ*
vs SS *jambūdvīpa-lavaṇodādayaḥ* — "Lavaṇa etc." vs "Lavaṇoda etc.," the same ocean under a fuller name).

- **3.7**: there are islands and oceans bearing auspicious names — Jambūdvīpa, the Lavaṇa (Salt) Ocean, and
  so on.
- **3.8** (*dvir dvir viṣkambhāḥ pūrva-pūrva-parikṣepiṇo valayākṛtayaḥ*): each successive ring is **double**
  the breadth of the one it encircles — a geometric doubling out to the innumerable-th ring
  (Svayambhūramaṇa, the last ocean).
- **3.9** (*tanmadhye merunābhir vṛtto yojanaśatasahasra-viṣkambho jambūdvīpaḥ*): at the centre, round "as
  a potter's wheel," Jambūdvīpa — 100,000 yojanas across, with **Mount Meru as its navel** (*nābhi*), whose
  base touches the first infernal land and whose peak touches the celestial region: the axis of the whole
  three-world stack.
- **3.10**: Jambūdvīpa's seven continents (*varṣa/kṣetra*), south to north: **Bharata**, Haimavata, Hari,
  **Videha**, Ramyaka, Hairaṇyavata, **Airāvata**. The SBT preserves a striking commentarial argument about
  direction (east for Bharata's people is west for east-Videha's) from which Tatia draws the observation
  that the ancient Jaina geographers' Jambū behaves like a sphere rather than a flat disc — reported here
  as the SBT's own reasoning, not as a modern-science vindication.
- **3.11**: the six east-west mountain ranges dividing the continents: Himavān, Mahāhimavān, Niṣadha, Nīla,
  Rukmin, Śikharin. **Here the recensions part company**: the SB puts every further detail in commentary;
  the SS unrolls it as scripture —

## THE FORK — SS 3.12–3.32: the sacred-geography block (Digambara-only, 21 sūtras)

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — block promotion, the reading room's second and largest instance.** Content
survey below (sūtra-by-sūtra padaccheda deferred; the block is a gazetteer, and its finding is structural).
IAST from the VKJ appendix; Tatia prints each with an "(SS 3.x)" label inside his SB-ordered text.

| SS sūtras | content |
|---|---|
| 3.12–3.13 | the six mountains' colours and build: golden as Chinese silk, Arjuna-tree white, rising-sun crimson, sapphire blue, silver white, golden — sides "spotted with variegated jewels," equal in girth at base, middle and peak |
| 3.14–3.18 | the six summit-lakes (Padma, Mahāpadma, Tigiñcha, Kesarī, Mahāpuṇḍarīka, Puṇḍarīka); the first 1000×500 yojanas and 10 deep, with a one-yojana lotus at its centre; lakes and lotuses doubling progressively |
| 3.19 | the six goddesses dwelling in palaces **on the lotuses** — Śrī (Fortune), Hrī (Modesty), Dhṛti (Patience), Kīrti (Fame), Buddhi (Wisdom), Lakṣmī (Wealth) — with pit-measured lifespans and retinues of co-chief and counsellor gods |
| 3.20–3.23 | the fourteen rivers (Gaṅgā, Sindhu, Rohit, Rohitāsyā, Harit, Harikāntā, Sītā, Sītodā, Nārī, Narakāntā, Suvarṇakūlā, Rūpyakūlā, Raktā, Raktodā); odd-numbered flow east, the rest west; Gaṅgā and Sindhu each with 14,000 tributaries, doubling per continent to Videha then halving |
| 3.24–3.26 | Bharata's width fixed at **526 6/19 yojanas**; continents and mountains doubling in width to Videha then mirroring; north like south |
| 3.27–3.28 | **the time-cycles**: only Bharata and Airāvata ride the six-spoke descending and ascending half-cycles (*utsarpiṇī/avasarpiṇī* — this is the TS's own textual anchor for the existing `utsarpini-avasarpini.md` node); the other continents hold constant conditions. The commentary dates our own position: the fifth descending aeon ("privation"), begun three years and eight months after Mahāvīra's liberation, running 21,000 years |
| 3.29–3.31 | lifespans/statures/meal-intervals of the stable continents' inhabitants (one/two/three pit-measured periods; 2000/4000/6000 bows; meals every two/three/four days); Videha's humans live numerable years (up to one *pūrvakoṭi*) |
| 3.32 | the closing ratio: Bharata's width = **1/190 of Jambūdvīpa's diameter** — the elegant 1,2,4,8,16,32,64,32,16,8,4,2,1 doubling-halving series summing to 190 units, from which SS 3.24's 526 6/19 follows arithmetically (100,000 ÷ 190) |

**What the fork commits you to** is the same as Adhyāya 7's, at scale: for the Digambara tradition this
gazetteer — down to the goddesses' names and the rivers' tributary-counts — is *sūtra*, as canonical as the
definition of hiṃsā; for the Śvetāmbara tradition it is *bhāṣya*, exposition on a leaner scripture. Note the
two block-promotions run the **same direction** (Digambara mūla richer in both) — opposite to Adhyāya 9's
Śvetāmbara-only sūtras. Neither recension is simply "the longer one"; it varies chapter by chapter, which is
itself evidence the two textual traditions crystallized independently rather than one abridging the other.

At SB 3.12 / SS 3.33, in Tatia's own words, "the SB, SBT and SS traditions converge once more."

## SB 3.12–3.18 / SS 3.33–3.39 — the human world and its limits (content survey)

**Recension status:** parallel at +21 offset; one trivial orthographic variant (SB 3.17 *parāpare* vs SS
3.38 *parāvare*, "maximum-and-minimum").

- **SB 3.12 / SS 3.33** (*dvir dhātakīkhaṇḍe*): Dhātakīkhaṇḍa, the second island-ring, carries **twice**
  Jambū's geography — two Merus, two Bharatas, two of everything, the continent-count doubling with the
  ring's width.
- **SB 3.13 / SS 3.34** (*puṣkarārdhe ca*): and the same doubled set again in the **inner half** of Puṣkara
  Island.
- **SB 3.14 / SS 3.35** (*prāṅ mānuṣottarān manuṣyāḥ*): "humans exist up to Mount Mānuṣottara" — the
  mountain-ring bisecting Puṣkara whose name means "furthest limit of human habitation." Humanity's habitat
  is thus exactly **two-and-a-half islands**: Jambū + Dhātakīkhaṇḍa + inner Puṣkara = 5 Bharatas, 5
  Airāvatas, 5 Videhas … 35 continents in all. Beyond the wall: animals only.
- **SB 3.15 / SS 3.36** (*āryā mlecchāś ca*): humans fall into two classes. MW's entry for *mleccha* gives
  the term's Brahmanical career ("a foreigner, barbarian, non-Āryan, man of an outcast race, any person who
  does not speak Sanskrit and does not conform to the usual Hindū institutions"); the Jain commentarial
  taxonomies differ from that and from each other — SB: ārya by race, family, profession, expertise or
  language; SS: some āryas with supernatural powers, the rest classed by country of birth, race,
  profession, conduct or enlightened world-view, and (SS only) some mlecchas born **inside** the
  spiritual-effort lands. Tatia's translator note is candid that the full commentarial descriptions are "a
  mixture of myth, history and politics" (dynasty lists, animal-faced island peoples) — this unit reports
  the classification as the text's own social geography and does not adjudicate it.
- **SB 3.16 / SS 3.37** (*bharatairāvata-videhāḥ karmabhūmayo'nyatra devakurūttarakurubhyaḥ*): the
  **karmabhūmi** sūtra — spiritual effort (and therefore liberation) is possible only in the 5 Bharatas, 5
  Airāvatas and 5 Videhas, minus the Deva-Kuru and Uttara-Kuru pleasure-lands (where "supreme plenty…
  is detrimental to spiritual effort": needs met by wish-granting trees, no suffering to awaken anyone).
  The commentary's Jina-arithmetic: 32 sub-continents per Videha × 5 = 160 "conquest" provinces; at least
  20 Jinas alive at any moment, at most **170** (160 + one per Bharata and Airāvata); in Bharata/Airāvata
  the 24-Jina sequences run only during aeons 3–4 of the descent and 2–4 of the ascent (cross-ref SS 3.27;
  the `tirthankara.md` node's cycle-distribution doctrine is this passage).
- **SB 3.17 / SS 3.38**: human lifespan — minimum one *antarmuhūrta* ("intra-hour"), maximum three
  pit-measured periods (*palyopama*).
- **SB 3.18 / SS 3.39** (*tiryagyonijānāṃ ca*): "and likewise for the animal-born." The SB commentary's
  species table (pure earth-bodied 12,000 years; fire-bodied three days and nights; two-sensed twelve
  years; rational aquatics one pūrvakoṭi…) is the quantitative companion to the ekendriya taxonomy of
  Adhyāya 2.
