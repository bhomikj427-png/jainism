# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 6 (The Inflow of Karma)

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra, Adhyāya 6. Having covered the soul (Adhyāya 2) and, in the chapters between (3–5,
not yet drafted in this reading room — see work queue), the cosmos and matter, the treatise turns to the third
*tattva* named at 1.4: *āsrava*, karmic influx. This chapter defines action (*yoga*) as influx's mechanism,
splits influx by moral valence and duration, and works systematically through the specific causes of each of
the eight karma-types (already the subject of the existing `karma-prakriti.md` concept node).

**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at equal weight, no spine. **A running one-sūtra
Digambara-behind offset holds for almost the entire chapter**, but — unlike Adhyāya 2's untraced gap — this
chapter's own contents table (given by Tatia's apparatus) shows the offset is clean and fully accounted for:
Śvetāmbara's 6.3–6.4 (good actions cause beneficial karma-influx; evil actions cause harmful karma-influx,
as two separate sūtras) are **one single sūtra in Digambara (SS 6.3)** — the first absorption, after which
Digambara runs consistently one behind (SB 6.5 = SS 6.4, ... SB 6.22 = SS 6.23) **until sūtra 6.24, where a
second absorption partially self-corrects**: Śvetāmbara's 6.18 (causes of human birth: reduced aggression,
reduced possessiveness, *and* a gentle/straightforward nature, all in one sūtra) is **split into two sūtras in
Digambara** (SS 6.17: reduced aggression and possessiveness; SS 6.18: gentle nature, separately) — a genuine
**split fork**, the mirror image of the 6.3/6.3 merge, and by Tatia's own count this second fork does not
fully cancel the first (Digambara still runs one sūtra behind at the chapter's end: SB 6.26 = SS 6.27, per
the chapter's own contents table, meaning a net *two*-sūtra divergence must in fact be present somewhere in
this run for the visible one-sūtra-behind figure to hold at 6.26/6.27 despite one merge and one split — **this
reading room did not fully reconcile the arithmetic this pass and flags it as an open item**, rather than
asserting a clean resolution it has not verified sūtra-by-sūtra). **A separate, genuine doctrinal divergence**
sits at sūtra 6.20/SS 6.21: Śvetāmbara lists the causes of birth as a god (attachment-tinged self-restraint,
partial/lay restraint, involuntary karma-purging, misguided austerities) as one sūtra, and Tatia's own
commentary records that the SS tradition explicitly *adds* the enlightened world-view itself as a further,
independent cause — not a wording variant but a substantive doctrinal addition, given its own sūtra number
(SS 6.21) rather than folded into the shared sūtra.

## Sūtras 6.1–6.2 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *kāya-vāṅ-manaḥ karma yogaḥ* / *sa āsravaḥ*

**Padaccheda:** *Yoga* here is *not* the meditative-discipline sense treated in `yoga-darshana.md` — the TS uses
it in its literal MW sense, "yoking, joining, application," specifically the vibrational "activity" of body
(*kāya*), speech (*vāc*), and mind (*manas*). This activity (*karma* in its verbal, "action," sense — distinct
from *karma* the accumulated-matter noun the rest of the chapter concerns) **is** influx (*āsrava*, already the
`asrava.md` node's own term).

**Disambiguation:** Both Tatia and the existing `asrava.md` concept node agree: the soul's beginningless
karmic body channels the soul's own infinite power into a limited, karma-mediated form that vibrates the body,
speech-organ, and mind; this vibration is what draws further karmic matter inward. An omniscient soul's total
elimination of destructive karma removes the channeling constraint entirely — a boundary case this sūtra pair
doesn't itself state but which Tatia's commentary draws out.

**Assembled reading:** The activity of body, speech, and mind is action (*yoga*). That [threefold action] is
influx.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtras 6.1–6.2.

## Sūtras 6.3–6.4 (Śvet.) / 6.3 (Dig., merged) — the merge fork
Good action causes beneficial (*puṇya*) karmic influx; evil action causes harmful (*pāpa*) influx — both
already the subject of the existing `punya.md`/`papa.md` nodes. Śvetāmbara gives these as two sūtras;
Digambara's Sarvārthasiddhi tradition states both in a single sūtra (SS 6.3) — the chapter's first numbering
fork, a clean merge with no content difference (unlike the doctrinal 6.20/SS 6.21 addition noted above). Tatia
records the SS's own gloss that an act's good/evil valence is fixed by the actor's *intention*, and that even a
"good" action's resulting bondage always carries some non-beneficial karma-residue (knowledge-covering,
intuition-covering, deluding, obstructive) alongside its beneficial component — moral action attenuates but
does not fully escape the general mechanics of bondage.

## Sūtras 6.5–6.10 (Śvet.) / 6.4–6.9 (Dig.) — how passion determines bondage-duration
An action performed by a soul still driven by passion (*kaṣāya*, the `kashaya.md` node's own term) causes
long-term karmic bondage; the same act performed by a passion-free soul (paradigmatically, an advanced ascetic)
binds and releases within a single time-unit — near-instantaneous. The chapter then names the "doors" through
which long-term influx specifically enters: the five senses, the four passions, five categories of
transgression (violence, lying, theft, unchastity, possessiveness — the negative mirror of the five great vows
that Adhyāya 7, next in this reading room's queue, formalizes), and a scriptural list of twenty-five further
psychological "urges" spanning everything from doctrinal misinterpretation to environmental damage (Tatia
reproduces the full list; not re-transcribed here — see Sources). The *degree* of resulting bondage is then
further modulated by intensity of passion, whether the act was done knowingly, the energy invested, and the
instrument used (6.7/SS 6.6); and finally the chapter names the "instruments" of influx as both sentient (the
soul's own intending, preparing, and acting) and non-sentient (the body and physical implements used), with a
combinatorial 3×3×3×4 = 108-mode breakdown of the sentient instrument alone (intention/preparation/commission ×
body/speech/mind × self-done/instigated/approved × the four passions) at 6.9/SS 6.8 — both recensions agree on
every count in this run.

## Sūtras 6.11–6.15 (Śvet.) / 6.10–6.14 (Dig.) — causes of the four "ghāti"-adjacent karma types
Slander, concealment of one's own or others' knowledge, envy, obstructiveness toward learning, and disrespect
for the scripture or its keepers cause knowledge- and intuition-covering influx (6.11/SS 6.10). Inflicting pain,
grief, or violence — on oneself or others, motive-dependent (Tatia notes a surgeon's or an ascetic's own
self-mortification does not count, since the *intent* is not harmful) — causes pain-producing influx (6.12/SS
6.11), while compassion, charity, attachment-tinged self-restraint, blameless conduct, forbearance, and freedom
from greed cause pleasure-producing influx (6.13/SS 6.12) — Tatia's translator's note flags this sūtra as a
notable *expansion* of an older, narrower canonical source (the *Bhagavatī*'s restriction of pleasure-karma's
cause to compassion alone), an internal-tradition development worth recording as such rather than treating the
TS's fuller list as if it had always been the whole tradition's position. Maligning the Jinas, scripture,
monastic order, or gods causes view-deluding influx (6.14/SS 6.13); the rise of the four passions or nine
"quasi-passions" (laughter, distaste, ennui, grief, fear, disgust, and the three gender-linked sexual
dispositions — the same nine already implicated in the `kashaya.md` node's broader treatment) causes
conduct-deluding influx (6.15/SS 6.14).

## Sūtras 6.16–6.20 (Śvet.) / 6.15–6.21 (Dig.) — causes of lifespan-karma (which realm one is reborn into)
Habitual violence and extreme possessiveness cause rebirth as an infernal being; deceitfulness causes rebirth
as an animal (a category Tatia's note clarifies includes one-sensed elemental-bodied beings and plants, matching
the taxonomy already treated in the Adhyāya 2 unit of this reading room); mild aggression/possessiveness plus a
gentle, straightforward disposition cause human rebirth — **the site of the split fork detailed in the
recension-model note above** (one Śvetāmbara sūtra vs. two Digambara sūtras for the same content); amorality
and self-indulgence are named a common aggravating cause across all three lower realms (6.19); and
attachment-tinged self-restraint, partial/lay vow-keeping, involuntary karma-shedding, or misguided austerity
cause rebirth as a god — **the site of the doctrinal-addition fork**, where the Digambara/SS tradition's own
sūtra (6.21) adds the enlightened world-view itself as a further cause of divine rebirth, matching (per Tatia)
an SBT commentarial gloss that reaches the same expanded list without needing a separate sūtra of its own.

## Sūtras 6.21–6.26 (Śvet.) / 6.22–6.27 (Dig.) — causes of body-type and social-status karma
Crooked, misleading action causes inauspicious body-karma; its opposite (straightforwardness, harmony) causes
auspicious body-karma (6.21–6.22/SS 6.22–6.23). Sūtra 6.23/SS 6.24 gives the chapter's longest single list: a
set of causes (Tatia's source numbers them sixteen; a variant scriptural list elsewhere gives twenty, per
Tatia's own note, substituting the last item for five further devotional qualities) whose cultivation shapes
the karma that determines a **Tīrthaṅkara's** own future embodiment — purity of world-view, humility,
faultless vow-observance, sustained study of the seven *tattva*s, dread of transmigration, charity and
austerity, harmony-building within the monastic order, service to ascetics, devotion to the Jina/teacher/
learned-monks/scripture, faithful practice of the six compulsory monastic duties, and active promotion of the
path. Defaming others while praising oneself, or concealing others' merits while dwelling on their faults,
causes low-status karma (6.24/SS 6.25); the reverse, paired with humility, causes high-status karma (6.25/SS
6.26). Obstructing any of the five capacities named already at 2.4–2.5 (beneficence, gain, satisfaction,
comfort, power) causes obstructive-karma influx (6.26/SS 6.27), the chapter's final sūtra.
