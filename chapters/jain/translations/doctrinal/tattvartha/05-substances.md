# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 5: The Non-Soul Substances (ajīva)

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī, c. 2nd–5th c. CE — dating contested, not resolved here)
**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at **equal weight, no spine** (see `INDEX.md` §A). Digambara
readings anchored to Ācārya Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*; Śvetāmbara readings anchored to Umāsvāti's own
*Svopajña-bhāṣya* (tradition ascribes the bhāṣya to the sūtra's author in the Śvetāmbara lineage only).
**Editions/translations used this unit:** Vijay K. Jain (2018, *Ācārya Umāsvāmī's Tattvārthasūtra — with
Sarvārthasiddhi*, via wisdomlib.org mirror); J.L. Jaini (1920, *Tattvarthadhigama Sutra*, Sacred Books of the
Jainas vol. 2, via jainworld.com mirror); Nathmal Tatia (1994, *That Which Is*, Yale/Sacred Literature Trust,
via archive.org OCR text — retrieved by direct download + local grep after the standard fetch tool truncated
the document); Monier-Williams *Sanskrit-English Dictionary* (via Cologne Digital Sanskrit Dictionaries /
wisdomlib.org). **Three genuinely independent translations** now compared — confidence on the translation
panel is **high**: all three converge on the doctrinal sense, and Tatia's wording diverges just enough from
the other two (see comparison below) to confirm they are not silently copying one another.

## TS Dig. 5.1 / Śvet. 5.1

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical — same wording, same sūtra number. Multiple independent
secondary descriptions of the Digambara/Śvetāmbara textual divergence in the Tattvārtha Sūtra place the known
discrepancies elsewhere (chapter divisions, sūtra counts later in some chapters, some interpolations); no source
found records 5.1 itself as contested in wording or numbering. Divergence in Adhyāya 5 numbering (if any turns
up later in this chapter — the queue continues to 5.2 onward) will get its own side-by-side table when reached.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):**
> ajīvakāyā dharmādharmākāśapudgalāḥ || 5.1 ||

**Devanāgarī:**
> अजीवकाया धर्माधर्माकाशपुद्गलाः ॥ ५.१ ॥

*(Devanāgarī and IAST as given at wisdomlib.org's mirror of Vijay K. Jain's edition; cross-checked against the
independent transliteration on jainworld.com, which agrees on the word sequence.)*

|------|------------------|------------------------------|-----------------|

| jīva | m., stem in compound | "any living being, anything living"; "the principle of life, vital breath, the living or personal soul" | MW, s.v. *jīva* |

| dharma | m., first member of dvandva, nom. pl. *-āḥ* | general Sanskrit range: "that which is established or firm," law, custom, duty, virtue, religious merit — **no Jain "medium of motion" sense appears in the general MW entry** | MW, s.v. *dharma* |

| pudgala | m., fourth member of dvandva | general/Purāṇic: "beautiful, handsome" (adj.); as noun: "the body"; **MW separately flags "(with Jainas) material object including atoms"** as a distinct Jain technical sense already lexicalized | MW, s.v. *pudgala* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):**

Two words in this sūtra cannot be correctly read off the general lexicon alone — this is the case the
prototype was chosen to test.

1. **Why "kāya"?** *Kāya* ordinarily means a physical body. The *Sarvārthasiddhi* commentary explains the
   extension explicitly: the term is applied to dharma, adharma, ākāśa, and pudgala **by analogy** to a
   physical body, because — like a body — each is a single whole made of a manifoldness of space-points
   (*bahupradeśī*, "having many *pradeśa*s"). The commentary further states this is *why* the fifth ajīva
   substance, **time (kāla)**, is deliberately left out of this sūtra's scope: kāla has no second space-point
   to aggregate, so it cannot take the *-kāya* label. This is a targeted, commentary-driven exclusion, not an
   oversight — Adhyāya 5 addresses kāla separately later in the chapter. (Source: Sarvārthasiddhi, as reported
   in Vijay K. Jain's edition/commentary, wisdomlib.org mirror.)
2. **Why "dharma"/"adharma" here mean "medium of motion/rest," not "duty"/"vice"?** The general MW entries
   for *dharma* and *adharma* carry no trace of this sense — a reader working from the dictionary alone would
   default to the ethical/legal meaning that dominates elsewhere in Sanskrit literature (and elsewhere even
   within Jain ethical vocabulary — see the *unrelated* sense of *dharma* as "the teaching/path," a separate
   node). The narrowing to "principle enabling motion" / "principle enabling rest" is supplied entirely by the
   Jain commentarial-technical tradition and is stated identically across the exegetical summaries consulted:
   "the functions of the media of motion (dharma) and rest (adharma) are to assist (*upagraha*) motion (*gati*)
   and rest respectively" (Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.17 gloss, cited via the same commentarial line as this sūtra —
   internal-consistency check: the technical sense introduced at 5.1 is confirmed, not contradicted, by 5.17
   later in the same chapter). This is a clean case of "lexicon gives the outer bound, commentary decides which
   sense wins" (CLAUDE.md §4, signal 3 vs. signal 2).

**Assembled reading:** The sūtra names, as a set, the ajīva ("non-soul") substances that qualify as *kāya*
("body-like," i.e. extended across many space-points): dharma (the principle enabling motion), adharma (the
principle enabling rest), ākāśa (space), and pudgala (matter). All four share the negative mark of lacking
*jīva*'s defining characteristic (sentience/life), and are grouped here specifically because each — unlike the
still-to-come fifth ajīva substance, time — occupies a plurality of space-points and so merits the "body"
label by the commentary's stated analogy.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- J.L. Jaini (1920, Sacred Books of the Jainas, mirrored verbatim at jainworld.com): "The non-living substances
  (bodies) are the medium of motion, the medium of rest, space and matter."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, via wisdomlib.org): "The non-[soul] substances (bodies) — ajīvakāya — are the medium of
  motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma), the space (ākāśa) and the matter (pudgala)."
- Nathmal Tatia (1994, *That Which Is*, p. 123): "**The media of motion and of rest, space and matter are
  extended non-sentient entities.**" His accompanying note glosses this directly: the four are "extended in
  space," each built of *pradeśa* ("units," his term for the smallest indivisible part of the substance) —
  i.e. Tatia unpacks *-kāya* straight into "extended," rather than keeping the literal "(bodies)" gloss Jaini
  and Vijay K. Jain both retain.
- **All three agree** on the core doctrinal content: dharma = medium of motion, adharma = medium of rest,
  ākāśa/pudgala = space/matter, all four grouped as extended non-sentient entities. **Genuine, informative
  divergence** on one point: Jaini and Vijay K. Jain translate *-kāya* literally as "(bodies)" and leave the
  space-point reasoning to a separate commentary note, while Tatia folds the commentarial payload (extension
  across multiple space-points) directly into the running translation as "extended." Both choices are
  defensible — literal-plus-footnote versus interpretive-inline — and the difference is a real translator
  choice, not textual disagreement; it is exactly the kind of divergence that shows these three are
  independent renderings rather than copies of one another.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED — labelled, never feeds disambiguation):** Popular Jain-apologetic literature
sometimes reaches for *dharma*/*adharma* as "medium of motion/rest" and analogizes them to the 19th-century
physics postulate of a luminiferous **ether**, or more loosely to modern "space." At least one such source
explicitly available online (jainworld.jainworld.com, "Jain Metaphysics and Science: A Comparison") states its
own caution on this point: dharma is **not** a medium of motion in the ether sense, dharma/adharma are
non-material (ether, as historically conceived, was a material-like medium), and Einstein's relativity — where
space and time are not absolute and are bound up with matter — is explicitly **not** what Jain cosmology
describes (Jain kāla is treated as discrete time-atoms occupying fixed space-points, not a relativistic
space-time). Recorded here as `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`: real surface-level vocabulary overlap
("medium," "motion," "space" as a substance) invites the comparison, but the physical content does not match —
neither the historical ether concept nor general relativity is what Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.1 is describing. This
box does not feed back into the disambiguation above, which rests entirely on the Sarvārthasiddhi/bhāṣya
tradition.

## TS Dig. 5.2–5.3 / Śvet. 5.2 — NUMBERING FORK (merge)

**Recension status:** DIVERGENT — a genuine numbering fork, not just a wording variant. The Digambara
(Sarvārthasiddhi) recension gives **two** sūtras; the Śvetāmbara recension used by Tatia (1994) **merges them
into one**. Confirmed by cross-checking three independent sources: wisdomlib's mirror of Vijay K. Jain/SS
(two verses, docs 1084756–57), jainworld's mirror of Jaini/SBJ (two sūtras, 5.2–5.3), and Tatia's own inline
cross-reference apparatus, which glosses his single 5.2 as "(SS 5.2–5.3)."

| aspect | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi) | Śvetāmbara (Tatia, representing the bhāṣya-line numbering) |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| number | Dig. 5.2 + Dig. 5.3 | Śvet. 5.2 |
| text (IAST) | *dravyāṇi* (5.2) / *jīvāśca* (5.3) | *dravyāṇi jīvāśca* (one sūtra, same words, no sandhi change) |
| Devanāgarī | द्रव्याणि ॥५.२॥ / जीवाश्च ॥५.३॥ | — (Tatia's edition not independently checked for Devanāgarī; IAST only, per his romanized text) |
| key gloss / commentary | SS on 5.2: substance = "that which is attained by its own modes (*paryāya*) or which attains modes" — dharma/adharma/ākāśa/pudgala (named in 5.1) are declared *dravya*. SS on 5.3: souls too are *dravya* — stated separately, the commentary says, specifically **"to exclude the earth, etc., accepted as substances in other systems"** (i.e. a polemical sūtra aimed at rival schools that count earth/water/fire/air as separate elemental substances; Jainism folds those under *pudgala*). | Tatia's single sūtra states the same two facts in one breath; his note: "these entities are substances. Souls are also substances. In all there are five substances: the four non-sentient entities... and souls." |
| what it commits you to | Splitting into two sūtras foregrounds the anti-rival-school polemic (5.3) as its own doctrinal unit, worth commentary in its own right. | Merging treats "ajīva-substances are substances" and "souls are substances too" as one continuous thought — the polemical point is folded in rather than headlined. |

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range (MW) | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------------|-----------------|
| dravya | n. nom. pl. *-āṇi* | "a thing, substance, object, matter... an elementary substance (substratum of properties)... one of the categories in Vaiśeṣika" — general Sanskrit already carries a philosophical-substance sense, unlike *dharma* at 5.1 | MW, s.v. *dravya* |
| jīva | m. nom. pl. *-āḥ* (+ *ca* "and/also," sandhi → *-āśca*) | as at 5.1: "any living being... the living or personal soul" | MW, s.v. *jīva* |

No real disambiguation needed here — *dravya* and *jīva* both carry their general Sanskrit senses straight into the sūtra; the only interpretive work is structural (which sūtra-count each recension assigns), not lexical.

**Assembled reading:** The four ajīva substances named in 5.1 are declared to be *dravya* ("substances" in the technical sense the chapter will keep using); souls are then explicitly added to that same category — a move the Sarvārthasiddhi glosses as directed against rival (likely Vaiśeṣika/Nyāya-style) enumerations that would treat earth, water, fire, and air as substances in their own right rather than as configurations of *pudgala*.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- J.L. Jaini/jainworld (Dig. numbering): 5.2 "These (four) are substances (dravyas)." / 5.3 "Souls are the living things and are also substances."
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib (Dig. numbering): 5.2 "These–dharma, adharma, ākāśa and pudgala–are substances (dravya)." / 5.3 "The souls (jīva) also are substances (dravya)."
- Tatia (Śvet. numbering, p. 123): 5.2 "These entities are substances. Souls are also substances." — same content, one sūtra instead of two, matching the merge already established structurally above.

**Modern comparison:** N/A — a bare ontological classification sūtra ("X counts as a substance"); no sourced popular physics-conflation found for it.

## TS Dig. 5.4 / Śvet. 5.3

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical in content; numbering offset by the 5.2–5.3 merge above (Dig.
5.4 = Śvet. 5.3) — not a new divergence, just the running consequence of the earlier fork.

**Text (IAST):** nityāvasthitāny arūpāṇi (ca) || Dig. 5.4 / Śvet. 5.3 ||
**Devanāgarī:** नित्यावस्थितान्यरूपाणि ॥ ५.४ ॥

**Padaccheda:**

| word | form | attested meaning-range (MW) | source |
|------|------|------------------------------|--------|
| nitya | adj. | "innate, native, one's own; continual, perpetual, eternal" | MW, s.v. *nitya* |
| avasthita | past participle (< *ava-sthā*) | "standing firm, fixed, settled, remaining, stationary" | MW, s.v. *avasthita* (compound analysis; base verb *sthā* "to stand") |
| a-rūpa | negative + *rūpa* | *rūpa* general range: "outward appearance, form, shape, figure, colour" — negated: "formless, colourless" | MW, s.v. *rūpa* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Sarvārthasiddhi glosses the three terms as three distinct, additive
claims about *all six* substances at once (dharma, adharma, ākāśa, pudgala, jīva, and — introduced later in the
chapter — kāla): **nitya** = they never lose "common marks, such as existence," i.e. none of the six substance
*kinds* is ever created or destroyed as a kind; **avasthita** = "the fixed number is never violated... these do
not at any time transgress the number six" — a headcount claim, not a claim about any individual thing's
permanence; **arūpa** = lacking colour, and by extension (since Jain epistemology treats colour, taste, smell,
and touch as inseparably co-occurring) lacking taste/smell/touch too — i.e. non-material. This last point is
the pivot for the very next sūtra, which names the one exception.

**Assembled reading:** All six substance-kinds are eternal as kinds, fixed at exactly six in number, and
(with the coming exception of matter) without sensory form.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Jaini/jainworld: "Six substances are eternal, non-destructible, and fixed in number." (folds in the ch. 5
  running total of six, and drops the *arūpa* clause — jainworld's snippet is a paraphrase-length summary here
  more than the other two, likely compressed by the mirror's own editor rather than Jaini's original wording.)
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib: "The substances (dravya) are eternal (nitya), fixed-in-number (avasthita) and
  colourless (arūpī)." — keeps all three clauses distinct, matching the Sanskrit's three-part structure.
- Tatia, p. 123 (Śvet. 5.3, cross-ref. "SS 5.4"): "These substances are eternal, their number is fixed and
  they are devoid of material attributes." — all three clauses present, closest in structure to Vijay K. Jain
  despite being an independent rendering; the jainworld/Jaini snippet's compression is the outlier here, which
  is itself a useful independence check (two sources keep the three-part structure the Sanskrit has; the third
  looks like an abbreviating paraphrase rather than a disagreement).

**Modern comparison:** N/A.

**Sources:** wisdomlib.org doc1084758; jainworld.com chapter-5 page; Tatia (1994) p. 123 (OCR line ~12050); MW
as above.

## TS Dig. 5.5 / Śvet. 5.4

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical; numbering offset carried forward (Dig. 5.5 = Śvet. 5.4).

**Text (IAST):** rūpiṇaḥ pudgalāḥ || Dig. 5.5 / Śvet. 5.4 ||
**Devanāgarī:** रूपिणः पुद्गलाः ॥ ५.५ ॥

**Padaccheda:**

| word | form | attested meaning-range (MW) | source |
|------|------|------------------------------|--------|
| rūpin | adj., nom. pl. *rūpiṇaḥ* (< *rūpa* + *-in* possessive suffix) | "possessing rūpa" — see *rūpa* at 5.4 above: form/shape/colour | MW, s.v. *rūpa* (the *-in* possessive is standard Sanskrit morphology, not a separate MW headword needed) |
| pudgala | m. nom. pl. | as at 5.1: MW already flags the Jain technical sense ("material object including atoms") directly | MW, s.v. *pudgala* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The direct pendant to 5.4's *arūpa* clause — Sarvārthasiddhi: "*rūpa*...
[is] the effect of the aggregation of colour, shape, etc.," inseparably bound up with taste and smell as well
(same inseparability principle used at 5.4). This sūtra names the single exception to the otherwise-universal
formlessness of substances: matter (*pudgala*) alone is *rūpin*.

**Assembled reading:** Of the six substance-kinds, matter alone possesses sensory form (colour, and with it
taste, smell, touch) — the other five are formless, as just stated in 5.4.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Jaini/jainworld: "Matter has taste, smell, colour and touch." (unpacks *rūpin* into its four co-occurring
  qualities directly, rather than keeping "having form" as a single word.)
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib: "Things which have form–rūpī–constitute the matter (pudgala)."
- Tatia, p. 123 (Śvet. 5.4, cross-ref. "SS 5.5"): "The clusters of matter and single atoms, however, have
  material qualities." — his own note then supplies the same four qualities (touch, taste, smell, colour) that
  Jaini's translation states inline, confirming the two are independently arriving at the same four-quality
  unpacking of *rūpin* rather than one copying the other's phrasing choice.

**Modern comparison:** N/A.

**Sources:** wisdomlib.org doc1084759; jainworld.com chapter-5 page; Tatia (1994) p. 123 (OCR line ~12053); MW
as above.

## TS Dig. 5.6 / Śvet. 5.5

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical; numbering offset carried forward (Dig. 5.6 = Śvet. 5.5).

**Text (IAST):** ā ākāśād ekadravyāṇi || Dig. 5.6 / Śvet. 5.5 ||
**Devanāgarī:** आ आकाशादेकद्रव्याणि ॥ ५.६ ॥

**Padaccheda:**

| word | form | attested meaning-range (MW) | source |
|------|------|------------------------------|--------|
| ā | preposition/particle, here "up to, as far as" (governing ablative *ākāśāt*) | standard Sanskrit "up to, until" sense with ablative | MW, s.v. *ā* |
| ākāśāt | ablative sing. of *ākāśa* | as at 5.1: "the ethereal fluid... pervading the universe" | MW, s.v. *ākāśa* |
| eka-dravya | compound, "single/one substance," nom. pl. *-āṇi* agreeing with the implied dharma/adharma/ākāśa | *eka* "one, single, alone"; *dravya* as at 5.2 | MW, s.v. *eka*, *dravya* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The sūtra is elliptical — it names no subject, relying on the reader to
supply "dharma, adharma [up to] ākāśa" from the immediately preceding sūtras (an internal-consistency link
back to 5.1's enumeration order, which lists dharma, adharma, ākāśa, pudgala — this sūtra picks the first
three, stopping "up to ākāśa" and excluding pudgala, which was just marked as the one substance with parts/
plurality via matter-clusters in 5.5). Sarvārthasiddhi: these three are singular *as substances*, even though
dharma/adharma have countlessly many space-points and ākāśa infinitely many (previewing 5.8–5.9 below) — the
oneness claim is about substance-identity, not about internal spatial structure. The commentary explicitly
distinguishes this from souls and matter, "which are genuinely multiple" (many separate souls, many separate
atoms/clusters).

**Assembled reading:** Of the six substance-kinds, the three named in 5.1's opening series up through ākāśa —
dharma, adharma, and ākāśa — are each, individually, a single continuous whole (not a class with many
members), unlike matter (many atoms/clusters) and souls (many individual souls).

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Jaini/jainworld: "Media of motion, rest and space each is a single continuous extension."
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib: "Up to the space (ākāśa), each substance (dravya) is an indivisible whole (i.e.,
  single continuum)." — keeps the elliptical "up to ākāśa" phrasing closest to the Sanskrit's own ellipsis.
- Tatia, p. 124 (Śvet. 5.5, cross-ref. "SS 5.6"): "The first three substances are each a single indivisible
  whole, that is, they are each one substance." — resolves the ellipsis explicitly ("the first three") rather
  than preserving "up to ākāśa" the way Vijay K. Jain does; a genuine translator-choice divergence (resolve the
  ellipsis vs. keep it), not a doctrinal disagreement — all three agree on which three substances are meant.

**Modern comparison:** N/A.

**Sources:** wisdomlib.org doc1084760; jainworld.com chapter-5 page; Tatia (1994) p. 124 (OCR line ~12065); MW
as above.

| niṣ-kriya | negative prefix *niṣ-* + *kriyā* | *kriyā*: "doing, performing... action, undertaking, activity, work" — negated: "without action/activity" | MW, s.v. *kriyā* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Continues the same elliptical subject as 5.6 (dharma, adharma, ākāśa —
"these three," per Sarvārthasiddhi, explicitly cross-referenced as such in the wisdomlib mirror's translation).
The commentary draws a careful distinction that matters for not over-reading this sūtra: lacking *kriyā*
("activity" in the specific sense of "movement from one place to another") does **not** mean lacking all
change — the commentary explicitly affirms these three still undergo origination and destruction *of mode*
("six different steps of infinitesimal changes") through internal causes, while also serving as the
instrumental cause of others' motion/rest/accommodation (dharma enables motion, adharma enables rest, ākāśa
enables accommodation) without themselves relocating — compared, in the commentary, to how "the eyes help in
sight" without the eyes themselves being the seeing.

**Assembled reading:** Dharma, adharma, and ākāśa do not move from place to place (unlike matter and souls),
though each still undergoes its own internal modal change and each still causally enables a corresponding
activity in other things (motion, rest, or spatial accommodation respectively) without itself being that
activity.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Jaini/jainworld: "These three are without activity since spread throughout universe."
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib: "These three [the medium of motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma) and the
  space (ākāśa)] are also without-movement (niṣkriya)."
- Tatia, p. 124 (Śvet. 5.6, cross-ref. "SS 5.7"): "Each of the three substances referred to above is
  motionless." His translator's note flags that "some commentators interpreted 'motionless' as immutability,
  but the SBT [Śvetāmbara Bhāṣya Tradition] rejects this interpretation" — an explicit, sourced instance of the
  commentarial tradition itself warning against over-reading "without activity" as "unchanging," which is
  exactly the caution built into the Disambiguation above.

**Modern comparison:** N/A — though note in-file: this is the kind of sūtra ("space itself doesn't move, but
enables motion") that superficially invites an "absolute space" (Newtonian) comparison. No sourced instance of
that specific comparison being made was found this pass; not asserted without a citation (CLAUDE.md §0/§1).

**Sources:** wisdomlib.org doc1084761; jainworld.com chapter-5 page; Tatia (1994) p. 124 (OCR line ~12081); MW
as above.

## TS Dig. 5.8 / Śvet. 5.7–5.8 — NUMBERING FORK (split)

**Recension status:** DIVERGENT — the mirror image of the 5.2–5.3 fork above. The Digambara recension states
the space-point count for dharma, adharma, **and** each individual soul in **one** sūtra; the Śvetāmbara
recension (Tatia) **splits** this into two sūtras — one for dharma/adharma, one for the soul alone. Confirmed
by wisdomlib (Vijay K. Jain/SS, one verse, doc 1084762, explicitly naming all three in its single Sanskrit
line) against Tatia's own cross-reference apparatus, which glosses his 5.7 as "(SS 5.8 in part)" and his 5.8
as "(SS 5.8 in part)" — i.e. Tatia's edition itself records that it is dividing a single Digambara sūtra.

| Devanāgarī | असंख्येयाः प्रदेशाः धर्माधर्मैकजीवानाम् ॥ ५.८ ॥ | (Tatia romanized only, not independently checked for Devanāgarī) |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both *asaṃkhyeya* and *pradeśa* repeat the pattern already established at

distinction for *asaṃkhyeya*; a bounded spot/region for *pradeśa*), and it is Sarvārthasiddhi specifically that
supplies (a) the graded three-tier technical scale of "innumerable" and (b) the precise technical definition of
one *pradeśa* as exactly the space occupied by one indivisible atom (*paramāṇu*) — a definition that only
becomes fully checkable once 5.11 (*nāṇoḥ*, "no [additional space-point] for the atom") is reached later in the
chapter.

**Assembled reading:** Dharma, adharma, and each individual soul each possess an innumerable (specifically:
middle-tier-innumerable, per the commentary's three-way scale) number of space-points — units of space each
sized to hold exactly one atom. The soul's magnitude matches dharma/adharma's, but unlike them the soul's
space-points can contract or expand to fit a body of varying size, per that soul's karma.

**Published translations (comparison only):**

  of rest, there are an innumerable number of units." / 5.8 "There are innumerable soul units in a soul." —
  the split is purely structural (one predicate, two grammatical subjects moved to two sūtras); no content
  disagreement with the Digambara single-sūtra statement, as the divergence table above already establishes.

**Modern comparison:** N/A. (A "quantized/discrete space" vs. "continuum" framing is imaginable here, but no
sourced popular comparison of Jain *pradeśa*-counting to, e.g., Planck-scale space quantization was found this

## TS Dig. 5.9 / Śvet. 5.9 — numbering RESYNCS here

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical, and — after the 5.2–5.3 merge (Śvetāmbara −1) and the 5.8
split (Śvetāmbara +1) exactly offset each other — **the two numbering tracks land back on the same number**,
confirmed independently by Tatia's own text carrying no "(SS ...)" cross-reference note at 5.9 (the only sūtra
in this stretch where he supplies none — consistent with there being nothing to cross-reference, since the
numbers already agree) and by wisdomlib's doc1084763 independently being labelled 5.9 for the same content.
This is exactly the kind of walk-order bookkeeping §A calls for — recorded here as a checkable fact, not
asserted from convenience.

**Text (IAST):** ākāśasya anantāḥ || 5.9 (both recensions) ||
**Devanāgarī:** आकाशस्यानन्ताः ॥ ५.९ ॥

**Padaccheda:**

| word | form | attested meaning-range (MW) | source |
|------|------|------------------------------|--------|
| ākāśasya | genitive sing. of *ākāśa* | as at 5.1/5.6 | MW, s.v. *ākāśa* |
| ananta | adj., nom. pl. *-āḥ* (agreeing with an implied *pradeśāḥ*, "space-points," carried over from 5.8) | "endless, boundless, eternal, infinite" | MW, s.v. *ananta* |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Straightforward continuation of 5.8's elliptical predicate (*pradeśāḥ*,
"space-points," is understood but not repeated) — the one substance-kind whose space-point count breaks the
"innumerable" pattern just established for dharma/adharma/soul. Sarvārthasiddhi specifies the reason: "space
pervades both the universe (*loka*) and the non-universe (*aloka*); infinite means endless" — i.e. ākāśa's
count is *ananta* rather than merely *asaṃkhyeya* precisely because ākāśa, unique among the six substances,
extends beyond the bounded cosmos (*lokākāśa*) into unbounded *alokākāśa*, so no finite (even if "innumerable")
tally can apply to it. This distinction — bounded-but-huge ("innumerable") vs. genuinely unbounded ("infinite")
— is a graded technical scale, not two words for the same idea, and the chapter is careful to keep them apart.

**Assembled reading:** Space alone (not dharma, adharma, or any soul) has infinitely many space-points, because
space alone extends past the bounded universe into limitless non-universe space.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Jaini/jainworld: "Space points in space are infinite."
- Vijay K. Jain/wisdomlib: "The substance of space (ākāśadravya) has infinite (ananta) space-points
  (pradeśa)."
- Tatia, p. 125 (Śvet. 5.9, no SS cross-reference given — consistent with the numbering resync noted above):
  "There are an infinite number of space units in space." His accompanying note adds the *loka*/*aloka* split
  explicitly: "space is divided into two parts, cosmic and transcosmic... it is only in space taken as a whole,
  both cosmic and transcosmic, that there are infinite space units" — matching Sarvārthasiddhi's reasoning as
  reported via Vijay K. Jain above, independently.

**Modern comparison:** N/A.

**Sources:** wisdomlib.org doc1084763; jainworld.com chapter-5 page; Tatia (1994) p. 125 (OCR lines
~12154–12173); MW as above.
