**Text — Both recensions (IAST):**
> ajīvakāyā dharmādharmākāśapudgalāḥ || 5.1 ||

**Devanāgarī:**

**Disambiguation (from commentary):**

Two words in this sūtra cannot be correctly read off the general lexicon alone — this is the case the
prototype was chosen to test.

1. **Why "kāya"?** *Kāya* ordinarily means a physical body. The *Sarvārthasiddhi* commentary explains the
   extension explicitly: the term is applied to dharma, adharma, ākāśa, and pudgala **by analogy** to a
   physical body, because — like a body — each is a single whole made of a manifoldness of space-points
   (*bahupradeśī*, "having many *pradeśa*s"). The commentary further states this is *why* the fifth ajīva
   substance, **time (kāla)**, is deliberately left out of this sūtra's scope: kāla has no second space-point
   to aggregate, so it cannot take the *-kāya* label. This is a targeted, commentary-driven exclusion, not an
   oversight — Adhyāya 5 addresses kāla separately later in the chapter. (Source: Sarvārthasiddhi, as reported
   in Vijay K. Jain's edition/commentary, wisdomlib.org mirror.)
2. **Why "dharma"/"adharma" here mean "medium of motion/rest," not "duty"/"vice"?** The general MW entries
   for *dharma* and *adharma* carry no trace of this sense — a reader working from the dictionary alone would
   default to the ethical/legal meaning that dominates elsewhere in Sanskrit literature (and elsewhere even
   within Jain ethical vocabulary — see the *unrelated* sense of *dharma* as "the teaching/path," a separate
   node). The narrowing to "principle enabling motion" / "principle enabling rest" is supplied entirely by the
   Jain commentarial-technical tradition and is stated identically across the exegetical summaries consulted:
   "the functions of the media of motion (dharma) and rest (adharma) are to assist (*upagraha*) motion (*gati*)
   and rest respectively" (Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.17 gloss, cited via the same commentarial line as this sūtra —
   internal-consistency check: the technical sense introduced at 5.1 is confirmed, not contradicted, by 5.17
   later in the same chapter). This is a clean case of "lexicon gives the outer bound, commentary decides which
   sense wins" (CLAUDE.md §4, signal 3 vs. signal 2).

**Assembled reading:** The sūtra names, as a set, the ajīva ("non-soul") substances that qualify as *kāya*
("body-like," i.e. extended across many space-points): dharma (the principle enabling motion), adharma (the
principle enabling rest), ākāśa (space), and pudgala (matter). All four share the negative mark of lacking
*jīva*'s defining characteristic (sentience/life), and are grouped here specifically because each — unlike the
still-to-come fifth ajīva substance, time — occupies a plurality of space-points and so merits the "body"
label by the commentary's stated analogy.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- J.L. Jaini (1920, Sacred Books of the Jainas, mirrored verbatim at jainworld.com): "The non-living substances
  (bodies) are the medium of motion, the medium of rest, space and matter."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, via wisdomlib.org): "The non-[soul] substances (bodies) — ajīvakāya — are the medium of
  motion (dharma), the medium of rest (adharma), the space (ākāśa) and the matter (pudgala)."
- Nathmal Tatia (1994, *That Which Is*, p. 123): "**The media of motion and of rest, space and matter are
  extended non-sentient entities.**" His accompanying note glosses this directly: the four are "extended in
  space," each built of *pradeśa* ("units," his term for the smallest indivisible part of the substance) —
  i.e. Tatia unpacks *-kāya* straight into "extended," rather than keeping the literal "(bodies)" gloss Jaini
  and Vijay K. Jain both retain.
- **All three agree** on the core doctrinal content: dharma = medium of motion, adharma = medium of rest,
  ākāśa/pudgala = space/matter, all four grouped as extended non-sentient entities. **Genuine, informative
  divergence** on one point: Jaini and Vijay K. Jain translate *-kāya* literally as "(bodies)" and leave the
  space-point reasoning to a separate commentary note, while Tatia folds the commentarial payload (extension
  across multiple space-points) directly into the running translation as "extended." Both choices are
  defensible — literal-plus-footnote versus interpretive-inline — and the difference is a real translator
  choice, not textual disagreement; it is exactly the kind of divergence that shows these three are
  independent renderings rather than copies of one another.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED — labelled, never feeds disambiguation):** Popular Jain-apologetic literature
sometimes reaches for *dharma*/*adharma* as "medium of motion/rest" and analogizes them to the 19th-century
physics postulate of a luminiferous **ether**, or more loosely to modern "space." At least one such source
explicitly available online (jainworld.jainworld.com, "Jain Metaphysics and Science: A Comparison") states its
own caution on this point: dharma is **not** a medium of motion in the ether sense, dharma/adharma are
non-material (ether, as historically conceived, was a material-like medium), and Einstein's relativity — where
space and time are not absolute and are bound up with matter — is explicitly **not** what Jain cosmology
describes (Jain kāla is treated as discrete time-atoms occupying fixed space-points, not a relativistic
space-time). Recorded here as `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`: real surface-level vocabulary overlap
("medium," "motion," "space" as a substance) invites the comparison, but the physical content does not match —
neither the historical ether concept nor general relativity is what Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.1 is describing. This
box does not feed back into the disambiguation above, which rests entirely on the Sarvārthasiddhi/bhāṣya
tradition.
