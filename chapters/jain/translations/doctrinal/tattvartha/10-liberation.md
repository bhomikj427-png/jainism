# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 10: Liberation (mokṣa)

> **Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī), Adhyāya 10 — omniscience, the definition of liberation,
> the liberated soul's upward flight, and the twelve gateways of investigation. The shortest chapter of the
> treatise and its close.
> **Recensions:** Digambara (SS = Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, sūtras 10.1–10.9) and Śvetāmbara
> (SB = Umāsvāti's *Svopajña-bhāṣya*, SBT = ṭīkā, sūtras 10.1–10.7) — **equal weight, no spine**.
> **Editions used:** Nathmal Tatia (tr.), *That Which Is*, 1994 (archive.org OCR
> `ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`, read locally); Vijay K. Jain (ed./tr.), 2018 (archive.org OCR
> `Tattvartha18CompleteWeb`, read locally, incl. the complete Chapter-10 Devanāgarī+IAST appendix).
> **Status:** drafted (first pass, both recensions). **With this unit, all ten adhyāyas of the Tattvārtha
> Sūtra have a first pass in this reading room.**
> **Walk-order note:** Śvetāmbara sequence, both numbers printed. Table-of-contents choice, not favoritism.

## Chapter shape (read this first)

Seven Śvetāmbara sūtras, nine Digambara — and the difference decomposes with textbook neatness into one of
each fork-type this reading room has catalogued across the other nine chapters: **one merge** (SB 10.2 +
10.3 → SS 10.2), **one split** (SB 10.4 → SS 10.3 + 10.4), and **two single-sūtra block promotions** (SS
10.7, the four similes, and SS 10.8, the dharmāstikāya argument — both carried by the SB as commentary).
Net: 7 − 1 + 1 + 2 = 9. Clean arithmetic, nothing unexplained.

The chapter's *doctrinal* divergence, though, is the deepest in the whole text, and it sits invisibly under
a **shared** mūla: the twelve-gateways sūtra (SB 10.7 / SS 10.9) is word-for-word common ground, but at its
fourth gateway (*liṅga*) the two commentarial traditions state the positions that historically divide the
sects — the Śvetāmbara tradition that gender and dress have **no bearing** on liberation, the Digambara
tradition that the liberated must be **male and nude**. The strīmokṣa (women's-liberation) controversy,
already flagged from the sociological side in the `digambara.md`/`svetambara.md` concept nodes, here gets
its primary-text location: not in any sūtra, but in what the commentaries do with one word of a sūtra both
recensions accept.

## SB 10.1 / SS 10.1 — omniscience

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *mohakṣayāj jñānadarśanāvaraṇāntarāyakṣayāc ca kevalam*

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| *moha-kṣayāt* | abl. sg. | *moha* "loss of consciousness, delusion, infatuation" (MW); *kṣaya* "destruction, diminution, wearing away" — the Jain technical "elimination [of a karma-type]" is fixed by TS 2.1's five-states doctrine, not the lexicon | MW; TS 2.1 (this reading room's Adhyāya 2 unit) |
| *jñāna-darśana-āvaraṇa-antarāya-kṣayāt* | abl. sg., four-member compound | the remaining three **destructive (ghāti) karmas**: knowledge-covering, intuition-covering, obstructive (*antarāya* — MW "intervention, obstacle") — the four-karma set anchored at TS 8.5 and in `karma-prakriti.md` | MW; TS 8 unit |
| *ca* | conjunction | "and [consequently]" — both commentaries read a causal sequence, not a mere list: deluding karma first, the other three then eliminated together | — |
| *kevalam* | neut. nom. | MW *kevala* "alone, only, mere, sole; whole, entire; pure, uncompounded" — the Jain technical sense (omniscience, the fifth jñāna) is a doctrinal narrowing carried by the tradition; both the "sole/isolated" and "complete" branches of the MW range are audible in it | MW; `kevala-jnana.md` |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The sūtra's ablative sequencing is the doctrine: **delusion falls
first, and its fall entails the fall of the rest.** The SS maps this onto the guṇasthāna ladder explicitly
(view-delusion and the tenacious passions eliminated between stages 4 and 7; the climb to stage 12, where
deluding karma is "totally and finally expelled"; then the three remaining destructive karmas eliminated
"all at once" — within an intra-hour, per the SB's path-summary). The SB (at its 10.2) hands the omniscient
one his titles: "the perfectly pure, the enlightened one, the all-knowing, the all-intuiting, the
victorious, the absolutely alone."

**Assembled reading:** From the elimination of delusion, and [thereupon] from the elimination of the
knowledge-covering, intuition-covering and obstructive [karmas], omniscience (*kevala*) arises.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Omniscience arises when deluding karma is eliminated and, as a result, knowledge-covering, intuition-covering and obstructive karma are eliminated."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): omniscience (kevalajñāna) on the destruction of delusion (moha) and of knowledge- and perception-covering and obstructive karmas.

## SB 10.2 + 10.3 / SS 10.2 — the definition of liberation (merge fork + wording variant)

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — two Śvetāmbara sūtras merge into one Digambara sūtra, with a real
lexical variant in the definition itself.**

| aspect | Śvetāmbara | Digambara (Sarvārthasiddhi) |
|--------|------------|------------------------------|
| number | 10.2 *bandhahetvabhāva-nirjarābhyām* + 10.3 *kṛtsnakarma-**kṣayo** mokṣaḥ* | 10.2 *bandhahetvabhāva-nirjarābhyāṁ kṛtsnakarma-**vipramokṣo** mokṣaḥ* |
| the definition-word | *kṣaya* — "destruction/elimination" of all karma | *vipramokṣa* — "release/letting-go" of all karma (MW *vi-pra-√muc*: "to free oneself from, release") |
| structure | mechanism sūtra + definition sūtra | one sentence: mechanism and definition fused |

The doctrine is identical — **no new bondage** (its five causes of 8.1 are gone: four eliminated outright,
and the fifth, *yoga*/activity, now yields only "instantaneous" bondage that ceases the next moment) **plus
the wearing-off** (*nirjarā*) of what remains, first the four destructive karmas, then, at the appointed
end of the lifespan-karma, the four non-destructive ones (sensation, lifespan, body, status). But the two
recensions reach for different words at the crux: the Śvetāmbara text defines mokṣa as karma's
*destruction*, the Digambara as the soul's *release from* karma — the same event named from the karma's
side and from the soul's side. Tatia's SS-sourced note on the instantaneous bondage of the omniscient
preserves a striking Dhavalā quotation-chain: it is "neither bound nor unbound… neither expelled nor not
expelled. It is ineffable."

**Assembled reading:** Through the absence of the causes of bondage and through wearing-off, the
elimination of (Śvet.) / release from (Dig.) all karma whatsoever — that is liberation.

## SB 10.4 / SS 10.3–10.4 — what survives liberation (split fork)

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — one Śvetāmbara sūtra splits into two Digambara sūtras, with a small
wording variant** (Śvet. *aupaśamikādi-bhavyatva-**abhāvāc** ca…* — "and from the cessation of…"; Dig.
10.3 *aupaśamikādi-bhavyatvānāṁ ca* + 10.4 *anyatra kevalasamyaktva-jñāna-darśana-siddhatvebhyaḥ*).

**Assembled reading:** Liberation also involves the cessation of the [five] states (the *bhāvas* of TS
2.1–2.7: subsidential, etc.) **and** of the capacity-for-liberation itself (*bhavyatva*) — **except**
perfect enlightened world-view, perfect knowledge, perfect intuition, and the state of being liberated
(*siddhatva*), which never cease.

The exception-list is the text's positive description of the siddha: what liberation *keeps*. Two
commentarial notes deserve record: (1) *bhavyatva* — the worthiness-for-liberation that distinguished
liberatable souls all along (2.7) — is itself intrinsic yet **ends** at liberation, having discharged its
office: capacity lapses when actuality arrives. (2) The SS adds that infinite energy and bliss persist "as
inseparable companions of perfect knowledge and intuition," and separately disposes of an elegant
objection: if the soul sizes itself to its body (TS 5.16), should a disembodied soul not expand to fill
the cosmos? No — expansion and contraction were **body-karma effects**, and there is no karma left to
drive them.

## SB 10.5–10.6 / SS 10.5–10.8 — the upward flight (shared core + two Digambara promotions)

**Recension status:** 10.5 and the causes-sūtra shared (Śvet. 10.6 ends with an extra word, *tadgatiḥ*,
absent from SS 10.6); **then two sūtras that exist only in the Digambara mūla** — the similes (SS 10.7)
and the boundary argument (SS 10.8) — both present in the SB as commentary: the reading room's smallest
block promotions, same type as Adhyāya 7's bhāvanā block and Adhyāya 3's geography block, and again in the
Digambara direction.

- **SB 10.5 / SS 10.5** (*tadanantaram ūrdhvaṁ gacchaty ā lokāntāt*): "immediately thereupon [the soul]
  goes upward to the border of the cosmos." Three events in **one time-unit**, simultaneous as cause,
  process and fulfilment: separation from the body, upward motion, arrival. Tatia's note preserves the
  scholastic refinement that the soul passes "without touching the intermediate space units" — *touchless
  moving*.
- **SB 10.6 / SS 10.6**: the four causes of the flight — antecedent impetus (*pūrvaprayoga*), freedom from
  clinging karmic particles (*asaṅgatva*), severance of bondage (*bandhaccheda*), and the soul's **innate
  upward-tending mode** (*tathāgatipariṇāma* — cf. TS 2.26–29's transit-geometry: the liberated soul's is
  the zero-turn straight ascent).
- **SS 10.7 (Dig. mūla; SB commentary)**: the four similes, one per cause — **the potter's wheel** that
  keeps spinning after the rod is withdrawn; **the gourd** coated in earth that sinks, then bobs up as the
  coats dissolve; **the castor seed** flung upward as the pod splits; **the flame** that rises because
  rising is what flame does. The gourd is the whole Jain karma-ontology in one image: bondage is a
  *coating*, literal added mass, and liberation is buoyancy restored.
- **SS 10.8 (Dig. mūla; SB commentary)** (*dharmāstikāyābhāvāt*): why the soul stops at the border and
  does not exit the cosmos — **beyond it there is no medium of motion** (dharmāstikāya, TS 5.17). The
  system's most distinctive metaphysical posit (see `dharma-dravya.md`) is deployed at its most dramatic:
  the same substance that makes motion possible anywhere makes liberation's terminus definite. The siddha
  rests at the summit forever not because anything holds it but because *going further is not a possible
  event*.

## SB 10.7 / SS 10.9 — the twelve gateways, and the divergence hidden inside one of them

**Recension status:** mūla shared (minor internal compound variants); **the commentaries diverge
doctrinally at gateways 4, 5 and 9 — including the sect-defining strīmokṣa split.**

**Text (IAST, Śvet.):** *kṣetra-kāla-gati-liṅga-tīrtha-cāritra-pratyekabuddhabodhita-jñānā-'vagāhanā-'ntara-
saṁkhyā-'lpabahutvataḥ sādhyāḥ*

**Assembled reading:** The liberated are to be investigated through twelve gateways: place, time, realm of
birth, gender/dress (*liṅga*), ford (*tīrtha*), conduct, self-enlightened vs instructed, knowledge, height
(*avagāhanā*), interval, number, and the relative numerical strength across the preceding eleven.

Survey of the commentarial content, with the divergences loud:

- **Place/time/realm**: liberation occurs at the cosmos-summit; the liberating life must be in one of the
  fifteen karmabhūmis (TS 3.16/SS 3.37) and, in cyclic lands, in specific aeons — with the humane exception
  both traditions preserve for the **abducted** (carried out of a karmabhūmi mid-path, they can be
  liberated anywhere, in any aeon). The instant of liberation happens "where there is no time," beyond the
  luminous gods' clocks (TS 4.15).
- **Gateway 4, liṅga — THE divergence.**

  | | Śvetāmbara (SB/SBT) | Digambara (SS) |
  |---|---|---|
  | reading of *liṅga* | gender **and dress** — and *neither has any bearing*: "female, male or hermaphroditic" souls are liberated; "even a householder wearing their daily garb can be liberated" | freedom from sexual disposition required — **and the liberated must also be physically male and nude** |
  | what it commits you to | women attain mokṣa; monastic nudity not a precondition | no female liberation in that body; the sky-clad ascetic form is soteriologically necessary |

  This is the textual heart of the historical strīmokṣa controversy (see the `digambara.md`,
  `svetambara.md` and `aparigraha.md` nodes and teaching-layer Ch. 10): the two positions sit as
  commentary on the **same word of the same sūtra** — a sharper case than any mūla fork in this reading
  room of how recensional identity lives in the commentary layer.
- **Gateway 5, tīrtha**: liberation under a ford-founder's dispensation or outside it; the SB/SBT extends
  all its categories "also to women" (the same divergence propagating); the gateway also houses the
  tīrthaṅkara / non-tīrthaṅkara distinction (`tirthankara.md`).
- **Gateway 9, height**: a bare-number divergence — SB: maximum 500 bows, minimum just under seven cubits;
  SS: maximum **525** bows, minimum about **two cubits**. (Liberated souls retain two-thirds of their final
  height — the siddha has a *size*, fixed forever, an arresting corollary both traditions accept.)
- **Gateways 10–11**: liberation can be continuous for at most eight time-units; at most **108** souls are
  liberated in one time-unit; intermissions can stretch to six months. The apparatus of relative
  numerical strength (gateway 12) then ranks every preceding gateway's classes — with Tatia's honest
  footnote that "many more" throughout translates the technical *saṁkhyeya-guṇa*, "numerably more."

**The SB's closing gifts** (commentary on this final sūtra): the **siddha-śilā** — the liberated souls'
abode as a wheel of white land 45 × 10⁵ yojanas across, eight yojanas thick at the centre, thinning to
less "than the thinnest wing of a butterfly," like a white parasol unfolded downward; the triple negative
definition of siddha-bliss (**unlimited** unlike sense-pleasure, **unconditioned** unlike relief from
pain, **uncaused** unlike the fruit of good karma); a recapitulation of the entire treatise as one
soul's itinerary — from the first opening of the enlightened world-view (1.1–1.3) through the vow- and
austerity-machinery (chapters 6–9) to the elimination cascade of this chapter — with a 36-item catalogue
of the supernatural powers the advanced ascetic acquires *and ignores* ("the ascetic has no interest in
any of them"); and, in the VKJ edition, the traditional benediction that closes the whole text: reading
the ten-chapter Tattvārthasūtra with understanding "earns the fruit of a day's fasting."

sūtra at all, but in commentary on a shared one.
