# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 9 (saṃvara / nirjarā): Stoppage and Shedding of Karma

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra, Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī. **Recensions used:** Digambara (Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, cited as "SS" in Tatia's translator apparatus) and Śvetāmbara (Umāsvāti's own *Svopajña-bhāṣya*, cited as "SB," with Siddhasenagaṇi's *ṭīkā*, cited as "SBT"). **Editions:** Nathmal Tatia (tr.), *That Which Is: Tattvārthasūtra* (HarperCollins/Sacred Literature Trust, 1994) — primary text for this unit, downloaded directly from archive.org (`ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`, OCR `_djvu.txt`, re-located this session via the `/metadata/` JSON endpoint, same technique as prior units); Vijay K. Jain (tr.), *Tattvartha Sutra* (Sarvārthasiddhi-based, 2018), downloaded from archive.org (`Tattvartha18CompleteWeb`) as the second, independent, Digambara-sourced translation. **Status:** drafted — first pass, both recensions.

**Scope note, matching the depth pattern of prior adhyāyas:** sūtras 9.1–9.7 (the definition of *saṃvara*, its seven causes, and the opening of the twelve reflections) are covered at full padaccheda depth. Sūtras 9.8–9.26 (the twenty-two hardships, the five stages of conduct, and the external/internal austerities through the nine penances) are covered as a content survey — these are long enumerative lists where Tatia's own apparatus reports *no* recension divergence, so the payoff of full transcription is low. Sūtras 9.27–9.49 (the *dhyāna*, meditation, section) return to fuller treatment, because this is where the chapter's genuine recension forks — merges, splits, a content-order swap, and two outright unique-to-one-recension sūtras — are concentrated, and they are exactly the kind of finding this reading-room exists to surface rather than average away.

## Dig. 9.1 / Śvet. 9.1 — Stoppage defined

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical for this sūtra (no SS-annotation in Tatia's apparatus; content and structure match Vijay K. Jain's independent translation).

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *āsravanirodhaḥ saṃvaraḥ*

**Assembled reading:** Stopping the inflow of karma (*āsrava*, Ch. 6) is *saṃvara* (stoppage/inhibition). Both editions independently divide this into two aspects using different English vocabulary for the same underlying distinction — Tatia: "psychic" and "physical" inhibition; Vijay K. Jain (rendering the Sarvārthasiddhi commentary): *bhāva-saṃvara* (psychic-stoppage, the cessation of the transmigration-driving mental activities) and *dravya-saṃvara* (material-stoppage, the resulting interruption of actual karmic-matter intake). The wording differs enough between the two translations to confirm they are independently derived, not one copying the other, while agreeing on the substance: mental disengagement is the cause, cessation of matter-influx is the effect.

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both the SS (via Vijay K. Jain) and the SBT (via Tatia) agree that stoppage operates across the fourteen *guṇasthānas* (spiritual stages, TS 9.1's own commentary cross-references sūtra 1.8/1.3's stage-list) — progress in inhibition tracks progress up the stages, complete stoppage being possible only immediately before liberation.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Stopping the inflow of karma is inhibition."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, rendering SS): "The obstruction (*nirodha*) of influx (*āsrava*) is stoppage (*saṃvara*)."

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A — this is internal karma-mechanics, not a physics-adjacent claim.

## Dig. 9.2 / Śvet. 9.2 — The seven causes of stoppage

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (no SS-annotation).

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *sa gupti-samiti-dharmānupreksā-parīṣahajaya-cāritraiḥ*

**Assembled reading:** That stoppage [comes about] through: *gupti* (guarding/self-control of body-speech-mind), *samiti* (careful movement), *dharma* (the ten moral virtues), *anuprekṣā* (the twelve reflections), *parīṣaha-jaya* (conquering hardships), and *cāritra* (conduct/self-restraint) — six named causes, though Tatia's commentary notes "seven principal inhibitors" are actually delivered across this sūtra and the next (9.3 adds austerity/*tapas* as an eighth cause that does double duty, also producing *nirjarā*).

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**
| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|-----------------|
| gupti | fem. nom. pl. (instr. compound) | "guarding, protection, restraint" — narrowed by commentary to the threefold body/speech/mind control | Tatia's translator apparatus (commentary-supplied technical sense) |
| samiti | fem. | "coming together" (MW general sense) — narrowed to "careful/circumspect movement" in ascetic praxis | Tatia's translator apparatus |
| anupreksā | fem. | "reflection, contemplation" | Tatia's translator apparatus |
| parīṣaha-jaya | masc. compound | "conquest (*jaya*) of hardships/afflictions (*parīṣaha*)" | Tatia's translator apparatus |
| cāritra | neut. | "conduct" — already a full concept node in this corpus (Ch. 08) | corpus-internal (`caritra.md`) |

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), p. 219.

## Dig. 9.3 / Śvet. 9.3 — Austerity does double duty

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *tapasā nirjarā ca*

**Assembled reading:** "Austerities wear off karma as well as inhibiting it" (Tatia) — *tapas* is named twice over: once among the seven causes of *saṃvara* (9.2, as "austerity"), and again here as the specific mechanism that produces *nirjarā* (the shedding of karma already bound, the chapter's second named subject). Tatia's commentary records the SS's rationale for the sequencing of 9.2: the causes of inhibition are listed specifically to *exclude* practices inspired by attachment/hatred/delusion (pilgrimage, ritual ablution, deluded ordination, worship of deities for personal ends) that Jain doctrine holds actually *attract* rather than inhibit karma — a polemical, boundary-drawing function embedded directly in the sūtra sequence, not merely descriptive enumeration.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), p. 219.

## Dig. 9.4 / Śvet. 9.4 — Guarding (*gupti*)

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (no SS-annotation).

**Text (IAST):** *samyag yoga-nigraho guptiḥ*

**Assembled reading:** "Guarding is enlightened control of the threefold activities of body, speech and mind." Tatia's commentary distinguishes the three sub-guardings in detail: bodily guarding controls voluntary movement (walking, sitting, handling articles) to avoid harming life; speech guarding is exercised especially when seeking alms or discussing illness — Tatia notes the SBT-specific practice of "covering the mouth while seeking food and drink from householders... to avoid injuring small lifeforms," a concrete ascetic-discipline detail attributed specifically to the Śvetāmbara *ṭīkā*; mental guarding is refraining from sinful intention, culminating (at its purest) in avoiding *all* worldly thought, wholesome or unwholesome.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), pp. 219–220.

## Dig. 9.5 / Śvet. 9.5 — Careful movement (*samiti*)

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text (IAST):** *īryā-bhāṣaiṣaṇā-dāna-nikṣepotsargāḥ samitayaḥ*

**Assembled reading:** The five *samitis*: walking carefully (*īryā*), speaking carefully (*bhāṣā*), seeking alms carefully (*eṣaṇā*), handling objects of daily use carefully (*ādāna-nikṣepa*), and disposing of excreta correctly (*utsarga*) — a footnote in Tatia records that the small broom Jain ascetics use to sweep aside insects before sitting/walking is the concrete instrument associated with the first and fourth of these.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), p. 220.

## Dig. 9.6 / Śvet. 9.6 — The ten moral virtues (*daśa-dharma*)

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (no SS-annotation on the sūtra itself, though individual items carry SB/SS commentarial variants noted below).

**Text (IAST):** *uttama-kṣamā-mārdavā-rjava-śauca-satya-saṃyama-tapas-tyāgā-kiñcanya-brahmacaryāṇi dharmaḥ*

**Assembled reading — the ten virtues, each glossed from Tatia's commentary:**
1. **Forgiveness** (*kṣamā*) — controlling anger; SB's distinctive elaboration gives a graduated series of reasons to forgive an aggressor (he did not act physically / did not kill / did not kill successfully / did not distract you from the religious path), always tracing the ultimate cause back to one's own past karma.
2. **Humility** (*mārdava*) — subduing pride in race, family, wealth, intellect, or learning; SB itemizes eight varieties of pride by object (paternal/maternal superiority, beauty, fortune, intellect, scriptural learning, prosperity, power).
3. **Straightforwardness** (*ārjava*) — sincere, honest intention; SB adds "avoiding controversy."
4. **Purity/freedom from greed** (*śauca*) — SB lists the mind-polluting passions greed produces: anger, pride, deceit, violence, falsehood.
5. **Truthfulness** (*satya*) — refraining from harsh speech, back-biting, garrulity.
6. **Self-restraint** (*saṃyama*) — abstaining from injuring any life-form, subtle or gross.
7. **Austerity** (*tapas*) — mortification for the soul's regeneration; cross-referenced forward to its own six-plus-six subtype taxonomy at 9.19–9.20.
8. **Renunciation** (*tyāga*) — abandonment of possessive attitudes; SS itemizes external objects (broom, pots) *permitted* in its ascetic discipline — a footnote flags a genuine SB/SS material-culture difference: "pots and cloth are not mentioned in the SS as the orders of this tradition do not allow these items," a concrete doctrinal trace of the historical Digambara/Śvetāmbara split over ascetic nudity and possession, surfacing here rather than in the more obviously sectarian chapters.
9. **Detachment** (*ākiñcanya*) — relinquishing even the sense of "mineness," a refinement of renunciation.
10. **Continence** (*brahmacarya*) — residing with a teacher; SB distinguishes five kinds of teacher by function (initiator, nature-instructor, junior/senior scripture-instructor, expert in the scripture's inner meaning).

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Item 8's footnote is the sharpest finding in this sūtra — a live doctrinal fingerprint of the historical Digambara (no-possessions, unclothed) vs. Śvetāmbara (permitted robes and begging-bowl) split, embedded in a commentarial aside rather than argued explicitly, exactly the kind of detail this reading-room's method is built to surface rather than let pass as a footnote curiosity.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), pp. 220–222.

## Dig. 9.7 / Śvet. 9.7 — The twelve reflections (*dvādaśa-anuprekṣā*), opening

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text (IAST):** *anityā-śaraṇa-saṃsārai-katvā-nyatvā-śucitvā-śrava-saṃvara-nirjarā-loka-bodhidurlabha-dharmasvākhyātatvā-nucintanam anuprekṣāḥ*

**Assembled reading:** Twelve topics of reflection: impermanence, helplessness, the cycle of birth-and-death, solitariness, otherness of the body [from the self], impurity of the body, inflow of karma, inhibiting karma, wearing off karma, the nature of the cosmos, the rarity of enlightenment, and the lucid exposition of the doctrine. Tatia's commentary on the twelfth notes its function is specifically to build confidence and doctrinal certainty in the aspirant, naming the five great vows (Ch. 08, *mahāvratas*) and the seven categories of truth (Ch. 01, *tattva*) as "the constituents of the path of liberation" that reflection twelve is meant to secure assent to. **Not transcribed at full depth**: reflections 1–11's individual elaborations (content-survey scope trade-off, consistent with prior adhyāyas' handling of long enumerations).

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), pp. 222–223.

## Dig./Śvet. 9.8–9.26 — Content survey (hardships, conduct-stages, austerities, penances)

No recension divergence is reported by Tatia's apparatus anywhere in this stretch — both editions carry the same 19-sūtra run in lockstep, 1:1 numbered.

- **9.8–9.9**: The twenty-two *parīṣaha* (hardships) are introduced as preventing deviation from the path and wearing off bound karma; the full list (hunger, thirst, cold, heat, insect-bites, nudity, ennui, [the presence of] women, travel, uncomfortable seat/posture, sleeping-place, reproach, injury, seeking alms, lack of gain, illness, thorny-grass contact, dirt, [excess] honour, [lack of] learning, lack of intelligence, loss of faith) is given in one compound sūtra. TS 9.10–9.17 (not transcribed here) go on to cross-tabulate exactly which hardships apply at which spiritual stage and to which class of destructive karma — the corpus's `dhyana-jain.md` and `gunasthana.md` nodes already carry adjacent material.
- **9.18**: The five stages of ascetic conduct — initiation, ordination, purification-through-service, self-restraint-with-subtle-flickering-greed (10th *guṇasthāna*), and perfect conduct (11th+).
- **9.19–9.20**: Austerity (*tapas*, the seventh cause) splits into six **external** forms (fasting, semi-fasting, restricted food-variety/begging-manner, giving up stimulating food, solitary dwelling, bodily mortification) and six **internal** forms (penance, reverence, service, scriptural study, renunciation, meditation) — Tatia's commentary distinguishes an austerity from a mere hardship (9.9) on the ground that austerity is *deliberately undertaken* by the soul to purge passion, while a hardship simply *occurs*.
- **9.21–9.26**: The internal austerities are broken out numerically (nine penances, four reverences, ten services, five kinds of scriptural study, two kinds of renunciation) and the nine penances are individually named (confession, repentance, combined confession-repentance, inspection of received articles, abandoning unfit articles, austerity [as its own sub-penance], lowering of seniority, segregation, reordination). Meditation, the sixth internal austerity, is explicitly deferred: "meditation... will be considered in 9.27" (Tatia's own forward cross-reference) — the transition into the section below.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), pp. 223–232 (content-survey range).

## Dig./Śvet. 9.27–9.49 — Meditation (*dhyāna*): the chapter's recension-fork cluster

This is where the two recensions genuinely diverge — not merely in numbering but, twice, in what content each one assigns its own sūtra number to at all. The table below walks the full concordance; **bold** rows mark a fork.

| Dig. (SS) | Śvet. (SB, Tatia's own numbering) | content | fork type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9.27 | **9.27 + 9.28** | concentration of thought on a single object, lasting an intra-hour | **split** — one Dig. sūtra becomes two Śvet. sūtras |
| 9.28 | 9.29 | four kinds of meditation named: mournful (*ārta*), wrathful (*raudra*), analytic (*dharmya*), white (*śukla*) | — |
| 9.29 | 9.30 | the last two (analytic, white) alone lead toward liberation | — |
| 9.30 | 9.31 | dwelling on contact with disagreeable objects [mournful meditation, type 1] | — |
| **9.32** | **9.32** | dwelling on ridding oneself of *unpleasant feelings* | **content-order swap** — see note below |
| **9.31** | **9.33** | dwelling on *recovering contact* with an agreeable object | **content-order swap** |
| 9.33 | 9.34 | intense anxiety over unfulfilled desires for future lives [mournful meditation, type 4] | — |
| 9.34 | 9.35 | who is capable of mournful meditation (lower spiritual stages) | — |
| 9.35 | 9.36 | dwelling on violence, falsehood, theft etc. [wrathful meditation] | — |
| — | **9.37 (not in SS)** | dwelling on investigating the essence of scriptural commandments, using logic and philosophical standpoints | **Śvet.-only addition** |
| — | **9.38 (not in SS)** | analytic meditation *also* occurs at the 11th/12th spiritual stages (suppressed/eliminated passions) | **Śvet.-only addition** |
| 9.37 | **9.39 + 9.40** | first two varieties of white meditation possible at the 11th/12th stages; qualification: conversant with the early (now-lost) scriptures | **split** — mirrors the 9.27/28 pattern |
| 9.38 | 9.41 | last two varieties of white meditation belong to the omniscient (13th/14th stages) | — |
| 9.39 | 9.42 | the four varieties of white meditation named: multiple contemplation, unitary contemplation, subtle infallible physical activity, irreversible stillness | — |
| 9.40 | 9.43 | the four varieties correlated with three/any-one/only-bodily/no activity respectively | — |
| 9.41 | 9.44 | first two varieties: one substance as object, accompanied by movement | — |
| **9.42** | **(absorbed, no separate Śvet. number)** | the second variety is devoid of movement | **Dig.-only sūtra** — the mirror-image case: SB's own text includes this content but folds it into the surrounding commentary/root-text rather than granting it an independent sūtra number |
| 9.43 | 9.45 | contemplation = pondering the scriptures | — |
| 9.44 | 9.46 | movement = transit between object/word/mental-verbal-physical activity | — |
| 9.45 | 9.47 | karmic-particle suppression/elimination increases innumerably across ten named spiritual stages | — |
| 9.46 | 9.48 | five classes of unbound ascetics (husk / tainted / deficient-in-mores / unbound / successful) | — |
| 9.47 | 9.49 | the five classes examined by eight criteria (self-restraint, learning, vow-violation, etc.) | — |

**Chapter-end fork census.** Five distinct events, not one blurred pattern:
1. **Two splits** (Dig. 9.27→Śvet. 9.27–28; Dig. 9.37→Śvet. 9.39–40) — a single Digambara sūtra rendered as two in the Śvetāmbara recension, the same split-pattern documented repeatedly in earlier adhyāyas of this reading room.
2. **One content-order swap** (Dig. 9.31/9.32 present the *same two topics* — recovering contact with an agreeable object, and ridding oneself of unpleasant feelings — but in the *opposite sequence* to Śvet. 9.32/9.33). This is a structurally distinct kind of fork from a merge or split: the sūtra *count* is unaffected, but the *order* of exposition differs between recensions — not previously seen in this reading room's prior adhyāyas (5, 6, 8), which recorded only merges, splits, and content-variants, never a pure reordering.
3. **Two Śvetāmbara-only sūtras** (9.37, 9.38 — investigating scriptural essence via logic; analytic meditation extending to the 11th/12th stages) with no Digambara counterpart at all, per Tatia's own explicit "(not in SS)" / "(not SS)" annotations.
4. **One Digambara-only sūtra** (9.42 — the second white-meditation variety being "devoid of movement"), the mirror-image case: content present in Tatia's own translation and attributed to the SS, but absorbed into the Śvetāmbara text without an independent sūtra number of its own.
5. **Net count**: Digambara ends at 9.47; Śvetāmbara ends at 9.49 — a +2 Śvetāmbara-over-Digambara offset by the chapter's end, arithmetically consistent with the two splits (+2) and the two Śvetāmbara-only additions (+2) roughly offsetting against the one Digambara-only addition (which Śvetāmbara absorbed rather than separately numbering, so it does not subtract from the Śvetāmbara total) — recorded as *consistent*, unlike Adhyāya 6's and 2's previously-flagged unresolved end-of-chapter arithmetic gaps.

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The two Śvetāmbara-only sūtras (9.37, 9.38) are genuine doctrinal additions, not paraphrase — 9.37 introduces a specific analytical procedure (deriving scriptural meaning via logic and the seven *naya* philosophical standpoints, cross-referenced by Tatia to TS 1.34–1.35, already covered in this reading room's own Adhyāya 1 unit) that the Digambara Sarvārthasiddhi simply does not carry as separate textual material at this point.
