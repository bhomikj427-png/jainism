# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 7: The Vows (vrata)

> **Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī), Adhyāya 7 — the five vows, their supporting practices,
> the definitions of the five sins, householder vs ascetic observance, the seven supplementary vows,
> sallekhanā, the transgression catalogues, and charity.
> **Recensions:** Digambara (SS = Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*, sūtras 7.1–7.39) and Śvetāmbara
> (SB = Umāsvāti's *Svopajña-bhāṣya*, SBT = ṭīkā thereon, sūtras 7.1–7.34) — **equal weight, no spine**.
> **Editions used:** Nathmal Tatia (tr.), *That Which Is: Tattvārthasūtra*, 1994 (Śvetāmbara-based, with an
> explicit SS-divergence apparatus; archive.org OCR `ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`, downloaded
> and read locally); Vijay K. Jain (ed./tr.), *Ācārya Umāsvāmī's Tattvārthasūtra with Explanation in English
> from Ācārya Pūjyapāda's Sarvārthasiddhi*, 2018 (Digambara-based; archive.org OCR `Tattvartha18CompleteWeb`,
> downloaded and read locally, including its full Devanāgarī+IAST sūtra appendix for Chapter 7).
> **Status:** drafted (first pass, both recensions).
> **Walk-order note:** entries follow the Śvetāmbara sequence with both numbers printed on every entry
> (`SB 7.x / SS 7.y`); the Digambara-only block SS 7.4–7.8 is walked at its own position. This ordering is a
> table-of-contents choice, not favoritism.

---

## Chapter shape and THE fork (read this first)

This chapter has the **cleanest and largest single numbering fork in the reading room so far**: one five-sūtra
Digambara-only block, and nothing else. **SS 7.4–7.8 — the five sūtras listing the five supporting practices
(bhāvanā) for each great vow — do not exist as sūtras in the Śvetāmbara text.** Tatia's apparatus states it
directly: "Sutras 7.4–7.8 are not included in the SB version of the sutras but they do appear in the
commentary for 7.3 with slight variations." The same content sits at **commentary level** in the Śvetāmbara
tradition and at **mūla level** in the Digambara tradition — and, as the side-by-side below shows, it is *not
the same content* item-for-item.

Net arithmetic: from SB 7.4 onward the offset is a constant **+5** (SB 7.4 = SS 7.9 … SB 7.34 = SS 7.39), and
the chapter-end counts (Śvet. 34, Dig. 39) are exactly explained — no residual gap, unlike the still-open
arithmetic items in Adhyāyas 2 and 6. This is also a **new fork type** for the reading room's census: not a
merge, not a split, not a content-variant, not a reorder, but a **block promotion** — material one recension
carries as bhāṣya, the other carries as scripture. (The nearest prior relative is Adhyāya 9's Digambara-only
9.42, which Tatia folds into prose — but that was one sūtra; this is five, and the content itself diverges.)

## SB 7.1 / SS 7.1

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (mūla). SS raises a distinctive commentarial problem — see below.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *hiṃsā'nṛtasteyābrahmaparigrahebhyo viratir vratam*
(SS as printed in Vijay K. Jain's appendix: *himsā anṛtasteyābrahmaparigrahebhyo viratirvratam*; Tatia's
romanization is identical in substance.)

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range** (each row cited to a published lexicon/word-index):

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| *hiṃsā* | fem. noun, first member of the ablative compound | "injury, harm (to life or property), hurt, mischief, wrong" — MW itself already classifies it three ways: mental (malice), verbal (abusive language), personal (acts of violence) | MW, via wisdomlib.org |
| *anṛta* | neut. noun (a + ṛta, "un-truth") | "falsehood"; the negation of *ṛta* (cosmic/moral order — see the `rta.md` concept node for that word's own Vedic career) | MW (an-ṛta s.v.) |
| *steya* | neut. noun | "theft, robbery, larceny; anything stolen" | MW |
| *abrahma* | neut. noun (a + brahman/brahmacarya) | "unchastity" — the negation of *brahmacarya* in its "continence" sense | MW (via brahmacarya entry); cf. `brahmacarya.md` |
| *parigraha* | masc. noun | very wide MW range: "seizing, grasping, taking, accepting, possession, property; marrying; dependents/retinue; worldly possessions…" — the Jain technical narrowing (attachment itself, internal/external) is supplied by commentary, not the lexicon | MW, via wisdomlib.org |
| *virati* | fem. noun, with ablative "from…" | "cessation, desisting, abstinence" — same gloss row already established in the Adhyāya 8 unit (8.1's *a-virati* is this word negated) | MW |
| *vratam* | neut. noun, nom. sg. (predicate) | "will, command, law, ordinance, rule; obedience; sphere of action, conduct, manner; **a religious vow or practice, any pious observance**; any vow or firm purpose, resolve" | MW, via wisdomlib.org |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both commentaries take *vrata* in MW's "self-imposed observance"
sense and sharpen it to a **deliberately declared** undertaking: SS — "a deliberately declared and
self-imposed observance… a declaration in form of 'this must be done' or 'this must not be done'" (Vijay
K. Jain tr.); Tatia's SB-based rendering — "a self-imposed obligation as to what one ought to do, and not
do," practised "in thought, word and deed." Both commentaries make the *ablative* construction do the work:
the vow is not the five sins' opposite virtues as free-standing positives but *abstinence-from* the five.
Both also give the same fence simile: non-violence is the field/corn, the other four vows the protecting
fence — *ahiṃsā* is the principal vow and the others exist to protect it.

**A commentarial problem the SS raises and resolves (recorded because it shows the system self-testing):**
vows are listed under *saṃvara*'s causes (9.2's self-control and regulation; self-restraint among the ten
virtues of 9.6) — yet this chapter's own frame (and SB commentary at 7.1) makes vow-observance a cause of
**beneficial karmic inflow** (see 6.3). How can one practice both *stop* karma and *generate* it? SS: each
vow has a **detached and an attached aspect** — practised with detachment it inhibits inflow; practised
with worldly attachment (e.g. non-violence as compassionate engagement) it generates beneficial karma.
Tatia's translator note adds the radical consequence: even acts of non-violence performed *with attachment*
obstruct liberation. VKJ's SS wording resolves the same tension as dissociation (*nivṛtti*) vs indulgence
(*pravṛtti*) aspects of the same observance. (This is the same puṇya-is-still-bondage logic the `punya.md`
node and the Adhyāya 6/8 units document.)

**Assembled reading:** Abstinence (*virati*) from violence, falsehood, stealing, unchastity, and
possessiveness — this fivefold abstinence is the vow (*vrata*).

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Abstinence from violence, falsehood, stealing, carnality and possessiveness — these are the vows."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): "Desisting (virati) from injury (himsā), falsehood (anṛta), stealing (steya), unchastity (abrahma) and attachment-to-possessions (parigraha) is the (fivefold) vow (vrata)."
- The two agree exactly on structure; the only divergence is register ("carnality" vs "unchastity" for *abrahma*).

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A.

**Links to concept nodes:** [[ahimsa]] [[satya]] [[asteya]] [[brahmacarya]] [[aparigraha]] — each of the
five abstinences has a full concept-node treatment (teaching layer Ch. 08); this unit adds the sūtra-level
text those nodes rest on.

## SB 7.2 / SS 7.2

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *deśasarvato'ṇumahatī*

**Assembled reading:** By partial (*deśa*) or complete (*sarva*) abstinence, the vow is respectively small
(*aṇu[vrata]*) or great (*mahā[vrata]*). Both commentaries supply *virati* from 7.1. The one-word economy of
the sūtra carries the entire lay/ascetic architecture of Jain practice: the same five vows, two grades of
observance. (Full doctrinal treatment: `anuvrata.md`; the mahāvrata side is in the `ahimsa.md`/`aparigraha.md`
cluster and teaching-layer Ch. 08.)

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Partial abstinence is a small vow and complete abstinence is a great vow."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): "The vow is of two kinds, minor-vow (aṇuvrata) and great-vow (mahāvrata), from the abstinence (virati) being partial or complete."

**Links to concept nodes:** [[anuvrata]]

## SB 7.3 / SS 7.3

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (mūla) — but this sūtra is the hinge of THE fork: what
follows it differs structurally between the recensions.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *tatsthairyārthaṃ bhāvanāḥ pañca pañca*

**Assembled reading:** For the stabilizing (*sthairya*, "firmness/steadiness" — MW) of each vow there are
five supporting practices/contemplations (*bhāvanā*), five per vow. Tatia renders *bhāvanā* "supporting
practices"; VKJ "observances (contemplations)". Both note these concern the ascetic's great vows, with
little bearing on the lay small vows.

## THE FORK — SS 7.4–7.8: the five bhāvanā sūtras (Digambara-only block)

**Recension status: DIVERGENT — structurally AND in content.** In the Digambara text these are five
independent sūtras. In the Śvetāmbara text they are **not sūtras at all**: the equivalent lists appear inside
the SB *commentary* on 7.3, "with slight variations" (Tatia's apparatus). And the variations are real — for
two of the five vows the lists differ item-by-item, not just in order. Both recensions' versions are given
equal depth below; the SS column is mūla, the SB column is bhāṣya — that asymmetry of textual *status* is
itself the fork.

| vow supported | Digambara — SS mūla (sūtra, IAST from VKJ appendix) | Śvetāmbara — SB commentary on 7.3 (per Tatia) | divergence verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| non-violence (SS 7.4) | *vāṅmanoguptīryādānanikṣepaṇasamityālokitapānabhojanāni pañca* — control of speech, control of mind, care in walking (*īryā*), care in picking up and putting down (*ādānanikṣepaṇa-samiti*), inspecting food and drink (*ālokita-pāna-bhojana*) | care in walking, control of mind, **care in seeking alms**, care in handling implements, inspecting food and drink **in daylight** | **content variant**: SS has speech-control; SB instead has alms-seeking care, and adds the daylight specification |
| truthfulness (SS 7.5) | *krodhalobhabhīrutvahāsyapratyākhyānānyanuvīcibhāṣaṇaṃ ca pañca* — giving up anger, greed, fear, and jest, and speech following reflection (*anuvīci-bhāṣaṇa*) | same five items | order only — Tatia: "The SB gives the same in a different order" |
| non-stealing (SS 7.6) | *śūnyāgāravimocitāvāsaparoparodhākaraṇabhaikṣyaśuddhisadharmāvisaṃvādāḥ pañca* — dwelling in a solitary place (empty house, cave), dwelling in a deserted place, not obstructing other ascetics' access, begging food strictly per scripture (*bhaikṣya-śuddhi*), no disputes with fellow ascetics over common articles | seeking shelter only after permission and consideration; re-checking the shelter's continued availability; ascertaining the limits of the shelter; seeking shelter alongside a fellow monastic; accepting food and drink only when approved by the teacher | **the widest divergence of the five**: same domain (monastic shelter and alms) but the two lists share barely a single item |
| chastity (SS 7.7) | *strīrāgakathāśravaṇatanmanoharāṅganirīkṣaṇapūrvaratānusmaraṇavṛṣyeṣṭarasasvaśarīrasaṃskāratyāgāḥ pañca* — avoiding: listening to lewd stories about women, gazing at arousing parts of a woman's body, recalling past sexual experience, stimulating/delicious food and drink, decorating one's own body | avoiding: **places inhabited by women, animals and hermaphrodites**; lewd stories; gazing; recalling past experience; stimulating food and drink | **content variant**: SB's first item (segregated lodging) replaces SS's last (body-decoration) |
| non-possessiveness (SS 7.8) | *manojñāmanojñendriyaviṣayarāgadveṣavarjanāni pañca* — giving up attachment to agreeable and aversion to disagreeable objects of the five senses | same list | agreement — Tatia: "The SB gives the same list" |

**What this fork commits you to:** on the Digambara side, the bhāvanās are *scripture* — as fixed and
citable as the vows themselves; on the Śvetāmbara side they are *commentary* — authoritative exposition, but
of a different textual rank, and the tradition felt free to transmit partly different lists. Note the
direction of asymmetry is the reverse of the recensions' usual stereotype (the Śvetāmbara canon is the larger
one elsewhere — cf. Adhyāya 9's two Śvetāmbara-only sūtras); here it is the Digambara text that carries more
sūtras. A per-chapter, per-case matter — not a global rule.

## SB 7.4–7.7 / SS 7.9–7.12 — strengthening the vows generally (content survey)

**Recension status:** parallel content, offset now +5; no further divergence reported by Tatia's apparatus
across this run.

- **SB 7.4 / SS 7.9** (*hiṃsādiṣv ihāmutra cāpāyāvadyadarśanam*): contemplate the calamities and censure
  (*apāya-avadya*) that violence etc. bring **in this life and the next** — seeing their full horror supplies
  the moral strength to keep the vows.
- **SB 7.5 / SS 7.10** (*duḥkham eva vā*): "or [contemplate that] they are nothing but suffering" — the
  five sins as universal sources of *duḥkha*, harming perpetrator and victim alike.
- **SB 7.6 / SS 7.11** (*maitrīpramodakāruṇyamādhyasthyāni ca sattvaguṇādhikakliśyamānāvineyeṣu*): cultivate
  the **four social attitudes**, each keyed to its proper object — **friendliness (*maitrī*) toward all living
  beings, delight (*pramoda*) toward those of superior quality, compassion (*kāruṇya*) toward the afflicted,
  equanimity (*mādhyasthya*) toward the unteachable/vainglorious**. The SB attaches the famous scriptural
  resolve, quoted by Tatia: "I forgive all creatures. I cultivate friendliness with all. I harbour resentment
  against none." → This sūtra is the TS's own textual anchor for the `maitri-jain.md` concept node, and the
  structural sibling of the Buddhist brahmavihāras documented there (same four-slot architecture, different
  metaphysical home) — the comparison lives in that node, typed, not re-derived here.
- **SB 7.7 / SS 7.12** (*jagatkāyasvabhāvau vā saṃvegavairāgyārtham*): contemplate the nature of the world
  and of the body, to quicken *saṃvega* (spiritual urgency/fear of worldly life) and *vairāgya* (disinterest).
  SB glosses the world as "transformation of substances" (origination/cessation/continuity — the 5.29-30
  *sat* definition applied devotionally) and the body as "ephemeral, miserable, worthless and impure"; SS
  emphasizes transmigration through cosmic space (cross-ref 3.1–3.6) with nothing abiding.

## SB 7.8 / SS 7.13 — the definition of violence

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (mūla). This is the chapter's doctrinal center.

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *pramattayogāt prāṇavyaparopaṇaṃ hiṃsā*

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| *pramatta* | past participle of *pra-√mad*, first member of compound | from *pramāda*: "intoxication; carelessness, negligence, indifference" (same MW row established at 8.1, where *pramāda* is a cause of bondage) — here the participial "negligent/passion-affected" | MW |
| *yogāt* | masc. noun, ablative sg. | "yoking, activity, application" — the Jain technical sense (threefold activity of body/speech/mind) is fixed at TS 6.1–6.2, not by the lexicon | MW; TS 6.1 (this reading room's Adhyāya 6 unit) |
| *prāṇa* | masc. noun, first member | "breath, vitality, life" — the Jain technical count of **ten vitalities** (five senses, three activities, lifespan, respiration) is supplied by commentary with cross-ref to TS 2.14 | MW; VKJ at SS 7.13 |
| *vyaparopaṇa* | neut. verbal noun (vi-apa-√ruh caus.) | "cutting off, depriving, removal" — "severance" in both translations | MW |
| *hiṃsā* | fem. noun, nom. (predicate) | see 7.1 row | MW |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The load-bearing word is the ablative *pramattayogāt* — violence is
severance of vitalities **arising from passion-tinged activity**, not severance as such. Both traditions'
commentaries drive the same two corollaries, and the SS makes them vivid by quotation: (1) *injury without
passion is not hiṃsā* — "A tiny insect may be trampled to death on the track under the foot of an ascetic of
restrained movement… because there is no attachment or hatred, no bondage whatsoever is created"
(Pravacanasāra 3.16, quoted in SS per Tatia); the samiti-observing ascetic "does not cause bondage even if
he has caused injury" (VKJ's SS). (2) *Passion without injury IS hiṃsā* — "A person under the sway of
passion kills himself at the outset, even though another creature might or might not have been killed"
(SS, per both Tatia and VKJ, the latter as scripture-quote). The SS also cites Siddhasenadvātriṃśikā 3.16
and Pravacanasāra 3.17 to the same effect. Violence is thus relocated from the physical event to the
disposition — which is exactly what makes vow-keeping possible for an embodied being who cannot avoid all
mechanical harm.

**Assembled reading:** The severance of vitalities (*prāṇa*), when it arises from activity tinged with
passion/negligence (*pramatta-yoga*), is violence (*hiṃsā*).

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Taking life away out of passion is violence."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): "The severance of vitalities (prāṇa) due to the activity of the soul tinged with passionate disposition — pramattayoga — is injury (hiṃsā)."
- Same doctrine; Tatia compresses *prāṇa-vyaparopaṇa* to "taking life away," VKJ keeps the technical decomposition.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A. (The temptation here is legal, not physical — reading
*pramattayogāt* as mens rea in the modern criminal-law sense. The parallel is genuinely structural
(culpability tracks disposition, not outcome) but the frames differ: the Jain analysis is karmic-ontological
— passion binds karma-matter to one's own soul regardless of outcome, the victim's fate being "immaterial"
(SS) — whereas mens rea allocates liability for a harm to *another*. `structurally-parallel-to` at most;
not pursued further, no node exists to carry it.)

**Links to concept nodes:** [[ahimsa]] [[kashaya]]

## SB 7.9–7.12 / SS 7.14–7.17 — the remaining four definitions

**Recension status:** all four identical in both recensions (Tatia's apparatus silent; VKJ appendix matches).

- **SB 7.9 / SS 7.14** — *asadabhidhānam anṛtam*: "speaking the untrue is falsehood." SB distinguishes three
  kinds: denial of truth (e.g. "there is no soul"), whimsical statements (calling a cow a horse), despicable
  comments (harsh words, back-biting — blameworthy *even when true*, since the truth-vow is subordinate to
  non-violence).
- **SB 7.10 / SS 7.15** — *adattādānaṃ steyam*: "taking the not-given is stealing." Both commentaries make
  **motive** the crucial factor: SB — even a blade of grass, if taken with the motive of theft; SS — open
  roads and common facilities are not theft; "it is the contamination of the mind that determines the
  immorality."
- **SB 7.11 / SS 7.16** — *maithunam abrahma*: "coupling is unchastity." SS derives the desire from the rise
  of conduct-deluding karma; both note copulation entails violating all the other vows (killing mobile and
  immobile beings, etc.). Tatia's translator note reads the target as free sexual activity outside marriage
  (the lay-vow calibration of 7.15 confirms this).
- **SB 7.12 / SS 7.17** — *mūrcchā parigrahaḥ* — full depth below.

## SB 7.12 / SS 7.17 — the definition of possessiveness

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical (mūla).

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):** *mūrcchā parigrahaḥ*

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range:**

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| *mūrcchā* | fem. noun, nom. sg. | "fainting, swooning; **spiritual ignorance or delusion**; a process in calcining metals; the rising of sounds; growth, increase" (Apte); "fainting, loss of consciousness or sense; delusion" (Shabda-Sāgara) — the "infatuation/clinging" sense operative here is the delusion branch, not the swoon branch | Apte and Shabda-Sāgara, via Cologne lexica/wisdomlib.org (the wisdomlib page does not carry MW's own entry for this headword) |
| *parigrahaḥ* | masc. noun, nom. sg. (predicate) | see 7.1 row — MW's range runs from "seizing, taking, accepting" through "possession, property, wife, household, retinue" | MW, via wisdomlib.org |

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** The sūtra's force is the *identification*: possessiveness is not the
owning but the **clinging** (*mūrcchā*). SB: "desire, coveting, craving, longing, yearning, greed, clinging."
SS: even with no external possession at all, "a person obsessed with the sense of mineness has
possessiveness"; conversely the SS explicitly tests and rejects the contention that the soul's own
knowledge and intuition count as possessions — enlightened cognition is passion-free, so the criterion
(clinging, not having) excludes it. VKJ's SS at 7.13 closes the loop with the hiṃsā definition: "just as
infatuation (mūrcchā) is called attachment-to-possession (parigraha), the disposition of the soul tinged
with attachment (rāga) is the cause of injury (hiṃsā)" — the same disposition-over-event relocation in both
definitions.

**Assembled reading:** Clinging/infatuation (*mūrcchā*) is possessiveness (*parigraha*).

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "Clinging is possessiveness."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018, per SS): infatuation (*mūrcchā*) is attachment-to-possessions (*parigraha*).

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A.

## SB 7.13–7.16 / SS 7.18–7.21 — who observes the vows (content survey)

- **SB 7.13 / SS 7.18** — *niḥśalyo vratī*: "the vower must be free of the thorns (*śalya*)." Three thorns:
  deceit (*māyā*), desire for worldly reward through austerity (*nidāna*), and deluded world-view
  (*mithyādarśana*). With any of them present, no vow is properly observed.
- **SB 7.14 / SS 7.19** — *agāry anagāraś ca*: vowers are twofold — the housed (*agārin*, householder) and
  the houseless (*anagāra*, mendicant).
- **SB 7.15 / SS 7.20** — *aṇuvrato'gārī*: the householder is the small-vower. The commentaries calibrate
  each vow to lay life: non-violence restricted to not injuring **mobile (two-plus-sensed) beings**
  intentionally (SS: avoiding injury to mobile beings; SBT: desisting from *intended* violence — a slight
  but genuine difference of emphasis in where the lay line is drawn); truth restricted to statements that
  ruin homes and villages; non-stealing to legally/socially actionable taking; chastity to one's spouse;
  non-possessiveness to voluntarily capping cattle, corn, land etc. (→ full node: `anuvrata.md`)
- **SB 7.16 / SS 7.21** — the **seven supplementary vows** (*śīla*, "mores"), one long compound sūtra
  (Śvet. *dig-deśā-'narthadaṇḍavirati-sāmāyika-pauṣadhopavāso-pabhogaparibhogaparimāṇā-'tithisaṃvibhāga-vrata-sampannaś ca*;
  SS reads *proṣadhopavāsa* for *pauṣadhopavāsa* — orthographic): limiting the region of movement (*dik*),
  further limiting it (*deśa*), abstaining from purposeless sin (*anarthadaṇḍa-virati*), periodic
  equanimity-practice (*sāmāyika*), fasting on sacred days (*pauṣadha/proṣadha-upavāsa*), limiting consumables
  and non-consumables (*upabhoga-paribhoga-parimāṇa*), and offering alms to wandering ascetics
  (*atithi-saṃvibhāga*).
  **A genuine SBT-vs-SS classificatory divergence, mūla identical:** SBT groups supplementaries 1, 3, 6 as
  lifelong "subsidiary vows" and 2, 4, 5, 7 as occasion-based "trainee's vows"; SS groups the **first three**
  as subsidiary (guṇavrata) and the **remaining four** as trainee's vows (śikṣāvrata). Same seven items, two
  different internal taxonomies — the guṇavrata/śikṣāvrata line is drawn in different places by the two
  commentarial traditions. (Same fork-genus as Adhyāya 5's SS-vs-SBT atomic-bonding divergence: commentaries
  disagreeing over a shared mūla.)

**Links to concept nodes:** [[anuvrata]] [[shad-avashyaka]] (sāmāyika is the first āvaśyaka there)

## SB 7.17 / SS 7.22 — sallekhanā

**Recension status:** mūla near-identical with one **word-form variant**: Śvet. *māraṇāntikīṃ
**saṃlekhanāṃ** joṣitā* vs Dig. (VKJ appendix) *māraṇāntikīṃ **sallekhanāṃ** joṣitā* — the
*saṃlekhanā/sallekhanā* alternation (assimilated vs unassimilated prefix) is orthographic/phonological, not
doctrinal; recorded because the corpus's own node key (`sallekhana.md`) follows the Digambara form.

**Assembled reading:** The householder should willingly embrace (*joṣitā* — both commentaries stress the
word excludes compulsion) the end-of-life (*māraṇāntikī*) emaciation-rite (*sallekhanā*) — the graduated
withdrawal from food undertaken only when death approaches or the vows can no longer be sustained.

**Both commentaries pre-empt the suicide objection in parallel terms:** suicide is killing oneself *out of
passion* (anger, agony, frustration); sallekhanā is undertaken with joy and calm precisely to conquer
passion — and by 7.8/SS 7.13's own definition, where there is no *pramattayoga* there is no hiṃsā. SS (per
VKJ): "In sallekhanā there are no associated faults due to negligence (pramāda)… the person observing
sallekhanā is free from attachment (rāga)." The SB details the descending regimen (reduce diet → periodic
fasting → ascetic restraint → total abstention amid the reflections and meditations of Adhyāya 9). The full
contested-status treatment (including the modern legal controversy) lives in the `sallekhana.md` node —
which this sūtra anchors textually.

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Tatia (1994): "The householder should become a practitioner of the penitential rite of emaciation of the passions by a course of fasting which spans a number of years and ends in death."
- Vijay K. Jain (2018): "The householder observes sallekhanā at the end of his life" (with the SS gloss "emaciating… the passions in the proper manner").

## SB 7.18–7.32 / SS 7.23–7.37 — the transgression catalogues (content survey)

**Recension status:** parallel throughout at the +5 offset; mūla essentially shared, with three small
wording variants noted at the end of this section. Five transgressions (*aticāra*) are listed for the
enlightened world-view, then five for each of the five small vows, the seven supplementaries, and
sallekhanā — fourteen pentads, a lay examination-of-conscience in sūtra form.

| SB / SS | of what | the five transgressions (compressed) |
|---|---|---|
| 7.18 / 7.23 | enlightened world-view | suspicion about the Jina's doctrine; misguided hankering for heterodox views; doubt; praising heretical doctrines; familiarity with them (SB subdivides the heresies into 363 varieties — cross-ref 8.1) |
| 7.19 / 7.24 | (header sūtra) | "five each, in order, for the vows and mores" |
| 7.20 / 7.25 | non-violence (aṇuvrata) | tethering; beating; piercing the skin; overloading; withholding food and drink |
| 7.21 / 7.26 | truthfulness | wrong instruction; divulging secrets; forgery; misappropriating deposits; disclosing confidences |
| 7.22 / 7.27 | non-stealing | abetting theft; receiving stolen goods; evading customs; false weights and measures; counterfeits |
| 7.23 / 7.28 | chastity | matchmaking (for others); promiscuity; sex with the unattached/whores; unnatural practices; intense sexual obsession |
| 7.24 / 7.29 | non-possessiveness | exceeding one's set limits of land/buildings; silver/gold; livestock/grain; slaves/servants; base metals and utensils |
| 7.25 / 7.30 | 1st śīla (dik) | exceeding the set limits upward, downward, horizontally; enlarging the area; forgetting the limits |
| 7.26 / 7.31 | 2nd śīla (deśa) | importing from beyond the limit; sending a servant beyond it; calling out beyond it; gesturing beyond it; throwing objects beyond it |
| 7.27 / 7.32 | 3rd śīla (anarthadaṇḍa) | vulgar jokes (*kandarpa*); jokes with gesticulation (*kautkucya*); garrulity (*maukharya*); heedless over-action (*asamīkṣyādhikaraṇa*); **fifth member varies — see below** |
| 7.28 / 7.33 | 4th śīla (sāmāyika) | misdirected body-, speech-, and mind-activity; lack of earnestness; wandering of mind |
| 7.29 / 7.34 | 5th śīla (pauṣadha/proṣadha fast) | excreting, taking up [SB adds: and putting down], and spreading mats on uninspected/unswept ground; lack of earnestness; wandering of mind |
| 7.30 / 7.35 | 6th śīla (consumables) | eating animate (sacitta) food; food in contact with the animate; mixed with the animate; alcohol/fermented things; half-cooked food |
| 7.31 / 7.36 | 7th śīla (alms) | placing alms on animate things (green leaves); covering with the animate; passing off one's food as another's; envious/competitive giving; untimely offering |
| 7.32 / 7.37 | sallekhanā | wishing for longer life; wishing for quicker death; attachment to friends; clinging to remembered pleasures; bargaining for future reward (*nidāna*) |

**Three mūla wording variants inside this run (minor, recorded so the concordance is exact):**
1. **SB 7.21 *rahasyābhyākhyāna* vs SS 7.26 *raho'bhyākhyāna*** — same word (*rahas*, "secret") in two
   morphological dressings; no doctrinal difference.
2. **SB 7.27 ends *…upabhogādhikatvāni* ("excessive USE of consumer goods" — Tatia) vs SS 7.32 ends
   *…upabhogaparibhogānarthakyāni* ("keeping too many consumables and non-consumables… accumulation beyond
   one's needs" — VKJ).** A genuine small content variant, the only one in the fourteen pentads: the
   Śvetāmbara fifth transgression targets excessive *consumption*, the Digambara one purposeless
   *accumulation*. Both sit under the same śīla (purposeless sin), so the practical upshot converges, but
   the words — and both editions' translations — genuinely differ.
3. **SB 7.29 *ādāna-nikṣepa* (picking up **and putting down**) vs SS 7.34 *ādāna* alone; SB 7.32
   *nidāna-karaṇāni* vs SS 7.37 *nidānāni*** — compound-length differences with no semantic daylight.

## SB 7.33–7.34 / SS 7.38–7.39 — charity (content survey)

**Recension status:** both recensions identical; the chapter's two closing sūtras.

- **SB 7.33 / SS 7.38** — *anugrahārthaṃ svasyātisargo dānam*: "charity (*dāna*) is the giving-over of
  one's own [possessions] for the benefit [of self and other]." Tatia's rendering makes the reflexive
  explicit: the giver gives **for his own benefit**, with gratitude toward the recipient — giving weakens
  karmic bondage in the *giver*; the recipient is the occasion, not the beneficiary that matters karmically.
- **SB 7.34 / SS 7.39** — *vidhidravyadātṛpātraviśeṣāt tadviśeṣaḥ*: the worth of the gift varies with four
  factors — manner (*vidhi*), thing given (*dravya* — the ordinary "stuff" sense, not the six-substances
  technical sense of Adhyāya 5, a same-word-different-register case the reading room has now met repeatedly),
  giver (*dātṛ*), and recipient (*pātra*, "worthy vessel"). SB and SS unpack the four slightly differently
  (SB: propriety of place/time, quality of alms, giver's freedom from envy and expectation, recipient's
  faith/knowledge/conduct; SS: regard in giving, usefulness to the recipient's austerities, giver's freedom
  from envy and depression, recipient's self-restraint) — expository, not doctrinal, divergence.
- → The `dana.md` concept node carries this sūtra-pair's doctrine forward, including its cross-tradition
  comparison with Hindu and Buddhist dāna.
