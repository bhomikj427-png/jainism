# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 2 (The Nature of the Soul)

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra, Adhyāya 2. Having named the seven *tattva*s in 1.4, the treatise now turns to the
first of them, *jīva* (the soul) — its karma-caused states, its defining characteristic (sentience/*upayoga*,
already a fully-treated concept node), the worldly/liberated split, the senses, and (from roughly sūtra 2.30
onward, content-surveyed below) the mechanics of transmigration, birth, body-type, gender, and lifespan.
**As with Adhyāya 1, much of this chapter's core vocabulary already has dedicated concept-node treatment** —
`jiva`, `ajiva`, `gunasthana`, `leshya`, `samsara`, `upayoga` — this unit links to those rather than re-deriving
them, and focuses on the sūtra text, numbering, and recension-level findings unique to this reading room.

**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at equal weight, no spine. **A running one-sūtra Digambara-behind
offset begins at 2.13** and persists to the chapter's end (confirmed against Tatia's own apparatus through
2.28; the Contents table for the whole chapter shows SS 2.53 as the final sūtra against SB/SBT's 2.52 — so the
offset does *not* simply hold at "-1" throughout; see the numbering table note below), the same *kind* of
finding as Adhyāya 1's absorption fork, but here interleaved with **two genuine content-variant sūtras**, not
mere renumbering:
- **Sūtra 2.13 (SB/SBT) vs. its SS variant**: Śvetāmbara names three immobile-being types — earth-bodied,
  water-bodied, plant-bodied. **Digambara's version adds fire-bodied and air-bodied to the same "immobile"
  category** — five types, not three. This is not a numbering quirk; it is a substantive classificatory
  disagreement about whether fire- and air-bodied beings count as "mobile" or "immobile." Sūtra 2.14 is the
  Śvetāmbara recension's *own* patch for this: it separately declares fire and air mobile (alongside
  two-or-more-sensed beings), which the Digambara recension does not need because it already placed them under
  immobile in 2.13 (Digambara 2.14's variant accordingly drops the fire/air clause, listing only
  two-or-more-sensed beings as mobile). **Both recensions reach the same eventual five-fold elemental-body
  taxonomy; they simply draw the mobile/immobile line differently across two paired sūtras rather than one.**
  Tatia's own commentary notes the Śvetāmbara (SBT) position holds that fire- and air-bodied beings only move
  under external causation, not by their own volition, and so are formally "immobile" despite appearing to
  move — the same distinction the Digambara sūtra wording encodes directly rather than leaving to commentary.
- **Sūtra 2.19 vs. its absence in SS, and 2.23 vs. its SS variant**: a further small run of sūtras where
  Śvetāmbara states a point (2.19, that sentient application concerns only touch etc.) that Digambara's SS
  omits as a separate sūtra, and where 2.23's wording shifts to match whichever elemental-body list order the
  recension used at 2.13/2.14 — a downstream consequence of the 2.13/2.14 fork, not an independent one.
- **Net numbering:** Śvetāmbara runs 2.1–2.52; Digambara's own internal count in the Contents table given by
  Tatia's apparatus shows 2.1–2.53 — i.e. the running "-1" offset from the 2.13 fork is **not preserved to the
  end of the chapter**; at least one further absorption/split elsewhere in 2.30–2.52 must restore a sūtra to the
  Digambara count. This reading room did not trace that second correction sūtra-by-sūtra this pass (the birth/
  body/gender/lifespan material, sūtras roughly 2.30–2.52, is covered at content-survey depth below, per the
  same scope trade-off used in Adhyāya 1) — **flagged explicitly as an open item**, not silently resolved.

**Editions/translations used this unit:** Tatia (1994) as the primary source (same OCR file as Adhyāya 1,
downloaded once and read for this chapter too — not re-fetched); Vijay K. Jain (2018) as cross-check for the
foundational sūtras (2.1, 2.8); existing concept nodes (`jiva.md`, `ajiva.md`, `gunasthana.md`, `leshya.md`,
`samsara.md`, `upayoga.md`) for term-level lexical range, not re-derived.
**Confidence:** high on sūtras 2.1–2.29 (read in full, cross-checked, forks confirmed against Tatia's own
apparatus); medium on the 2.30–2.52 content survey (summarized from the chapter's own front-matter contents

## Sūtra 2.1 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *aupaśamika-kṣāyikau bhāvau miśraś ca jīvasya svatattvam audayika-pāriṇāmikau ca*

**Padaccheda:** Five *bhāva* ("state," MW) types named by their karmic cause: *aupaśamika* (from *upaśama*,
"subsidence/suppression" — MW), *kṣāyika* (from *kṣaya*, "destruction/elimination" — MW), *miśra* ("mixed"),
*audayika* (from *udaya*, "rising, arising" — MW), *pāriṇāmika* (from *pariṇāma*, "transformation" — MW, the
same root already flagged in the Adhyāya 5 unit as also a Sāṃkhya technical term, unrelated here).

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions and Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi-derived commentary agree exactly on the
five-fold scheme: states from suppressing karma, from eliminating karma, from a suppression/elimination mix,
from karma's active rising, and states innate to the soul regardless of karma. Both sources note the soul,
though sized and shaped by karma, lacks matter's own sensory qualities (touch/taste/smell/colour) — a clean
boundary-marker distinguishing *jīva* from `pudgala` (already treated in the Adhyāya 5 unit).

**Assembled reading:** The states caused by the suppression, elimination, and mixed suppression-and-elimination
[of karma], together with the states caused by karma's rising, and the innate states, are the soul's own nature.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 2.1; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 2.1.

**Links:** [[jiva]], [[gunasthana]] — this sūtra's five-fold *bhāva* scheme is the textual ancestor of the
fourteen-stage *guṇasthāna* ladder documented in the existing `gunasthana.md` node; cross-reference, not
re-derived.

## Sūtras 2.2–2.7 — both recensions (identical in substance)

The five classes of 2.1 are populated with specific counts and named states: **2 states** from suppression
(right faith and right conduct, both necessarily short-lived since suppression is temporary); **9 states** from
total elimination of the four destructive karma-types (right faith, right conduct, omniscience, perfect
intuition, and five limitless capacities — fearlessness, provisioning, environment-creation, paraphernalia,
and omnipotence); **21 states** from the mixed suppression/elimination of the destructive karmas (four kinds
of valid knowledge, three of deluded knowledge, three of intuition, five potentials, right faith, right
conduct, and "partial restraint/partial non-restraint" — limited self-discipline); **21 states** from karma's
active rising (transmigration across the four birth-realms, the four passions, three genders, deluded
world-view, ignorance, non-restraint, the unliberated condition, and the six *leśyā* psychic-colourings —
already the subject of the existing `leshya.md` node); and finally the **innate states** (2.7) — being a soul at
all, and being either capable (*bhavya*) or incapable (*abhavya*) of liberation, a distinction this reading room
flags as doctrinally load-bearing (some souls are永 permanently incapable of liberation on this account) but does
not further adjudicate here — some souls are permanently incapable of liberation on this account. Both
recensions agree on every count and category; Tatia records SBT's mention of a further "combined" sixth class
(mixtures of the above) as a scriptural aside, not a sūtra-level divergence.

## Sūtras 2.8–2.9 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST, 2.8):** *upayogo lakṣaṇam*

Sentience (*upayoga* — the existing `upayoga.md` node's own term) is declared the soul's defining characteristic
(*lakṣaṇa*, "mark, sign, defining property" — MW). 2.9 splits *upayoga* into two: determinate (*jñāna*,
knowledge, eight varieties per the already-covered 1.9/1.32 scheme) and indeterminate (*darśana*, intuition,
four varieties: visual, non-visual, clairvoyant, omniscient). Both recensions and Tatia's translator's note
agree there is scholarly disagreement on whether an omniscient soul has knowledge and intuition
*simultaneously* — flagged by Tatia as a genuine unresolved question within the tradition itself (not a
Digambara/Śvetāmbara split, but a live internal debate both recensions record).

## Sūtra 2.10 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *saṃsārīno muktāś ca* — souls are either worldly (*saṃsārin*, already the `samsara.md` node's
own term) or liberated (*mukta*). SS elaborates transmigration through five kinds of change: of matter-clusters,
space-occupied, time-units, birth-realm, and karmic state — cross-referenced to the existing `samsara.md` node
rather than re-derived.

## Sūtras 2.11–2.14 — worldly souls: mind, mobility, and the fork detailed above

Souls with a mind (*manaska*) can think and remember; those without (born by spontaneous agglutination of
matter rather than sexual/womb reproduction, per both recensions) cannot. Souls are further split into mobile
(*trasa*) and immobile (*sthāvara*) — the site of the 2.13/2.14 content-variant fork detailed in the
recension-model note above.

## Sūtras 2.15–2.25 — the senses and rational beings

Both recensions agree: there are five senses (touch, taste, smell, sight, hearing), each analysed in two ways —
as physical organs/organ-capacity (*dravyendriya*) and as the soul's own potential/active-application
(*bhāvendriya*). One-sensed beings (the five elemental-bodied types, per the 2.13/2.14 taxonomy) have only
touch; the count rises with worm/ant/bee/human-type exemplars given for two, three, four, and five senses
respectively (2.24/SS 2.23) — Tatia reproduces a Jain natural-history table (worms/molluscs at two senses;
ants/termites at three; wasps/moths/scorpions at four; fish/birds/quadrupeds/humans/celestials/infernals at
five) that both recensions share without variation. Five-sensed beings split further into those with a
(physical) mind, born of a womb, and those without, born by agglutination (2.25/SS 2.24) — only the
mind-possessing are rational (*saṃjñin*), capable of memory and forethought.

## Sūtras 2.26–2.29 — the soul's motion in transit between births

A distinctive piece of Jain cosmological mechanics, agreed by both recensions: a soul in transit after death,
before its new body forms, moves only by the power of its subtle karmic body (2.26/SS 2.25), and does so
strictly **along straight lines following the rows of cosmic space-units** (2.27/SS 2.26) — never diagonally,
never curving, matching the already-established space-point grid described in the Adhyāya 5 unit of this
reading room. A **liberated** soul's final transit is always a single straight line, no turns, from the point
of liberation straight up to the summit of occupied cosmic space (2.28/SS 2.27) — but a **worldly**, still-
transmigrating soul may need up to **three turns** depending on the relative position of its death-place and
birth-place within the polygonal shape of cosmic space (2.29/SS 2.28), since a soul cannot travel through the
unoccupied trans-cosmic void, which lacks *dharma-dravya*, the medium-of-motion substance already treated in
the Adhyāya 5 unit as a preconditon for any movement at all. Both recensions agree throughout this run; Tatia's
diagrams (figures 3–6 in the source) illustrate the zero/one/two/three-turn cases geometrically, matching the
sūtra text exactly.

## Sūtras 2.30–2.52/53 — content survey (births, body-types, gender, lifespan)

Per this chapter's own front-matter contents table (both recensions' sūtra ranges given side by side by
Tatia's apparatus), the remainder of Adhyāya 2 covers: the three types of birth (2.32/SS 2.31 — reported as
*sammūrcchana* spontaneous-agglutination birth, *garbha* womb-birth, and *upapāda* spontaneous-manifestation
birth proper to hell- and heaven-beings); the nine varieties of birthplace (2.33/SS 2.32); the specific
life-form species belonging to each of the three birth-types (2.34–2.36/SS 2.33–2.35); the five types of body
— gross physical (*audārika*), transformable (*vaikriyika*), projectable (*āhāraka*), karmic (*kārmaṇa*), and
fiery/luminous (*taijasa*) — across a long run of sūtras (2.37–2.49/SS 2.36–2.49); the three genders (2.50–2.51/
SS 2.50–2.52); and lifespans (2.52/SS 2.53). **Not read sūtra-by-sūtra this pass** — this is a genuine scope
boundary of the current unit, not a claim that this material is unimportant; a future pass should read this
run in full, the way 2.1–2.29 was read this session, particularly since the five-body-type taxonomy has
direct bearing on already-documented cosmological material (the celestial-being and infernal-being chapters
still queued as units #11–12 in this reading room's own work queue).
