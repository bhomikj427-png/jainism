# Tattvārtha Sūtra — Adhyāya 1 (Right Faith and Knowledge)

**Text:** Tattvārtha Sūtra (Umāsvāti/Umāsvāmī), Adhyāya 1. This chapter opens the entire treatise: it states the
three-jewel path to liberation (§1.1), defines the seven *tattva*s truth-categories the rest of the work
elaborates (§1.4), introduces the four-gateway/six-gateway/eight-gateway analytical schemes used throughout the
text, enumerates the five kinds of knowledge, and closes with the *naya* (philosophical-standpoint) doctrine.
**Much of this chapter's technical vocabulary already has dedicated concept-node treatment elsewhere in this
project's graph** — `ratnatraya`, `upayoga`, `naya` and its named sub-types (`naigama-naya`, `sangraha-naya`,
`vyavahara-naya`, `dravyarthika-naya`, `paryayarthika-naya`), `pramana`, and all five *jñāna*s
(`mati-jnana`, `shruta-jnana`, `avadhi-jnana`, `manah-paryaya-jnana`, `kevala-jnana`, `paroksha-jnana`) — per
`chapters/jain/translations/../../INDEX.md` Ch. 07 and Ch. 02. **This entry does not re-derive those terms'
lexical range from scratch; it links to the existing concept files for definitions and focuses on what those
files don't cover: the sūtra-by-sūtra text, its Digambara/Śvetāmbara numbering, and the sūtra-level grammar and
disambiguation.**

**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at equal weight, no spine (per `INDEX.md` §A). **This chapter has
a genuine, tabulated numbering fork starting partway through**, not a uniform offset like Adhyāya 5's:
- **Sūtras 1.1–1.20 run identically numbered in both traditions** (per Tatia's translator apparatus, which
  flags every fork explicitly and is silent here).
- **Śvetāmbara (SB/SBT) 1.21** ("*dvividho'vadhiḥ*" — clairvoyance is of two types) **has no Digambara (SS)
  counterpart as a separate sūtra**; the Digambara recension folds this directly into what becomes its own
  1.21. From here on, **Digambara numbering runs one behind Śvetāmbara** (SB 1.22 = SS 1.21, SB 1.23 = SS 1.22,
  … SB 1.33 = SS 1.32) — a single dropped-sūtra offset, structurally the mirror image of Adhyāya 5's
  numbering-*merge* forks, but here a numbering-*absorption*.
- **A second, content-bearing fork sits inside sūtra 16** (SB/SBT 1.16 vs. its "SS variant"): both give the
  same four-part contrast of "objects graspable by relatively pure vs. impure mental faculties," but the
  Śvetāmbara wording for two of the four contrasted qualities is *niḥsṛta* ("emerged/independent") and
  *sāndigdha* ("ambiguous"), while the Digambara/SS wording gives *anukta* ("unspoken") and reverses the
  exposed/unexposed contrast — Tatia's own translator's note explains the SS terms track a different
  distinction (whole-vs-part access to the object; described-vs-undescribed) than the SB terms do. **This is a
  genuine wording-and-sense variant, not mere renumbering** — the same category of fork as TS 5.37/Śvet.5.36 in
  the Adhyāya 5 unit of this reading room.
- **SB/SBT 1.34–1.35 vs. SS's single combined 1.33**: the Śvetāmbara recension lists five *naya*s in 1.34 and
  gives their sub-type counts in a separate 1.35; the Digambara/SS recension gives **seven** *naya*s (adding
  *śabda*'s two further subdivisions, *samabhirūḍha* and *evambhūta*, as standpoints in their own right rather
  than as sub-types of the fifth) folded into a single sūtra, numbered 1.33. **Net effect: Digambara Adhyāya 1
  has 33 sūtras where Śvetāmbara has 35** — one dropped at the 1.21 absorption, one more at the 1.34/1.35
  merger. This is the same "two different traditions' sūtra-count doesn't match, and the individual causes are
  each worth recording rather than averaged away" finding this reading room keeps making.

**Editions/translations used this unit:**
- **Nathmal Tatia (1994, *That Whichh Is*)**, via archive.org OCR (`ThatWhichIsTattvarthaSutraNathaMalaTatia`),
  downloaded directly and read locally — the most valuable single source for this chapter because Tatia's own

- **Vijay K. Jain (2018, *Ācārya Umāsvāmī's Tattvārthasūtra — With Explanation... from Ācārya Pūjyapāda's

  commentarial reading, particularly of sūtra 1.1's *ratnatraya* definitions.
- **Jaini/jainworld** (short single-sentence renderings of sūtras 1–12), used as a third cross-check on the
  foundational sūtras.
- **Existing concept-node files** (`ratnatraya.md`, `naya.md`, `pramana.md`, the five *jñāna* files,
  `paroksha-jnana.md`) for term-level Monier-Williams lexical range — **not re-derived here**, linked instead.
**Confidence:** high on the sūtra text, numbering, and recension-fork findings above (three-way cross-checked,
with Tatia's own apparatus doing much of the confirmation work); medium on some of the finer sub-type technical
distinctions in 1.13–1.35, where this pass is a content survey rather than a full padaccheda (see format note
below).
**Status:** drafted — first pass, both recensions, sūtras 1.1–1.35 (Śvetāmbara count) / 1.1–1.33 (Digambara
count).

**Format note (a deliberate, stated scope choice, same principle as the Kalyāṇamandira unit):** Sūtras 1.1–1.12
— the *ratnatraya* opening, the seven *tattva*s, the four/six/eight analytical gateways, the *pramāṇa*/*naya*
distinction, and the *parokṣa*/*pratyakṣa* knowledge-split — are treated at full sūtra-by-sūtra depth, since
this is genuinely new reading-room material (the sūtra text and its grammar, not yet covered anywhere else in
this project). Sūtras 1.13–1.35 — the detailed internal taxonomy of each of the five *jñāna*s (empirical

rules of all five, and the *naya* list) — are covered as a **content survey in original descriptive prose**,
citing but not transcribing the three sources, both because (a) the term-level definitions this material turns

independent full translations of over twenty additional sūtras verse-by-verse would reproduce the bulk of
several still-in-copyright works, the same concern already flagged and worked around in the Kalyāṇamandira unit.

## Sūtra 1.1 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *samyag-darśana-jñāna-cāritrāṇi mokṣa-mārgaḥ*

**Padaccheda:** *samyak* ("right, proper, complete" — MW) qualifies all three of *darśana* ("seeing," here
"faith/right world-view"), *jñāna* ("knowledge"), and *cāritra* ("conduct"); the compound's three members are
each independently attested as the technical triad *ratnatraya* ("three jewels") — see the existing
`ratnatraya.md` concept node for the term's full lexical and cross-tradition treatment. *Mārga* ("path," MW) in
the singular — not "paths" — is the sūtra's own point: Umāsvāti places all three nouns in a single compound
governed by one singular predicate noun, grammatically asserting that the three are jointly, not severally, the
path.

**Disambiguation (from commentary):** Both Tatia (reporting the SS commentary) and Vijay K. Jain (writing from
Sarvārthasiddhi directly) independently read the sūtra's grammatical number (singular *mārgaḥ* for three
coordinated nouns) as a deliberate polemical move: it rules out rival doctrines that knowledge alone, or faith
alone, or conduct alone, could constitute the path. Both sources cite this as Umāsvāti positioning the text
against Sāṃkhya-Yoga, Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika, and Buddhist accounts of liberated selfhood (per Tatia's translator's
note) — an opening move of the *treatise*, not a claim this reading room can verify against those rival schools'
own texts within this unit; recorded as reported by the commentary, not independently checked against a Nyāya
or Sāṃkhya primary source here.

**Assembled reading:** Right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct, together, constitute the path to
liberation.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.1 and commentary; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.1 and Sarvārthasiddhi
commentary; MW s.v. *samyak*, *darśana*, *jñāna*, *cāritra*, *mārga* (via the existing `ratnatraya.md` node,
not re-derived here).

**Links:** [[ratnatraya]] — *is-a-type-of* target already established in that file.

## Sūtra 1.2 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *tattvārtha-śraddhānaṃ samyag-darśanam*

**Padaccheda:** *tattva* ("thatness, true state, reality" — MW) + *artha* ("meaning, object, thing" — MW) — the
compound is glossed by both Digambara (Vijay K. Jain/Sarvārthasiddhi) and Śvetāmbara (Tatia/SB) commentary the
same way: the true nature of a substance, ascertained as it is. *Śraddhāna* ("belief, faith, trust" — MW) supplies
the verb-notion; the sūtra defines *samyag-darśana* ("right faith," from 1.1) as belief in *tattvārtha* so
construed.

**Disambiguation:** Both traditions agree this sūtra deliberately narrows *darśana*'s ordinary sense ("seeing,"
attested first in MW) to "faith/belief," since the surrounding treatise concerns a path to liberation, not
optics — Tatia states this explicitly, and Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi-derived commentary reaches the same
narrowing independently. No disagreement between the recensions on sense; this sūtra is a clean CONVERGED case.

**Assembled reading:** Belief in the substances, ascertained as they are, is right faith.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.2; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.2; Jaini/jainworld, sūtra 2 (independent
third-source confirmation of sense).

## Sūtra 1.3 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *tan nisargād adhigamād vā*

**Padaccheda:** *nisarga* ("own-nature, natural disposition" — MW) vs. *adhigama* ("acquisition, learning,
comprehension" — MW), joined by disjunctive *vā* ("or"). *Tat* ("that") back-refers to *samyag-darśanam* of 1.2.

**Disambiguation:** Both sources agree this sūtra gives right faith two possible origins — arising spontaneously
from the soul's own nature, or arising through instruction/study — without ranking one as superior to the other;
Tatia's commentary adds the doctrinal point (shared, not contested) that the *internal* cause in both cases is
identical (subsidence/destruction of faith-deluding karma), and only the *external* trigger differs.

**Assembled reading:** That [right faith] is attained by intuition/own-nature, or by acquisition [of knowledge/
instruction].

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.3; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.3.

## Sūtra 1.4 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *jīvājīvāsrava-bandha-saṃvara-nirjarā-mokṣās tattvam*

**Padaccheda:** A seven-member dvandva compound: *jīva* ("soul"), *ajīva* ("non-soul"), *āsrava* ("influx"),
*bandha* ("bondage"), *saṃvara* ("stoppage"), *nirjarā* ("gradual dissociation"), *mokṣa* ("liberation") — all
seven already independently treated as concept nodes (`jiva`, `karma`, `asrava`, `bandha`, `samvara`, `nirjara`,
`moksa` per Ch. 03 of the teaching layer). *Tattvam* ("reality, truth-category," singular collective) names the
whole compound as the treatise's subject matter.

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions agree on exactly these seven; Tatia notes some *other* scriptures list nine
(splitting off *puṇya* and *pāpa*, meritorious/demeritorious karma, as their own categories — matching the
already-documented `punya`/`papa` concept nodes' status as karma-subtypes rather than independent *tattva*s),
with SBT folding them into bondage and SS treating them as spanning both influx and bondage depending on
whether the karma is in its formative or accomplished state — a commentarial-elaboration divergence Tatia
records but which does not change either recension's *sūtra text* here (both give exactly seven).

**Assembled reading:** The soul, the non-soul, influx, bondage, stoppage, dissociation, and liberation
[from karmas] are the seven categories of truth.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.4 and translator's note; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.4.

**Links:** cross-references only — `jiva`, `asrava`, `bandha`, `samvara`, `nirjara`, `moksa` are already written
concept nodes per the existing graph; this unit adds no new edges to them.

## Sūtra 1.5 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *nāma-sthāpanā-dravya-bhāvatas tan-nyāsaḥ*

**Padaccheda:** *nāma* ("name"), *sthāpanā* ("installation, representation, symbol" — MW), *dravya* ("substance,"
here in its *potential/not-yet-actual* sense), *bhāva* ("actual/current state" — MW) — the four-fold *nikṣepa*
("installation, placement" — MW, matching the already-written `nikshepa`-adjacent naya-disambiguation material
in Ch. 02/07 of the teaching layer) scheme for analysing any of the seven *tattva*s (or anything else) four ways.

**Disambiguation:** Both traditions agree on the four-fold scheme and its four members; Tatia's worked example
(a college building called "All Souls" as a *name*-instance of "soul," without being one) matches the general
structure of *nikṣepa* as already described in this project's Ch. 07 chapter — this sūtra is this scheme's own
textual root within the TS, worth noting as a cross-reference rather than re-deriving the concept from scratch.

**Assembled reading:** The categories of truth [and anything else under discussion] are analysed by name,
representation, [potential] substance, and [actual] state.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.5; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.5.

**Links:** [[nikshepa]] *if written as a standalone node* — cross-reference; the existing Ch. 07 chapter already
covers *nikṣepa* under `naya` disambiguation material.

## Sūtra 1.6 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *pramāṇa-nayair adhigamaḥ*

**Padaccheda:** *pramāṇa* ("means/instrument of valid knowledge" — already an existing concept node, `pramana.md`)
and *naya* ("partial standpoint" — existing node `naya.md`), instrumental plural, governing *adhigamaḥ*
("comprehension, understanding" — MW, same root as 1.3's *adhigama*).

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions and all three translations agree: the *tattva*s (and everything installed
via 1.5's four-fold scheme) are known in full via *pramāṇa* and in delimited, single-aspect fashion via *naya*.
Tatia's commentary (SS-sourced) frames *naya* as what remains once a *pramāṇa*'s total, unlimited grasp of an
object is narrowed to one particular aspect — matching, independently, the existing `naya.md` and `pramana.md`
concept nodes' own framing of the pramāṇa/naya relationship. This sūtra is the TS's own textual anchor for that
already-documented distinction.

**Assembled reading:** [The categories of truth] are comprehended through the means of valid knowledge and the
partial standpoints.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.6; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.6.

**Links:** [[pramana]], [[naya]] — this sūtra is the TS's textual root for both already-written nodes;
cross-reference, not a new derivation.

## Sūtra 1.7 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *nirdeśa-svāmitva-sādhana-adhikaraṇa-sthiti-vidhānataḥ*

**Padaccheda:** Six ablative-case nouns naming a second analytical scheme (distinct from 1.5's four-fold
*nikṣepa*): *nirdeśa* ("definition, description" — MW), *svāmitva* ("ownership, possession" — MW), *sādhana*
("means, cause" — MW), *adhikaraṇa* ("location, substratum" — MW), *sthiti* ("duration" — MW), *vidhāna*
("variety, kind" — MW).

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions agree on the six-fold scheme; Tatia's worked example (applying all six to
the soul and, in turn, to right faith as the soul's attribute) matches Vijay K. Jain's Sarvārthasiddhi-derived
treatment in structure, though Tatia's is the fuller of the two sources consulted this pass.

**Assembled reading:** [The categories of truth, and their attributes,] are understood in detail through
definition, ownership, cause, location, duration, and variety.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.7; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.7.

## Sūtra 1.8 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *sat-saṃkhyā-kṣetra-sparśana-kāla-antara-bhāva-alpabahutvaiśca*

**Padaccheda:** An eight-member instrumental compound, a third and most granular analytical scheme: *sat*
("existence"), *saṃkhyā* ("number"), *kṣetra* ("field/area occupied"), *sparśana* ("field touched," a
distinct and wider notion than mere occupancy — Tatia's note explains the touched field includes adjoining
space-points), *kāla* ("time-span"), *antara* ("interval/time-lapse between occurrences"), *bhāva* ("state" —
here specifically the three karma-related states of a quality: suppressed, eliminated, or partially
both), *alpabahutva* ("relative fewness/greatness," i.e. comparative quantity across the states).

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions agree on the eight-fold scheme; the worked example in both Tatia and Vijay
K. Jain applies all eight to *samyag-darśana* specifically (continuing 1.7's example), giving the same
Jain cosmological figures for "innumerable" vs. "infinite" souls with right faith already treated at
length in the Adhyāya 5 unit of this reading room (space-point counts, the three-tier "innumerable" scale) —
cross-referenced rather than re-derived here.

**Assembled reading:** [The categories of truth] are further explored through existence, number, field occupied,
field touched, duration, interval, state, and relative quantity.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.8; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.8.

## Sūtra 1.9 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):** *mati-śruta-avadhi-manaḥparyaya-kevalāni jñānam*

**Padaccheda:** The five-member enumeration naming the five *jñāna*s, each already an existing, fully-treated
concept node: *mati* (`mati-jnana.md`), *śruta* (`shruta-jnana.md`), *avadhi* (`avadhi-jnana.md`),
*manaḥparyaya* (`manah-paryaya-jnana.md`), *kevala* (`kevala-jnana.md`).

**Disambiguation:** Uncontested; all three sources and the existing concept nodes agree on the enumeration and
its order (sensory → scriptural → clairvoyant → telepathic → omniscient), matching the graded-capacity
progression already documented across those five files.

**Assembled reading:** Knowledge is of five kinds: sensory, scriptural, clairvoyant, telepathic, and omniscient.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtra 1.9; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtra 1.9; Jaini/jainworld, sūtra 9.

**Links:** [[mati-jnana]], [[shruta-jnana]], [[avadhi-jnana]], [[manah-paryaya-jnana]], [[kevala-jnana]] — this
sūtra is the TS's textual root for all five nodes; cross-reference only.

## Sūtras 1.10–1.12 — both recensions (identical)

**Text (IAST):**
> 1.10 *tat pramāṇe*
> 1.11 *ādye parokṣam*
> 1.12 *pratyakṣam anyat*

**Padaccheda:** *Pramāṇa* (again, "valid-knowledge instrument") here classified into exactly **two** kinds — a
Jain-specific innovation Tatia's translator's note flags explicitly: the wider Indian philosophical tradition
more often uses a four-fold *pramāṇa* scheme (perception, inference, analogy, verbal testimony), whereas the
Jain tradition's two-fold split by *directness* (not by *method*) is its own contribution. *Ādya* ("the first
[two]" — i.e., *mati* and *śruta*) are labelled *parokṣa* ("indirect, mediate" — existing node
`paroksha-jnana.md`); *anya* ("the other [three]" — *avadhi*, *manaḥparyaya*, *kevala*) are *pratyakṣa*
("direct, immediate").

**Disambiguation:** Both recensions and all three sources agree, and the split is exactly the *paroksha-jnana*
concept node's own already-documented "Jain tradition inverts the Nyāya/Buddhist/Mīmāṃsā sense of
pratyakṣa/parokṣa" finding (cross-referenced in that file to Ch. 05 of the teaching layer) — this sūtra pair is
the TS's own textual root for that already-flagged cross-tradition divergence, not a new finding this unit
needs to re-derive.

**Assembled reading:** These [five kinds of knowledge] are [classed under two kinds of] valid-knowledge-means.
The first two are indirect. The remaining three are direct.

**Sources:** Tatia (1994), sūtras 1.10–1.12 and translator's note; Vijay K. Jain (2018), sūtras 1.10–1.12.

**Links:** [[paroksha-jnana]] — this sūtra pair is the textual root for the already-documented
pratyakṣa/parokṣa inversion; cross-reference only.

## Sūtras 1.13–1.35 (Śvetāmbara) / 1.13–1.33 (Digambara) — content survey

### 1.13 — the four aspects of empirical knowledge
*Mati-jñāna* is further analysed into four synonymous-but-distinct aspects: memory (*smṛti*), recognition
(*saṃjñā*), reasoning (*cintā*), and apprehension (*abhinibodha*) — Tatia notes this four-fold analysis was
accepted by all later Jain logicians as the standard starting point for further elaboration. Matches and
extends the existing `mati-jnana.md` node's treatment; not re-derived in full here.

### 1.14–1.19 — how empirical knowledge arises
Sensory knowledge arises from the senses, the mind, or both together (1.14) — beings without a mind (plants,
simple organisms) are limited to touch alone, per both recensions. It proceeds through **four stages**: bare
inarticulate sensation (*avagraha*), specific inquiry (*īhā*), articulate comprehension (*avāya*), and retention/
imprint (*dhāraṇā*) (1.15). Sūtra 1.16 carries the content-variant fork already flagged above (pure vs. impure
mental faculties, described differently by the two recensions). 1.17–1.19 refine which stage applies to which
kind of contact-object, and explicitly rule out the eye and mind from the "bare contact" stage (1.19), with
Tatia's translator's note flagging this as a deliberate rejection of a rival (non-Jain) theory that sight
involves physical rays contacting the eye.

### 1.20 — scriptural knowledge
*Śruta-jñāna* arises only in the wake of *mati-jñāna* (both recensions agree on this dependency) and comprises
the canonical "Inner Corpus" of twelve texts (attributed to the Jinas and their immediate disciples) and an
"Outer Corpus" of many further texts composed by later ascetics for disciples of lesser capacity — matches and
extends the existing `shruta-jnana.md` node.

### 1.21–1.23 (Śvet.) / 1.21–1.22 (Dig.) — clairvoyance's two types and six sub-types
This is the location of the chapter's main numbering-absorption fork (see recension-model note above):
clairvoyance (*avadhi*) is innate in hell- and heaven-beings (arising simply from birth into those realms) or
acquired through partial karma-elimination in humans and animals, the latter further sub-divided into six types
differing in how the clairvoyant range behaves over time (fixed to a place, place-independent-but-temporary,
contracting, expanding, oscillating, or permanent-until-liberation) — both recensions agree on the substance of
this taxonomy; only the sūtra-count differs, per the numbering note above.

### 1.24–1.26 (Śvet.) / 1.23–1.25 (Dig.) — mind-reading's two types and its difference from clairvoyance
*Manaḥparyaya-jñāna* has simple (*ṛju-mati*) and complex (*vipula-mati*) types, differing in purity, permanence,
depth, and infallibility (the complex type, once arisen, persists until omniscience). Both recensions agree on
a four-factor comparison distinguishing mind-reading from clairvoyance: required purity, spatial range (mind-
reading confined to the human realm, clairvoyance far wider), eligible subjects (mind-reading only for advanced
human ascetics), and depth vs. breadth of what each can know.

### 1.27–1.30 (Śvet.) / 1.26–1.29 (Dig.) — the domains of the five knowledges
Sensory and scriptural knowledge range over all six substances but not all their modes (1.27); clairvoyance
over all matter, not all its modes (1.28); mind-reading over only an infinitesimal part of clairvoyance's own
domain (1.29); omniscience alone over all substances in all modes, without restriction (1.30) — both recensions
agree throughout this run without variation beyond the numbering offset already noted.

### 1.31 (Śvet.) / 1.30 (Dig.) — co-occurrence
Up to four of the five knowledges can be simultaneously present in one soul (never omniscience alongside the
other four, since omniscience requires *total* elimination of knowledge-covering karma, which by definition
forecloses the partial-elimination states the other four depend on) — Tatia records a reported minority view
(SB) that the other four persist but are "outshone" by omniscience the way stars are outshone by the risen sun,
against which the SBT commentary objects as unscriptural; both recensions' sūtra *text* agrees, the divergence
is confined to commentarial elaboration.

### 1.32–1.33 (Śvet.) / 1.31–1.32 (Dig.) — the enlightened/deluded doubling
Sensory, scriptural, and clairvoyant knowledge each have a deluded counterpart (governed by whether the knower's
underlying *darśana*, world-view, is itself right or deluded), bringing the total count of knowledge-varieties
to eight when the three deluded forms are added to the five of 1.9 — mind-reading and omniscience alone are
always, inherently non-deluded. A person with deluded world-view is compared (1.33/1.32) to someone insane,
unable to distinguish true from false regardless of how much raw cognitive content they process.

### 1.34–1.35 (Śvet.) / 1.33 (Dig.) — the *naya* (philosophical-standpoint) list
This is the chapter's second numbering fork (see recension-model note above): Śvetāmbara names five *naya*s —
*naigama* (the "common person's," non-distinguishing view), *saṃgraha* (the generic/class view), *vyavahāra*
(the practical/functional view), *ṛjusūtra* (the linear, present-moment-only view), and *śabda* (the literal,
word-precise view, itself with three further sub-types per 1.35) — while Digambara/SS names **seven**,
promoting two of Śvetāmbara's *śabda* sub-types (*samabhirūḍha*, the etymological view; *evambhūta*, the
strict actuality-of-present-action view) to full standing as independent *naya*s in their own right, all folded
into one sūtra (1.33) rather than split across two. Both recensions agree these are two-way classified as
substance-focused or mode-focused (echoing 1.6), and both already have fuller treatment in the existing
`naya.md`, `naigama-naya.md`, `sangraha-naya.md`, `vyavahara-naya.md`, `dravyarthika-naya.md`, and
`paryayarthika-naya.md` concept nodes — this survey confirms the TS 1.34/1.35 (or SS 1.33) sūtra text as those
nodes' shared textual root, without re-deriving their content.
