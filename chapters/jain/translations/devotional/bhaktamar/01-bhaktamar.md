# Bhaktāmara Stotra — Verse 1

**Text:** Bhaktāmara Stotra ("Hymn of the Immortals' Devotion"), in praise of Ṛṣabhanātha/Ādinātha, the first
Tīrthaṅkara.
**Author:** Ācārya Mānatuṅga. Dating is genuinely unresolved, not merely uncited-by-us: proposed dates range
from c. 300 CE to c. 1050 CE across the scholarship, with recent opinion favouring the second half of the 6th
century CE — but the earliest surviving commentary is 14th-century, leaving a multi-century gap the sources
themselves flag as hard to explain if the 6th-century date is right. Treat authorship date as **contested**,
not a settled fact this file can assert.
**Recension model:** Digambara and Śvetāmbara at **equal weight, no spine** (see `INDEX.md` §A). The
Śvetāmbara recitation runs 44 verses; the Digambara recitation runs 48, with four extra verses (numbered
32–35 in the 48-verse count) describing the eight *prātihārya* (miraculous accompaniments of a Jina's
omniscience) that Śvetāmbara sources treat as a later interpolation. **Verse 1 itself is unaffected by this
split** — three independent sources consulted (English Wikipedia's sourced claim, Jainpedia/Nalini Balbir, and
the Digambara Vijay K. Jain 2023 edition, which numbers this verse 1 of 48 with identical content to what the
44-verse sources give as their verse 1) agree the divergence begins only around verse 31/32, not here.
**Editions/translations used this unit:** Vijay K. Jain (2023, *Ācārya Mānatuṅga's Bhaktāmara Stotra — With
Hindi and English Rendering*, Digambara-oriented, via archive.org OCR text, downloaded directly and grepped
locally); Jainpedia's academic overview article (contributed by **Nalini Balbir**, Professor of Indology,
Sorbonne Nouvelle — a named academic source, SOAS Centre of Jaina Studies host); a **widely-mirrored English
paraphrase** found in near-identical wording across several Jain devotional websites (Tattva Gyan, Jain Square,
Bhaktinidhi, and others) — **flagged, not treated as independent confirmation**: the wording is close enough
across sites that this looks like one uncredited translation copied repeatedly, not three separate scholarly
efforts (CLAUDE.md §4: "copies are not confirmation"). **Confidence on the translation panel is therefore
medium**: 2 solid independently-attributed sources (Vijay K. Jain; Balbir/Jainpedia) plus 1 flagged
likely-derivative source used only as a cross-check, not as a third vote. A bounded search (~5 queries) did not
turn up a clearly distinct third named scholarly translation (e.g. a Champat Rai Jain or comparable early
20th-century rendering) for this verse; left as an open item, same pattern as the TS 5.1 Tatia gap that was
later closed by a more targeted fetch.
**Status:** in-progress (this file currently covers verse 1 only — prototype unit, per work-queue item #2;
**stop here for user approval before continuing**, per the devotional format's own queue entry).

## Bhaktāmara v.1 — both recensions (Digambara v.1 / Śvetāmbara v.1)

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical for this verse (see recension-model note above).

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):**
> bhaktāmara-praṇata-mauli-maṇi-prabhāṇām
> udyotakaṁ dalita-pāpa-tamo-vitānam |
> samyak praṇamya jina-pāda-yugaṁ yugādāv
> ālambanaṁ bhava-jale patatāṁ janānām || 1 ||

*(IAST normalized from the sandhi-joined line-break forms given by sources — e.g. "prabhāṇā-mudyotakam" is
*prabhāṇām* + *udyotakam*, and "yugādāvālambanam" is *yugādau* + *ālambanam* — both ordinary vowel sandhi, not
separate words. Devanāgarī not independently verified against a critical edition this pass; omitted rather
than reproduced from an unverified OCR pass (per CLAUDE.md §3, "omit if unsure — never fabricate script").)*

**Meter:** Vasantatilakā — 14 syllables per pāda, a fixed long-short pattern (the sakvarī-class meter used
throughout the Bhaktāmara Stotra's core verses), per the Wikipedia article's sourced description.

**Disambiguation (from commentary/internal consistency — devotional-verse equivalent of the bhāṣya check):**

1. **The double sense of *yuga* is a deliberate figure, not an ambiguity to resolve.** The word appears twice
   in the same verse with two different attested MW senses — "pair" (*jina-pāda-yugam*, "the pair of the
   Jina's feet") and "age/era" (*yugādau*, "at the beginning of the [current cosmic] age"). Classical Sanskrit
   *kāvya* regularly exploits exactly this kind of repeated-word double-sense (a *yamaka*-type figure) as
   deliberate poetic craft. Both English sources compared below independently render both senses correctly and
   distinctly ("the duo of your Feet" / "pair of feet" vs. "the era that marked the commencement of the
   land-of-action" / "at the beginning of the age") — internal consistency across independent translators is
   itself the check here, standing in for a formal commentary since this is devotional *kāvya*, not a
   philosophical sūtra with a bhāṣya tradition attached the way Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.1 has.
2. **Why *tamas* means "darkness of sin," not the Sāṃkhya *guṇa*.** MW's entry for *tamas* explicitly carries a
   Sāṃkhya-philosophical technical sense (one of three *guṇa*s constituting matter and consciousness) alongside
   the plain literal sense "darkness, gloom." That technical sense belongs to a **different philosophical
   system** (Sāṃkhya, not Jainism) and is not the operative reading here — the compound *pāpa-tamas* ("darkness
   of sin/evil-karma") reads as a straightforward metaphor (sin figured as darkness the Jina's radiance
   dispels), consistent across every translation compared below, none of which invokes *guṇa*-theory. Flagged
   explicitly so the lexicon's broader range is not mistaken for license to import a different tradition's
   metaphysics into a Jain devotional line — the same discipline CLAUDE.md §0 asks for with modern-physics
   readings applies equally to cross-tradition Indian-philosophy readings.
3. **Why *jina* needs no disambiguation.** Unlike *pudgala* at Tattvārtha Sūtra 5.1, Monier-Williams already
   lexicalizes the Jain sectarian sense of *jina* directly in the general dictionary entry — no commentarial
   narrowing is required to get from "victor" (the adjectival root sense) to "the Jina," the object of this
   entire hymn.

**Assembled reading:** Having properly bowed to the pair of the Jina's feet — feet that brighten the radiance
of the crest-jewels in the crowns of the devoted immortals (gods) who bow before them, and that shatter the
spreading darkness of sin — [feet that have been] the refuge, from the very beginning of the [current cosmic]
age, for people falling into the ocean of worldly existence...
*(Grammatically this verse is one long dependent clause; several sources note it is designed to be read
together with verse 2, where the main clause — the poet's stated intent to compose this hymn despite his own
limitations — arrives. Not resolved within this single-verse prototype unit; flagged for continuation.)*

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Vijay K. Jain (2023, Digambara edition, via archive.org OCR): "The duo of your Feet enhances the effulgence
  of the jewels studded in the diadems of the devas who bow down in your devotion; the duo of your Feet
  destroys the expanse of the darkness of the evil-karmas (*pāpa*); the duo of your Feet had provided refuge to
  the living-beings getting immersed in the ocean of worldly-existence in the era that marked the commencement
  of the land-of-action (as against the land-of-enjoyment); after making obeisance humble to the duo of your
  Feet—" (continues into v.2, matching the internal cross-reference noted above).
- Nalini Balbir / Jainpedia (academic, paraphrase rather than full running translation): "[the feet of the
  Jina which enhance] the brightness of the jewels in the crowns lowered by the devoted gods."
- Widely-mirrored popular paraphrase (Tattva Gyan / Jain Square / Bhaktinidhi, near-identical wording across
  sites — **flagged as likely one uncredited source, not three independent ones**): "When the Devas bow down
  in reverence at the feet of the victorious one Bhagwan Aadinath, the gems in the crowns of their heads
  radiate brightness which pales in comparison to the dazzle of his lotus feet. Mankind is drowned in the ocean
  of worldly attachments and surrounded by darkness of sins and ignorance... He who submits himself at his feet
  crosses the barriers of rebirth and moves into the state of liberation."
- **Agreement:** all render the core image identically (gods' crown-jewels brightened by the Jina's feet;
  those feet also dispel sin's darkness and offer refuge from worldly existence). **Divergence:** the popular
  paraphrase compresses and reorders freely (moving "drowned in the ocean" earlier, adding an interpretive
  gloss "crosses the barriers of rebirth" not literally in this verse's Sanskrit — that specific claim belongs
  to the verse's implication, not its explicit wording, so it reads as commentary bleeding into "translation").
  Vijay K. Jain's rendering stays closest to the Sanskrit's own clause order and explicitly parenthesizes
  Sanskrit terms (*pāpa*) rather than fully dissolving them into English — the more literal of the two
  attributed sources.
