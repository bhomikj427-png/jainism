# Svayambhūstotra — Ācārya Samantabhadra: Adoration of the Twenty-four Tīrthaṅkaras

**Text:** *Svayambhūstotra* (also *Svayambhū-stotram* / *Stuti-vidyā*), Ācārya Samantabhadra. Date **contested** —
Jaina tradition and Vijay K. Jain place Samantabhadra in the latter 2nd c. CE; academic datings range c. 2nd–6th
c. CE (not resolved here). 143 verses, organised as 24 *jina-stuti*s (one hymn per Tīrthaṅkara, Ṛṣabha →
Mahāvīra), preceded by the one-line invocation *svayambhuve namastubhyam*.

**Recension model:** **Single recension — NO Digambara/Śvetāmbara mūla fork.** Samantabhadra is a
Digambara-tradition ācārya and the *Svayambhūstotra* is transmitted as one Sanskrit text; there is no two-recension
apparatus of the kind the *Tattvārtha Sūtra* carries, and Śvetāmbaras do not traditionally recite it. So the
equal-weight side-by-side machinery used for the Tattvārtha units is **N/A here** (as it was for Kalyāṇamandira).
One genuine *textual* crux is flagged in-file (v.5, *vaṣa bho* — an apparent edition corruption of *vṛṣabho*);
that is a manuscript/edition matter, **not** a sectarian recension split.

**Editions/sources used this unit:**
1. **Base text (Sanskrit — Devanāgarī + transliteration):** *sanskritdocuments.org*, `svayambhUstotram` (ITRANS
   `.itx` + HTML, last updated 22 Apr 2023), input by volunteers, licensed for study — **the independent text
   witness** (not derived from the translation below). Devanāgarī quoted verbatim from this file; IAST is the
   mechanical transliteration of that Devanāgarī (script conversion, not interpretation).
2. **English translation + word-meaning:** **Vijay K. Jain (2015),** *Ācārya Samantabhadra's Svayambhūstotra –
   Adoration of The Twenty-four Tīrthaṅkara*, Vikalp Printers, Dehradun — retrieved as archive.org OCR
   (`SvayambhuWebCover`) by direct download + local grep (the fetch tool truncated the OCR; the Devanāgarī/IAST in
   that OCR was unusable, so the base text is taken from source 1, and only Jain's clean **English** is used). This
   is the same edition family already trusted in this reading-room for Bhaktāmar and the Tattvārtha
   *Sarvārthasiddhi*.
3. **Lexical range:** Monier-Williams *Sanskrit-English Dictionary* (cited by headword, *s.v.*) — the independent
   lexicon layer.

**Sourcing honesty (per §B):** Only **one** full scholarly English translation (Vijay K. Jain) could be located
after searching (no independent second English rendering of the complete stotra was found — cf. the parallel
single-translation situation for Bhaktāmar's later verses). Genuine independence is therefore secured at the
**text layer** (sanskritdocuments base text, independent of Jain's edition) and the **lexical layer** (MW,
independent of any translation); the padaccheda below is derived from MW directly, *not* read off the translation.
Where Jain's English and the MW-grounded assembled reading agree, that is cross-layer convergence, and it is noted.
**Confidence: medium** on the assembled readings (single translation; base text from a study website, not a
critical edition with apparatus).

**Status:** drafted — **first pass.** Full-depth treatment of §1 Ṛṣabhanātha (vv. 1–5) and §24 Mahāvīra (vv.

Remaining verses (6–135) are surveyed, not yet given per-verse padaccheda — logged as an open item.

## §0 What this text is, and why it matters for THIS project

The *Svayambhūstotra* is a **devotional** hymn, but it is the devotional work of the tradition's greatest
**logician** — Samantabhadra, the most forceful early proponent of **anekāntavāda**. Two features make it a high-
value reading-room unit rather than mere praise-poetry:

1. **Deliberate appropriation of Brahmanical divine epithets.** The very title-word *svayambhū* ("self-existent")
   is, in Brahmanical usage, a standard name of the **creator god** (Brahmā / Viṣṇu). Samantabhadra applies it —
   and *prajāpati* (v.2), *brahma-pada* and *īśvara* (v.4) — to a Tīrthaṅkara who is "self-become" **through his
   own spiritual effort**, and who is emphatically **not a creator**. Reading these words off the general lexicon
   gives the theistic sense; the Jaina doctrinal sense is the point of contrast. This is the disambiguation the
   reading-room exists to make explicit (and a textbook `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` case).
2. **The doctrine is stated inside the praise.** The closing Mahāvīra hymn names **syādvāda** outright (v.138) and
   signs off by calling the Jina's teaching *naya*-based and "all-round-good" (v.143) — a direct textual anchor for
   the already-written concept nodes [[syadvada]], [[anekantavada]], [[naya]], [[saptabhangi]].

**Structural note (countable from the text):** the hymn gives **5 verses to each Tīrthaṅkara except three** —
#18 Aranātha gets **20** (vv. 86–105), #22 Neminātha gets **10** (vv. 121–130), #24 Mahāvīra gets **8** (vv.
136–143). 21×5 + 20 + 10 + 8 = **143**. The long Aranātha and Neminātha sections are where the stotra's doctrinal
excursuses cluster (observed, not here attributed to a cause — that would need a source).

**Meter:** the stotra is a **metrical showcase**, changing meter from section to section; several sections (esp.
the Mahāvīra hymn) are elaborate **yamaka** (sound-repetition) *citra-kāvya*. Per-verse syllable counts are given
below where treated; precise meter-names are left un-asserted pending a prosody source (flagged, not faked).

## Maṅgala (invocation)

**Devanāgarī:** स्वयम्भुवे नमस्तुभ्यम् ।
**IAST:** *svayambhuve namastubhyam* |
**Reading:** "Homage to you, O Self-Existent One (*svayambhū*)." — *svayambhuve* is **dative** (to the svayambhū);
the whole stotra is addressed to the Jina under this epithet. The appropriation flagged in §0 is announced in the
first three words. (Source 1.)

## §1 — Śrī Ṛṣabhanātha / Ādinātha jina-stuti (vv. 1–5)  — FULL DEPTH

> **Syntactic note:** vv. 1–5 are **one sentence.** Verses 1–4 are relative clauses (*yena… yaḥ… yaḥ… yo…*)
> describing Ṛṣabha; the main clause and finite verb arrive only in v.5 — *sa … nābhi-nandano jinaḥ … **punātu**
> ceto mama* ("may that Jina, son of Nābhi, … **purify** my mind"). Each verse below is glossed on its own, but the
> grammatical spine runs across all five.

### v.1

**Text — Devanāgarī (source 1):**
> स्वयम्भुवां भूत-हितेन भूतले
> समञ्जस-ज्ञान-विभूति-चक्षुषा ।
> विराजितं येन विधुन्वता तमः
> क्षपाकरेणेव गुणौत्करैः करैः ॥ १॥

**IAST (transliteration of the above):**
> *svayambhuvāṃ bhūta-hitena bhūtale / samañjasa-jñāna-vibhūti-cakṣuṣā |*
> *virājitaṃ yena vidhunvatā tamaḥ / kṣapākareṇeva guṇautkaraiḥ karaiḥ || 1 ||*

**Syllables:** 12 per pāda (four pādas).

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range** (each row cited to Monier-Williams by headword):

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range (MW) | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------------|----------------|
| svayambhū | m. (here *svayambhuvāṃ*) | "self-existing, self-produced, independent"; **as a proper name → Brahmā, Viṣṇu, Śiva, Kāma** | MW s.v. *svayambhū* |
| bhūta | n., first member of cpd | "become, been; that which is or exists; a living being, creature; an element" | MW s.v. *bhūta* |
| hita | n. | "beneficial, wholesome; benefit, welfare, good" → *bhūta-hitena* "by [his] benefiting of creatures" | MW s.v. *hita* |
| bhū-tala | n. loc. *bhūtale* | "surface of the earth, ground" | MW s.v. *bhūtala* |
| samañjasa | adj. | "right, proper, correct, fit, sound" | MW s.v. *samañjasa* |
| jñāna | n. | "knowing, knowledge, cognition" | MW s.v. *jñāna* |
| vibhūti | f. | "expansion, plenitude; might, power, greatness, glory, splendour" | MW s.v. *vibhūti* |
| cakṣus | n. (instr. *cakṣuṣā*) | "the eye; the faculty of sight" → here the "eye" of right knowledge | MW s.v. *cakṣus* |
| virājita | ppp. | "shining forth, made resplendent, illuminated" | MW s.v. *virāj* |
| vidhunvat | pr.pt. (√dhū) | "shaking off, dispelling, driving away" | MW s.v. *dhū* |
| tamas | n. | "darkness, gloom; (fig.) ignorance, mental darkness" | MW s.v. *tamas* |
| kṣapā-kara | m. | "'night-maker', the **moon**" (*kṣapā* night + *kara* maker) | MW s.v. *kṣapākara* |
| guṇa-utkara | m. | "a multitude / mass (of); heap" → *guṇautkaraiḥ* "with masses of virtues/qualities" | MW s.v. *utkara*, *guṇa* |
| kara | m. | **double sense**: "a ray of light" **and** "a hand" — with the moon, "ray" | MW s.v. *kara* |

**Disambiguation:** the pivotal word is **svayambhū**. MW's primary lexical output is the theistic proper name
(Brahmā/Viṣṇu). The verse's own frame overrides that: the being so named "dispels darkness/ignorance (*tamaḥ*)
with the eye of right knowledge (*samañjasa-jñāna*)" and "graced the earth for the benefit of creatures" — i.e. a
Tīrthaṅkara-teacher, self-become through his *own* realization, **not a world-creator**. The moon-simile
(*kṣapākareṇeva … karaiḥ*) turns on the pun *kara* (moon's *rays* / a *hand*): the Jina dispels the darkness of
ignorance with rays-that-are-virtues as the moon dispels night's darkness with its rays.

**Assembled reading:** "That Self-Existent One, by whom — for the good of all creatures, with the eye of proper
knowledge and glory — this earth was made resplendent as he dispelled the darkness [of ignorance], like the
night-making moon [dispelling darkness] with its rays that are masses of virtues …" (continues into v.5).

**Published translation (comparison):** Vijay K. Jain (2015): "Lord Ṛṣabha Deva, the self-enlightened and the
saviour of the living beings, had graced this earth. He had the grandeur of right knowledge as his eyes and had
cast the divine light of his discourses to destroy the darkness of ignorance, just as the rays of the moon destroy
the darkness of the night." — **Note:** Jain renders *svayambhū* as "self-enlightened," which is exactly the
de-theologized, effort-based sense argued from the commentary tradition; his English silently makes the
disambiguation this file makes explicit.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A.

**Links to concept nodes:** [[kevala-jnana]] (the "eye of proper knowledge" = omniscience) · [[mahavira]] /
[[parshvanatha]] (fellow Tīrthaṅkaras). Cross-ref: the *svayambhū* appropriation is a `shares-vocabulary-with` /
`often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent` relation to Brahmanical [[brahman]] theism — the Jina is called by the
creator-god's name but is **not** a creator (see §0).

### v.2 — *prajāpati* appropriated

**Devanāgarī:**
> प्रजापतिर्यः प्रथमं जिजीविषूः
> शशास कृष्यादिषु कर्मसु प्रजाः ।
> प्रबुद्धतत्त्वः पुनरद्भुतोदयो
> ममत्वतो निर्विविदे विदांवरः ॥ २॥

**IAST:** *prajāpatir yaḥ prathamaṃ jijīviṣūḥ / śaśāsa kṛṣyādiṣu karmasu prajāḥ | prabuddha-tattvaḥ punar
adbhutodayo / mamatvato nirvivide vidāṃvaraḥ || 2 ||*

**Key gloss + disambiguation:** **prajāpati** (MW s.v.: "lord of creatures; a creator; Brahmā") — again a
Brahmanical creator-title. Here it is *earned functionally*: Ṛṣabha "taught the people (*prajāḥ*), wishing to live
(*jijīviṣūḥ*), the works beginning with agriculture (*kṛṣy-ādiṣu karmasu*)" — he is "lord of creatures" as the
**civilizational teacher** who instituted livelihood at the *bhoga-bhūmi → karma-bhūmi* transition, *not* as their
metaphysical maker. Then, "having awakened to reality (*prabuddha-tattvaḥ*), best of knowers (*vidāṃvaraḥ*), he
became detached from possessiveness (*mamatvato nirvivide*)."

**Vijay K. Jain (2015):** "…as the Bharata region, due to the passage of time, was turning from the land of
glorious abundance and enjoyment (bhogabhūmi) to the land of action (karmabhūmi), he taught the people the means
of livelihood, like cultivation. Later on, although endowed with immense splendour, having acquired the true
knowledge of the reality of substances, souls and non-souls, he renounced all attachment to worldly objects."

**Links:** [[karma]] (here in the ordinary sense "work/occupation," not karmic matter — a lexical trap worth
noting). Modern comparison: N/A.

### v.3 — the renunciation

**Devanāgarī:**
> विहाय यः सागर-वारि-वाससं
> वधूमिवेमां वसुधा-वधूं सतीम् ।
> मुमुक्षुरिक्ष्वाकु कुलादिरात्मवान्
> प्रभुः प्रवव्राज सहिष्णुरच्युतः ॥ ३॥

**IAST:** *vihāya yaḥ sāgara-vāri-vāsasaṃ / vadhūm ivemāṃ vasudhā-vadhūṃ satīm | mumukṣur ikṣvāku-kulādir ātmavān
/ prabhuḥ pravavrāja sahiṣṇur acyutaḥ || 3 ||*

**Sense (MW-grounded + Jain):** "Abandoning this **chaste bride the Earth** (*vasudhā-vadhūṃ satīm*) — clothed, as
it were, in the ocean-waters as her garment (*sāgara-vāri-vāsasaṃ*) — desiring liberation (*mumukṣuḥ*), first of
the **Ikṣvāku** line, self-possessed (*ātmavān*), enduring (*sahiṣṇuḥ*), unfallen (*acyutaḥ*), the Lord went
forth (*pravavrāja*) [into asceticism]." **Note:** *acyuta* ("unfallen, imperishable") is another Viṣṇu-epithet
here naturalized to the steadfast ascetic. Jain reads *pravavrāja* as embracing "the noble asceticism, free from
all vestiges of clothes (**digambara**)" — tying the going-forth to the [[digambara]] nudity ideal.

**Links:** [[aparigraha]] · [[digambara]] · [[moksha]] (*mumukṣu* = liberation-seeker). Modern comparison: N/A.

### v.4 — *brahma-pada* and *īśvara* appropriated

**Devanāgarī:**
> स्व-दोष-मूलं स्व-समाधि-तेजसा
> निनाय यो निर्दय-भस्मसात्क्रियाम् ।
> जगाद तत्त्वं जगतेऽर्थिनेऽञ्जसा
> बभूव च ब्रह्म-पदामृतेश्वरः ॥ ४॥

**IAST:** *sva-doṣa-mūlaṃ sva-samādhi-tejasā / nināya yo nirdaya-bhasmasātkriyām | jagāda tattvaṃ jagate 'rthine
'ñjasā / babhūva ca brahma-padāmṛteśvaraḥ || 4 ||*

**Key gloss + disambiguation:** the climax epithet is **brahma-pada-amṛta-īśvara**. Each member is a Brahmanical
loaded term — *brahman* (the Absolute), *amṛta* (immortal nectar), *īśvara* ("Lord, God, the Supreme Ruler"; MW
s.v. *īśvara*: "master, lord … the Supreme Being, God"). Read theistically it would name God. The Jaina sense:
Ṛṣabha "reduced the root of his own faults to ashes without pity (*nirdaya-bhasmasāt*) by the fire of his own
meditative absorption (*sva-samādhi-tejasā*), expounded reality (*tattvam*) plainly to the world's seeker
(*jagate 'rthine*), and **became lord of the deathless station of *brahman*** (*brahma-padāmṛteśvaraḥ*)" — where
*brahma-pada* = the **liberated state**, not the Vedāntic Absolute, and *īśvara* = "master of [that state]," a
*liberated soul*, **not** a governing God. The appropriation is now total and deliberate.

**Vijay K. Jain (2015):** "Lord Ṛṣabha Deva destroyed ruthlessly, with the powerful fire of pure concentration,
all incursions of the inimical (**ghātiyā**) karmas in his soul, discoursed on the reality of substances for the
benefit of those seeking Truth, and finally got engrossed in the ambrosial bliss appertaining to the liberated
soul." — Jain glosses *brahma-pada-amṛta* as the "ambrosial bliss of the liberated soul," i.e. **mokṣa**, not
theistic Brahman.

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED):** N/A. But note this verse is the crux for the honest cross-tradition edge:
Jaina *brahma-pada* here `shares-vocabulary-with` Vedāntic [[brahman]] and is **NOT** equivalent to it (no
creator, no single Absolute; a plurality of liberated souls). See [[moksha]], [[siddha]].

**Links:** [[karma-prakriti]] / ghātiyā karmas · [[samadhi]] (if written) · [[moksha]] · [[brahman]]
(NOT-equivalent).

### v.5 — the finite verb (*punātu*) + a textual crux

**Devanāgarī (as printed in source 1):**
> स विश्व-चक्षुर्वष भोऽर्चितः सतां
> समग्र-विद्याऽऽत्म-वपुर्निरञ्जनः ।
> पुनातु चेतो मम नाभि-नन्दनो
> जिनोऽजित-दुल्लक-वादि-शासनः ॥ ५॥

**IAST (of the printed text):** *sa viśva-cakṣur **vaṣa bho** 'rcitaḥ satāṃ / samagra-vidyātma-vapur nirañjanaḥ |
punātu ceto mama nābhi-nandano / jino 'jita-**dullaka**-vādi-śāsanaḥ || 5 ||*

**⚠ Textual crux (edition note, not a recension fork):** the printed *vaṣa bho* is almost certainly a
transmission corruption of **vṛṣabho** (*vṛṣabhaḥ arcitaḥ satāṃ* = "the Bull [Ṛṣabha], worshipped by the good").
Jain's translation ("May Lord Ṛṣabha Deva … worshipped by the nobility") reflects *vṛṣabho 'rcitaḥ satām*, confirming
the reading. Likewise **dullaka** is obscure (possibly a Prakritism or corruption); Jain reads the compound
*jino 'jita-…-vādi-śāsanaḥ* as "the Jina whose teaching subjugates the unconquered [absolutist] disputants." Both
are flagged, **not silently emended** — the base text is a study-website file, not a critical edition with apparatus.

**Sense:** "May **that** Jina — the world's eye (*viśva-cakṣuḥ*), [the Bull,] worshipped by the good, whose very
body is entire knowledge (*samagra-vidyātma-vapuḥ*), spotless (*nirañjanaḥ*), the delighter of Nābhi
(*nābhi-nandanaḥ* = son of Nābhirāja), whose teaching [subdues] the disputants — **purify my mind** (*punātu ceto
mama*)."

**Vijay K. Jain (2015):** "May Lord Ṛṣabha Deva, the possessor of the power to see simultaneously the whole world,
worshipped by the nobility, having omniscience as the attribute of his soul which has been rid of all karmic dirt,
subjugator of the doctrine of the absolutists (**ekāntavādī**), and worthy son of Nābhirāja … purify my soul."
— Jain's "subjugator of the doctrine of the absolutists (ekāntavādī)" makes the anekānta polemic explicit already
in the first hymn.

**Links:** [[kevala-jnana]] (*samagra-vidyā* = omniscience) · [[anekantavada]] (the anti-*ekānta* thrust) ·
[[kevala-jnana]]. Modern comparison: N/A.

**§1 finding:** the opening hymn systematically renames a Tīrthaṅkara with the creator-god's titles — *svayambhū,
prajāpati, acyuta, brahma-pada, īśvara* — and every one is doctrinally reinterpreted as **self-effort and
liberation**, never divine creation. This is the stotra's signature move and its central interest for a
comparative-philosophy corpus: praise vocabulary borrowed wholesale from theism, semantics inverted.

## §24 — Śrī Mahāvīra jina-stuti (vv. 136–143)  — FULL DEPTH (the doctrinal close)

The final hymn is an extended **yamaka** *citra-kāvya*: each verse repeats a near-identical string of syllables
with different meanings (e.g. v.136 *…bhāsitayā / …bhā'sitayā / …śobhāsitayā*; v.137 *…-śāsana-vibhavaḥ* four times;
v.138 *syādvādaḥ / a-syādvādaḥ*). Samantabhadra ends the whole work with a phonetic-metrical bravura piece that
simultaneously **states the doctrine**. Two verses are treated at full depth; the rest are surveyed.

### v.138 — the *syādvāda* verse (the doctrinal core of the whole stotra)

**Devanāgarī:**
> अनवद्यः स्याद्वादस्तव
> दृष्टेष्टाविरोधतः स्याद्वादः ।
> इतरो न स्याद्वादो सद्वितय-
> विरोधान्मुनीश्वराऽस्याद्वादः ॥ १३८॥

**IAST:** *anavadyaḥ syādvādas tava / dṛṣṭeṣṭāvirodhataḥ syādvādaḥ | itaro na syādvādo sadvitaya- / virodhān
munīśvarā 'syādvādaḥ || 138 ||*

**Padaccheda — word-split & lexical range** (MW by headword):

| word | grammatical form | attested meaning-range (MW) | lexical source |
|------|------------------|------------------------------|----------------|
| anavadya | adj. | "faultless, blameless, irreproachable" | MW s.v. *anavadya* |
| syād-vāda | m. | "the Jaina doctrine of 'may be' / conditional assertion" (*syāt* "it may be", 3 sg. opt. √as + *vāda* "assertion, doctrine") | MW s.v. *syādvāda*, *vāda* |
| dṛṣṭa | ppp./n. | "seen, perceived; that which is perceived" → the **empirically perceived** | MW s.v. *dṛś* |
| iṣṭa | ppp./n. | "wished, desired; **approved, accepted, held authoritative**" (√iṣ) → the **[scripturally] accepted** | MW s.v. *iṣṭa* |
| avirodha | m. | "absence of contradiction, non-opposition, compatibility" | MW s.v. *avirodha* |
| itara | pron. adj. | "other, the other (of two)" → the rival (absolutist) doctrine | MW s.v. *itara* |
| sad-vitaya (sat-dvitaya) | n. | "the real/valid **pair**" (*dvitaya* "twofold, a pair") | MW s.v. *dvitaya* |
| virodha | m. | "opposition, contradiction, incompatibility" | MW s.v. *virodha* |
| munīśvara | m. voc. | "lord of sages" (*muni* + *īśvara*) — address to Mahāvīra | MW s.v. *muni*, *īśvara* |
| a-syād-vāda | m. | "**not** the doctrine qualified by *syāt*; non-syādvāda" | (privative *a-* + *syādvāda*) |

**Disambiguation (the crux — *dṛṣṭa-iṣṭa*):** literally, "your syādvāda is faultless **because of its
non-contradiction with the perceived (*dṛṣṭa*) and the accepted (*iṣṭa*)** (*dṛṣṭa-iṣṭa-avirodhataḥ*); the other's
[doctrine] is *not* syādvāda, because of its contradiction with **the valid pair** (*sad-vitaya-virodhāt*)."
Vijay K. Jain glosses *dṛṣṭa / iṣṭa* doctrinally as the **two pramāṇas** — *pratyakṣa* (direct) and *parokṣa*
(indirect) knowledge — and *sad-vitaya* as that same pair. The literal MW reading (perceived / authoritatively-
accepted) and Jain's pramāṇa gloss **converge**: *dṛṣṭa* ≈ perception/*pratyakṣa*, *iṣṭa* ≈ the accepted/āgama ≈
*parokṣa*. The point: the *syāt*-qualified doctrine contradicts neither what is perceived nor what is
authoritatively held; the *un*-qualified (absolutist, *ekānta*) doctrine contradicts both, and so fails.

**Assembled reading:** "Faultless is your **syādvāda**, O Lord of sages, since it does not conflict with the
perceived or the accepted; the other [doctrine] is **not** syādvāda — it contradicts the valid pair [of
knowledge-sources], and so is **a-syādvāda** [no true doctrine at all]."

**Vijay K. Jain (2015):** "O Supreme Sage! Being qualified by the word 'syāt' (meaning, conditional, from a
particular standpoint), your doctrine of conditional predications (syādvāda) is flawless as it is not opposed to
the two kinds of valid knowledge (pramāṇa) — direct (pratyakṣa) and indirect (parokṣa). The wisdom propounded by
others, not being qualified by the word 'syāt', is fallacious as it is opposed to both, the direct as well as the
indirect knowledge."

**Modern comparison (QUARANTINED — labelled, does NOT feed the disambiguation above):** verse 138 is the classic
magnet for the **"syādvāda = fuzzy logic / many-valued logic / quantum superposition"** equation. That equation is
`often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`: *syāt* is a **standpoint-relativizing operator** ("from a given respect")
asserting non-contradiction across viewpoints, **not** a truth-degree in [0,1] and **not** a physical
superposition. The reading-room's job is to keep the two apart; this project already maps that distinction at the
node level. See [[fuzzy-logic]], [[saptabhangi]], [[catuskoti]] — the parallel is structural at most, never an
identity.

**Links:** [[syadvada]] · [[anekantavada]] · [[saptabhangi]] · [[pramana]] · [[paroksa-jnana]] · [[fuzzy-logic]]
(NOT-equivalent).

### v.143 — the signature verse (*naya* + the *samantabhadra* pun)

**Devanāgarī:**
> बहुगुण-सम्पदसकलं
> परमतपि मधुर वचन-विन्यास-कलम् ।
> नय-भक्तयवतंस-कलं
> तव देव ! मतं समन्तभद्रं सकलम् ॥ १४३॥

**IAST:** *bahuguṇa-sampad asakalaṃ / paramatapi madhura vacana-vinyāsa-kalam | naya-bhaktayavataṃsa-kalaṃ / tava
deva! mataṃ **samantabhadraṃ** sakalam || 143 ||*

**Key gloss + śleṣa:** the last pāda, *tava deva! mataṃ samantabhadraṃ sakalam*, means "Your doctrine, O God, is
**samantabhadra** and complete (*sakalam*)" — where **samantabhadra** as an adjective = "auspicious/good on all
sides, all-round-excellent" (*samanta* "all-around" + *bhadra* "good, auspicious"; MW s.v. *samanta*, *bhadra*).
It is simultaneously the **author's own name** — the classic Sanskrit *bhaṇita*/signature device folded into a
pun. The contrast: rival doctrines (*para-matam*), "though sweet in the arrangement of words" (*madhura
vacana-vinyāsa-…*), are "**devoid of essential qualities**" (*asakalam* / lacking); the Jina's doctrine, crowned
by **naya** (*naya-bhakti…avataṃsa*), is all-encompassing and wholly laudable.

**Vijay K. Jain (2015):** "O Lord! The doctrines of the others, though pleasant in articulation and composition,
are void of essential qualities. But your doctrine which relies on **naya** (dealing, for the moment, only with a
particular point of view, not denying the existence of the remaining points of view …) and its classifications is
admirably all-encompassing, and entirely laudable."

**Links:** [[naya]] · [[anekantavada]] · [[syadvada]]. The verse closes the whole stotra by grounding devotion in
the *naya*/anekānta method — praise resolves into epistemology. Modern comparison: N/A.

### vv. 136–137, 139–142 — survey

- **v.136:** opening simile — "O Vīra, you shine on earth with glory born of your aggregate of virtues, as the
  moon (jasmine-bright) shines among the stars." Pure yamaka (*…bhāsitayā / bhā'sitayā / śobhāsitayā*).
- **v.137:** "Your teaching's majesty (*śāsana-vibhava*) triumphs even in this degenerate age (*kalāv api* = the
  *pañcama kāla*); accomplished sages, having invalidated the world's so-called deities, adore your doctrine."
  (Note the polemic against rival "deities" — of a piece with the §1 appropriation theme.)
- **v.139:** "Worshipped by gods and demons (*surāsura-mahitaḥ*) … supreme benefactor of the three worlds
  (*loka-traya-parama-hita*), radiant with the unobscured light [of omniscience]." — again a yamaka on
  *-mahitaḥ/-amahitaḥ/-hitaḥ*.
- **v.140:** the Jina's virtue-ornaments delight the assembly (*samavasaraṇa*); his splendour surpasses the moon.
  (References the eight *prātihārya*.)
- **v.141:** "free of pride and deceit (*gata-mada-māya*)… bestower of the wish for liberation (*kāmada*
  toward *mumukṣus*); you taught the vows and sense-restraint."
- **v.142:** the elephant-simile — as a rutting elephant streams temple-fluid and rends the mountainside, so you
  moved gloriously, "streaming" the doctrines of non-injury (*ahiṃsā*) and freedom from blemish. Links [[ahimsa]].

**§24 finding:** the closing hymn is where **devotion and doctrine fuse** — the yamaka virtuosity carries the two
most doctrinally loaded statements in the stotra (v.138 *syādvāda*, v.143 *naya* + the author-signature). For this
corpus, v.138 is a primary-source textual anchor for [[syadvada]] on par with *Tattvārtha* 5.32 (*arpitānarpita-
siddheḥ*) for [[anekantavada]].

## Reasoning across signals (§4)

- **Base text vs. translation are independent:** Devanāgarī/IAST from sanskritdocuments.org; English from Vijay K.
  Jain (2015). Neither derives from the other, so their agreement (e.g. on *svayambhū* = "self-enlightened",
  *brahma-pada* = liberated bliss, v.138 = syādvāda) is genuine cross-source convergence, not an echo.
- **Lexicon is the independent third leg:** every padaccheda gloss is from Monier-Williams *s.v.*, derived without
  reference to the translation. Where MW's primary sense (theistic proper names for *svayambhū*, *prajāpati*,
  *īśvara*) diverges from the doctrinal reading, that divergence is **the finding** — the disambiguation is done by
  the verse's own frame and the Jaina commentarial tradition Jain represents, exactly as §4 requires.
- **Weak independence acknowledged:** only one full scholarly English translation located → confidence **medium**,
  not high. A critical edition with apparatus (and a genuine second translation, e.g. an older SBJ rendering if one
  exists) would raise it; logged as an open item.
- **No commentary ṭīkā consulted directly:** unlike the Tattvārtha units (which had *Sarvārthasiddhi*), the
  disambiguation here leans on Jain's edition-notes rather than a fetched independent ṭīkā. Flagged.
