# Kalyāṇamandira Stotra — Verse 1

**Text:** Kalyāṇamandira Stotra ("Hymn of the Abode of Auspiciousness"), also called the *Pārśvanātha Stotra*, in
praise of Pārśvanātha, the 23rd Tīrthaṅkara. Traditionally paired with the Bhaktāmara Stotra as its sister-hymn:
both are in the Vasantatilakā metre (Kalyāṇamandira's closing verse 44 alone shifts to Āryā), both describe the
eight *prātihārya* (divine accompaniments of a Jina — Kalyāṇamandira v.19–26, Bhaktāmara v.28–35), and Vijay K.
Jain's 2024 edition explicitly juxtaposes the two hymns' *prātihārya* sections for the reader. **Unlike
Bhaktāmara, no Digambara/Śvetāmbara verse-count fork was found for this hymn** — both traditions recite the
same 44 verses (source: Vijay K. Jain 2024 edition, itself explicit that the hymn "is read with great devotion
... both Digambara and Śvetāmbara"; Wikipedia's *Kalyanmandirastotra* article, sourced independently, gives the
same 44-verse count and places it among the Śvetāmbara Navasmaraṇa). This is a genuine point of contrast with
Bhaktāmara worth recording, not an oversight.

**Author:** Attribution is **contested on identity framing, though the two named candidates converge on one
person** — worth stating carefully rather than smoothing over:
- Vijay K. Jain's 2024 edition titles the work "**Ācārya Kumudacandra's** Kalyāṇamandira Stotra" but states in
  its own introduction that Kumudacandra is "**also known as Ācārya Siddhasena Divākara**" — i.e. this source
  treats the two names as the *same individual*, not two rival authors.
- English Wikipedia's *Siddhasenadivākarasūri* article lists Kalyāṇamandira Stotra as one of his works under
  the name Siddhasena Divākara alone, with no mention of "Kumudacandra" or of any dispute — a silence, not an
  independent confirmation, since it simply doesn't engage the question.

**What both sources actually disagree on is the date, not the person:**

| reading | who holds it (source) | what it commits you to |
|---|---|---|
| c. 6th century CE | Muni Ajitasāgara, cited in Vijay K. Jain (2024) intro | Places the author close to Mānatuṅga (Bhaktāmara), consistent with the two hymns' formal/thematic pairing |
| 12th century Vikrama Saṃvat (≈ 11th–12th c. CE) | Dr. Darbarilal Kothia, cited in Vijay K. Jain (2024) intro — argued from a recorded debate between "Kumudacandra" and the 12th-century-VS logician Vādideva Sūri | A far later date, centuries after Mānatuṅga; would make the Bhaktāmara pairing a later literary/liturgical convention rather than near-contemporary composition |
| "4th or 5th century CE" (i.e., Gupta-period) | English Wikipedia, *Siddhasenadivākarasūri* article (undated single figure, no dispute noted) | The earliest of the three estimates; taken from the same broader Siddhasena-corpus dating (*Sanmati-tarka*, *Nyāyāvatāra*) as his logical works, not from Kalyāṇamandira-specific evidence |

Vijay K. Jain's own text flags this as unresolved ("Authors and researchers are not unanimous as regards the
time of Ācārya Kumudacandra"). **Status: contested — treat any single date asserted elsewhere as unsourced.**

## Kalyāṇamandira v.1 — both recensions

**Recension status:** Both recensions identical for this verse (no fork found for this hymn at all — see note
above).

**Text — Both recensions (IAST):**
> kalyāṇamandiram udāram avadya-bhedi
> bhītābhaya-pradam anindita-m-aṅghri-padmam
> saṃsāra-sāgara-nimajjad-aśeṣa-jantu-
> potāyamānam abhinamya jineśvarasya

**Devanāgarī** (bhaktamar.in, cross-checked against Jain Square's partial quote):
> कल्याणमन्दिरमुदारमवद्यभेदि भीताभयप्रदमनिन्दितमङ्घ्रिपद्मम् ।
> संसारसागरनिमज्जदशेषजन्तु-पोतायमानमभिनम्य जिनेश्वरस्य ॥ १ ॥

**Meter:** Vasantatilakā — the same 14-syllable-per-pāda metre used throughout Bhaktāmara's core verses and
throughout this hymn except its closing verse 44 (Āryā). Per Vijay K. Jain (2024) intro: "each with fourteen
letters... the entire verse... has fifty-six letters."

**Disambiguation (from internal consistency and grammar — devotional-verse equivalent of the bhāṣya check):**

1. **The whole verse is one long compound sentence built on *abhinamya* ("having bowed") as its gerund, with
   *jineśvarasya* (genitive, "of the Lord of Jinas") as its object** — grammatically identical in shape to
   Bhaktāmara v.1's own "*praṇamya jina-pāda-yugam*" opening. Both hymns open with a bowing-gerund clause loaded
   with descriptive compounds before naming what is bowed to; this is a shared opening convention of the genre,
   not a coincidence specific to either hymn.
2. **Why *pota* means "boat," not "young animal/plant."** MW gives *pota* two live senses. The governing
   compound is *saṃsāra-sāgara-nimajjad-aśeṣa-jantu-potāyamānam* — "[the feet] that function-as-a-*pota* for
   all creatures sinking in the ocean of saṃsāra." A boat, not a young animal, is what keeps sinking creatures
   afloat in an ocean; the surrounding *sāgara* (ocean) and *nimajjat* (sinking) vocabulary fixes the "vessel"
   sense unambiguously. Both translations compared below render it as "boat"/"life-boat," confirming this is not
   contested among translators — it is a clean case of context resolving a genuinely two-way lexical entry,
   parallel to the *dyut*/"illuminates" pattern noted in the Bhaktāmara file, but here the ambiguity is
   resolved by the sentence's own imagery rather than by a bhāṣya.
3. **-āyamāna is grammar, not lexicon, and that is exactly what makes the boat-image work.** The denominative
   *kyaṅ*-suffix ("acts/behaves as X") is what lets the poet say the Jina's feet *function as* a boat rather
   than flatly *are* one — a live metaphor grammatically marked as a metaphor. This is the same "morphology
   supplies what the dictionary entry alone doesn't" pattern flagged repeatedly in the TS units (e.g. TS 5.1's
   *-kāya*), just from grammar rather than a commentarial gloss, since this stotra carries no bhāṣya tradition.
4. **anindita-m-aṅghri-padmam**: the *-m-* is euphonic glide (Sanskrit *yaṇ/anunāsika* sandhi joining a
   vowel-final word to a vowel-initial one), not a separate morpheme — flagged only so it is not mistaken for a
   dropped word.

**Assembled reading:** Having bowed to the lotus-feet of the Lord of Jinas [Pārśvanātha] — feet that are an
abode of auspiciousness, exalted, splitters of all fault/vice, bestowers of fearlessness on the frightened,
irreproachable, and that function as a boat for every creature, without exception, sinking in the ocean of
worldly existence...
*(Like Bhaktāmara v.1, this is grammatically one dependent clause; the main clause — the poet's declared,
self-deprecating intent to compose this praise despite Bṛhaspati himself being unequal to the task — arrives in
verse 2, per Vijay K. Jain's introduction, which quotes verse 2's content in the same breath as verse 1's. Not
resolved within this single-verse prototype unit.)*

**Published translations (comparison only):**
- Vijay K. Jain (2024): "After bowing to the Lotus Feet of Lord Jinendra who is a temple of propitiousness,
  benevolent, destroyer of all evils, bestower of fearlessness to the frightened, laudable, and like a life-boat
  for all living-beings getting submerged in the ocean of worldly-existence—" (continues directly into v.2 in
  the source, matching the same across-verse grammatical continuation noted for Bhaktāmara v.1/v.2).
- Jain Square: "Having bowed to the lotus feet of that Jinesvara (Tirthankara, Lord Parsvanatha), the Feet which
  embody bliss and safety for the terrified, they are a life-boat for all beings sinking in the ocean of
  existence." Independent wording from Vijay K. Jain's (e.g. "temple of propitiousness" vs. no equivalent
  rendering of *kalyāṇamandira* as a compound at all in this shorter paraphrase) — confirms two genuinely
  separate renderings rather than one copied source, though it also shows Jain Square compresses/paraphrases
  rather than translating every compound.
- Both agree on the two load-bearing images: fearlessness-for-the-frightened and boat-for-the-sinking. Neither
  disagrees on sense; the divergence is one of completeness/register, not of reading.
