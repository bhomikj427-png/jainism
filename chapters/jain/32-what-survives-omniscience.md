## How to use this chapter

Ch 26 built the Jain karma machinery and then, in §6.4, named its own hole:

> "TS 8.1 says that in advanced ascetics **only *yoga* remains** as a cause of bondage… So influx does not stop when passion does: a kevalin still acts, and activity still attracts. What that residual influx amounts to, and how it is discharged, is *aghāti*-karma territory… **the machinery described here is the *ghāti* half of the machine.**"

This is the other half. It answers a question that any karma-as-matter theory must face, and that Jainism answers with unusual mechanical precision:

> **If activity itself draws karma in, how can an omniscient being who still walks, speaks and teaches avoid re-accumulating what it has just destroyed?**

## 1. The four non-destructive karmas {#four}

TS 8.4 (Ch 26 §5) divides the eight *mūla-prakṛti*s into two families. The ***ghāti*** ("destructive") four obscure the soul's intrinsic powers. The ***aghāti*** ("non-destructive") four do not:

> the aghātiyā karmas "**do not obscure the real attributes of the soul, but affect only the external environments of the soul and pertain to the body**," and they are "responsible for the reborn soul's physical and mental circumstances, longevity, spiritual potential and experience of pleasant and unpleasant sensations."

| karma | what it fixes | sub-types | sūtra |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***vedanīya*** — feeling-producing | pleasure and pain as **sensation** | **2** — *sāta*, which "causes a feeling of pleasure, created, e.g. by licking something sweet," and *asāta*, which "causes the feeling of pain, created, e.g. if one is hurt by a sword" | TS 8.8 ⚠️ *not fetched* |
| ***āyuḥ*** — life-determining | the length of this life and the realm it is lived in | **4** — *naraka*, *tiryañc* (plant and animal), *manuṣya*, *deva* | **TS 8.10**, fetched: *nārakatairyagyonamānuṣadaivāni* |
| ***nāma*** — physique-making | body, form, sense-class, gait, symmetry, colour — **and Tīrthaṅkarahood** | **42** in the sūtra; **93** in the systematised list (§2) | **TS 8.11**, fetched |
| ***gotra*** — status-determining | the family one is born into | **2** — **TS 8.12**, fetched: *uccairnīcaiśca* |

Pūjyapāda glosses *gotra* concretely: high status is birth "in a high or noble family of great respectability and prestige," low status "in a low-status family, lacking in prestige and respectability," the distinction reflecting family traditions of noble against vicious conduct. And on *āyuḥ* he notes that "**the life-duration (*āyuḥ-prāṇa*) holds the most important place**" among the vitalities, as "the primary cause of life."

**These four are what a body is.** Not metaphorically — in a system where karma is fine matter (Ch 26 §1), your lifespan, your physique, your social station and the pleasantness of your sensations are four species of particle currently adhering to you.

## 2. Forty-two against ninety-three {#counts}

### 2.1 The discrepancy

The standard modern statement of Jain karma theory gives **148 *uttara-prakṛti*s** (sub-types):

> 5 *jñānāvaraṇa* + 9 *darśanāvaraṇa* + 2 *vedanīya* + 28 *mohanīya* + 4 *āyuḥ* + **93 *nāma*** + 2 *gotra* + 5 *antarāya* = **148**

But **TS 8.11, fetched directly, enumerates 42** for *nāma*:

| group | count | what |
|---|---|---|
| primary subdivisions | **21** | *gati, jāti, śarīra, aṅgopāṅga, nirmāṇa, bandhana, saṃghāta, saṃsthāna, saṃhanana, sparśa, rasa, gandha, varṇa, ānupūrvya, agurulaghu, upaghāta, paraghāta, ātapa, udyota, ucchvāsa, vihāyogati* |
| ten paired opposites (*setara*, "and their opposites") | **20** | *pratyeka-śarīra · trasa · subhaga · susvara · śubha · sūkṣma · paryāpti · sthira · ādeya · yaśaḥkīrti* — each with its opposite |
| and one more | **1** | ***tīrthakaratva*** |

### 2.2 Where the gap is

Every other count the corpus has checked against the sūtra **matches** the systematised list: *vedanīya* 2, *āyuḥ* 4, *gotra* 2, *jñānāvaraṇa* 5 (TS 8.6), and *mohanīya* **28** (TS 8.9, fetched for Ch 26).

> **The entire 148-vs-sūtra gap is inside *nāma*, and it is exactly 93 − 42 = 51.**

The corpus does **not** call one figure right. **42** is what the root sūtra as translated enumerates. **93** is what the later *karma-grantha* systematisation reaches by expanding the compound categories — *gati* into its four, *jāti* into its five, *śarīra* into its five, the colours into five, the tastes into five, and so on.

### 2.3 Why it matters

Because the numbers are quoted constantly, and almost always without their provenance:

> **The figures most often given for Jain karma theory are the commentarial tradition's, not the root text's.**

That is a §0 point of the plainest kind. It says nothing against the 148-scheme — expanding a compound category into its members is ordinary scholastic work and may well be what the sūtra intends. It says only that when someone writes "the Tattvārtha Sūtra enumerates 148 sub-types of karma," the sentence is **not true as stated**, and the difference is one specific karma and one specific number.

This finding was only available because three sūtras were **fetched directly** rather than taken from a summary. It is the clearest demonstration in the corpus of why the charter's §4 signal 2 — the commentarial anchor, *mūla* plus *bhāṣya* — is not a formality.

## 3. Why the aghāti family is the interesting one {#interesting}

Destroying the four *ghāti* karmas produces **omniscience**, not liberation. The four *aghāti* karmas survive that event and keep the body running. Hence the two-tier holy-being scheme Ch 10 records:

| | what has happened | what remains |
|---|---|---|
| **arihant** (*sayoga-kevalin*) | all four *ghāti* karmas destroyed; ***kevala-jñāna*** has dawned | *vedanīya*, *āyuḥ*, *nāma*, *gotra* — so still a body, a lifespan, sensations, a social identity. **And still teaching** |
| **siddha** | the *aghāti* karmas exhausted at death — "as soon as the aghātiyā karmas are exhausted" | nothing |

So:

> **The aghāti karmas are precisely the difference between the omniscient teacher and the liberated soul** — the residue that keeps an enlightened being in the world long enough to be of use to it.

A tradition without this category would have to make omniscience and liberation **the same event**, and would then have no account of why a Tīrthaṅkara preaches at all. The category is doing real structural work, and it is why the Jain scheme has an *arihant* as well as a *siddha* where other traditions have one terminal state.

### 3.1 And they cannot be attacked by knowledge

The *ghāti* karmas fall to insight. The *aghāti* karmas **fall only when they run out**. That single asymmetry explains the shape of the top of the guṇasthāna ladder (Ch 03):

> the last stages are not about **seeing more clearly** but about **stopping activity**.

Which is why the residual influx of an omniscient being needs a doctrine of its own — §5.

## 4. The tradition's highest office is a species of bondage {#tirthankara}

Item 42 of TS 8.11 is ***tīrthakaratva***. Being a ford-maker "procures the position of a ford-maker," and it sits **inside the body-making karma**, one item among forty-two.

Read that plainly. **Tīrthaṅkarahood is technically a karma** — favourable, still *pudgala*, still to be shed.

This is a sharper claim than the familiar "even good karma binds," and worth separating from it:

| claim | what it says |
|---|---|
| "even merit binds" | *puṇya* is auspicious karma and still karma; golden fetters are fetters. A **moral** point, and a common one |
| *tīrthakaratva* is item 42 of *nāma*-karma | the tradition locates **its own supreme figure** inside the machinery it is trying to dismantle. A **structural** point, and much rarer |

There is no embarrassment in it. In a system where karma is matter adhering by activity and passion, *everything* about an embodied being is karmically constituted, including the office of being the being who teaches the way out. A Tīrthaṅkara is not exempt from the physics; a Tīrthaṅkara is what the physics produces when the *ghāti* karmas are gone and this particular *nāma* sub-type is in play.

Compare Ch 10 on *puṇya* and Ch 03 on the four bondage-aspects. And compare Ch 27's treatment of Buddhist omniscience, where a founder's cognitive perfection is argued for as an **epistemological** result — the Jain move is the reverse in an instructive way: the founder's *office* is derived from the same material mechanism as everything else.

## 5. Īryāpathika: influx that does not bind {#iryapathika}

### 5.1 The sūtra

**TS 6.4**, fetched with Pūjyapāda's *Sarvārthasiddhi*:

> ***sakaṣāyākaṣāyayoḥ sāmparāyikaryāpathayoḥ***
> "Activity with-passions and without-passions causes influx of karmas, those that extend transmigration — *sāmparāyika* — and those that **shorten** transmigration — *īryāpatha* — respectively."

And Pūjyapāda localises both on the ladder:

> "Influx of *sāmparāyika* karmas takes place in case of persons with-passions and who are wrong-believers… **from the first stage up to the tenth stage.**"
> "Influx of the *īryāpatha* karmas takes place in case of saints free from passions, **from the eleventh stage till the thirteenth stage**. The *īryāpatha* karmas **do not have the power to bind with the soul**."

### 5.2 The mechanism, in four rows

Ch 03 gives *āsrava* two independent factors. Separate them, and the whole doctrine falls out:

| guṇasthāna | *yoga* (activity of mind, speech, body) | *kaṣāya* (passion) | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1–10** | present | present | ***sāmparāyika*** — genuine bondage; saṃsāra extended |
| **11–13** | present | **absent** | ***īryāpathika*** — influx occurs and **does not stick** |
| **14** (*ayoga-kevalin*) | **absent** | absent | **no influx at all** |

> **Yoga opens the channel; kaṣāya is the adhesive.** Remove the adhesive and matter still enters but slides off.

Ch 03's `asrava.md` material already stated that mechanism — but from **search aggregation only**, with the file itself noting that the two-kind distinction was "not fetched from a critical edition commentary directly." TS 6.4 is now fetched. **The mechanism has moved from commentarial report to text-grounded claim**, and this chapter exists partly to record that upgrade.

### 5.3 What actually gets bound, and for how long

The technical description is startlingly specific. The karma bound in *īryāpathika* influx is **a single species — *sāta-vedanīya***, the pleasure-producing *aghāti* karma from §1 — and it is

> "**bound in one instant and experienced in the next**,"

with **none of the duration-bondage (*sthiti-bandha*)** that mundane souls incur.

So this is not "less bondage." It is a **different kind of event**: an influx with *prakṛti* and *pradeśa* aspects (Ch 03's four bondage-aspects) but effectively **no duration and no intensity** — which is why the sūtra can say it *shortens* transmigration rather than extending it.

That the residue is *sāta*- and not *asāta*-vedanīya is itself loaded: **a passionless being's remaining karmic traffic is, by construction, the pleasant kind.**

⚠️ **Provenance flag.** The *sāta-vedanīya* identification and the one-instant structure are the most interesting claims in this section and rest on the **weakest** source-set here — an academic paper read at abstract level plus a tradition-internal site. TS 6.4 gives the doctrine; it does not give this detail. Flagged in place.

## 6. Stage eleven, and what *akaṣāya* means {#eleven}

There is an awkwardness in §5.1 worth pressing rather than smoothing.

Guṇasthāna **11** is *upaśānta-moha*, where the passions are **suppressed, not destroyed** — the sources are explicit that this is "only suppression or subsidence (**and not annihilation**) of the Karma," and it is the stage from which a soul can **fall back**. Yet TS 6.4's commentary already counts influx there as *īryāpathika*.

That fixes what *akaṣāya* means in the sūtra:

> **not "has eradicated passion" but "is not currently acting out of passion."**

The criterion is **operational**, not biographical. And that reading is also what makes stage 11's reversibility intelligible: the suppressed passion can resurface, and the influx immediately becomes *sāmparāyika* again. A biographical criterion could not accommodate a soul that has the property and then loses it.

### 6.1 Why the doctrine is structurally necessary

Without *īryāpathika* influx, Jainism faces a dilemma with no good horn:

- **If activity always binds**, the *sayoga-kevalin* rebinds karma with every sermon, and **omniscience is self-undermining** — the teaching that liberates others re-enslaves the teacher.
- **If the omniscient being does not act at all**, there is **no Tīrthaṅkara preaching**, and the whole institution of the *tīrtha* — the ford, the community — has no cause.

The doctrine buys both: the arihant acts, teaches, and accumulates nothing that lasts. The *aghāti* karmas already in place run their course; nothing new adheres. At stage 14 — the *ayoga-kevalin* stage, whose duration is fixed at **the time taken to utter five short syllables** — activity itself ceases, the remaining *aghāti* karmas are "simultaneously exhausted," and the soul becomes a **siddha**.

### 6.2 The un-mystical answer

Notice what the tradition does **not** say. It does not say the enlightened being is *beyond* karma, or above the law, or in a state where the ordinary rules are suspended. It says the enlightened being

> **fails to satisfy one of the two necessary conditions for adhesion.**

That is a **mechanical answer to a soteriological problem**, and it is characteristic of Jainism throughout. The system's most exalted claims are cashed out in the same physics as its most ordinary ones — which is exactly what Ch 26 found for karma generally, and what Ch 01 found for *pudgala*.

## 7. The same problem, answered differently {#comparison}

Every tradition with a liberated-but-still-embodied figure faces one question: **why does the body persist, and why does what it does not re-entangle it?** Two answers, and the contrast is exact:

| | Jainism | Advaita |
|---|---|---|
| the state | *sayoga-kevalin* — omniscient, still karmically embodied, awaiting stage 14 | ***jīvanmukta*** — already liberated, with a body running down |
| the mechanism | ***īryāpathika*** — the second condition for adhesion (passion) is absent, so new karma **does not stick** | ***prārabdha*** — karma already released, exhausting itself like a potter's wheel after the hand is lifted |
| what it denies | **new adhesion** | **new relevance** |
| the residue | four *aghāti* karmas, materially present, to be exhausted | a body whose reality was never the issue |

> **Jainism denies new adhesion. Advaita denies new relevance.**

The corpus stores this as **`structurally-parallel-to` *and* `often-conflated-with-NOT-equivalent`** — the sanctioned two-type pattern — because the parallel is real and the states are not the same. **The kevalin is still karmically embodied and has stage 14 ahead of it; the jīvanmukta is already free and the body is a leftover.** One is a being in the last phase of a process; the other is a being for whom the process was never quite what it appeared to be.

That difference is not incidental. It follows directly from what each tradition thinks the body **is** — real matter adhering to a real soul, or an appearance whose status was the question all along.
