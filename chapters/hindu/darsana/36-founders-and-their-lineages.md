# Chapter 36 — Founders and the Lineages That Made Them

> **Primary concepts:** `nāthamuni` · `yāmunācārya` · `rāmānuja` · `madhva` · `śrīkaṇṭha` · `śivādvaita`
> **Folder:** `hindu/darsana/` — with [Ch 11 (The Vedānta Family)](11-vedanta.md), [Ch 25 (Who Wrote the Systems)](25-commentators-and-authors.md) and [Ch 30 (Where Does Ignorance Live?)](30-locus-of-ignorance.md).
> **Reads after:** [Ch 11](11-vedanta.md), which set out the three great Vedāntas as doctrines. This chapter is about the **people** the doctrines are named after — and about the fact that, for more than forty batches, this corpus held **schools whose founders had no node.**

---

## How to use this chapter

Ch 11 described Advaita, Viśiṣṭādvaita and Dvaita as positions: what each says about Brahman, selves, matter and liberation. Those descriptions were accurate and they have not changed. What was missing was stranger than a gap in the doctrine.

**`vishishtadvaita.md` stood for over forty batches as a school with no node for Rāmānuja. `dvaita-vedanta.md` stood the same way for Madhva.** Both men were named across fifteen or more concept files. Neither existed as a node. Batch 45 wrote them, and writing them produced a result that could not have been seen from the doctrine side:

> **On the philosophy, the sources converge completely. On the men, they do not converge at all.**

That asymmetry is this chapter's subject. It is not a complaint about bad record-keeping. It is a fact about **what a lineage is for** — and the lineages in this chapter turn out to be doing something quite specific with their founders, consistently enough that it can be stated as a pattern and tested.

Four things to carry out:

1. **The 120-year problem** — and why it is arithmetic rather than testimony.
2. **Succession without teaching** — the Śrī Vaiṣṇava chain's central link is a *posthumous designation*, and that is structural, not decorative.
3. **The distinction between inventing a man, inventing a text, and inventing a *Vedānta*** — the most useful analytical tool in the chapter, and one this corpus had to build in order to *decline* an exciting claim.
4. **What a founder's node is actually for**, once the doctrine node already exists.

## 1. Two lineages, four founders, and one school with no founder at all {#cast}

| # | who | lineage | what the tradition credits him with |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Nāthamuni** | Śrī Vaiṣṇava — first of the *munitraya* | compiling the **Nālāyira Divya Prabandham**; the lost *Nyāyatattva* |
| 2 | **Yāmunācārya** (*Āḷavandār*) | Śrī Vaiṣṇava — second; Nāthamuni's grandson | the earliest **surviving** Śrī Vaiṣṇava philosophy: *Āgamaprāmāṇya*, *Siddhitraya*, *Gītārthasaṅgraha* |
| 3 | **Rāmānuja** | Śrī Vaiṣṇava — third | systematising **[Viśiṣṭādvaita](11-vedanta.md)**; the *Śrī Bhāṣya* |
| 4 | **Madhva** | Dvaita / Tattvavāda | founding **[Dvaita](11-vedanta.md)**; ~37 works |
| 5 | **Śrīkaṇṭha** | Śaiva Vedānta | the *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya* — Śiva as Brahman |
| — | **Śivādvaita** | *the school* | …constituted, four centuries later, by **[Appayya Dīkṣita](../../cross-tradition/34-the-commentator.md)** |

The last row is not a mistake. Śivādvaita is the case where the school's relation to its founder is fully exposed, and §6 treats it as the chapter's limiting case.

## 2. The 120-year problem {#lifespans}

Start with a piece of arithmetic, because it is the cleanest result in the chapter and it needed no new sources — only three nodes written in one batch and then laid side by side.

| ācārya | conventional dates | span |
|---|---|---|
| **Nāthamuni** | 823–951 *(alternative: 582–922)* | **128 years** *(340)* |
| **Yāmuna** | 918–1038 *(also 917–1042)* | **120** *(125)* |
| **Rāmānuja** | 1017–1137 | **120 years** |

**Three consecutive ācāryas of one lineage, each assigned a lifespan no human reaches.** Wikipedia itself calls Nāthamuni's alternative dating implausible at "400+ years."

One such figure is a hagiographic flourish. Two is a coincidence. **Three in a row, in a single succession, is a convention** — 120 years being the conventional ideal full human span. The *munitraya*'s dates are a system of stitching, not a record.

### 2.1 What survives the observation {#survives}

This is where it would be easy to overreach, so the boundary is worth drawing precisely.

**What does not survive:** any endpoint. Not 1017, not 1137, not 918.

**What does survive:** the **9th–12th century placement** and, more importantly, the **order** — Nāthamuni before Yāmuna before Rāmānuja. Both are constrained by evidence independent of the lifespans: the citation record (Yāmuna quotes Nāthamuni; Rāmānuja and later authors quote both) and, for Rāmānuja, external material.

That is exactly the distinction [Ch 35 §4.1](../shastra/35-dating-a-literature-without-dates.md#inverse) draws for the Āyurvedic commentators: **citation evidence is ordinal, not cardinal.** It fixes a sequence. Dates enter where the sequence touches something externally anchored. The *munitraya* is the same structure with a hagiographic numbering laid over the top — and the numbering can be stripped off without disturbing the sequence underneath.

### 2.2 And it strengthens a verdict already on record {#sydnor}

`ramanuja.md` records **Jon Paul Sydnor**'s judgement that reconstructing Rāmānuja's "historical life" is "nearly impossible," and that **John B. Carman**'s revised dating of c. 1077–1157 — built from "11th- and 12th-century temple records and regional literature *outside* the Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition" — though "well-reasoned," is speculative and "**ultimately unverifiable**."

Sydnor argues that about one man. The arithmetic above extends it to the lineage: **a tradition that hands out 120-year lifespans to three ācāryas running is not a tradition keeping records.** The corpus reached that independently, from three nodes, and it is the strongest support Sydnor's position gets here.

⚠ Note carefully what is *not* being said. The claim is not that the Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition lied, nor that these men did not exist, nor that Carman's method is bad. It is narrower: **the precision of these dates is not evidential.** A source can be entirely reliable about who taught whom and entirely conventional about how long they lived, because those are different kinds of statement doing different jobs.

## 3. Succession without teaching {#succession}

Now the second structural feature, and it is peculiar enough that it took two nodes to see.

**Rāmānuja and Yāmuna never met.** Rāmānuja reached Śrīraṅgam to find him dead. This is not a modern scholarly deflation — it is what **the Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition itself says**, and the hagiographies supply the mechanism: either three curled fingers uncurling, or the corpse rising to name its heir.

So **the central link of the chain transmits authority without content.**

### 3.1 What Wikipedia says, against what Wikipedia says {#wiki}

`yamunacarya.md` maps three incompatible descriptions of the relation:

| reading | source |
|---|---|
| Yāmuna "became **preceptor** to Rāmānuja" | Wikipedia |
| Rāmānuja's **guru's guru** | a web aggregator |
| **Neither — they never met**; the designation was posthumous | IEP (Ranganathan); *Encyclopedia of Religion* (Carman); the tradition |

The third is best attested. The first two are **not different evidence** — they are the ordinary compression by which "his successor in the lineage" becomes "his student." And the compression is invisible once made, which is why it is worth logging.

Note that Wikipedia's own article undercuts its summary line. It reports that before dying Yāmuna expressed **three unfulfilled wishes** — among them that commentaries be composed on the major texts — and that Raṅganātha instructed him to send for Rāmānuja from Kāñcīpuram. **A dying man's programme, handed on.** On that account the *Śrī Bhāṣya* discharges a wish, not a course of study.

### 3.2 Where the teaching actually came from {#teachers}

Rāmānuja's instruction came from elsewhere entirely: **Yādavaprakāśa**, whom he broke with; **Mahāpūrṇa**, for six months; and the **śūdra devotee Kāñcīpūrṇa** as spiritual mentor.

So the tradition's own account gives us a founder whose formal authority descends from a man he never met, and whose actual formation came from three people the lineage does not name itself after.

> **What a *paramparā* transmits is legitimacy, and legitimacy does not require contact.** That is not a defect in the institution — it is what the institution is *for*. A succession list answers "by what right does this man speak?", not "who taught him?" Reading it as an educational record is the error.

## 4. What a founder's node is for {#founder-node}

With the doctrine already written in Ch 11, what does a node on the *man* add? Rāmānuja's answers it in one line: **it holds the disputes the doctrine node has no room for.** Three of them, of three different kinds.

**(a) Dates** — §2 above. Traditional 1017–1137, Carman's c. 1077–1157, Sydnor's "neither is establishable."

**(b) Works — and this one has doctrinal consequences.** Nine works are traditionally attributed. The sources disagree about which survive scrutiny:

| reading | who | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **three** are secure — *Śrī Bhāṣya*, *Vedārthasaṅgraha*, *Bhagavadgītā-bhāṣya*; everything else doubted, **including the Gadya Traya** | IEP (Ranganathan) | the devotional prose-hymns are **not the philosopher's** |
| **four** — those three **plus** the Gadya Traya | Wikipedia, citing Sydnor | the surrender-theology has a first-person authorial voice |

That is not bibliographic housekeeping. Ch 11 and `vishishtadvaita.md` present **prapatti**, total self-surrender, as one of the two soteriological means of the school. On the IEP's reading, **the first-person devotional voice behind prapatti is not Rāmānuja's** — the doctrine would rest on the commentaries alone. `vishishtadvaita.md` has been qualified accordingly.

**(c) Whether the biography is history.** Sydnor's position, §2.2.

### 4.1 A caution that generalises {#carman-caution}

One item from `ramanuja.md` deserves lifting out, because it is a reading error anyone could make and this corpus nearly did.

The revised 1077–1157 dating is **Carman's**. Yet the *Encyclopedia of Religion* article on Rāmānuja **signed by Carman** prints **1017–1137** — the traditional dates.

Whether that is editorial house-style, an artefact of the entry's date, or Carman distinguishing "the tradition's dates" from his own reconstruction is **not established**. So the corpus takes only the safe form of the lesson:

> **A scholar's name attached to a date in a reference work is not evidence that he argued for that date.**

That belongs beside §6.2's larger version of the same error — reading a *title* as a thesis.

## 5. Madhva: when a charge looks like a finding {#madhva}

Madhva's node splits the same way — the doctrine converges, the man does not — but his second dispute is of a different order, and it is the best methodological specimen in the chapter.

**On dates:** four reference works, four answers. Karl H. Potter (*Encyclopedia of Religion*) 1238–1317; *World Religions Reference Library* c. 1199–1276; *Encyclopedia of World Biography* c. 1197–1276; and **John Bowker**, honestly, "dates uncertain, ranging from 1199–1278 to 1238–1317." B. N. K. Sharma himself published the earlier bracket in 1962 and the later in 2000. `dvaita-vedanta.md` printed 1238–1317 flat, and has been qualified.

**On his sources — the real dispute.** Madhva quotes, as scriptural authority, roughly forty texts — *Paiṅgi-śruti*, *Bhāllaveya-śruti*, *Sauparṇa-śruti*, *Vātsa-śruti*, *Brahma-Tarka*, *Parama-śruti* — for which no surviving manuscript contains the quoted passage.

| reading | who |
|---|---|
| **Madhva wrote them himself** — the titles are "'work-titles', which Madhva employs as a loop to hang up his literary compositions," done sincerely, being "firmly convinced that he was acting on the command of Viṣṇu" | **Roque Mesquita**, Vienna, 1997 / Eng. 2000 |
| **The sources are rare, not fictitious**; Mesquita's "research is monumentally incomplete" and he is "genuinely unfamiliar with the spread of the Vedic corpora" | **Shrisha Rao & B. N. K. Sharma**, *Asiatische Studien* LVII/1 (2003) |

### 5.1 One charge, propagated twice {#one-charge}

Here is the finding, and it is not about who is right.

Read casually, the literature shows **a 16th-century polemicist and a modern Viennese indologist independently reaching the same verdict** — apparent convergence, and strong. Read with provenance, it is **one list, propagated twice**:

> **Appayya Dīkṣita (16th c.)**, in the *Madhvatantramukhamardana*, compiles the list of Madhva's suspect citations — "on his own, with no reference to previous views," and as the **first** clear accuser, three centuries after Madhva.
> → **Mesquita (1997)** "cites with apparent approval the list of unfamiliar sources … objected to by Appayyadīkṣita, and proceeds to assume **without verification** that all of them … must indeed be unknown."
> → **Wikipedia** reports Mesquita's conclusion as modern philology *and*, in a separate paragraph, Appayya's accusation as historical precedent.

Rao and Sharma's sharpest objection is exactly this — that Mesquita is "putting too much faith in a medieval traditionalist's claims, a rather odd thing for a modern scholar to do," and that he **fails even to mention** the Mādhva replies to Appayya by **Vijayīndra Tīrtha** and **Nārāyaṇācārya**.

**Had `madhva.md` been written from the encyclopedias alone, it would have recorded convergence and been wrong about the evidence.** That is [§4 of the charter](../../../CLAUDE.md) in one case: *many sources are reworkings of one earlier source; copies are not confirmation.*

### 5.2 What the corpus does and does not conclude {#madhva-verdict}

Rao and Sharma answer by the right method — hunting **independent pre-Madhva attestation** for each allegedly fictitious title. For *Paiṅgi* alone they produce eight, including Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya* (showing the *Kalpasūtra* was available to *him*), four manuscripts in Oppert's 1885 catalogue, and a *Paiṅgi-śruti* quoted by **Śaṅkara**. Two arguments from silence tighten it: **nobody accused Madhva for three centuries**; and the charge is **absent from the *Nyāyāmṛta*–*Advaitasiddhi* debate**, where [Vyāsatīrtha](../../cross-tradition/37-the-dialecticians.md) *quotes* the disputed sources as authorities and Madhusūdana, "who did not even refrain from name-calling," never alleges forgery.

⚠ **The corpus does not conclude that Mesquita is refuted.** Rao and Sharma are Dvaita scholars writing in defence; their evidence is avowedly circumstantial; and they establish that a *recension of that name existed*, not that *Madhva's quoted words are in it*.

What is established is narrower and firmer: **the charge has one 16th-century polemical origin, not two independent ones, and the two learned Advaitins best placed to press it did not.**

## 6. Śivādvaita: a school founded by moving one text across an axis {#sivadvaita}

Now the limiting case, and the reason the table in §1 has a row with no founder.

**Śivādvaita** identifies Brahman with **Śiva** and takes **Śrīkaṇṭha**'s *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya* as its root commentary. Its constitution as an *independent school* is the work of **Appayya Dīkṣita** in the 16th century — who won from a 1580 inscription the title *śaivādvaitaikasāmrājya*, "sole emperor of Śaiva Advaita."

**Duquette states the mechanism exactly**, and it is not a vague "reinterpretation":

> Appayya's interpretation of Śrīkaṇṭha's ***pariṇāmavāda* as *vivartavāda*** establishes Śivādvaita Vedānta as an independent school, distinct from the mainstream Śaiva Siddhānta of this period.

Both terms are already nodes, and they are **the axis on which Ch 11 sorts Vedānta**:

| | [pariṇāmavāda](11-vedanta.md) | [vivartavāda](11-vedanta.md) |
|---|---|---|
| the claim | causes **really transform** into effects | the cause **appears** to transform without really doing so |
| the world is | a true modification of Brahman | appearance |
| whose | Bhedābheda, Viśiṣṭādvaita, Sāṃkhya | Advaita |

Śrīkaṇṭha's *bhāṣya* is a **pariṇāmavāda** text — Śiva really becomes the world, which is why the school is a *qualified* non-dualism and sits naturally beside Śaiva Siddhānta. **Appayya reads it as *vivartavāda*** — and the school is thereby detached from Śaiva Siddhānta and stood up as Vedānta in its own right.

> A school founded not by a new revelation, a new text or a new deity, but **by relocating an existing commentary on the causation question.**

⚠ **And here is what the corpus should register about itself.** The *pariṇāma*/*vivarta* distinction was inherited from Ch 11 as **a taxonomy for sorting schools**. It turns out to have been, in at least one case, **the instrument that made a school.** Move a text across the line and its community's affiliation moves with it. A classification scheme that can do that is not a neutral description of the terrain.

### 6.1 The internal evidence that makes the dispute real {#mismatch}

There is a concrete, checkable datum under all of this: **Appayya's doctrine and Śrīkaṇṭha's do not match.**

Śrīkaṇṭha holds a *qualified* non-dualism — Śiva as supreme personal deity possessing qualities, the universe emerging from God's energy while remaining grounded in divine reality, "avoiding both pure monism and strict dualism" (Dasgupta). Appayya "affirms a form of **pure** non-dualism," and in the *Śivādvaitanirṇaya* "recasts Śrīkaṇṭha's work." Dasgupta describes the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* as "attempting to **harmonize** [Śrīkaṇṭha's positions] with Śaṅkara's monism."

**Harmonising is not expounding.** So the *Śivārkamaṇidīpikā* is a commentary whose author holds a different position from the text he comments on — and that is visible without reading a word of the modern scholarship.

### 6.2 Three things that can be invented, and only one of them was {#invention}

Śrīkaṇṭha is `contested` across **five** readings, from an ancient pre-Rāmānuja ācārya (one Śaiva lineage account gives **3100 BCE at Warangal**) through Dasgupta's "it is not possible for us as yet to locate his time exactly" to Duquette's 14th–15th century.

**Lawrence McCrea**'s 2016 paper is titled for the invention of **Śrīkaṇṭha's *Vedānta***. A popular web source encountered while writing the node had already slid from that to "**Śrīkaṇṭha Śivācārya was a creation of Appayya Dīkṣita**."

The node refuses the slide by making a distinction that is worth memorising:

| | what it would mean |
|---|---|
| inventing a **man** | fabricating an author who never existed |
| inventing a **text** | forging a *bhāṣya* and backdating it |
| inventing a ***Vedānta*** | taking a **real text by a real author** and constructing out of it a philosophical system it did not previously constitute |

**Only the third is what the title states** — and it is by far the most ordinary of the three, because it is simply *what a powerful commentator does*. Wikipedia's summary of McCrea has Appayya **retrieving** "the obscure commentary of Śrīkaṇṭha": the commentary was **found, not forged**. What was invented was the *Vedānta* read out of it, and the audience that received it as one.

> This is [§0 of the charter](../../../CLAUDE.md) in a domain far from physics. The failure mode is identical to the "the ancient sages already knew quantum mechanics" slide: **take a real, defensible scholarly claim and upgrade it one notch into a more exciting one.** The corpus caught a live specimen of it here, and Ch 35 §10 shows the same discipline applied to a temptation of our own making.

⚠ **McCrea's paper itself was never obtained** — Springer, PhilPapers and ResearchGate all refused. What is recorded is a citation plus a third party's summary: one level better than a bare title, one level short of having read it.

## 7. Compilation, again {#compilation}

Ch 35 §5.3 proposed a rule: *in a literature transmitted by quotation, compilation is a survival strategy — usually somebody else's.* **Nāthamuni is the same rule outside medicine, and in a sharper form.**

He did two things, and **neither survives in his own hand**:

1. He **compiled the *Nālāyira Divya Prabandham***, the four thousand Tamil verses of the twelve Āḻvārs, and set them to music for temple recitation.
2. He wrote the ***Nyāyatattva*** and the ***Yogarahasya***. **Both are lost.**

The first act is the one that matters most, and it is easy to undervalue because it is editorial rather than doctrinal. It is what makes Śrī Vaiṣṇavism **a Sanskrit philosophical tradition with a Tamil devotional canon inside it.** Everything Ch 11 and Ch 18 record about the school's devotional character — [Lakṣmī](../devotional/18-hindu-deities.md) as necessary mediator, prapatti, the whole affective register — descends from **an act of gathering**, not from a doctrine.

### 7.1 A recovery-legend, read for what survives the miracle {#prabandham}

The tradition: Nāthamuni heard only **ten** hymns of Nammāḻvār at Kumbakonam; went to Tirukkurugūr; chanted the associated verses **12,000 times** beneath a sacred tree; whereupon **Nammāḻvār appeared and gave him all four thousand** compositions.

That is recorded as what the tradition says about its own canon, not as an event. But its structure repays attention, because the account is unusually candid: **it concedes that the corpus was lost and that only a fragment was in circulation**, and supplies a supernatural mechanism for the remainder.

Strip the miracle and an ordinary, entirely credible claim remains: *one man assembled a scattered oral corpus, and the tradition could not say from where.* **A text-critical statement dressed as a vision.**

### 7.2 The lost work, recovered from its citations {#nyayatattva}

The *Nyāyatattva* is being reconstructed from quotation in Yāmuna, Rāmānuja, Sudarśanasūri and Veṅkaṭanātha — of which **Vedānta Deśika alone supplies forty-four citations**. Its thesis: God is *abhinna-nimittopādāna-kāraṇa*, **the non-different instrumental *and* material cause**, against the Naiyāyika position that keeps the two apart and leaves God merely instrumental.

Two consequences:

- **The title is not an attack.** *Nyāyatattva*, "the truth of Nyāya," **appropriates the Naiyāyika apparatus and rejects only its conclusion.** Compare §6.2 from the other direction: a title can conceal that a school's *method* is being adopted, just as it can conceal who the real opponent is.
- **It moves the doctrine's floor.** God as material cause is exactly what makes Viśiṣṭādvaita a *pariṇāma* Vedānta (§6). On this evidence the position is in the lineage **two generations before Rāmānuja**, in a work no longer extant — established on **citation evidence**, not on tradition's say-so.

## 8. One passage that inverts when moved {#agamapramanya}

A single example, from the one primary text this chapter's nodes read at first hand — Yāmuna's ***Āgamaprāmāṇya***, in the 1972 Ubhaya Vedanta Granthamala volume.

Early in the work stands this:

> "We do not regard Brahmins as a distinct species, different from the species man"

Read alone, it makes a 10th-century Vaiṣṇava sound like a critic of caste.

**It is not Yāmuna's thesis, and its purpose is restrictive.** The sentence sits inside an Objection/Refutation exchange in which the speaker is arguing **against** Bhāgavata authority. The Objection proposes that Bhāgavatas are exemplary *because* they are Brahmins; this Refutation denies that *brāhmaṇatva* is a perceptible natural kind — tuft and sacred thread prove nothing, being "worn illegally by blackguards" — and concludes that the only criterion is **settled usage**, which does not call Bhāgavatas Brahmins. It then cites Manu to class *Sātvata* as a low-caste term.

**A nominalist premise driving an exclusionary conclusion — and it is the objection Yāmuna's book exists to defeat.**

> **In a dialectical text, a sentence's position is part of its meaning.** Sanskrit śāstra is built out of *pūrvapakṣa* and *siddhānta*, and a quotation lifted from a *pūrvapakṣa* can carry the opposite of the author's view. This is the commonest way a genuine quotation becomes a false claim.

⚠ Two honest caveats, from the node: the OCR of the scanned volume has **page-order disturbances**, so passages were located by the translation's own section numbers; and the dialectical attribution rests on the **printed "Objection / Refutation" labels**, not on inference from content.

What the *Āgamaprāmāṇya* actually argues is worth a line, because it is a good piece of philosophy: it defends the **Pāñcarātra Āgamas' Vedic-grade authority** on Mīmāṃsā's *own* ground, accepting the *śabda*-pramāṇa framework and the *apauruṣeya* criterion and then denying that Pāñcarātra falls outside them. A second thrust it must answer runs by inference — "Pāñcarātra Śāstra is non-authoritative, because it is **non-Vedic, like Buddhism**."

## 9. What this chapter rests on {#provenance}

The evidence here is **better** than Ch 35's, because it is not one scholar. **IEP** (Shyam Ranganathan on Rāmānuja), the ***Encyclopedia of Religion*** (Carman on Rāmānuja, **Karl H. Potter** on Madhva), **John Bowker**'s *Concise Oxford Dictionary of World Religions*, **Rao & Sharma** in *Asiatische Studien* (peer-reviewed, read in full), **Dasgupta**, **Duquette**, and — for Yāmuna — a **1972 critical edition with Sanskrit commentary**, the *Siddhitraya* with Uttamūr T. Vīrarāghavācārya's *Gūḍhaprakāśa*.

**What is weak, and named as such:**

- **No primary Sanskrit was read** for Rāmānuja, Madhva or Śrīkaṇṭha. Not the *Śrī Bhāṣya*, not the *Anuvyākhyāna*, not the *Brahmamīmāṃsābhāṣya*.
- **Three key papers were never obtained**: McCrea 2016, Mesquita's monograph (known only through Rao & Sharma's verbatim quotation of it), and Sydnor 2012.
- **Rao & Sharma are interested parties** — Dvaita scholars answering an attack on Madhva — and are used for bibliography, chronology and their quotation of others, not for verdicts. ⚠ Weighting a peer-reviewed source is not deferring to it: they call Appayya "17th cent." in one section and "three centuries later" than Madhva nine pages on. Only the second fits `appayya-dikshita.md`'s **inscription-anchored** c. 1520–1593, and the corpus corrected rather than adopted.
- **`ramanuja.md`'s account of the *saptavidhā anupapatti*** — the seven untenables of avidyā — asserts only the scheme's existence, its first member (*āśraya*), and its target. Grimes 1990 proves the scheme; the enumerated list came back only from a listserv and a blog, which name six between them and disagree. [Ch 37](../../cross-tradition/37-the-dialecticians.md) holds the other end of that quarrel.

**Upgrade paths, in order of value:** Sydnor 2012 and Carman's *The Theology of Rāmānuja* (1974); Mesquita's monograph itself; McCrea 2016; van Buitenen's 1971 edition of the *Āgamaprāmāṇya*; Vijayīndra Tīrtha's *Madhvamatamukhabhūṣaṇa* — the 16th-century reply to Appayya that Mesquita omitted.

**One dead lead, logged with its cause.** A 2025 conference abstract on reconstructing the *Nyāyatattva* had a URL that **now serves cryptocurrency-casino spam**. The paper is real; the domain is not to be cited or re-fetched. *A plausible academic URL that has been repurposed is a failure mode worth naming.*
